## Summary

Dame Vivienne Westwood (8 April 1941 – 29 December 2022) was a British fashion designer who brought punk and new wave clothing into mainstream fashion. Working with Malcolm McLaren from her King's Road shop in the 1970s, she dressed the Sex Pistols and created the ripped, safety-pinned, slogan-covered look that defined British punk.

From 1981 she moved to the catwalk and built an independent label known for corsets, tartan, platform shoes and historical tailoring reinterpreted with irreverence. She was named British Designer of the Year in 1990 and 1991, appointed OBE in 1992 and DBE in 2006.

In her later decades Westwood used her fame for activism on climate change, civil liberties and nuclear disarmament, becoming one of the most recognisable political voices in fashion until her death at 81.

## Early Life & Background

Westwood was born Vivienne Isabel Swire in Tintwistle, then in Cheshire and now in Derbyshire, the daughter of a cotton weaver and a greengrocer. Her family moved to Harrow, north-west London, in 1958. She briefly attended Harrow Art School but left after one term, later saying she did not know how a working-class girl could make a living in art. She trained as a primary school teacher and made jewellery to sell on Portobello Road.

In 1962 she married Derek Westwood, with whom she had a son, Ben, in 1963. The marriage ended after she met Malcolm McLaren, then an art student. Their son Joseph Corré was born in 1967.

## Rise to Prominence

In 1971 Westwood and McLaren opened a shop at 430 King's Road, Chelsea. Its name and stock changed repeatedly: Let It Rock (1971), Too Fast to Live, Too Young to Die (1972), SEX (1974), Seditionaries (1976) and World's End (1980). The SEX and Seditionaries periods produced bondage trousers, mohair jumpers and provocative T-shirts that McLaren's band, the Sex Pistols, wore on stage.

Westwood's first runway collection, "Pirate", for autumn/winter 1981, launched the New Romantic look. It was followed by "Savage" (1982), "Buffalo Girls" (1982), "Witches" (1983) and, after her split from McLaren in the mid-1980s, the "Mini-Crini" (1985) and "Harris Tweed" (1987) collections that established her signature blend of English tailoring and historical silhouette.

## Key Achievements

- Named British Designer of the Year in both 1990 and 1991.
- Appointed OBE in 1992 and Dame Commander (DBE) in 2006 for services to fashion.
- The 1993 "Anglomania" show, at which Naomi Campbell fell in 12-inch platform shoes, became one of fashion's most reproduced images.
- The Victoria and Albert Museum staged a retrospective, "Vivienne Westwood: 34 Years in Fashion", in 2004, its largest exhibition for a living British designer at the time.
- Built Vivienne Westwood Ltd into a global company with Gold Label, Red Label, Anglomania and menswear lines, run with her husband and creative partner Andreas Kronthaler, whom she married in 1992.
- Launched the Climate Revolution campaign in 2012 and worked with Greenpeace, Cool Earth and Liberty; in 2020 she was suspended in a giant birdcage outside the Old Bailey in support of Julian Assange.

## Controversies & Criticism

Early punk garments used swastikas and other shock imagery, which drew accusations of trivialising fascism. Her 1989 Tatler cover impersonating Margaret Thatcher offended the Prime Minister's supporters. Critics noted a tension between her "buy less, choose well" message and running a luxury business with hundreds of products a season. In 2014 reports that her companies were controlled through a Luxembourg holding structure prompted questions about tax, and her support for Assange and criticism of the Iraq war and fracking made her a polarising public figure.

## Legacy & Influence

Westwood is credited with turning street subculture into high fashion and with reviving the corset, the crinoline and tartan for modern wear. Designers including Alexander McQueen, John Galliano and Marc Jacobs cited her influence, and her archive continues to be exhibited and referenced in museum collections worldwide. Kronthaler has continued the label since her death.
