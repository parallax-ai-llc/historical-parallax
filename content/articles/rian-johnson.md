## Summary

Rian Johnson is an American film director, screenwriter, and producer known for genre-bending films that combine classical storytelling structures with modern sensibilities. He emerged from the independent scene with the neo-noir *Brick* (2005) and went on to direct the time-travel thriller *Looper* (2012), the blockbuster *Star Wars: The Last Jedi* (2017), and the whodunit franchise that began with *Knives Out* (2019).

Johnson matters because he has repeatedly revived older forms, such as hardboiled detective fiction and the Agatha Christie-style mystery, for contemporary audiences while retaining creative control over his projects. His work has earned two Academy Award nominations for screenwriting, and his production company T-Street has become a notable independent force in film and television.

## Early Life & Background

Johnson was born on December 17, 1973, in Silver Spring, Maryland, and grew up in San Clemente, California. He developed an early interest in filmmaking and attended the University of Southern California School of Cinematic Arts, graduating in 1996. During his student years he made short films and began writing the screenplay that would become *Brick*, drawing on the novels of Dashiell Hammett. After graduation he worked in editing and other production jobs while spending several years trying to finance his first feature.

## Rise to Prominence

*Brick* was shot in San Clemente on a budget of roughly $450,000, with Joseph Gordon-Levitt as a high school student investigating a murder using the language of 1940s detective fiction. The film premiered at the Sundance Film Festival in 2005, where it won the Special Jury Prize for Originality of Vision. His follow-up, the caper *The Brothers Bloom* (2008), received mixed reviews and modest box office returns.

Between features, Johnson directed three episodes of AMC's *Breaking Bad*: "Fly" (2010), "Fifty-One" (2012), and "Ozymandias" (2013). The last of these is frequently cited among the best television episodes ever made. *Looper* (2012), starring Gordon-Levitt and Bruce Willis, was made for about $30 million and grossed more than $176 million worldwide, establishing Johnson as a commercially viable director.

## Key Achievements

- **Star Wars: The Last Jedi (2017)**: Johnson wrote and directed the eighth episode of the Skywalker saga, which grossed over $1.3 billion worldwide and received strong critical reviews.
- **Knives Out (2019)**: Made for around $40 million, the film earned more than $310 million globally and brought Johnson an Academy Award nomination for Best Original Screenplay. In March 2021 Netflix acquired two sequels for a reported $469 million.
- **Glass Onion: A Knives Out Mystery (2022)**: The sequel earned a second Oscar nomination, for Best Adapted Screenplay. A third film, *Wake Up Dead Man*, premiered at the Toronto International Film Festival in September 2025 and was released on Netflix in December 2025.
- **Poker Face (2023–)**: Johnson created the Peacock mystery series starring Natasha Lyonne, which received Emmy nominations and a second season in 2025.
- **T-Street**: Founded in 2019 with longtime producing partner Ram Bergman, the company produces Johnson's own work alongside projects by other filmmakers.

## Controversies & Criticism

*The Last Jedi* produced one of the most polarized fan responses in the franchise's history. Critics of the film objected to its treatment of Luke Skywalker, its subplot involving the character Rose Tico, and its subversion of questions raised by *The Force Awakens*. Mark Hamill publicly voiced disagreement with aspects of Luke's arc before later expressing support for the film. Actress Kelly Marie Tran left social media in 2018 after sustained harassment from some fans. Johnson's willingness to engage with detractors online drew both praise and accusations of dismissiveness. A separate *Star Wars* trilogy announced for him in November 2017 has not materialized.

Johnson has also been criticized, and has partly agreed, regarding Netflix's one-week theatrical release for *Glass Onion*, which he said he wished had been longer.

## Legacy & Influence

Johnson has helped demonstrate that original, mid-budget genre films can succeed commercially in an era dominated by franchises. The *Knives Out* series contributed to a broader revival of the ensemble murder mystery in film and television. His long-running collaborations, including scores by his cousin Nathan Johnson and producing by Bergman, exemplify a model of sustained independent authorship within the studio and streaming systems.
