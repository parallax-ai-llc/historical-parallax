## Summary

Justine Triet is a French film director and screenwriter best known for *Anatomy of a Fall* (2023), the courtroom drama that won the Palme d'Or at the Cannes Film Festival and the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay. She became only the third woman to win the Palme d'Or as a solo director, after Jane Campion in 1993 and Julia Ducournau in 2021.

Her work moves between documentary and fiction, and her feature films share a fascination with women under pressure, the instability of truth, and the way private life is reconstructed by institutions such as courts, clinics, and the media. She frequently collaborates with actor Virginie Efira and with her partner, the filmmaker Arthur Harari, who co-wrote *Sibyl* and *Anatomy of a Fall*.

Triet's rise from festival sidebars to the top prizes in world cinema within a decade has made her one of the most prominent French filmmakers of her generation and a frequent reference point in debates about women directors, French cultural policy, and the international reach of French-language film.

## Early Life & Background

Triet was born in July 1978 in Fécamp, a port town in Normandy. She studied fine arts at the École nationale supérieure des Beaux-Arts de Paris, where she gravitated toward video and painting before turning to filmmaking. Her early work was documentary. *Sur place* (2007) filmed the 2006 student protests against the CPE labour contract, and *Solférino* (2008) observed crowds outside the Socialist Party headquarters on the night of the 2007 presidential election. *Des ombres dans la maison* (2010), shot in São Paulo, followed residents of a housing project. Her fiction short *Vilaine fille, mauvais garçon* (2012) was selected at the Locarno Film Festival, and its cast included Laetitia Dosch, who would star in her first feature.

## Rise to Prominence

Her debut feature, *La Bataille de Solférino* (2013, released internationally as *Age of Panic*), placed a fictional custody dispute inside real footage of the 2012 election night in Paris. Shown in the ACID sidebar at Cannes, it established her mix of chaos, comedy, and improvised naturalism. *Victoria* (2016, *In Bed with Victoria*) opened Critics' Week at Cannes and drew about 700,000 admissions in France, an unusual result for an independent comedy; it earned Virginie Efira a César nomination for Best Actress. *Sibyl* (2019), about a psychotherapist who exploits a patient's confessions for a novel, marked Triet's first selection in the main Cannes competition.

## Key Achievements

*Anatomy of a Fall* premiered in competition at Cannes in May 2023 and won the Palme d'Or. The film, starring Sandra Hüller as a novelist tried for her husband's death, grossed more than $30 million worldwide and became one of the highest-earning French-language films of the decade internationally. It received five Academy Award nominations, including Best Picture, Best Director, and Best Actress, and won Best Original Screenplay for Triet and Harari. It also won two Golden Globes, the BAFTA for Best Original Screenplay, and six César Awards in February 2024, including Best Film and Best Director. Snoop, the border collie who played the guide dog Messi, won the Palm Dog at Cannes.

## Controversies & Criticism

Triet's Palme d'Or acceptance speech in 2023 criticised the French government for pushing through pension reform despite mass protests and accused it of undermining the "cultural exception" that funds French cinema. Culture Minister Rima Abdul Malak responded that she was "flabbergasted" by what she called an ungrateful speech from a filmmaker whose work had benefited from public funding, and several politicians and producers echoed the criticism. Months later, France's Oscar selection committee chose *The Taste of Things* rather than *Anatomy of a Fall* as its submission for Best International Feature, a decision widely read as political, although the committee denied it. Some critics also questioned the film's ambiguity about guilt and its portrayal of the French judicial process.

## Legacy & Influence

Triet's success demonstrated that a dialogue-heavy French drama with a female protagonist could compete at the highest level of both the festival circuit and the Anglophone awards season. Her screenplay is now a common teaching example for courtroom structure and unreliable narration, and *Anatomy of a Fall* revived interest in the legal drama across European production. She has also become a reference figure for filmmakers who argue that public funding and artistic independence must coexist, a debate her speech pushed into mainstream French politics.
