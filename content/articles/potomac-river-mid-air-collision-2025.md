## Summary

On the night of 29 January 2025, American Eagle Flight 5342, a PSA Airlines Bombardier CRJ700 arriving from Wichita, Kansas, collided with a U.S. Army UH-60 Black Hawk helicopter over the Potomac River on final approach to Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport [^1]. All 67 people aboard the two aircraft were killed, 64 on the airliner and three soldiers on the helicopter [^1]. The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) called it the deadliest U.S. aviation accident since November 2001 [^2]. Its investigation blamed systemic failures in airspace design, safety oversight and risk management by the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and the Army [^2].

## Background

Reagan National is one of the busiest runways in the United States and sits in airspace crowded with military, police and medical helicopters that use published low-altitude routes along the Potomac [^1]. Helicopters on the route near the airport were required to stay at or below 200 feet, which left only a small vertical gap below airliners approaching Runway 33 [^1]. The NTSB later found that the FAA's helicopter route design did not provide adequate separation from commercial approach paths, and that neither aircraft carried collision-avoidance technology suited to low-altitude flying [^2].

## The Event

The Black Hawk, call sign PAT25, was flying a night training and evaluation flight from Davison Army Airfield at Fort Belvoir with Captain Rebecca Lobach, Chief Warrant Officer 2 Andrew Eaves and Staff Sergeant Ryan O'Hara aboard [^1]. The CRJ700, registration N709PS, carried 60 passengers and four crew and had been asked by controllers to circle to Runway 33 [^1]. The two aircraft collided at about 8:48 p.m. EST and fell into the river; there were no survivors [^1][^2]. Investigators found the helicopter was at about 278 feet at impact, above its route ceiling [^1]. Among the dead were 28 members of the figure skating community, including young athletes returning from a development camp, parents, and coaches such as 1994 world pairs champions Evgenia Shishkova and Vadim Naumov [^1].

## Response and Aftermath

On 31 January 2025 the FAA restricted helicopter traffic on two routes near the airport to police, medical, air defense and presidential flights [^1]. Families filed claims and lawsuits against the government and the airlines, and on 17 December 2025 the federal government admitted in a court filing that it was partially at fault and had breached its duty of care [^1]. The Senate passed the ROTOR Act by unanimous consent on 18 December 2025 to require aircraft, including military aircraft, to broadcast and receive ADS-B position data [^3]. The NTSB adopted its findings at a board meeting on 27 January 2026 and issued 50 safety recommendations, 33 of them to the FAA and eight to the Army [^2].

## Positive Perspectives

- The FAA moved within two days to curtail helicopter flights on the routes that crossed the airport's approach paths [^1].
- The NTSB completed a full investigation in about one year and issued 50 recommendations on route design, air traffic control, safety management and collision-avoidance technology [^2].
- The federal government's admission of partial liability was seen as an important step for victims' families seeking compensation [^1].
- The Senate passed the ROTOR Act unanimously, aiming to close the military ADS-B gap identified after the crash [^3].
- The NTSB found that ADS-B In alerting could have given the airline crew about 59 seconds of warning, pointing to a concrete fix [^2].

## Negative Perspectives

- The NTSB described the hazards as "serious and long-standing safety gaps" in the capital's airspace that had not been addressed before the crash [^2].
- Investigators cited a high controller workload and the use of separate radio frequencies for the helicopter and the airliner [^2].
- The Army was found to lack effective safety management for operations in civilian airspace, and the helicopter flew above its permitted altitude [^1][^2].
- The ROTOR Act failed to reach the two-thirds majority needed in the House on 24 February 2026, after more than 130 Republicans voted against it [^4].
- On the eve of the House vote, the Pentagon reversed its earlier support for the ROTOR Act, citing budgetary burdens and operational security risks [^5].

## Recent News

NTSB Chair Jennifer Homendy testified on the final report before the Senate Commerce Committee on 12 February 2026 [^6]. The final report was published in February 2026 with 74 findings and 50 recommendations [^1]. After the House vote failed in February 2026, the ROTOR Act was left to the slower standard legislative process [^4].
