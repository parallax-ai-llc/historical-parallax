## Summary
On 29 December 2024, Jeju Air Flight 2216, a Boeing 737-800 from Bangkok, belly-landed at Muan International Airport in South Korea after a bird strike, overran the runway and crashed into a concrete-cored berm supporting an instrument landing system antenna [^1]. All 175 passengers and four of the six crew died; two flight attendants seated in the detached tail survived [^1]. It was the deadliest air disaster in South Korea and the deadliest involving a South Korean airliner since 1997 [^1]. The investigation has been contested, with debate over pilot actions and the role of the berm [^2][^3].

## Background
The crash occurred two days after acting prime minister Choi Sang-mok became acting president, following the impeachments of President Yoon Suk Yeol over his December 2024 martial law declaration and of acting president Han Duck-soo [^1]. Muan airport's runway 19 localizer antenna sat on an earth berm encasing a concrete structure beyond the runway end [^1]. Jeju Air, a low-cost carrier, had no fatal accidents in its previous 19 years [^1].

## The Event
As the jet approached Muan, birds identified as Baikal teal, a migratory duck, struck both engines, with the right engine badly damaged [^1]. The pilots declared a mayday and went around [^1]. Both the cockpit voice recorder and flight data recorder stopped working seconds before the mayday call [^1]. On the second approach, from the opposite direction, the landing gear was not deployed, and at 9:03 a.m. the aircraft touched down far along the runway on its engine nacelles [^1]. Data disclosed in January 2026 put the jet's speed at about 374 km/h on touchdown; reports of its speed when it reached the embankment about 30 seconds later ranged from roughly 232 to 280 km/h [^3][^4]. It hit the approach lights and then the berm and exploded; both engines were embedded in the structure [^1].

## Response and Aftermath
The government declared Muan a special disaster zone and set a national mourning period until 5 January 2025, with nearly 158,000 people visiting memorial altars [^1]. Police searched Muan airport, a regional aviation office and Jeju Air's headquarters on 2 January 2025 [^1]. The director of the Aviation and Railway Accident Investigation Board (ARAIB) resigned on 7 January, and a preliminary report was released on 27 January 2025 [^1]. In July 2025 a planned briefing was cancelled after families rejected findings indicating the crew had shut down the less-damaged left engine instead of the right [^2].

## Positive Perspectives
- The government declared a special disaster zone, set up 90 memorial altars in 17 cities, and Jeju Air deployed 260 employees to assist families in Muan [^1].
- The National Assembly created a cross-party special committee to investigate the crash [^1].
- In December 2025 the Anti-Corruption and Civil Rights Commission ruled that the localizer embankment violated rules requiring frangible structures [^1].
- Parliament passed a bill on 22 December 2025 to launch an independent probe, responding to families' demands [^1].
- On the first anniversary, President Lee Jae Myung apologised to victims' families [^1].

## Negative Perspectives
- The rigid concrete berm, rather than a frangible structure, sharply increased the death toll, according to experts and a government-commissioned analysis [^1][^3].
- Families said the July 2025 findings put "all the blame on the dead birds and the dead pilots," and the pilots' union called them a "one-sided announcement" [^2].
- The loss of both flight recorders before the mayday left gaps in the record of the final minutes [^1].
- ARAIB delayed its interim report in December 2025, missing an ICAO expectation for an update within a year [^1].
- Families criticised Jeju Air and officials for a lack of real-time updates in the hours after the crash [^1].

## Recent News
In January 2026, analysis for ARAIB by the Computational Structural Engineering Institute of Korea, released by lawmaker Jeong Jun-ho, concluded that without the localizer embankment the aircraft would have slid to a stop "with no fatalities," and estimated impact forces of at least 20 times gravity [^3]. ARAIB had said its final report was expected in June 2026 [^2].
