## Summary

Laufey Lín Bing Jónsdóttir, known professionally as Laufey, is an Icelandic-Chinese singer, songwriter, cellist, pianist, and guitarist born on 23 April 1999 in Reykjavík. Her music blends jazz standards, classical orchestration, bossa nova, and contemporary pop songwriting, and she has become the most prominent young artist bringing the Great American Songbook tradition to a Gen Z streaming audience.

Her second studio album, *Bewitched* (2023), won the Grammy Award for Best Traditional Pop Vocal Album at the 66th ceremony in February 2024 and set the record for the largest single-day streaming debut for a jazz album on Spotify. Her third album, *A Matter of Time* (2025), expanded her sound toward orchestral pop and earned further Grammy recognition.

Laufey matters because she demonstrated that jazz-inflected, acoustically arranged music could achieve mainstream commercial scale through TikTok and streaming, opening a lane for a generation of listeners who had little prior exposure to the genre.

## Early Life & Background

Laufey was born in Reykjavík to an Icelandic father and a Chinese mother, Lin Wei, a classical violinist who performed with the Iceland Symphony Orchestra. Her maternal grandfather was a violin professor at the Central Conservatory of Music in Beijing, and she spent part of her childhood in Washington, D.C., and Beijing. Her twin sister, Júnía, is a visual artist and handles much of her creative direction.

She began piano at age four and cello at eight. At 15, she performed as a cello soloist with the Iceland Symphony Orchestra. She reached the final of *Ísland Got Talent* in 2014 and later competed on *The Voice Iceland*. In 2017 she enrolled at Berklee College of Music in Boston on a Presidential Scholarship, graduating in 2021.

## Rise to Prominence

Laufey released her debut single, "Street by Street," in April 2020 while still a student. It reached number one on Icelandic radio charts. Her debut EP, *Typical of Me*, followed in April 2021, and her first full-length album, *Everything I Know About Love*, arrived on 26 August 2022 via AWAL.

Her breakthrough came with "From the Start," released in May 2023, a bossa nova track that went viral on TikTok and became her first entry on the Billboard Hot 100. *Bewitched* was released on 8 September 2023, debuting at number 23 on the Billboard 200 and number one on the Jazz Albums chart. She also released *A Night at the Symphony* (2023), a live album recorded with the Iceland Symphony Orchestra.

## Key Achievements

- **Grammy Award**, Best Traditional Pop Vocal Album, for *Bewitched* (February 2024)
- **Streaming record**: *Bewitched* logged roughly 5.7 million Spotify streams on release day, the largest debut for a jazz album on the platform
- **Bewitched: The Goddess Edition** (April 2024), a deluxe reissue with four new songs
- **A Matter of Time** (22 August 2025), her third album, preceded by singles "Silver Lining," "Tough Luck," and "Lover Girl"; it was nominated for Best Traditional Pop Vocal Album at the 68th Grammy Awards in February 2026
- **Orchestral performances** with the Los Angeles Philharmonic at the Hollywood Bowl, the Boston Pops, and other major ensembles
- **Laufey Foundation**, launched in 2024 to fund youth music education and instrument access

## Controversies & Criticism

The most persistent debate around Laufey concerns genre classification. Some jazz musicians and critics argue that her work is pop music dressed in jazz aesthetics, and questioned whether *Bewitched* belonged in a jazz category. Laufey has responded that she does not call herself a jazz singer and sees her role as a bridge that introduces younger listeners to the tradition.

Critics have also described her songwriting as nostalgic pastiche, arguing that her arrangements imitate mid-century standards without formal innovation. Supporters counter that the same charge was leveled at Norah Jones and Michael Bublé, both of whom sustained long careers.

## Legacy & Influence

Laufey is credited with reviving commercial interest in orchestral and jazz-adjacent pop among listeners under 30. Her success prompted labels to sign similar acoustic, vocally driven artists, and her collaborations with Beabadoobee, dodie, and Norah Jones connected the bedroom-pop and traditional-pop worlds. Streaming data showed measurable spillover into catalogues by Chet Baker and Ella Fitzgerald after her viral moments. Her mixed Icelandic-Chinese background has also made her a visible figure for Asian-diaspora representation in Western popular music.
