## Summary

Bella Poarch is a Filipino-born American social media personality and singer who became one of the most-followed creators on TikTok after a single lip-sync clip in August 2020 went viral. That video, set to Millie B's "Soph Aspin Send" (popularly known as "M to the B"), became the most-liked TikTok post of all time, surpassing 50 million likes and turning a then-unknown U.S. Navy veteran into a global internet figure within weeks.

Poarch converted that attention into a music career, signing with Warner Records in 2021 and debuting with "Build a Bitch," which entered the Billboard Hot 100. Her trajectory is frequently cited as a case study in how short-form video platforms can create celebrities and launch recording careers without traditional industry gatekeeping.

Beyond her follower counts, Poarch has drawn attention for speaking openly about childhood abuse, military service, and mental health, and for controversies over a tattoo and rumored ties to other celebrities that accompanied her sudden rise.

## Early Life & Background

Poarch was born on February 8, 1997, in the Philippines. She has said she was found in a slum as an infant and adopted at around age three by a Filipino mother and an American father, then raised on a farm in the Philippines. She has described her adoptive upbringing as physically and emotionally abusive, including being made to do heavy farm labor as a young child.

When she was about 13, her family moved to San Francisco, California. In 2015, at age 18, she enlisted in the United States Navy, later saying she joined partly to escape her home environment. She served for roughly four years as an aviation ordnanceman, with postings that included Japan and Hawaii, and left the service in 2019.

## Rise to Prominence

Poarch created her TikTok account in early 2020. On August 17, 2020, she posted a close-up lip-sync clip to "M to the B," moving her head in time with the beat. The video accumulated tens of millions of likes within weeks and, by October 2020, held the record as the platform's most-liked video, a position it maintained for years. Her follower count surged past 20 million by late 2020 and exceeded 90 million by 2023, placing her among the top three most-followed accounts on the platform.

In May 2021 she signed with Warner Records and released her debut single "Build a Bitch" on May 14, 2021. The music video featured other internet personalities including Valkyrae, Mia Khalifa, Bretman Rock, and ZHC, and passed 100 million views on YouTube within about a month. Follow-up singles included "Inferno" (July 2021) with Sub Urban, and "Dolls" (2022), which led her debut EP of the same name, released August 12, 2022.

## Key Achievements

- Holder of the most-liked TikTok video, with more than 50 million likes, set in 2020.
- "Build a Bitch" debuted at number 56 on the Billboard Hot 100 and charted internationally, a rare feat for a creator's first release.
- Named to the Forbes 30 Under 30 list in 2022 in the social media category.
- Amassed one of the largest TikTok followings in the world, exceeding 90 million followers.

## Controversies & Criticism

In September 2020, Poarch faced backlash for a tattoo of the Japanese Rising Sun flag on her arm, a symbol many in Korea and other Asian countries associate with Japanese imperialism. She issued a public apology, said she had been unaware of its history, and later had the tattoo covered.

Around the same period, unverified rumors circulated of a relationship with rapper Tyga, which she denied. Critics have also questioned the depth of her creative involvement in her music and argued that her fame illustrates a platform algorithm rewarding simple, repetitive content. In 2022, court records revealed she had been married since 2019 and had filed for divorce, prompting commentary about a persona managed for public consumption.

## Legacy & Influence

Poarch is an emblematic figure of the TikTok era, demonstrating how a single short clip can produce mainstream fame and a record deal. Her openness about abuse, military service, and post-traumatic stress has been credited with encouraging discussion of mental health among younger audiences. She remains a reference point in discussions of creator-to-musician pipelines and Filipino representation in Western pop culture.
