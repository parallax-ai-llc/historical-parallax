## Summary

Virgil Abloh (September 30, 1980 – November 28, 2021) was an American fashion designer, entrepreneur, and DJ who became one of the most influential figures in fashion during the 2010s. He founded the luxury streetwear label Off-White in 2013 and in 2018 was appointed artistic director of menswear at Louis Vuitton, making him the first Black designer to hold that position at the French house and one of the few Black creative directors at a major European luxury brand.

Abloh's importance lies in how he dissolved the boundary between streetwear and high fashion. Trained as an engineer and architect rather than a fashion designer, he approached clothing through ideas borrowed from conceptual art, particularly the notion that altering an existing object by "three percent" could make it new. His collaborations with Nike, IKEA, and Evian, along with his work with Kanye West, brought luxury design language to a mass audience.

He died of cardiac angiosarcoma, a rare cancer he had kept private for two years, at age 41. His death prompted tributes from across fashion, music, and art, and his final Louis Vuitton collection was shown posthumously in Miami on November 30, 2021.

## Early Life & Background

Abloh was born in Rockford, Illinois, to Ghanaian immigrant parents. His mother was a seamstress who taught him the basics of sewing, and his father managed a paint company. He attended Boylan Catholic High School, where he skateboarded and absorbed hip-hop and skate culture.

He earned a Bachelor of Science in civil engineering from the University of Wisconsin–Madison in 2002 and a Master of Architecture from the Illinois Institute of Technology in 2006. At IIT he studied in a building designed by Rem Koolhaas, whose work shaped his interest in design that crossed disciplines. During graduate school he began writing about design and started producing T-shirts and graphics.

## Rise to Prominence

In 2009, Abloh and Kanye West interned together at Fendi in Rome, an experience that connected him to LVMH executives. West named him creative director of the agency Donda in 2010, and Abloh art-directed the 2011 album *Watch the Throne*, which earned a Grammy nomination for Best Recording Package.

His first label, Pyrex Vision, launched in 2012. It consisted largely of deadstock Ralph Lauren flannel shirts screen-printed with "PYREX 23" and resold at markups of several hundred percent, a move that served both as commentary and as a business. He shut it down after one season.

Off-White c/o Virgil Abloh was founded in Milan in 2013. Its quotation-mark labels, diagonal stripes, and industrial belts became recognizable signatures. In 2015 Off-White was a finalist for the LVMH Prize. Abloh's 2017 Nike collaboration, "The Ten," reworked ten classic sneakers including the Air Jordan 1 and Air Presto and became one of the most sought-after sneaker releases of the decade.

On March 26, 2018, Louis Vuitton named him artistic director of menswear. His debut show in June 2018 at the Palais-Royal in Paris featured a rainbow runway and a crowd that included West and Rihanna.

## Key Achievements

- **Louis Vuitton menswear** (2018–2021): eight collections, including the widely discussed Spring/Summer 2019 debut.
- **Off-White**: grew into a global brand; LVMH acquired a 60 percent stake in July 2021, with Abloh retaining 40 percent.
- **"Figures of Speech"** (2019): a mid-career retrospective at the Museum of Contemporary Art Chicago that later traveled to Atlanta, Boston, Brooklyn, and Doha.
- **Time 100** (2018): named one of the world's most influential people.
- **IKEA "Markerad"** (2019) and Evian, Moncler, and Mercedes-Benz collaborations extended his reach into furniture and industrial design.
- Founded the **Virgil Abloh "Post-Modern" Scholarship Fund** in 2020, raising $1 million for Black students in fashion.

## Controversies & Criticism

Abloh was frequently accused of appropriation and of relying on other people's ideas. Critics pointed to the Pyrex Vision resale model and to designs closely resembling existing work, including a 2019 dispute with artist Rhuigi Villaseñor's circle and a public exchange with Walter Van Beirendonck, who accused him of copying. Abloh defended his method as a deliberate "readymade" practice.

In June 2020, during the George Floyd protests, he was criticized for a post condemning looting and for publicizing a $50 donation to a bail fund; he apologized and clarified he had given more.

His 2019 remark that "streetwear's gonna die" drew backlash from the community that had elevated him.

## Legacy & Influence

Abloh opened doors for Black designers at the top of luxury fashion and made collaboration, irony, and open-source thinking central to how brands operate. Louis Vuitton named Pharrell Williams his successor in February 2023. In 2022 the Brooklyn Museum hosted the final stop of "Figures of Speech," and the Virgil Abloh Foundation continues his scholarship work. His influence remains visible in the mainstream acceptance of sneakers, graphic text, and deconstructed tailoring within luxury collections.
