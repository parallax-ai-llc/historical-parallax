## Summary

Valentino Clemente Ludovico Garavani, known simply as Valentino, is an Italian couturier who founded the Valentino fashion house in Rome in 1959 and led it for nearly five decades. He built one of the few Italian labels to compete directly with Paris haute couture, dressing Jacqueline Kennedy, Elizabeth Taylor, Audrey Hepburn and generations of royalty and film stars.

His work is identified with a particular shade of scarlet, widely called "Valentino red," and with an aesthetic of polished, romantic glamour that changed little across the decades. Together with his business and life partner Giancarlo Giammetti, he turned a small Roman atelier into a global brand before retiring in January 2008.

## Early Life & Background

Valentino was born on 11 May 1932 in Voghera, a town in Lombardy in northern Italy. His father, Mauro Garavani, ran an electrical supplies business, and his mother, Teresa de Biaggi, encouraged his interest in dress. He studied fashion illustration in Milan before moving to Paris in 1950, where he attended the École des Beaux-Arts and the Chambre Syndicale de la Couture Parisienne.

In Paris he apprenticed under Jean Dessès from 1950 to 1955 and then worked for Guy Laroche until 1957. These years gave him a technical grounding in the French couture tradition that shaped his entire career.

## Rise to Prominence

Valentino returned to Italy and opened his own house on Via Condotti in Rome in 1959, financed by his father. On 31 July 1960 he met Giancarlo Giammetti, an architecture student who became his partner and took over the business side of the company. Their partnership is credited with the house's commercial survival and growth.

His international breakthrough came in 1962 with a show at the Pitti Palace in Florence, which attracted American buyers and press. In 1967 he received the Neiman Marcus Award. In 1968 he presented an all-white "no-color" collection, and later that year Jacqueline Kennedy wore a Valentino dress for her wedding to Aristotle Onassis, cementing his global reputation. Ready-to-wear lines, licensing and boutiques followed through the 1970s and 1980s.

## Key Achievements

- Ran the Valentino house as creative director for 48 years, from 1959 to 2008.
- Established "Valentino red" as one of the most recognizable signature colors in fashion.
- Sold the company to Holding di Partecipazioni Industriali in 1998; the house was sold to Marzotto in 2002 and to the private equity firm Permira in 2007.
- Made a Grand Officer of the Order of Merit of the Italian Republic in 1985 and a Chevalier of the Légion d'honneur in 2006.
- Marked his 45th anniversary with a three-day celebration in Rome in July 2007 and presented his final haute couture show in Paris on 23 January 2008.
- Was the subject of the documentary "Valentino: The Last Emperor" (2008), directed by Matt Tyrnauer.

## Controversies & Criticism

Valentino's career produced fewer public disputes than those of many contemporaries, but some criticism has followed him. In 2009 Italian tax authorities investigated Valentino and Giammetti in connection with the proceeds of the sale of the company, a case that drew wide press attention. He has been criticized by animal rights groups for his long use of fur, which the Valentino brand only abandoned in 2021, after his retirement.

"The Last Emperor" also prompted debate about the opulence of his lifestyle, including multiple residences, a yacht and a private jet, at a time when haute couture was seen by some as increasingly detached from ordinary consumers. Critics have occasionally described his designs as unchanging, although admirers regard that consistency as the source of his identity.

## Legacy & Influence

Valentino is widely regarded as the last of the great postwar couturiers and as the designer who proved that an Italian house could rival Paris on its own terms. The house he built has continued under successive creative directors, including Alessandra Facchinetti, Maria Grazia Chiuri and Pierpaolo Piccioli, and Alessandro Michele, each of whom has worked with the codes he established.

His name remains attached to red-carpet dressing, evening wear and the color that bears it. He has also been active in philanthropy and in the arts, including the Valentino Garavani and Giancarlo Giammetti Foundation, which supports cultural and educational projects.
