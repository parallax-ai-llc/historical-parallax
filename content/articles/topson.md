## Summary

Topias Miikka "Topson" Taavitsainen (born 14 April 1998) is a Finnish professional Dota 2 player best known as the mid laner of OG during its back-to-back victories at The International 2018 and The International 2019. He is one of only a handful of players to have won the tournament twice, and one of the highest-earning esports competitors in history, with career prize money exceeding US$5 million.

Topson's rise was unusual even by esports standards. He joined OG in June 2018 as a largely unknown player with almost no top-tier experience and, within three months, was lifting the Aegis of Champions in Vancouver. His unconventional hero choices and aggressive, improvisational play became a signature of OG's championship era and influenced how the mid role was interpreted across the professional scene.

## Early Life & Background

Taavitsainen was born on 14 April 1998 in Finland. He began playing Dota as a child and, according to his own accounts, was competing in online matches from around the age of eight. As a teenager he focused on the game's ranked ladder rather than formal education or a conventional career path, reaching the upper tiers of the European matchmaking rankings.

Before joining a major organization, he played in low-profile teams and stacks such as SFTe-gaming and the semi-serious "5 Anti-Mage" lineup. His nickname is a contraction of his first name, Topias.

## Rise to Prominence

In May 2018, OG lost captain Tal "Fly" Aizik and Gustav "s4" Magnusson to Evil Geniuses weeks before the qualifiers for The International 2018. Johan "N0tail" Sundstein rebuilt the roster around Sébastien "Ceb" Debs, Anathan "ana" Pham, Jesse "JerAx" Vainikka, and the untested Topson.

The team won the European qualifier and entered TI8 as an outsider. OG went through the lower bracket in the group stage, then defeated Evil Geniuses, PSG.LGD, and PSG.LGD again in a 3–2 grand final on 25 August 2018. The team's share of the US$25.5 million prize pool was roughly US$11.2 million.

At The International 2019 in Shanghai, OG returned with the same five players and won again, beating Team Liquid 3–1 in the grand final on 25 August 2019. The prize pool of US$34.3 million was then the largest in esports history, and OG's winning share was about US$15.6 million.

## Key Achievements

- Two-time champion of The International (2018, 2019), the first roster to win consecutive titles.
- Career earnings above US$5 million, placing him among the top ten earners in esports.
- Popularized off-meta mid-lane picks, including Monkey King, Tiny, Zeus, and Invoker, with a fast-paced, fight-oriented style.
- Placed 7th–8th at The International 2021 with OG before announcing a break from competition in November 2021.
- Returned in 2022 with TSM, then joined Tundra Esports in 2024, finishing 3rd at The International 2024 in Copenhagen.

## Controversies & Criticism

Topson has attracted little personal controversy. Criticism has centered on his play rather than conduct. Analysts questioned whether his high-variance style could succeed outside OG's specific team structure, and his post-2019 results with OG and TSM were mixed. His extended absence from competition in 2021–2022 drew debate about whether players with early financial security remain motivated. His 2024 podium with Tundra addressed much of that skepticism.

## Legacy & Influence

Topson's career is frequently cited as proof that a talented ladder player can succeed at the highest level without a long tier-two apprenticeship. His hero pool and willingness to draft unconventional mid heroes in elimination matches broadened what teams considered viable, and his story features prominently in the Valve documentary series *True Sight* covering the 2018 and 2019 grand finals. Alongside teammates JerAx and Ceb, he helped make Finland and OG central to Dota 2's history.
