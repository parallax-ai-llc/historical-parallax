## Summary

Paul Skenes is an American professional baseball pitcher for the Pittsburgh Pirates of Major League Baseball (MLB). Selected first overall in the 2023 MLB Draft, he reached the majors in May 2024 and immediately became one of the sport's most dominant starters, winning the National League Rookie of the Year Award in 2024 and the National League Cy Young Award in 2025.

Skenes matters because of the speed and scale of his rise. He entered college as a two-way player at the Air Force Academy, transferred to Louisiana State University (LSU) for a single record-setting season that ended with a national championship, and then needed fewer than 12 months in the minor leagues before debuting in MLB. His combination of a fastball that regularly touches 100 mph and a hybrid sinker-splitter nicknamed the "splinker" made him a fixture of All-Star Games and Cy Young ballots from his first season onward.

## Early Life & Background

Skenes was born on May 29, 2002, in Fullerton, California, and grew up in Lake Forest in Orange County. He attended El Toro High School, where he played catcher and pitched but drew only modest recruiting attention. He enrolled at the United States Air Force Academy in 2020 and played for the Falcons in 2021 and 2022 as both a catcher and a pitcher.

In 2022 he hit .314 with 13 home runs while posting a 10-3 record and a 2.73 ERA on the mound, earning the John Olerud Award as college baseball's best two-way player. After that season he transferred to LSU, a move that also released him from the military service commitment that would have followed his junior year at Air Force.

## Rise to Prominence

At LSU in 2023, Skenes committed to pitching full-time and produced one of the greatest single seasons in college history. He went 12-2 with a 1.69 ERA and struck out 209 batters in 122.2 innings, a Southeastern Conference record. LSU won the College World Series, and Skenes was named the tournament's Most Outstanding Player. He also collected the Golden Spikes Award and the Dick Howser Trophy as the nation's top player.

The Pirates took him first overall on July 9, 2023, and signed him for a $9.2 million bonus, then the largest in draft history. After 34 minor-league innings across 2023 and early 2024, he debuted on May 11, 2024, against the Chicago Cubs.

## Key Achievements

- **2024 season:** 11-3 record, 1.96 ERA, and 170 strikeouts in 133 innings. He started the All-Star Game, the first rookie pitcher to do so since Fernando Valenzuela in 1981, won the NL Rookie of the Year Award unanimously, and finished third in Cy Young voting.
- **2025 season:** Led MLB with a 1.97 ERA over 187.2 innings with 216 strikeouts. Despite a 10-10 record on a losing team, he won the NL Cy Young Award unanimously, the first Pirate to win it since Doug Drabek in 1990.
- He joined Dwight Gooden as the only pitchers to win Rookie of the Year and the Cy Young in consecutive seasons.
- His 2024 debut Topps card with an embedded patch sold at auction for $1.11 million in 2025.

## Controversies & Criticism

Skenes himself has generated little personal controversy. Most criticism has surrounded the Pirates' handling of him. Fans and analysts questioned the team's decision to delay his 2024 call-up and, more broadly, whether a low-payroll franchise could build a contender around him before his free agency. His poor win-loss records, driven by weak run support, fueled persistent trade speculation and public frustration with ownership.

Debate has also followed his workload. Some observers argued that his innings totals and velocity carried elevated injury risk, while others noted that the Pirates had been comparatively cautious. His high-profile relationship with former LSU gymnast and influencer Olivia Dunne drew heavy media attention that occasionally overshadowed on-field coverage.

## Legacy & Influence

Though his career remains in its early stages, Skenes has already reshaped expectations for how quickly a drafted pitcher can dominate MLB. His path from military academy two-way player to Cy Young winner in four years is unprecedented, and his "splinker" has been widely imitated by other pitchers. In Pittsburgh he is regarded as the franchise's most important player since Andrew McCutchen and a central figure in the city's hopes for a return to contention.
