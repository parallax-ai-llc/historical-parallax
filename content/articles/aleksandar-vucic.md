## Summary

Aleksandar Vučić is a Serbian politician who has served as President of Serbia since 31 May 2017, following terms as Prime Minister (2014–2017) and First Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Defence (2012–2014). He co-founded the Serbian Progressive Party (SNS) in 2008 and led it from 2012 until May 2023, building it into the dominant force in Serbian politics.

Vučić began his career as a hardline nationalist in the Serbian Radical Party, serving as Minister of Information under Slobodan Milošević in the late 1990s. After 2008 he rebranded himself as a pro-European reformer while maintaining close ties with Russia and China. His tenure has been marked by economic growth, foreign investment, and progress in EU accession talks, alongside persistent criticism over democratic backsliding, media freedom, and concentration of power.

## Early Life & Background

Vučić was born on 5 March 1970 in Belgrade, then part of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia. His father, Anđelko, was an economist, and his mother, Angelina, a journalist. His family has roots in Čipuljić near Bugojno in Bosnia and Herzegovina. He graduated from the Faculty of Law at the University of Belgrade in 1994 and spent time in Brighton, England, where he studied English. During the Bosnian War he worked briefly as a journalist for a Bosnian Serb television channel in Pale.

## Rise to Prominence

Vučić joined the Serbian Radical Party (SRS) in 1993 and was elected to the National Assembly the same year, at age 23. He became the party's secretary-general in 1995. From 1998 to 2000 he served as Minister of Information in the government of Mirko Marjanović, overseeing a 1998 media law that imposed heavy fines on outlets critical of the Milošević regime.

After Tomislav Nikolić broke with SRS leader Vojislav Šešelj, Vučić followed him and co-founded the SNS in October 2008. The party won the 2012 parliamentary elections, and Vučić became First Deputy Prime Minister and Defence Minister. In the snap election of March 2014, the SNS-led coalition won roughly 48 percent of the vote, and Vučić became Prime Minister on 27 April 2014. He was re-elected in 2016 and then won the 2017 presidential election in the first round with about 55 percent. He secured a second presidential term in April 2022 with approximately 58.6 percent.

## Key Achievements

- **EU accession**: Serbia opened formal membership negotiations in January 2014, and Vučić's governments signed the 2013 Brussels Agreement and the 2023 Ohrid Agreement on normalising relations with Kosovo.
- **Economy**: Serbia's public debt fell from over 70 percent of GDP in 2015 to below 50 percent by 2024, with sustained foreign direct investment and unemployment declining from around 20 percent to single digits.
- **Infrastructure**: Major projects included the Belgrade Waterfront development, motorway construction, and Belgrade's selection to host EXPO 2027.
- **Defence and diplomacy**: In August 2024 Serbia agreed to purchase 12 Rafale fighter jets from France, while maintaining relations with Russia and China.

## Controversies & Criticism

Vučić's 1995 parliamentary statement, "for every Serb killed, we will kill 100 Muslims," made after the Srebrenica massacre, has followed him throughout his career. In 2015 he was attacked with stones while attending the Srebrenica commemoration at Potočari.

Freedom House downgraded Serbia from "free" to "partly free" in 2019, citing executive pressure on institutions and media. Reporters Without Borders has documented declining press freedom, and pro-government tabloids have been accused of smearing journalists and opposition figures. The 2016 illegal demolitions in Belgrade's Savamala district, the 2020 election boycott by opposition parties, and alleged irregularities in the December 2023 elections drew domestic and international criticism.

The collapse of a railway station canopy in Novi Sad on 1 November 2024, which killed 16 people, triggered months of student-led protests over corruption. Prime Minister Miloš Vučević resigned in January 2025, and a rally on 15 March 2025 in Belgrade was among the largest in Serbian history. Vučić has also faced criticism for refusing to impose sanctions on Russia after its 2022 invasion of Ukraine.

## Legacy & Influence

Vučić has dominated Serbian politics for over a decade, longer than any leader since Milošević. Supporters credit him with stability, economic modernisation, and pragmatic balancing between East and West. Critics describe his rule as a "stabilitocracy" in which Western tolerance of democratic erosion is exchanged for regional calm. His handling of the Kosovo question, EU accession, and the 2024–2025 protest movement will largely define how his era is judged.
