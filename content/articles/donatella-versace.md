## Summary

Donatella Versace is an Italian fashion designer and businesswoman who led the creative direction of Versace for more than 27 years. She took over the house after the murder of her brother, founder Gianni Versace, in 1997, and turned a family label facing an uncertain future into a global luxury brand valued in the billions.

Her tenure produced some of the most recognisable images in modern fashion, including the green jungle dress worn by Jennifer Lopez at the 2000 Grammy Awards, and she steered the company through two ownership changes. In April 2025 she stepped down as chief creative officer and became the brand's chief brand ambassador.

Beyond the runway, she became a cultural figure in her own right, known for her platinum hair, her close ties to musicians and film stars, and her public candour about addiction and recovery.

## Early Life & Background

Donatella Francesca Versace was born on 2 May 1955 in Reggio Calabria, in southern Italy. She was the youngest of four children of Antonio Versace, a salesman, and Francesca Versace, a dressmaker who ran her own atelier. Her older sister Tina died in childhood, and her brothers Santo (born 1944) and Gianni (born 1946) would later become her business partners.

She grew up around her mother's workshop and was dressed by Gianni from a young age, becoming his earliest muse. She studied languages at the University of Florence before moving to Milan in the 1970s to join Gianni, who founded Gianni Versace S.p.A. in 1978.

## Rise to Prominence

Within the company, Donatella worked first in public relations and then as Gianni's closest creative confidante, with a reputation for blunt feedback he valued. In 1989 Gianni launched the younger diffusion line Versus and handed its design to her, giving her a label of her own.

On 15 July 1997, Gianni was shot dead outside his Miami Beach home by Andrew Cunanan. His will left 50 percent of the company to Donatella's daughter Allegra, 30 percent to Santo, and 20 percent to Donatella. Within weeks she became creative director, presenting her first solo couture collection in Paris that October, while Santo ran the business.

She married the American model Paul Beck in 1983; they had two children, Allegra (born 1986) and Daniel (born 1989), and divorced in 2000.

## Key Achievements

- **The jungle dress (2000).** The plunging green silk-chiffon gown worn by Jennifer Lopez drew so many searches that Google cited it as a spur for creating Google Image Search. Lopez closed Versace's Milan show in a reissued version in September 2019.
- **Financial turnaround.** After heavy losses in the early 2000s, she kept the house independent until Blackstone bought a 20 percent stake in 2014 for €210 million, valuing Versace at roughly €1 billion.
- **Sale to Michael Kors (2018).** Michael Kors Holdings, later renamed Capri Holdings, acquired Versace for about $2.1 billion. She stayed on as creative director.
- **Sale to Prada (2025).** Capri agreed in April 2025 to sell Versace to the Prada Group for €1.25 billion, returning the brand to Italian ownership.
- **Recognition.** She received the Fashion Icon Award at The Fashion Awards in London in 2017 and was named to the Time 100 list in 2018.

## Controversies & Criticism

Versace struggled with cocaine addiction for years and entered rehabilitation in 2004 after a family intervention at Allegra's 18th birthday. She has spoken openly about it since.

In August 2019 the company apologised after a T-shirt listed Hong Kong and Macau as separate countries, prompting Chinese ambassador Yang Mi to end her contract. The family also publicly objected to the 2018 series *The Assassination of Gianni Versace*, calling it "a work of fiction." Critics have at times described her collections as over-reliant on Gianni's archive, while animal-rights campaigners pressed the brand until it went fur-free in 2018.

## Legacy & Influence

Donatella Versace preserved a founder-led fashion house through a sudden succession, one of the few such cases to end in growth rather than decline. Her celebrity-driven, unapologetically sexy aesthetic shaped red-carpet dressing for a generation and was embraced by pop culture, from Lady Gaga's 2013 song "Donatella" to countless runway tributes. Since handing design to Dario Vitale in 2025, she remains the public face of the brand her brother built.
