## Summary

On 26 November 2025 a fire broke out at Wang Fuk Court, a subsidised housing estate in Tai Po, Hong Kong. It spread through seven of the estate's eight 31-storey towers and burned for more than 44 hours [^1]. The fire killed 168 people, including one firefighter, and injured 79. It was Hong Kong's deadliest fire since 1948 and its first five-alarm fire since 2008 [^1]. Investigators blamed renovation materials wrapped around the buildings, including substandard safety netting and polystyrene foam boards over the windows. The disaster led to manslaughter and corruption charges, an independent inquiry, and controversy over arrests of critics under national security laws [^2][^3].

## Background

Wang Fuk Court was built in 1983. It has nearly 2,000 flats, and about 4,643 people lived there at the 2021 census, nearly 40 percent of them aged 65 or over [^1]. In 2024 the owners chose a HK$330 million exterior renovation by Prestige Construction & Engineering. All eight towers were wrapped in bamboo scaffolding, green netting, and foam boards [^1]. The Associated Press reported that residents had complained to officials from September 2024 that the netting and foam panels were a fire hazard. The Labour Department carried out 16 inspections from July 2024 and accepted the contractor's certificates [^4].

## The Event

The fire started at about 2 pm on 26 November 2025 in the netting on the lower floors of Wang Cheong House (Block F). It spread up the scaffolding and into the flats, then jumped to neighbouring blocks [^1]. Officials said the foam boards were "highly flammable" and helped the fire spread "at a terrible speed" [^4]. Fire services chief Andy Yeung said the alarm systems in all eight blocks were not working properly. Investigators later found that the sounders had been switched off during the works [^1]. Firefighter Ho Wai-ho, 37, died in the response [^1]. The fire was declared extinguished at 10:18 am on 28 November, after 44 hours and 18 minutes [^1].

## Response and Aftermath

Chief Executive John Lee called the fire a "major disaster" and set up working groups on investigation, emergency support, and temporary housing [^1]. Police arrested directors and a consultant of Prestige Construction, and the Independent Commission Against Corruption (ICAC) arrested others linked to the project [^4]. By 14 January 2026, police had arrested 22 people, 16 of them on suspicion of manslaughter, and the ICAC had arrested 14 on corruption charges [^1]. On 2 December 2025 Lee announced an Independent Committee chaired by High Court judge David Lok [^1][^2]. A relief fund raised more than HK$1.2 billion from public donations and government grants [^1].

## Positive Perspectives

- Lab tests and investigators quickly found the netting that failed fire tests, the foam boards, and the disabled alarms, which led to rapid arrests [^1][^3].
- The independent committee held public hearings through 2026 and also examined bid-rigging in Hong Kong's building maintenance industry [^2].
- In June 2026 prosecutors charged individuals and companies, including the main contractor, which addressed public demands for criminal accountability [^2].
- Public donations and government grants raised more than HK$1.2 billion for victims and displaced residents [^1].

## Negative Perspectives

- Residents had warned officials more than a year before the fire about flammable netting and foam. The Labour Department's response was later criticised [^4].
- Police detained student Miles Kwan, who had started a petition calling for an independent investigation, on suspicion of "seditious intention." A volunteer and former district councillor Kenneth Cheung were also arrested [^3].
- Beijing's Office for Safeguarding National Security warned that anyone using the disaster to "incite hatred" would be punished, and it summoned foreign journalists. Human Rights Watch said this pattern undermines accountability [^1][^3].
- The inquiry is an "Independent Committee" without the statutory powers of a commission of inquiry. Some survivors told AP that "everyone is just shirking responsibility" [^1][^2].

## Recent News

The independent committee began evidential hearings on 19 March 2026 and held five rounds of hearings by June [^2][^5]. In June 2026 seven people and two companies, Prestige Construction & Engineering and consultant Will Power Architects, were charged with offences including manslaughter and conspiracy to defraud [^2]. Prosecutors allege serious negligence in monitoring renovation materials, as well as bid-rigging [^2]. Final submissions were heard in mid-July 2026, and the committee is due to report about nine months after it was set up [^2].
