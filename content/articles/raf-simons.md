## Summary

Raf Simons is a Belgian fashion designer whose menswear label, founded in 1995, redefined the silhouette of young European tailoring and made youth subculture a central subject of runway fashion. Trained as an industrial designer rather than a fashion student, he built his reputation through slim, art- and music-referenced collections that became touchstones for designers and collectors alike.

He went on to lead three of the industry's most prominent houses in succession: Jil Sander (2005–2012), Christian Dior (2012–2015) and Calvin Klein (2016–2018). Since 2020 he has been co-creative director of Prada alongside Miuccia Prada. In 2017 he became the first designer to win the CFDA's Menswear and Womenswear Designer of the Year awards in the same year.

Simons closed his own label in November 2022 after 27 years, but its archive pieces remain among the most sought-after in secondary fashion markets, and his approach to blending art, music and fashion continues to shape contemporary design.

## Early Life & Background

Simons was born on 12 January 1968 in Neerpelt, a small town in the Belgian province of Limburg. His father was a night watchman in the army and his mother a house cleaner. He studied industrial and furniture design at the LUCA School of Arts in Genk, graduating in 1991.

During his studies he interned with the Antwerp designer Walter Van Beirendonck, who took him to Paris to see Martin Margiela's all-white show in 1989. Simons has repeatedly cited that show as the moment he decided to move from furniture into fashion. He worked briefly as a furniture designer for galleries and private clients before teaching himself pattern-cutting.

## Rise to Prominence

Simons presented his first menswear collection for Autumn/Winter 1995 as a video rather than a runway show, and staged his first Paris presentation in January 1997. Collections such as "Riot Riot Riot" (Autumn/Winter 2001) and "Virginia Creeper" (Autumn/Winter 2002) fused references to post-punk, rave culture and Kraftwerk with narrow tailoring and oversized outerwear. From 2000 to 2005 he also led the fashion department at the University of Applied Arts in Vienna.

In July 2005 he was appointed creative director of Jil Sander, where his minimalist, colour-driven womenswear earned critical acclaim. In April 2012 he succeeded John Galliano at Christian Dior. His debut haute couture show in July 2012, staged in rooms lined with fresh flowers, was documented in the 2014 film *Dior and I*. He left Dior in October 2015 and joined Calvin Klein as chief creative officer in August 2016, before departing in December 2018. Prada announced his appointment as co-creative director in February 2020, with his first collection shown in September 2020.

## Key Achievements

- Founded his eponymous label in 1995 and ran it for 27 years, with its final show for Spring/Summer 2023 held in London in October 2022.
- Won the Swiss Textiles Award in 2003 and CFDA awards in 2017 (Menswear and Womenswear) and 2018 (Womenswear).
- Long-running collaborations with Adidas (2013–2020), the textile company Kvadrat (from 2014), and artists including Sterling Ruby, Peter Saville and the Robert Mapplethorpe Foundation.
- Reintroduced the Bar jacket and archival codes at Dior while expanding the brand's global sales during his tenure.

## Controversies & Criticism

Simons's exit from Dior after just over three years drew attention to the pace of the luxury industry. He stated that eight collections a year left him "no time to think," which prompted wider debate about designer burnout. At Calvin Klein, parent company PVH publicly described his 205W39NYC collection line as "too fashion-forward" and ended his contract roughly eight months early, after the brand reported weaker-than-expected sales. Critics were divided over whether the failure reflected his creative direction or PVH's commercial expectations. The abrupt closure of his own label in 2022, announced on Instagram, also disappointed some collectors and staff.

## Legacy & Influence

Simons is widely credited with shaping the slim, youth-oriented menswear silhouette of the late 1990s and 2000s. His collections have influenced designers such as Kim Jones and the late Virgil Abloh, and his archive garments regularly sell for thousands of dollars at auction and on resale platforms. His collaborations with artists helped normalise the crossover between contemporary art and fashion, and his partnership with Miuccia Prada remains one of the few examples of shared creative leadership at a major luxury house.
