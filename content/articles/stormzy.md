## Summary

Stormzy, born Michael Ebenezer Kwadjo Omari Owuo Jr. on 26 July 1993, is a British rapper, singer, and philanthropist from Croydon, south London. He emerged from the grime scene in 2014 and, within three years, released the first grime album ever to reach number one on the UK Albums Chart. He has since released three consecutive chart-topping albums and won multiple Brit Awards, MOBO Awards, and Ivor Novello recognition.

His significance extends beyond music. Stormzy headlined Glastonbury's Pyramid Stage in 2019 as the first Black British solo artist to do so, using the set to address racial inequality and knife crime. Through the Stormzy Scholarship at Cambridge University, the #Merky Books publishing imprint, and a £10 million pledge to racial justice organisations, he has become one of the most prominent public figures in Britain to combine commercial success with structured social investment.

## Early Life & Background

Stormzy was born in Croydon to Ghanaian parents and grew up in Thornton Heath, raised mainly by his mother. He attended Harris Academy South Norwood, where he performed strongly academically, and later studied at Lewisham College. Before music became viable, he completed an apprenticeship in quality assurance at an oil refinery in Leamington Spa. He began rapping at age 11 and competed in local grime clashes in his teens, developing the freestyle skills that would later define his early releases.

## Rise to Prominence

His breakthrough came through the "Wicked Skengman" freestyle series on YouTube, beginning in 2013. In 2014 he won Best Grime Act at the MOBO Awards without a record label. "Know Me From" followed in 2015, and in December 2015 his freestyle "Shut Up" reached number eight on the UK Singles Chart after a fan campaign and a live performance at Anthony Joshua's ring walk. His debut album, *Gang Signs & Prayer*, was released on 24 February 2017 and debuted at number one, mixing grime with gospel tracks such as "Blinded by Your Grace, Pt. 2". At the 2018 Brit Awards it won British Album of the Year, and he also won British Male Solo Artist.

## Key Achievements

- **Three number-one albums**: *Gang Signs & Prayer* (2017), *Heavy Is the Head* (2019), and *This Is What I Mean* (2022).
- **Two UK number-one singles**: "Vossi Bop" (April 2019) and "Own It" with Ed Sheeran and Burna Boy (2019–2020).
- **Glastonbury 2019 headliner**, performing in a Union Jack stab-proof vest designed by Banksy.
- **Stormzy Scholarship** at the University of Cambridge, launched in 2018 to fund Black British students, expanded in 2021 with HSBC UK to support up to 30 students over three years.
- **#Merky Books**, an imprint with Penguin Random House founded in 2018 to publish underrepresented writers.
- **£10 million pledge** in June 2020, spread over ten years, to organisations tackling racial inequality in the UK.

## Controversies & Criticism

In November 2017, homophobic tweets he had posted in 2011 resurfaced; he apologised, calling the comments "unacceptable". A public feud with grime pioneer Wiley erupted in late 2019 over Stormzy's collaborations with Ed Sheeran, producing diss tracks including "Disappointed" and "Still Disappointed" in January 2020. His 2018 Brit Awards performance criticising Prime Minister Theresa May over the Grenfell Tower fire drew both praise and accusations of political grandstanding. In 2025 he received a nine-month driving ban after pleading guilty to using a mobile phone while driving, and a McDonald's partnership announced that year was ended within weeks following backlash from supporters of the boycott movement over the company's links to Israel.

## Legacy & Influence

Stormzy transformed grime from a largely underground London genre into mainstream British pop, proving that a grime artist could top the album chart, headline the country's largest festival, and sustain a decade-long career. His unusually direct engagement with politics, faith, and Black British identity gave younger UK rappers such as Dave and Little Simz a wider commercial lane. His scholarships, publishing imprint, and #Merky FC football initiative with Adidas have set a template for artist-led philanthropy focused on structural access rather than one-off charity.
