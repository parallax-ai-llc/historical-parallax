## Summary

The 2025 India-Pakistan conflict was a four-day exchange of missile, air, drone and artillery strikes between the two nuclear-armed neighbours from 7 to 10 May 2025 [^1]. It began when India launched Operation Sindoor against sites in Pakistan and Pakistan-administered Kashmir in response to the Pahalgam attack of 22 April, which killed 26 people [^1][^2]. Pakistan retaliated with Operation Bunyan-um-Marsoos, and a ceasefire took effect on the evening of 10 May [^1]. Both sides claimed victory, and casualty and aircraft-loss figures remain disputed [^1][^3].

## Background

India blamed the Pahalgam attack on The Resistance Front, which it describes as a proxy of the Pakistan-based Lashkar-e-Taiba; Pakistan denied involvement and called for a neutral investigation [^2]. In the days after the attack India suspended the Indus Waters Treaty and both countries expelled diplomats, closed border crossings and cancelled visas [^2]. Wikipedia describes the period between the attack and the strikes as a diplomatic crisis accompanied by border skirmishes [^1].

## The Event

On 7 May India struck nine sites in Pakistani Punjab and Pakistan-administered Kashmir, including targets in Muridke and Bahawalpur; the Indian government said it hit infrastructure linked to Lashkar-e-Taiba, Jaish-e-Mohammed and Hizbul Mujahideen and avoided military facilities [^1]. Pakistan said the strikes hit civilian areas, including mosques and homes [^1]. The same night, more than 110 aircraft took part in what Wikipedia describes as the largest beyond-visual-range air battle in the region's history [^1]. Pakistan claimed it shot down five or six Indian jets, including Rafales; India's Chief of Defence Staff Anil Chauhan acknowledged losses but rejected Pakistan's numbers [^1]. In the early hours of 10 May Indian missiles struck Pakistan's Nur Khan air base in Rawalpindi and other air bases [^1]. Later that day Pakistan launched Operation Bunyan-um-Marsoos, claiming to have hit 26 Indian military targets, including 15 air bases [^1]. A ceasefire began at 17:00 IST that day; explosions were reported over Srinagar and Jammu minutes after it was announced, but serious violations had stopped by 11 May [^1].

## Response and Aftermath

U.S. President Donald Trump announced the ceasefire on social media and later claimed credit for it, but Indian Defence Minister Rajnath Singh called that claim "baseless," saying talks occurred directly between India and Pakistan [^1]. India said 21 civilians and eight security personnel were killed, mostly by Pakistani shelling in the Poonch district, while Pakistan reported 40 civilians and 13 military personnel killed [^1]. India claimed at least 100 militants were killed in its strikes [^1]. On 20 May Pakistan's cabinet promoted army chief Asim Munir to field marshal, crediting his leadership in the conflict [^4]. In August 2025 Indian Air Force chief A.P. Singh said India had shot down five Pakistani fighter jets and one large aircraft; Pakistan's Defence Minister Khawaja Asif called the claim "implausible" and said no Pakistani aircraft was hit [^3][^5].

## Positive Perspectives

- The fighting stopped after four days, avoiding a wider war between nuclear-armed states [^1].
- The Indian government presented Operation Sindoor as a measured response that targeted militant infrastructure rather than military facilities [^1].
- Pakistan's government credited Asim Munir's "courageous leadership" in the conflict when it unanimously approved his promotion to field marshal [^4].
- After initial violations on the night of 10 May, the ceasefire stabilized from 11 May [^1].

## Negative Perspectives

- Civilians on both sides were killed by strikes and shelling, with India reporting 21 civilian deaths and Pakistan reporting 40 [^1].
- The two sides' claims about aircraft losses and casualties contradict each other and have not been independently resolved [^1][^3].
- Pakistan accused India of hitting mosques and residential areas, which India disputed [^1].
- Pakistan's defence minister noted that India made its aircraft claims only three months after the fighting and challenged both sides to submit aircraft inventories to an independent body [^3].
- India and the United States gave conflicting accounts of how the ceasefire was reached, with India rejecting Trump's claim of mediation [^1].

## Recent News

Analysts writing in 2026 described the May 2025 conflict as a key moment in the rise of Field Marshal Asim Munir's influence in Pakistan [^6].
