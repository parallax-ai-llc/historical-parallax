## Summary

Jordan Haworth Peele (born February 21, 1979) is an American film director, screenwriter, producer, and comedian. He first became widely known as a sketch performer on *MADtv* and as co-creator of the Comedy Central series *Key & Peele*, before reinventing himself as one of the most commercially and critically successful horror filmmakers of the 21st century.

His 2017 directorial debut, *Get Out*, earned him the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay, making him the first Black writer to win in that category. He followed it with *Us* (2019) and *Nope* (2022), each an original, socially conscious genre film that opened to large audiences without relying on an existing franchise.

Through his company Monkeypaw Productions, Peele has also shaped a broader slate of film and television that centers Black creators and perspectives, helping to make "social thriller" a recognized commercial category.

## Early Life & Background

Peele was born in New York City and raised on Manhattan's Upper West Side by his mother, Lucinda Williams, who is white. His father, Hayward Peele Jr., was Black and largely absent from his life. He attended The Computer School and the Calhoun School before enrolling at Sarah Lawrence College in 1997.

After two years he left college to pursue comedy, forming a duo with Rebecca Drysdale. He performed with Boom Chicago in Amsterdam and The Second City in Chicago, where he honed the sketch and improvisation skills that defined his early career.

## Rise to Prominence

Peele joined the Fox sketch series *MADtv* in 2003 and remained for five seasons, becoming known for impressions and character work. There he met Keegan-Michael Key, and in 2012 the two launched *Key & Peele* on Comedy Central. The show ran five seasons and 53 episodes through 2015, won a Peabody Award in 2013, and took the Primetime Emmy for Outstanding Variety Sketch Series in 2016. Its "Obama's Anger Translator" sketches became so popular that Peele performed the character alongside President Barack Obama at the 2015 White House Correspondents' Dinner.

In 2016 he co-wrote and starred in the action comedy *Keanu*. He then pivoted to horror with *Get Out*, produced on a budget of roughly $4.5 million. Released in February 2017, it grossed about $255 million worldwide and received four Oscar nominations, including Best Picture and Best Director.

## Key Achievements

- **Academy Award**, Best Original Screenplay, for *Get Out* (2018 ceremony).
- ***Us*** (2019) opened to about $71 million domestically, then the largest opening for an original horror film, and grossed roughly $255 million worldwide.
- ***Nope*** (2022) earned around $171 million worldwide and drew praise for its original science-fiction premise.
- **Monkeypaw Productions**, founded in 2012, produced Spike Lee's *BlacKkKlansman* (2018), a Best Picture nominee, as well as *Candyman* (2021), which Peele co-wrote, the animated film *Wendell & Wild* (2022), and *Him* (2025).
- He hosted and executive-produced the 2019 revival of *The Twilight Zone* and executive-produced *Lovecraft Country* (2020) and *Hunters* (2020–2023).

## Controversies & Criticism

In 2019 Peele remarked that he did not see himself casting a white man as a lead, adding that he had "seen that movie." The comment drew accusations of reverse discrimination from some commentators, while others defended it as a statement about representation. Peele also objected publicly when *Get Out* was submitted to the Golden Globes in the comedy category, arguing the classification trivialized the film's subject.

Critically, *The Twilight Zone* revival received mixed reviews and was cancelled after two seasons. Some critics found the plotting of *Us* and *Nope* less disciplined than *Get Out*, though both films were broadly well received.

## Legacy & Influence

Peele demonstrated that original, mid-budget horror built around Black protagonists and explicit social commentary could dominate the box office. The success of *Get Out* is widely credited with opening studio doors for a generation of Black genre filmmakers and with popularizing the term "social thriller." He has been compared to Rod Serling and Alfred Hitchcock for combining mass appeal with pointed cultural critique, and he remains one of the few directors whose name alone reliably draws audiences to an unknown premise.
