## Summary

Frederick Charles Freeman (born September 12, 1989) is an American professional baseball first baseman for the Los Angeles Dodgers of Major League Baseball (MLB). He spent the first twelve seasons of his career with the Atlanta Braves before joining the Dodgers in 2022. He holds dual American and Canadian citizenship through his parents and has represented Canada in the World Baseball Classic.

Freeman is widely regarded as one of the most consistent hitters of his generation. He won the 2020 National League Most Valuable Player Award, has been selected to eight All-Star Games, and has won three World Series titles: 2021 with Atlanta and 2024 and 2025 with Los Angeles. His performance in the 2024 World Series, including the first walk-off grand slam in Series history, earned him the World Series MVP award.

Beyond statistics, Freeman is known for his durability, his left-handed line-drive swing, and a reputation as one of the sport's most respected clubhouse figures.

## Early Life & Background

Freeman was born in Fountain Valley, California, to Fred and Rosemary Freeman, both of whom were born in Canada. His mother died of melanoma in 2000, when he was ten, an event he has cited as central to his outlook and work ethic. He grew up in Orange County and attended El Modena High School in Orange, California, where he was a standout pitcher and hitter.

Freeman committed to play college baseball at California State University, Fullerton, but the Atlanta Braves selected him in the second round, 78th overall, of the 2007 MLB draft. He signed for a reported $409,500 bonus and entered the Braves' minor-league system at age 17.

## Rise to Prominence

Freeman made his MLB debut on September 1, 2010. In 2011 he became Atlanta's everyday first baseman, hitting 21 home runs and finishing second to teammate Craig Kimbrel in NL Rookie of the Year voting. He earned his first All-Star selection in 2013 and, in February 2014, signed an eight-year, $135 million extension, then the largest contract in Braves history.

Through the Braves' rebuilding years of the mid-2010s he was the franchise's cornerstone, and he anchored the lineup as the team won four consecutive NL East titles from 2018 to 2021. After the 2021 championship, contract negotiations stalled, Atlanta acquired Matt Olson, and Freeman signed a six-year, $162 million deal with the Dodgers in March 2022.

## Key Achievements

- **2020 NL MVP:** In the pandemic-shortened 60-game season he batted .341 with 13 home runs, 53 RBIs, and a 1.102 OPS, leading the majors in doubles and runs.
- **World Series titles:** 2021 (Braves), 2024 and 2025 (Dodgers).
- **2024 World Series MVP:** He homered in each of the first four games against the New York Yankees, extended his Series home-run streak to six games across 2021 and 2024, and drove in 12 runs, tying Bobby Richardson's 1960 record. His Game 1 grand slam came while playing on a sprained ankle.
- **2025 World Series:** His 18th-inning home run won Game 3 against Toronto, making him the first player with walk-off homers in consecutive World Series.
- **Franchise records:** His 59 doubles in 2023 set a Dodgers single-season record; he also collected 211 hits that year.
- **Honors:** Eight All-Star selections (2013, 2014, 2018, 2019, 2021–2024), a 2018 Gold Glove, and Silver Slugger awards in 2019, 2020, 2021, and 2023.
- **Milestones:** 2,000th career hit in 2023 and 300th career home run in 2025.

## Controversies & Criticism

Freeman's departure from Atlanta generated lasting debate. Reports indicated his agents at Excel Sports Management may not have relayed the Braves' final offer, and Freeman dismissed the agency in June 2022 after an emotional return to Atlanta. The episode drew criticism from some commentators who questioned whether his public grief for the Braves was appropriate for a player who had chosen to leave. His former agent Casey Close later sued broadcaster Doug Gottlieb for defamation over the claim.

Some analysts have also argued his defensive metrics have not always matched his reputation, and that the heavily deferred structure of his Dodgers contract exemplifies a competitive imbalance in MLB spending.

## Legacy & Influence

Freeman is frequently described as the model of a modern first baseman: a high-average, high-on-base hitter with gap power and rare durability. His 2024 World Series is regarded as one of the greatest individual postseason performances in the sport's history, achieved while his son Maximus was recovering from Guillain-Barré syndrome earlier that summer.

His bridging of the Braves' rebuild and their 2021 title, followed by two Dodgers championships, has placed him in Hall of Fame conversations while still active.
