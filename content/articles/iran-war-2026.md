## Summary

On 28 February 2026, the United States and Israel launched large-scale air strikes on Iran. The first wave killed Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei and several other senior officials [^1][^2]. Iran answered with missile and drone attacks on Israel, on US bases, and on Gulf states, and it closed the Strait of Hormuz. That caused what the International Energy Agency's chief called the "greatest global energy security challenge in history" [^1]. A ceasefire in April and a US-Iran memorandum of understanding in June both broke down, and fighting resumed in July and again in September [^1][^6]. The war has killed thousands, mostly in Iran and Lebanon. It has reshaped Iran's leadership and disrupted global oil markets [^1][^3].

## Background

The war followed the June 2025 Twelve-Day War, in which Israel and the United States struck Iranian nuclear sites [^1]. Iran's economy was in crisis after UN sanctions were reimposed, and in January 2026 its security forces killed thousands of protesters. The US later cited that crackdown among its reasons for war [^1]. In January and February 2026 the US carried out its largest military buildup in the region since the 2003 invasion of Iraq. Negotiations over Iran's nuclear program were still under way when the strikes began [^1].

## The Event

Trump authorized Operation Epic Fury on 27 February. US and Israeli strikes, which Israel called Operation Roaring Lion, began the next morning [^1]. Three gatherings of Iranian officials were hit within seconds of each other, and Khamenei was among those killed [^1][^2]. Iran launched Operation True Promise IV against Israel and against targets in Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Jordan, and Iraq, and shipping through Hormuz stopped [^1]. On 1 March six US Army reservists were killed by an Iranian drone in Kuwait [^1]. On 9 March Iran's Assembly of Experts named Mojtaba Khamenei, the late leader's son, as supreme leader [^2]. Hezbollah resumed attacks on Israel, and Israel moved into southern Lebanon [^1].

Iran's UN ambassador said at least 1,332 Iranian civilians had been killed by 9 March [^2]. On 9 April, Iran's forensic medicine chief said more than 3,000 people had died in Iran since the war began [^3]. According to Wikipedia, Iranian missile attacks killed 24 civilians in Israel in the first 40 days [^1].

## Response and Aftermath

The UN Secretary-General and several governments condemned the US-Israeli strikes, while the UN Security Council condemned Iran's retaliatory attacks on Gulf states [^1]. On 7 April, after mediation by Pakistan's prime minister Shehbaz Sharif and army chief Asim Munir, Trump announced a two-week ceasefire on condition that Iran reopen the Strait of Hormuz [^4]. Talks in Islamabad on 11 and 12 April failed, and the US imposed a naval blockade on Iranian ports [^1]. On 17 June the two sides signed a 14-point memorandum of understanding, with Trump signing at the Palace of Versailles. It ended military operations, committed Iran to safe passage through Hormuz for 60 days, and committed the US to lift its blockade within 30 days [^5].

## Positive Perspectives

- Pakistani mediation produced the April ceasefire and the Islamabad talks, the highest-level US-Iran talks since 1979 [^1][^4].
- In the June memorandum Iran reaffirmed that it would not develop nuclear weapons, and both sides set a 60-day window to negotiate a final agreement [^5].
- A US Navy escort campaign helped restore oil flows through Hormuz to about 77 to 80 percent of pre-war levels by late September, according to Wikipedia [^1].
- The UN fact-finding mission began documenting alleged violations by all parties, which creates a record for future accountability [^7].

## Negative Perspectives

- In September 2026 the UN fact-finding mission found "reasonable grounds" to believe two US strikes on 28 February were war crimes. One of them, a Tomahawk strike on a primary school in Minab, killed 168 people, most of them children, according to Iranian state media [^7].
- Critics, including UN officials and international lawyers, described the initial attack as a war of aggression in violation of international law, and polls showed public opposition in the US [^1].
- Iranian attacks on commercial ships and Gulf states, and the closure of Hormuz, caused the largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market [^1].
- The July ceasefire collapse brought a renewed US blockade and air strikes. Oil prices rose about 20 percent as traffic through the strait fell [^6].

## Recent News

The June memorandum collapsed in early July 2026. Iran attacked tankers in the strait, the US revoked a sanctions waiver, and it reimposed its blockade alongside new air strikes [^6]. Hostilities slowed in August and resumed in September. Indirect talks were held on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly on 22 September, and on 26 September Trump rejected an Iranian ceasefire proposal [^1]. On 17 September the UN fact-finding mission reported possible US war crimes and Iranian crimes against humanity [^7].
