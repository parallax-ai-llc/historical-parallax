## Summary

Clairo, born Claire Elizabeth Cottrill on August 18, 1998, is an American singer-songwriter and musician. She first drew wide attention in 2017 with "Pretty Girl," a lo-fi bedroom-pop track whose webcam-shot video went viral on YouTube and became an early emblem of the DIY, internet-native pop that defined the late 2010s.

She has since released three studio albums: *Immunity* (2019), *Sling* (2021), and *Charm* (2024). Each marked a stylistic shift, moving from bedroom pop toward polished indie rock, then to sparse folk-inflected arrangements, and finally to warm, 1970s-influenced soul and soft rock. *Charm* earned her a Grammy nomination for Best Alternative Music Album.

Clairo's significance lies in her role as a bridge between social-media virality and critically respected songwriting. She has been discussed both as a success story of the streaming era and as a case study in the "industry plant" debate about privilege and access in music.

## Early Life & Background

Cottrill was born in Atlanta, Georgia, and grew up primarily in Carlisle, Massachusetts. Her father, Geoff Cottrill, was a marketing executive who held senior roles at Converse and Coca-Cola. She was diagnosed with juvenile rheumatoid arthritis, a chronic condition she has spoken about publicly.

She began writing and recording songs as a young teenager, uploading material to Bandcamp and SoundCloud under several names from around 2013. She attended Concord-Carlisle High School and later enrolled in the Bandier Program for the music industry at Syracuse University, which she eventually left to pursue music full time.

## Rise to Prominence

In August 2017, Clairo posted "Pretty Girl," recorded in her bedroom with a simple keyboard beat and a laptop webcam. The video accumulated tens of millions of views and led to a deal with Fader Label. The single "4EVER" followed in early 2018, and her debut EP, *diary 001*, was released in May 2018.

Her debut album, *Immunity*, arrived on August 2, 2019, produced by Rostam Batmanglij, formerly of Vampire Weekend. It included "Bags" and "Sofia," the latter of which was certified platinum in the United States and became her most-streamed song. The album transformed her critical standing, shifting perceptions from viral novelty to serious songwriter.

*Sling*, released July 16, 2021, was co-produced with Jack Antonoff and recorded at Allaire Studios in upstate New York. Quieter and more introspective, it debuted at number 17 on the Billboard 200. Its lead single, "Blouse," addressed being sexualized in professional settings. She also supported Dua Lipa on portions of the Future Nostalgia Tour in 2022.

## Key Achievements

- *Immunity* (2019) established her as a critical favorite and produced the platinum single "Sofia."
- *Sling* (2021) debuted at number 17 on the Billboard 200 and received broad critical praise.
- *Charm* (2024), co-produced with Leon Michels of El Michels Affair and released July 12, 2024, peaked at number 20 on the Billboard 200. Its tracks "Juna" and "Sexy to Someone" spread widely on TikTok.
- *Charm* was nominated for Best Alternative Music Album at the 67th Annual Grammy Awards in 2025.
- In 2020 she formed the side project Shelly with fellow musician Claud and two Syracuse friends.

## Controversies & Criticism

Soon after "Pretty Girl" went viral, critics online labeled Clairo an "industry plant," pointing to her father's marketing career and his connection to Fader Label's co-founder. Clairo acknowledged that her background gave her advantages but argued she had been releasing music independently for years before the video, and that the label deal came after, not before, the song's success. The debate became a widely cited example of how audiences scrutinize authenticity in DIY-branded artists.

Some listeners also criticized *Sling* as too subdued compared with her earlier work, though most critics regarded the restraint as deliberate.

## Legacy & Influence

Clairo helped define bedroom pop as a commercial genre, influencing a wave of artists who built audiences through self-recorded, low-fidelity uploads. Her progression from viral clip to Grammy-nominated album is often cited as evidence that internet-born careers can mature into sustained artistic ones. She has also been an openly bisexual public figure since 2018, and her candid discussion of chronic illness, industry sexism, and mental health has resonated with younger listeners.
