## Summary

Manolo Blahnik is a Spanish shoe designer and the founder of the luxury footwear house that bears his name. Born on 27 November 1942 in the Canary Islands, he built a business on hand-sketched, Italian-made shoes that he has personally designed for more than five decades, without licensing his name or selling the company.

He is widely regarded as one of the most influential footwear designers of the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries. His slender stiletto pumps and mules became shorthand for luxury, and the television series *Sex and the City* turned "Manolos" into a household word in the late 1990s and 2000s.

Blahnik remains the sole creative force behind the brand, which is still privately owned by his family. His work has been the subject of museum retrospectives, a feature documentary, and honours from the British, Spanish, and American fashion establishments.

## Early Life & Background

Blahnik was born in Santa Cruz de La Palma, on the Canary Island of La Palma, to a Czech father, Enman Blahnik, and a Spanish mother, Manuela Rodríguez. The family ran a banana plantation, and he was educated at home before attending school in Geneva. He later said he made shoes for lizards out of sweet wrappers as a child.

In 1965 he studied law and literature at the University of Geneva, then moved to Paris to study art at the École du Louvre and stage design. He settled in London in 1968, working at the Feathers boutique and writing for Italian *Vogue*.

## Rise to Prominence

A 1970 trip to New York proved decisive. Diana Vreeland, then editor of American *Vogue*, reviewed his portfolio of set designs and told him to concentrate on the shoes in his sketches. In 1971 he designed footwear for Ossie Clark's spring 1972 runway show; the rubber heels wobbled, a lesson he cited for decades afterwards.

In 1973 he bought the Zapata boutique on Old Church Street in Chelsea and renamed it Manolo Blahnik. In 1974 he became the first man to appear on the cover of British *Vogue*, photographed with Anjelica Huston. His first United States store opened in New York in 1979 through Bloomingdale's.

## Key Achievements

- **Awards:** Council of Fashion Designers of America Accessory Designer of the Year in 1987, 1990, and 1997; British Fashion Council awards in 1990 and 1999; the Neiman Marcus Award in 1991; an honorary Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 2007; and Spain's Gold Medal of Merit in the Fine Arts in 2012.
- **Exhibitions:** A retrospective at London's Design Museum in 2003, and the touring show *The Art of Shoes*, which opened in Milan in 2017 and travelled to the State Hermitage Museum, Prague, Madrid, and Toronto.
- **Cultural reach:** *Sex and the City* (1998–2004) and its 2008 film, which introduced the crystal-buckled Hangisi pump, made his name synonymous with luxury shoes. He created the footwear for Sofia Coppola's *Marie Antoinette* in 2006.
- **Collaborations:** Work with John Galliano at Dior, Calvin Klein, Isaac Mizrahi, Vetements in 2016, and a denim collection with Rihanna the same year.
- **Documentary:** *Manolo: The Boy Who Made Shoes for Lizards*, directed by Michael Roberts, was released in 2017.

## Controversies & Criticism

Blahnik has faced few personal scandals, but the brand has drawn criticism. Its prices, often several hundred to over a thousand dollars per pair, have been cited as emblematic of aspirational consumption promoted by *Sex and the City*, and Blahnik himself has expressed ambivalence about the show's effect on his image.

The company fought a 22-year trademark battle in China, where a local businessman registered the "Manolo" name in 1999. The Supreme People's Court ruled in the company's favour in June 2022. Animal-welfare groups have also criticised the label's use of exotic skins and fur.

## Legacy & Influence

Blahnik is credited with reviving the stiletto in the 1980s and 1990s and with elevating footwear to the status of couture. He still draws every design by hand and shapes prototype heels himself, a method that shaped a generation of designers who followed. His niece Kristina Blahnik has served as chief executive since 2013, keeping the business independent. He was named a Royal Designer for Industry in 2001, and his shoes are held in the permanent collections of major fashion museums.
