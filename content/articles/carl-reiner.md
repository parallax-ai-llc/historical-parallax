## Summary

Carl Reiner (March 20, 1922 – June 29, 2020) was an American comedian, actor, writer, director and producer whose career spanned more than seven decades, from the golden age of live television to streaming-era voice roles. He is best known as the creator of *The Dick Van Dyke Show* (1961–1966), as the straight man to Mel Brooks in the "2000 Year Old Man" routines, and as the director of a run of Steve Martin comedies including *The Jerk* (1979).

Reiner won nine Primetime Emmy Awards, a Grammy Award and the Mark Twain Prize for American Humor (2000). His work established a template for the workplace sitcom, and his collaborators and protégés, among them Brooks, Dick Van Dyke, Mary Tyler Moore and Steve Martin, shaped American comedy for generations. He was also the father of director Rob Reiner.

## Early Life & Background

Reiner was born in the Bronx, New York, to Jewish immigrants Irving Reiner, a watchmaker from Austria, and Bessie Mathias, from Romania. He attended Evander Childs High School and, at 16, was working as a machinist's helper when his brother Charles told him about a free Works Progress Administration drama workshop. Reiner enrolled and shifted toward acting.

During the Second World War he served in the United States Army Air Forces from 1942 to 1946. Initially trained as a French interpreter, he was reassigned to Special Services and toured the Pacific entertaining troops. He married singer Estelle Lebost in 1943; the marriage lasted 65 years until her death in 2008. The couple had three children: Rob, Annie and Lucas.

## Rise to Prominence

After the war Reiner appeared on Broadway in *Call Me Mister* (1947), *Inside U.S.A.* (1948) and *Alive and Kicking* (1950). His breakthrough came on television as a performer and writer on Sid Caesar's *Your Show of Shows* (1950–1954) and *Caesar's Hour* (1954–1957), working alongside Mel Brooks, Neil Simon and Larry Gelbart. He won Emmys for supporting actor in 1956 and 1957.

The "2000 Year Old Man" bit, improvised with Brooks at parties in the 1950s, was released as an album in 1960 and became a durable franchise; the 1997 revival won the 1999 Grammy for Best Spoken Comedy Album. Reiner's semi-autobiographical novel *Enter Laughing* (1958) became a Broadway play in 1963 and, in 1967, his first film as director.

## Key Achievements

Reiner created *The Dick Van Dyke Show* after his 1960 pilot *Head of the Family*, in which he starred, was rejected. Producer Sheldon Leonard advised recasting the lead, and the series with Van Dyke and Mary Tyler Moore ran for five seasons and 158 episodes. Reiner wrote dozens of scripts and appeared as the tyrannical host Alan Brady, collecting five Emmys for writing and producing between 1962 and 1966.

As a film director he made 14 features, including *Where's Poppa?* (1970), *Oh, God!* (1977), and four with Steve Martin: *The Jerk* (1979), *Dead Men Don't Wear Plaid* (1982), *The Man with Two Brains* (1983) and *All of Me* (1984). Late in life he reached new audiences as Saul Bloom in *Ocean's Eleven* (2001) and its two sequels, and as a voice actor on *Family Guy*, *Bob's Burgers* and *Toy Story 4* (2019). He published several memoirs, including *My Anecdotal Life* (2003), and was inducted into the Television Academy Hall of Fame in 1999.

## Controversies & Criticism

Reiner's career generated relatively little scandal, but some works drew objection. *Where's Poppa?* was criticized for its dark treatment of elder care and an original ending, cut before release, that implied incest. *The Jerk*'s opening line, "I was born a poor black child," has been debated as either satire of naïveté or a racial joke that has aged poorly. Several of his later films, notably *Fatal Instinct* (1993) and *That Old Feeling* (1997), were commercial and critical failures.

In his final decade Reiner became an outspoken critic of Donald Trump on social media, which drew both praise and complaints that a beloved entertainer had become overtly partisan. He was also a self-described atheist, a stance that occasionally attracted criticism from religious viewers.

## Legacy & Influence

Reiner died of natural causes at his Beverly Hills home on June 29, 2020, aged 98. *The Dick Van Dyke Show* remains a model for the writer-centered sitcom, and its blend of home and office storylines influenced series from *The Mary Tyler Moore Show* to *30 Rock*. His partnership with Brooks is regarded as one of the definitive comedy duos of the recording era, and his direction helped launch Steve Martin as a film star. Through Rob Reiner, director of *This Is Spinal Tap* and *When Harry Met Sally...*, his influence extended into a second generation of American filmmaking.
