## Summary

Vikas Khanna is an Indian-American chef, restaurateur, author, and filmmaker based in New York City. He is best known for Junoon, the Manhattan restaurant that held a Michelin star for six consecutive years from 2011 to 2016, and for his role as a judge and host on MasterChef India across multiple seasons.

Beyond the kitchen, Khanna has built a public profile through more than 30 cookbooks, documentary and feature films, and large-scale humanitarian work. His Feed India campaign, run remotely from New York during the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, distributed tens of millions of meals across India. He is frequently cited as one of the most visible ambassadors of Indian cuisine in the United States.

## Early Life & Background

Khanna was born on 14 November 1971 in Amritsar, Punjab, India. He was born with misaligned legs and wore wooden shoes until around age 13, which limited his mobility and kept him close to his grandmother's kitchen. He credits her, whom he called Biji, with teaching him traditional Punjabi cooking and the community-kitchen ethos of the Golden Temple's langar.

At 17 he started a small catering business, Lawrence Gardens, from his family home. He then studied at the Welcomgroup Graduate School of Hotel Administration in Manipal, graduating in 1991, and worked at Taj, Oberoi, and Leela hotel groups in India. He later took courses at the Culinary Institute of America, Cornell University, and New York University.

## Rise to Prominence

Khanna moved to New York in December 2000 with limited savings. He has described early hardship, including a period spent at a homeless shelter, before finding work in Manhattan kitchens. He gained early television exposure on Gordon Ramsay's Kitchen Nightmares in 2007, appearing as the chef of the restaurant Purnima, and on Throwdown! with Bobby Flay.

In 2010 he opened Junoon in Manhattan's Flatiron district. The restaurant received a Michelin star in 2011 and retained it through 2016. In 2011 he cooked at a fundraising dinner for President Barack Obama, and People magazine included him in its "Sexiest Man Alive" issue. He joined MasterChef India as a judge for seasons 2 through 4 between 2011 and 2015 and returned for later seasons in 2023 and 2024.

## Key Achievements

- **Michelin star**: Junoon held a star for six consecutive years, 2011–2016, a rare distinction for an Indian restaurant in the United States.
- **Utsav**: Published in 2015 after 12 years of work, this 1,200-page book on Indian festivals weighed roughly 12 kilograms and was presented to Pope Francis, Barack Obama, and Narendra Modi.
- **Feed India**: Launched in April 2020, the campaign coordinated donors and logistics partners to distribute more than 50 million meals during India's pandemic lockdowns.
- **Films**: He directed The Last Color (2019), starring Neena Gupta, which was eligible for Academy Award consideration, and the documentary Barefoot Empress (2022) about a 96-year-old Kerala literacy student.
- **Bungalow**: Opened in March 2024 in Manhattan's East Village, the restaurant drew months-long waitlists and was named among Esquire's best new restaurants in America.
- **Recognition**: Asia Society Game Changer Award (2020), James Beard Foundation nominations, and Amazon's Buried Seeds documentary (2019) about his life.

## Controversies & Criticism

Khanna's departure from Junoon in 2016 was reported to involve disagreements with owner Rajesh Bhardwaj over direction and ownership, though both parties kept public statements limited. Some food critics have questioned whether his celebrity has outpaced his day-to-day kitchen involvement, a common criticism of chef-brands.

MasterChef India, like other reality formats, has faced accusations of scripting and manufactured drama. During Feed India, Khanna was drawn into online disputes when trolls demanded he prioritise meals by religion. He publicly rejected this framing, but the episode illustrated the political scrutiny that accompanies high-profile philanthropy in India.

## Legacy & Influence

Khanna has helped shift Indian food in the United States from inexpensive takeaway toward fine dining, paving the way for a generation of Indian chefs seeking Michelin recognition. His cookbooks, television presence, and films have made him a mainstream cultural figure in India as well as a culinary one. His emphasis on hospitality as service, drawn from Sikh langar traditions, remains central to his public identity.
