## Summary

The Weeknd, born Abel Makkonen Tesfaye on February 16, 1990, is a Canadian singer, songwriter, and record producer. He emerged in 2011 through three anonymously released mixtapes that fused R&B, electronic production, and dark, drug-laden lyricism, a sound widely credited with reshaping the genre in the 2010s.

Over the following decade he became one of the most commercially successful artists in the world. His 2019 single "Blinding Lights" was named the top song in the history of the Billboard Hot 100 in 2021, and he headlined the Super Bowl LV halftime show the same year. He has won four Grammy Awards, though he publicly boycotted the ceremony from 2021 onward.

His work matters because it moved alternative R&B from an underground aesthetic into stadium pop while retaining a consistent cinematic persona. His 2025 album *Hurry Up Tomorrow* was framed as the conclusion of that persona.

## Early Life & Background

Tesfaye was born in Toronto, Ontario, and raised in the Scarborough district by his mother and grandmother after his parents separated. His parents had emigrated from Ethiopia, and Amharic was spoken at home. He attended Birchmount Park Collegiate Institute and West Hill Collegiate Institute but dropped out at 17. He has described this period as one of drug use and instability, and in 2008 he moved into a shared apartment in Toronto's Parkdale neighbourhood, an experience later reflected in his early lyrics.

## Rise to Prominence

In late 2010 he uploaded songs to YouTube under the name The Weeknd. Drake shared the tracks on his blog, and in March 2011 Tesfaye released the mixtape *House of Balloons* for free. *Thursday* followed in August and *Echoes of Silence* in December. The three were compiled as *Trilogy* in November 2012 after he signed with Republic Records through his own XO label.

His debut studio album *Kiss Land* (2013) reached number two on the Billboard 200. *Beauty Behind the Madness* (2015) delivered the number-one singles "Can't Feel My Face" and "The Hills", and "Earned It" earned an Academy Award nomination. *Starboy* (2016), with Daft Punk on the title track, also debuted at number one.

## Key Achievements

- **After Hours (2020)** produced "Blinding Lights", which became the first song to spend a full year in the Hot 100's top ten and was ranked the chart's all-time number-one song in November 2021.
- **Super Bowl LV halftime show** on February 7, 2021, which he reportedly funded with $7 million of his own money.
- **Four Grammy Awards**, including Best Urban Contemporary Album for *Beauty Behind the Madness* and *Starboy*.
- **Guinness World Records** in 2023 as the most-streamed artist on Spotify, the first to reach 100 million monthly listeners.
- **Dawn FM (2022)** and **Hurry Up Tomorrow (2025)** completed a trilogy of concept albums, the latter accompanied by a feature film.
- Appointed a **Goodwill Ambassador for the UN World Food Programme** in October 2021.

## Controversies & Criticism

In January 2015 he was arrested in Las Vegas for punching a police officer and pleaded no contest. Critics have long questioned the misogynistic and nihilistic content of his early lyrics, which he has said depict a character rather than an endorsement.

After *After Hours* received no nominations in November 2020, he called the Recording Academy "corrupt" and announced a boycott of the Grammys. *The Idol*, an HBO series he co-created and starred in with Sam Levinson in 2023, drew widespread criticism for its sexual content and reported troubled production, and was cancelled after one season. The 2025 film *Hurry Up Tomorrow* was also poorly reviewed. He has faced several copyright lawsuits, including one over "Call Out My Name" that was settled in 2023.

## Legacy & Influence

The Weeknd is widely regarded as the defining figure of the alternative R&B movement that followed his 2011 mixtapes, influencing artists such as Bryson Tiller, PartyNextDoor, and 6lack. His embrace of 1980s synth-pop on *After Hours* and *Dawn FM* helped drive a broader retro-synth revival in mainstream pop. Several of his singles hold RIAA Diamond certifications, and his After Hours til Dawn stadium tour (2022–2025) ranked among the highest-grossing tours by a solo artist. His stated plan to retire the stage name after *Hurry Up Tomorrow* has positioned his catalogue as a closed, era-defining body of work.
