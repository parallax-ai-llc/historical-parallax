## Summary
On 17 and 18 September 2024, thousands of pagers and hundreds of walkie-talkies used by Hezbollah members exploded almost simultaneously across Lebanon and parts of Syria [^1]. The attacks killed 42 people, including 12 civilians, and injured between 3,500 and 4,000, many with eye, face and hand wounds [^1]. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu acknowledged in November 2024 that he had approved the operation, Israel's first public admission of responsibility [^2]. The attacks opened a period of rapid escalation and drew condemnation from UN human rights officials, who said they may amount to war crimes [^3].

## Background
Hezbollah had exchanged fire with Israel since October 2023, and its leader Hassan Nasrallah told members to use pagers instead of mobile phones, which he said Israel had infiltrated [^1]. About five months before the blasts, Hezbollah bought pagers branded as Gold Apollo AR-924 [^1]. According to the Wikipedia account drawing on press investigations, Israel's Mossad had secretly manufactured the devices with the explosive PETN built in and sold them to Hezbollah through a shell company [^1]. Former Mossad agents later said walkie-talkies rigged as early as 2015 had been used mainly for surveillance before the pager plan [^1].

## The Event
At about 15:30 local time on 17 September 2024, pagers exploded in Hezbollah strongholds including Dahieh in Beirut, southern Lebanon and the Beqaa Valley, as well as in Damascus [^1]. Reports said the devices displayed a message or error that required pressing buttons, making it more likely the user held the pager close when it detonated [^1]. About 150 hospitals received casualties, and the Health Ministry told people to discard pagers and called health workers in [^1]. Iran's ambassador to Lebanon, Mojtaba Amani, lost an eye [^1]. The next day a second wave of explosions struck walkie-talkies, some going off at funerals for those killed the day before [^1][^2]. Of the 42 deaths, Wikipedia counts 12 from the pagers and 30 from the walkie-talkies, and the dead included children [^1].

## Response and Aftermath
Nasrallah called the blasts a "major blow" and an act of war [^1]. UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Turk told the Security Council that "simultaneous targeting of thousands of individuals" without knowing who held the devices violated international human rights law and, where applicable, humanitarian law, and he called for an independent investigation [^3]. Taiwanese authorities said no Taiwanese companies were involved [^1]. Israel killed Nasrallah in an airstrike on 27 September and began a ground invasion of southern Lebanon on 1 October, before a ceasefire took effect on 27 November 2024 [^1]. On 10 November 2024, Netanyahu's spokesman confirmed the prime minister had "greenlighted the pager operation" [^2].

## Positive Perspectives
- Israeli officials and supporters presented the operation as a precise strike on a hostile armed group's communications network [^1][^2].
- Some legal commentators argued the pagers were issued to Hezbollah members and it was "probably reasonable" to assume they would be held by their users [^1].
- Lebanon's health system handled a sudden mass-casualty event across about 150 hospitals, with blood donation appeals and ambulances sent from the north [^1].
- Former Mossad agents said the explosives were tested to limit harm to bystanders [^1].

## Negative Perspectives
- Volker Turk said booby-trapping harmless-looking portable objects is prohibited and that violence meant to spread terror among civilians is a war crime [^3].
- Amnesty International said the planners could not know who was near the devices and called for investigation as a possible war crime [^1].
- Human Rights Watch said an explosive whose location could not be reliably known would be "unlawfully indiscriminate" [^1].
- Civilians, including children, were among the dead, and many survivors were left blinded or with amputations [^1].
- The attack came a day after a US envoy warned Netanyahu against a major escalation in Lebanon [^1].

## Recent News
A lawsuit filed in Taiwan against Gold Apollo by 13 Lebanese people affected by the attacks was dismissed in early 2026 because the filings did not comply with Taiwanese law [^1].
