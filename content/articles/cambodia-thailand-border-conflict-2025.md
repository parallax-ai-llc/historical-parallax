## Summary

From 24 to 28 July 2025, Cambodia and Thailand fought their worst border war in more than a decade. Artillery, rocket fire, and Thai airstrikes hit several sectors of their disputed frontier [^1]. When Malaysia brokered a ceasefire on 28 July, at least 35 people had been killed and more than 260,000 had been displaced [^2]. The fighting also deepened a political crisis in Thailand and drew in ASEAN and the United States as mediators [^3]. The truce later broke down, and heavy fighting returned in December 2025 [^6].

## Background

The dispute goes back to unclear boundary lines in the 1904 and 1907 Franco-Siamese agreements. In 1962 the International Court of Justice awarded the Preah Vihear temple to Cambodia but did not settle the land around it, and the two countries fought there from 2008 to 2011 [^1]. Tensions rose again on 28 May 2025, when a Cambodian soldier was killed in a skirmish in the Emerald Triangle area [^1]. A phone call on 15 June between Thai Prime Minister Paetongtarn Shinawatra and Cambodian Senate President Hun Sen was then leaked. In it she called Hun Sen "uncle" and criticized a Thai army commander. The leak caused an uproar in Thailand, and the Constitutional Court removed her from office on 29 August [^5].

## The Event

On 23 July a landmine wounded Thai soldiers on patrol [^2][^1]. Fighting broke out on 24 July. Thailand reported that Cambodian BM-21 multiple rocket launchers hit Thai residential areas, a hospital, and a gas station, and the Royal Thai Air Force sent F-16s to strike Cambodian military positions [^1]. Clashes spread over five days along several stretches of the border, including the areas around ancient temples claimed by both countries [^1].

By the day of the ceasefire, at least 35 people had been killed. Euronews reported more than 260,000 displaced on both sides, including more than 139,000 in Thailand and 79,000 in Cambodia [^3]. Later tallies collected on Wikipedia are much higher and partly rely on open-source estimates, so the totals remain disputed [^1].

## Response and Aftermath

At talks in Putrajaya hosted by Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim, the two sides agreed on 28 July to an "immediate and unconditional" ceasefire starting at midnight [^2]. Al Jazeera reported that US President Donald Trump called both leaders and warned that he would not conclude trade deals with either country while the fighting went on [^4]. On 26 October, at the ASEAN summit in Kuala Lumpur, Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul and Cambodian Prime Minister Hun Manet signed what Trump called the Kuala Lumpur Peace Accord. It provided for heavy weapons to be withdrawn under an ASEAN observer team, cooperation on demining, and the release of 18 Cambodian soldiers held by Thailand [^4][^8]. Thailand stopped implementing the accord in November after a landmine blast maimed one of its soldiers. Daily Maverick put the July war's final toll at at least 48 dead and about 300,000 temporarily displaced. Fighting resumed on 7-8 December, including Thai airstrikes [^6]. A new truce signed on 27 December froze troop positions under ASEAN monitoring. It came after a 20-day round of fighting that killed at least 101 people and displaced more than 500,000 [^7].

## Positive Perspectives

- Malaysia, as ASEAN chair, got both sides to agree to a ceasefire within five days of the fighting starting [^2].
- The Kuala Lumpur accord set up a detailed framework with ASEAN observers, heavy-weapons withdrawal, and joint demining [^4].
- US economic pressure is credited by Al Jazeera with pushing both governments to negotiate [^4].
- The December truce added a monitored freeze of troop positions and a set timetable for returning prisoners [^7].

## Negative Perspectives

- The July ceasefire and the October accord both failed to stop renewed fighting in December [^6].
- Civilians on both sides were hit, and hundreds of thousands were displaced [^7].
- Analysts quoted by Al Jazeera warned that leaders in both countries had domestic political reasons to stir up nationalist feeling [^4].
- The leaked Paetongtarn-Hun Sen call showed how personal diplomacy can backfire, and it contributed to her removal from office [^5].
- Casualty figures are still disputed. Cambodia did not release complete military losses for the July fighting [^1].

## Recent News

According to Wikipedia, the 27 December 2025 ceasefire was still in effect as of May 2026, with sporadic incidents and continuing mistrust, and more than 20,000 Cambodian civilians remained displaced [^1].
