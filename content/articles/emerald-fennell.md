## Summary

Emerald Fennell is a British filmmaker, screenwriter, novelist and actress who became one of the most discussed directors of the 2020s. Her debut feature, *Promising Young Woman* (2020), won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay and made her the first British woman nominated for the Best Director Oscar.

She followed it with *Saltburn* (2023), a provocative class satire that became a streaming and social-media phenomenon, and *Wuthering Heights* (2026), a lavish and divisive adaptation of Emily Brontë's novel. Her work is marked by glossy surfaces, dark comedy and morally ambiguous protagonists.

Before directing, Fennell was known to audiences as an actress, notably as Camilla Parker Bowles in *The Crown*, and as the head writer of the second series of *Killing Eve*.

## Early Life & Background

Fennell was born on 1 October 1985 in Hammersmith, London. Her father is the jewellery designer Theo Fennell and her mother is the author Louise Fennell. She grew up in a wealthy, creative household in west London.

She was educated at Marlborough College in Wiltshire and read English at Greyfriars, Oxford. After graduating she trained informally through acting work rather than film school, taking small roles in television and film from the late 2000s.

Alongside acting she wrote fiction. She published the young-adult horror novel *Shiverton Hall* in 2013, a sequel in 2014, and the adult novel *Monsters* in 2015.

## Rise to Prominence

Fennell's first sustained visibility came as nurse Patsy Mount in the BBC drama *Call the Midwife* from 2013 to 2017. Film roles followed in *The Danish Girl* (2015) and *Vita & Virginia* (2018).

In 2019 she took over from Phoebe Waller-Bridge as head writer and showrunner of the second series of *Killing Eve*. The series earned her two Primetime Emmy nominations, for writing and for outstanding drama series.

That same year she was cast as Camilla Parker Bowles in the third and fourth seasons of Netflix's *The Crown*, a role she played through 2020 while finishing her first feature.

## Key Achievements

*Promising Young Woman*, starring Carey Mulligan, premiered at the Sundance Film Festival in January 2020. At the 2021 Academy Awards it received five nominations, including Best Picture and Best Director, and Fennell won Best Original Screenplay. The film also won two BAFTAs, for Outstanding British Film and Best Original Screenplay, plus the Writers Guild of America award.

In 2021 she wrote the book for Andrew Lloyd Webber's West End musical *Cinderella*.

*Saltburn*, starring Barry Keoghan, Jacob Elordi and Rosamund Pike, premiered at Telluride in August 2023 and was released by Amazon MGM Studios. It earned five BAFTA nominations and multiple Golden Globe nominations, and its final sequence set to Sophie Ellis-Bextor's "Murder on the Dancefloor" sent the 2001 song back into the UK top ten.

*Wuthering Heights*, starring Margot Robbie and Jacob Elordi, was released by Warner Bros. on 13 February 2026 as her largest production to date.

## Controversies & Criticism

*Promising Young Woman* drew debate over its ending, which some critics felt undercut its revenge premise, and over its treatment of sexual assault as genre material.

*Saltburn* was criticised for shallow class commentary, with several reviewers arguing that Fennell, herself from a privileged background, satirised the aristocracy while ultimately flattering it. Others found its shock scenes calculated rather than meaningful.

*Wuthering Heights* faced objections before release over casting, including Elordi's casting as Heathcliff, whom Brontë describes as dark-skinned, and over a marketing campaign that emphasised eroticism. Reviews were sharply split on its liberties with the novel.

## Legacy & Influence

Fennell has established a recognisable authorial brand within a decade: highly designed, pop-scored, deliberately confrontational films that generate argument as much as admiration. She is among a small number of women directors given studio-scale budgets on the strength of original, adult-oriented material.

Her Oscar win and nomination broadened the visibility of British women in directing, and *Saltburn*'s afterlife on TikTok showed how streaming-era films can dominate culture without a large theatrical run. Whether she is viewed as a provocateur or a serious satirist, her work has shaped how mid-2020s cinema handles class, desire and revenge.
