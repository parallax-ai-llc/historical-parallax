## Summary

Marco Pierre White, born 11 December 1961 in Leeds, England, is a British chef, restaurateur and author. In 1995, at the age of 33, he became the first British chef and the youngest chef at that time to be awarded three Michelin stars, for his restaurant The Restaurant Marco Pierre White at the Hyde Park Hotel in London.

White is widely described as the first celebrity chef of the modern era. His 1990 book *White Heat*, with photographs by Bob Carlos Clarke, presented a raw, cigarette-smoking image of kitchen life that reshaped public perceptions of professional cooking. Many of the best-known British chefs of the following generation, including Gordon Ramsay, trained under him.

In December 1999 he returned his three Michelin stars and retired from the stove, a decision he explained by saying he no longer wanted to be judged by people who knew less than he did. He has since worked as a restaurateur, television presenter and brand ambassador.

## Early Life & Background

White was born in Leeds to an English father, Frank White, who worked as a chef, and an Italian mother, Maria Rosa Gallina. His mother died of a brain haemorrhage in 1968, when he was six years old, an event he has described as defining his life.

He attended Allerton High School in Leeds and left at 16 with no qualifications. He began his training at the Hotel St George in Harrogate and then at the Box Tree in Ilkley, West Yorkshire, before moving to London in 1981 with, by his own account, a bag of books, a box of clothes and £7.36.

## Rise to Prominence

In London, White trained under Albert and Michel Roux at Le Gavroche, then under Pierre Koffmann at La Tante Claire, Raymond Blanc at Le Manoir aux Quat'Saisons and Nico Ladenis. He opened his first restaurant, Harveys, in Wandsworth Common in January 1987 at the age of 25. Harveys won its first Michelin star within a year and a second in 1988.

The publication of *White Heat* in 1990 brought him national attention. In 1993 he moved to the Hyde Park Hotel in Knightsbridge, and in 1995 the restaurant received its third Michelin star. He later relocated the operation to the Oak Room at Le Méridien Piccadilly in 1997.

## Key Achievements

- **Three Michelin stars in 1995**, the first British chef and, at 33, the youngest chef to reach that level at the time.
- **Harveys** (1987–1993), which earned two Michelin stars and became a training ground for chefs including Gordon Ramsay, Phil Howard and Eric Chavot.
- **Books** including *White Heat* (1990), *Wild Food from Land and Sea* (1994), *Canteen Cuisine* (1995) and the memoir *The Devil in the Kitchen* (2006).
- **Restaurant groups** including Marco Pierre White Steakhouse Bar & Grill and Mr White's, operating in numerous UK cities and abroad.
- **Television** work including *Hell's Kitchen* (UK, 2007 and 2009), *Marco's Great British Feast* (2008) and *MasterChef Australia*, where he appeared from 2011.

## Controversies & Criticism

White's kitchens in the 1980s and 1990s were known for a confrontational culture. He has acknowledged reducing Gordon Ramsay to tears and has described throwing cooks out of service. His long-running public feud with Ramsay, which included Ramsay filming at White's 2007 wedding without permission according to White, has been widely reported.

His decision to return his Michelin stars in 1999 drew both praise and scepticism. His later commercial work, notably a multi-year advertising partnership with Knorr stock cubes beginning in 2007, was criticised by some food writers as inconsistent with his earlier reputation.

His personal life, including a 2007 wedding to Mati Conejero and a lengthy divorce, has received extensive tabloid coverage. In 2016 he faced criticism for comments about the diet and physique of British teenagers. Several of his licensed restaurants have received negative reviews questioning the value of his name attached to franchised operations.

## Legacy & Influence

White's influence rests on two foundations. The first is his cooking, which fused classical French technique with British ingredients and produced dishes such as tagliatelle of oysters with caviar. The second is his role as mentor. A large share of London's leading chefs of the 2000s passed through Harveys or the Hyde Park Hotel.

*White Heat* remains one of the most influential cookbooks of its era and was reissued in 2015 for its 25th anniversary. His early retirement at 38 established a template for chefs stepping back from Michelin pressure, and his image as the "enfant terrible" of British cooking helped create the modern celebrity chef culture.
