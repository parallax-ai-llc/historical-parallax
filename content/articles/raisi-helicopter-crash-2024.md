## Summary
On 19 May 2024, a Bell 212 helicopter carrying Iranian President Ebrahim Raisi crashed in fog-covered mountains in Varzaqan County, East Azerbaijan Province, killing all eight people aboard [^1]. The dead included Foreign Minister Hossein Amir-Abdollahian, provincial governor Malek Rahmati, and the Supreme Leader's representative in the province, Mohammad Ali Al-e Hashem [^1]. Iran's official investigation concluded that thick fog and difficult weather caused the crash and found no evidence of foul play [^2]. Raisi's death triggered an early presidential election won by the reformist Masoud Pezeshkian [^3].

## Background
Raisi, a hardline cleric and former judiciary chief, had been president since 2021 and was widely seen as a possible successor to Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei [^1]. Rights groups accuse him of a role in the mass execution of political prisoners in 1988, which Human Rights Watch estimates killed 2,800 to 5,000 people, according to RFE/RL [^4]. Iranian officials have blamed US sanctions for the country's difficulty obtaining aviation parts [^5].

## The Event
Earlier that day Raisi had joined Azerbaijani President Ilham Aliyev to inaugurate the Giz Galasi hydroelectric complex on the border, and his party was flying in a convoy of three helicopters toward Tabriz [^1]. At about 13:30 local time the helicopter carrying Raisi, registration 6-9207 and operated by the Islamic Republic of Iran Air Force, lost contact and crashed near the village of Uzi in the Dizmar forest, at an altitude of about 2,200 metres [^1]. Heavy fog hampered search-and-rescue operations [^1]. Coordinates from a Turkish Bayraktar Akinci drone pinpointed the crash site on a steep slope, an account Iran later disputed, and the bodies were recovered on 20 May [^1]. The eight dead were five passengers and three crew [^1].

## Response and Aftermath
First Vice President Mohammad Mokhber became acting president, and Ali Bagheri became acting foreign minister [^1]. Iran declared national mourning, and funerals were held in Tabriz, Tehran, Qom, and Mashhad, where Raisi was buried [^1]. A snap presidential election was held on 28 June; in the 5 July runoff Pezeshkian defeated Saeed Jalili with more than 16 million votes to Jalili's 13 million, on a turnout of 49.8 percent [^3]. On 1 September 2024 Iran's armed forces released a final report attributing the crash to the "sudden emergence of a thick mass of dense and rising fog" and complex spring weather [^2].

## Positive Perspectives
- Iranian institutions carried out a constitutional transfer of power to an acting president and held a new election within about six weeks [^1][^3].
- The runoff turnout of 49.8 percent was higher than the first round's record-low 40 percent [^3].
- Iran's final report said technical, navigation, and procedural aspects of the flight were examined and that no evidence of sabotage or external interference was found [^2].
- International partners, including Turkey, assisted the search operation [^1].

## Negative Perspectives
- Former foreign minister Mohammad Javad Zarif blamed US sanctions on aviation parts for the crash, a claim Washington rejected as baseless, according to AFP reporting on Yahoo News [^5].
- Fars News reported in August that the aircraft carried two extra passengers against protocol, which the armed forces disputed, according to the Times of Israel [^2].
- Families of victims of the 1988 executions expressed relief at Raisi's death, telling RFE/RL that a "symbol of murder" was gone [^4].
- NBC News reported that some people shared videos purported to show celebratory fireworks, noting Raisi had overseen the crackdown on the 2022 protests [^6].
- Conspiracy theories circulated, including one that Raisi was eliminated as a rival in the succession to the Supreme Leader, according to Wikipedia; no evidence supported them [^1][^2].

## Recent News
No new official findings on the crash were verified after Iran's September 2024 final report [^2].
