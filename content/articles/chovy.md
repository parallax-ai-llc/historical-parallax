## Summary

Jeong Ji-hoon, known professionally as Chovy, is a South Korean professional League of Legends player who competes as the mid laner for Gen.G in League of Legends Champions Korea (LCK). Born on 3 March 2001, he debuted in 2018 with Griffin and has since played for DRX, Hanwha Life Esports, and Gen.G, where he became one of the most decorated domestic players in LCK history.

Chovy is widely regarded as one of the strongest laners the game has produced. His playstyle is defined by exceptional creep score (CS) leads, precise wave management, and consistency across a broad champion pool that includes Azir, Sylas, Corki, and Zoe. With Gen.G he won four consecutive LCK titles between 2022 and 2024 and the 2024 and 2025 Mid-Season Invitationals (MSI), establishing the team as the dominant force in Korea during that period.

Despite his domestic success, Chovy has never won the World Championship, and his international record remains the most debated aspect of his career.

## Early Life & Background

Jeong Ji-hoon was born in South Korea on 3 March 2001. Like many Korean professionals of his generation, he rose through the solo-queue ladder as a teenager and was scouted while still in secondary school. He joined the amateur roster of Griffin in 2017, when the team was competing in Challengers Korea, the second tier of Korean competition. Details about his family and formal education have not been widely publicized, and he has generally kept his private life out of the spotlight.

## Rise to Prominence

Griffin promoted to the LCK for the 2018 Summer Split, and the rookie roster immediately reached the final, losing to KT Rolster. Chovy's laning drew attention from the outset, and Griffin went on to reach the 2019 Spring and Summer finals as well, losing both to SK Telecom T1. He made his first Worlds appearance in 2019, where Griffin were eliminated in the quarterfinals by Invictus Gaming.

In 2020 he moved to DRX alongside Deft, Pyosik, Doran, and Keria, again reaching the Worlds quarterfinals. A single season with Hanwha Life Esports in 2021 produced a third quarterfinal exit.

Chovy joined Gen.G in late 2021. The move was the turning point of his career: Gen.G won the 2022 LCK Summer Split, his first professional title, and reached the Worlds semifinals that year.

## Key Achievements

- LCK champion: 2022 Summer, 2023 Spring, 2023 Summer, 2024 Spring, and the 2025 season, giving him five domestic titles.
- MSI champion in 2024 and 2025, the first back-to-back MSI titles by a Korean organization.
- Esports World Cup League of Legends champion in 2025 with Gen.G.
- Multiple LCK regular-season and finals MVP awards, along with repeated selections to the LCK All-Pro First Team.
- Widely cited as holding some of the highest average CS-per-minute figures and gold-lead statistics recorded in the LCK.

## Controversies & Criticism

Chovy has not been involved in personal scandals, but his career has attracted sustained criticism about international performance. Gen.G's 0-3 loss to T1 in the 2024 Worlds quarterfinals and the team's elimination by KT Rolster in the 2025 Worlds quarterfinals reinforced a narrative that he plays too passively in high-pressure best-of-five series. Critics argue his lane-focused style yields fewer decisive plays than rivals such as Faker or Knight.

His time on Griffin also coincided with the 2019 investigation into the club's management over the Kanavi contract affair. Chovy was not accused of wrongdoing, though the controversy overshadowed the roster's final months together.

## Legacy & Influence

Chovy's approach reshaped expectations for the mid lane. His emphasis on resource accumulation and wave control influenced a generation of Korean mid laners, and analysts frequently use his CS benchmarks as a reference point. He is also credited with anchoring the Gen.G dynasty that ended T1's domestic supremacy in the early 2020s. Whether he ultimately secures a World Championship, he is already regarded as one of the most accomplished players in LCK history.
