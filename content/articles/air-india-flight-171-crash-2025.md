## Summary

On 12 June 2025, Air India Flight 171, a Boeing 787-8 Dreamliner bound for London Gatwick, crashed about 32 seconds after taking off from Ahmedabad, India [^1]. Of the 242 people aboard, 241 were killed; the only survivor was passenger Vishwash Kumar Ramesh in seat 11A [^1]. The aircraft struck a hostel building for medical students, killing 19 people on the ground and bringing the total death toll to 260 [^1][^2]. It was the first fatal accident and hull loss involving a Boeing 787 since the type entered service in 2011, and the preliminary finding that both engines' fuel switches had moved to "cutoff" made it one of the most closely watched air accident investigations in years [^1][^2].

## Background

The aircraft, registered VT-ANB, was operating a scheduled service from Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel International Airport in Ahmedabad to London with 230 passengers and 12 crew [^1]. Air India is owned by the Tata Group [^1]. Among the passengers was former Gujarat chief minister Vijay Rupani [^1].

## The Event

The aircraft lifted off at 13:38:39 IST and began losing altitude almost immediately [^1]. According to the Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau's (AAIB) preliminary report, both engine fuel control switches moved from RUN to CUTOFF about three seconds after liftoff, one second apart, cutting fuel to both engines [^2]. The cockpit voice recording captured one pilot asking the other why he had cut off the fuel, and the other replying that he had not [^1][^2]. The switches were moved back to RUN about 10 and 14 seconds later and the engine computers attempted automatic restarts, but the aircraft could not recover and crashed into buildings of B. J. Medical College [^1][^2].

## Response and Aftermath

The AAIB released its 15-page preliminary report on 12 July 2025 [^2][^3]. Pilots' groups, including the Airline Pilots' Association of India, criticized the report as secretive and biased against the pilots [^4]. Air India completed precautionary checks of the fuel switch locks on its Boeing 787 and 737 fleets and found no problems [^5]. U.S. Federal Aviation Administration chief Bryan Bedford said he had "a high level of confidence" that the crash was not caused by a mechanical problem with the Boeing fuel control switches [^5]. The Tata Group announced compensation of 10 million rupees per deceased passenger's family [^1]. In September 2025 families of four passengers sued Boeing and switch maker Honeywell in Delaware Superior Court, alleging faulty fuel switches [^8].

## Positive Perspectives

- The AAIB issued a preliminary report within a month that set out the key flight-data findings [^2].
- Air India and regulators carried out fleet-wide fuel switch inspections that found no defects [^5].
- The FAA and Boeing shared their assessment of the switch design with other aviation authorities, according to Reuters reporting [^5].
- The Tata Group pledged compensation for each victim's family and covered medical costs for the injured [^1].

## Negative Perspectives

- Pilots' associations said the preliminary report appeared to presume pilot error before the evidence was complete [^4].
- The captain's father petitioned India's Supreme Court for an independent investigation that would look beyond deliberate pilot action [^3].
- Families said they were still waiting for answers a year later, and a lawyer said some had been sent documents offering payment if they waived future legal action [^6].
- The preliminary report noted that a 2018 FAA advisory on fuel switch locks had not required inspections, and Air India had not carried them out [^4].
- By the first anniversary, the AAIB had issued only an interim statement, as engine examinations were still under way [^3].

## Recent News

On the first anniversary, 12 June 2026, the AAIB issued only an interim statement rather than a final report, as engine examinations were continuing [^3]. In July 2026 the AAIB told India's Supreme Court that a draft final report was likely by October 2026 [^1], and on 2 September 2026 Civil Aviation Minister Ram Mohan Naidu said the probe was in its final stage [^7].
