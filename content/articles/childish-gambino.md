## Summary

Childish Gambino is the musical alias of Donald McKinley Glover Jr., an American rapper, singer, actor, writer, and director. Few contemporary entertainers have worked across as many fields at the same level: he has won Grammys for his music, Emmys for his television work, and Golden Globes for both acting and producing.

As a musician he moved from comedic, punchline-heavy rap on early mixtapes to genre-blending albums that drew on funk, soul, and psychedelia. His 2018 single "This Is America" became a cultural event, debuting at number one on the Billboard Hot 100 and winning four Grammys, including Record of the Year and Song of the Year.

Glover matters because he collapsed the line between comedian, musician, and auteur. His FX series *Atlanta* and his Childish Gambino records are often discussed together as a single body of work about Black identity, fame, and the internet.

## Early Life & Background

Glover was born on September 25, 1983, at Edwards Air Force Base in California and raised in Stone Mountain, Georgia. His father worked for the postal service and his mother ran a daycare; the family were Jehovah's Witnesses and also fostered children. He attended DeKalb School of the Arts and graduated from New York University's Tisch School of the Arts in 2006 with a degree in dramatic writing.

At NYU he co-founded the sketch group Derrick Comedy, whose videos gained a large early YouTube following. The group's feature film *Mystery Team* was released in 2009. The name Childish Gambino came from an online Wu-Tang Clan name generator.

## Rise to Prominence

At 23, Glover was hired by Tina Fey as a writer on NBC's *30 Rock*, working there from 2006 to 2009. He then played Troy Barnes on *Community* (2009–2014), which made him a recognizable television face while he self-released mixtapes such as *Sick Boi* (2008), *Poindexter* (2009), and *Culdesac* (2010).

His debut studio album *Camp* arrived on Glassnote Records on November 15, 2011. *Because the Internet* (December 10, 2013) earned two Grammy nominations and was accompanied by a screenplay and short film. *"Awaken, My Love!"* (December 2, 2016) marked a turn toward funk and soul; its single "Redbone" reached number 12 on the Hot 100 and won a Grammy for Best Traditional R&B Performance.

## Key Achievements

- **"This Is America" (2018):** Directed by Hiro Murai, the video's choreographed violence was widely analyzed. The song won Record of the Year, Song of the Year, Best Rap/Sung Performance, and Best Music Video at the 2019 Grammys, the first rap track to take the top two awards.
- ***Atlanta* (2016–2022):** Glover created, wrote, directed, and starred in the FX series. In 2017 he won Emmys for Outstanding Lead Actor in a Comedy Series and Outstanding Directing for a Comedy Series, the first Black director to win the latter. The show also won two Golden Globes.
- **Film roles:** *The Martian* (2015), *Spider-Man: Homecoming* (2017), Lando Calrissian in *Solo: A Star Wars Story* (2018), and Simba in *The Lion King* (2019).
- **Later albums:** *3.15.20* (2020), reissued as *Atavista* in 2024, and *Bando Stone & the World* (July 19, 2024), which he described as the final Childish Gambino album.
- **Television creator:** He co-created *Swarm* (2023) and the Amazon series *Mr. & Mrs. Smith* (2024), in which he also starred.

## Controversies & Criticism

*Camp* received sharply divided reviews, with Pitchfork awarding it 1.6 out of 10 and critics objecting to lyrics about women and Asian women in particular; Glover later acknowledged the early material's immaturity. In 2018 he sued Glassnote over royalty accounting, and the dispute was settled. Rapper Kidd Wes filed a copyright suit in 2021 alleging "This Is America" copied his 2016 song "Made in America"; a federal judge dismissed the case in 2023. In September 2024 he cancelled the remaining dates of his New World Tour, citing a health issue, and later disclosed that he had suffered a stroke.

## Legacy & Influence

Childish Gambino demonstrated that an internet-native comedian could become a credible, award-winning musician and then a prestige television auteur. "This Is America" set a template for music videos as political commentary, while *Atlanta* is credited with widening what a Black-led comedy could look and sound like. His decision to retire the alias in 2024 closed a discography that traced the evolution of a generation raised online.
