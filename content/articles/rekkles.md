## Summary

Carl Martin Erik Larsson, known as Rekkles, is a Swedish professional League of Legends player born on 20 September 1996. He is best known for his long association with Fnatic, where he became the most decorated European AD carry of his generation, and for later stints with G2 Esports, Karmine Corp and Los Ratones. Across more than a decade of competition he has won four European league titles, reached a World Championship final, and set the all-time kill record in Europe's top league.

Rekkles matters because he was one of the first European players to combine consistent mechanical excellence with a marketable public persona. He became a reference point for the bot lane role in the West, and his career choices, from leaving Fnatic in 2014 and 2020 to switching roles late in his career, repeatedly shaped conversation around the European scene.

## Early Life & Background

Larsson grew up in Sweden and played competitive youth football before an injury pushed him toward gaming. He began playing League of Legends during its early seasons and rose quickly through the European solo queue ladder while still in his mid-teens. His first team experience came in 2012 with amateur rosters such as Playing Ducks, where his laning and positioning drew attention from established organisations.

## Rise to Prominence

Fnatic signed Rekkles in 2012, but Riot Games' minimum age rule of 17 kept him off the starting roster until late 2013. He made his full LCS debut in the 2014 Spring split and immediately won the EU LCS title with Fnatic. After a disappointing exit at the 2014 World Championship, he left for Alliance, rebranded as Elements, for the 2015 Spring split. The move failed, and he returned to Fnatic in mid-2015. That roster went 18–0 in the 2015 Summer regular season, won the split, and reached the semifinals of the 2015 World Championship.

Fnatic won both the 2018 Spring and Summer splits, and Rekkles anchored the team that reached the 2018 World Championship final, losing 0–3 to Invictus Gaming. That remains the best result by a European team at Worlds. In November 2020 he left Fnatic for rival G2 Esports on a one-year contract, a move that ended with G2 missing Worlds 2021. He then joined Karmine Corp in France's LFL for 2022, winning the LFL Spring title and the European Masters. A 2023 return to Fnatic ended with a benching, after which he moved to T1 Esports Academy in Korea for 2024 and swapped to the support role. In 2025 he joined Los Ratones, the team founded by streamer Caedrel, winning the NLC and EMEA Masters Winter 2025 as a support.

## Key Achievements

- Four EU LCS titles with Fnatic: 2014 Spring, 2015 Summer, 2018 Spring and 2018 Summer.
- Runner-up at the 2018 World Championship, the deepest run by a Western team since 2011.
- LEC 2020 Summer regular season MVP and multiple All-Pro First Team selections.
- First player to record 1,000 kills in EU LCS/LEC history, and the league's all-time kill leader.
- LFL 2022 Spring champion and European Masters 2022 Spring champion with Karmine Corp.
- EMEA Masters Winter 2025 champion with Los Ratones after switching to support.

## Controversies & Criticism

Rekkles' 2020 departure from Fnatic to G2 drew heavy criticism from Fnatic fans, who viewed the switch to the organisation's main rival as a betrayal. Public disagreements between Rekkles and Fnatic management over how the exit unfolded added to the tension. His playstyle has also been a frequent point of debate. Critics labelled him a "KDA player" who prioritised safe, low-death games over aggressive, decisive plays, particularly after Fnatic's 0–3 loss to KOO Tigers at Worlds 2015 and its collapse from a 2–0 lead against Top Esports at Worlds 2020. In 2018 he was temporarily benched for Bwipo when the meta favoured non-marksman bot laners, raising questions about his champion flexibility.

## Legacy & Influence

Rekkles is widely regarded as the most accomplished AD carry in European League of Legends history. His longevity, spanning the EU LCS, LEC, LFL, LCK Challengers and EMEA regional leagues, is unusual in a scene where careers often end by the mid-20s. His late-career switch to support, undertaken at 27, became a model for veterans extending their careers through role changes. Beyond results, his large fanbase and candid public presence helped grow the profile of European esports and made the bot lane role a marquee position for a generation of Western players.
