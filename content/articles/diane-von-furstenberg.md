## Summary

Diane von Furstenberg (born Diane Simone Michelle Halfin, 31 December 1946) is a Belgian-born American fashion designer best known for the jersey wrap dress she introduced in 1974. The dress became one of the most recognizable garments of the twentieth century, selling in the millions within two years and putting her on the cover of Newsweek in March 1976 at the age of 29.

Her career has had two distinct acts. The first, in the 1970s, made her a household name before overexpansion and licensing diluted the brand. The second began in 1997, when she relaunched her company and rebuilt it into a global label. She served as president of the Council of Fashion Designers of America (CFDA) from 2006 to 2019 and has become a prominent philanthropist, particularly in New York City.

## Early Life & Background

Von Furstenberg was born in Brussels to Jewish parents. Her mother, Liliane Nahmias, was a Greek-born Holocaust survivor who had been liberated from Auschwitz roughly 18 months before Diane's birth. Her father, Leon Halfin, was a Romanian-born businessman. She has often cited her mother's survival as central to her outlook on fear and independence.

She was educated in Belgium, Spain, and Switzerland, and studied economics at the University of Geneva. There she met Prince Egon von Fürstenberg, whom she married on 16 July 1969. The couple had two children, Alexander (born 1970) and Tatiana (born 1971). Before moving to New York in 1970, she apprenticed with Italian textile manufacturer Angelo Ferretti, whose factory produced her first samples.

## Rise to Prominence

Von Furstenberg arrived in New York with a suitcase of jersey dresses and secured a meeting with Vogue editor Diana Vreeland, who encouraged her to show them. In 1974 she introduced the wrap dress, a printed knit garment that closed with a tie at the waist. It was affordable, easy to wear, and suited women entering the workforce in large numbers. By 1976 she had sold more than a million dresses, and Newsweek called her "the most marketable woman since Coco Chanel."

She separated from Egon in 1972 and the marriage was formally dissolved in 1983. In 1975 she launched the fragrance Tatiana, followed by a cosmetics line. By the late 1970s, however, the company was carrying large unsold inventory, and she sold her dress division and licensed her name across dozens of product categories. She moved to Paris in 1985 and founded a small publishing house, Salvy.

## Key Achievements

- **The wrap dress (1974)**, now held in the collection of the Costume Institute at the Metropolitan Museum of Art. Its 40th anniversary was marked by the exhibition "Journey of a Dress" at the Los Angeles County Museum of Art in 2014.
- **Second act.** A 1992 appearance on the shopping channel QVC selling her "Silk Assets" line reportedly moved $1.2 million of goods in two hours. She relaunched Diane von Furstenberg Studio in 1997 and expanded to more than 100 stores worldwide by the 2010s.
- **CFDA leadership.** She received the CFDA Lifetime Achievement Award in 2005 and led the organization for 13 years, introducing a 2007 health initiative addressing model age and weight.
- **Philanthropy.** With her husband Barry Diller, whom she married in 2001, she has been a major donor to the High Line, the Statue of Liberty Museum (opened 2019), and Little Island, the $260 million park that opened in May 2021.
- **Writing and film.** Her memoirs include *Diane: A Signature Life* (1998) and *The Woman I Wanted to Be* (2014). The documentary *Diane von Furstenberg: Woman in Charge* premiered in 2024.

## Controversies & Criticism

Critics have argued that her aggressive 1970s licensing devalued the brand and that the first company's collapse reflected poor financial discipline. In 2020 her company closed most of its stores, laid off a large share of its staff, and placed its UK arm into administration during the pandemic, prompting questions about its long-term viability. Some observers have also noted that her later ventures benefited from Diller's considerable wealth, and reviewers described the 2024 documentary as flattering rather than probing.

## Legacy & Influence

Von Furstenberg's wrap dress remains a fashion staple and a shorthand for 1970s working women's style. Her mentorship of younger designers through the CFDA and the DVF Awards, founded in 2010, extended her influence beyond her own label. She was named to the Time 100 in 2015.
