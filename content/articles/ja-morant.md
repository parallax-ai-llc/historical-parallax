## Summary

Temetrius Jamel "Ja" Morant (born August 10, 1999) is an American professional basketball player for the Memphis Grizzlies of the National Basketball Association (NBA). A 6-foot-2 point guard known for explosive athleticism and highlight-reel dunks, he was the second overall pick in the 2019 NBA Draft and won Rookie of the Year in 2020.

Morant became one of the league's most marketable young stars, earning two All-Star selections (2022, 2023), the 2022 Most Improved Player award, and an All-NBA Second Team honor. He transformed the Grizzlies from a rebuilding franchise into a Western Conference contender within three seasons.

His career has also been marked by off-court controversy, most notably two suspensions in 2023 for displaying firearms on social media, which cost him 33 games and reshaped public perception of his career trajectory.

## Early Life & Background

Morant was born in Dalzell, South Carolina, to Tee and Jamie Morant. His father played college basketball at Claflin University and was a high school teammate of Ray Allen; his mother played softball. Tee Morant trained his son with backyard drills, including jumping onto stacked tractor tires to build leg strength.

At Crestwood High School in Sumter, South Carolina, Morant averaged 27 points, 8 rebounds, and 8 assists as a senior but drew little attention from major programs. He was famously discovered in 2016 by Murray State assistant James Kane, who noticed him playing in an auxiliary gym at a camp in Spartanburg while looking for a snack.

## Rise to Prominence

At Murray State, Morant averaged 12.7 points and 6.3 assists as a freshman in 2017–18, earning Ohio Valley Conference Freshman of the Year. His sophomore season was historic: 24.5 points, 5.7 rebounds, and 10.0 assists per game, making him the first Division I player to average at least 20 points and 10 assists. He won the Bob Cousy Award and consensus first-team All-America honors. On March 21, 2019, he recorded a triple-double (17 points, 11 rebounds, 16 assists) in an NCAA Tournament upset of Marquette.

The Grizzlies selected him second overall on June 20, 2019. He averaged 17.8 points and 7.3 assists as a rookie, winning Rookie of the Year with 99 of 101 first-place votes.

## Key Achievements

- **2021–22 season:** Averaged 27.4 points, named an All-Star starter, Most Improved Player, and All-NBA Second Team. Set a franchise record with 52 points against San Antonio on February 28, 2022, and led Memphis to a 56–26 record and the second seed in the West.
- **Playoffs:** Scored 47 points against Utah in Game 2 of the 2021 first round, and led the Grizzlies past Minnesota in 2022 before losing to Golden State.
- **Contract:** Signed a five-year rookie maximum extension in July 2022 worth $193 million, with escalators up to roughly $231 million.
- **2023–24 return:** After his suspension, scored 34 points including a buzzer-beating game-winner against New Orleans on December 19, 2023, before a season-ending shoulder injury nine games later.

## Controversies & Criticism

In March 2023, Morant streamed himself holding a handgun at a Denver-area club on Instagram Live; the NBA suspended him eight games. In May 2023, a second video showed him displaying a gun in a car, and on June 16, 2023, Commissioner Adam Silver suspended him 25 games for the 2023–24 season. Morant also faced a civil lawsuit alleging he punched a 17-year-old during a pickup game at his home in July 2022; a Tennessee judge ruled in 2024 that he was entitled to self-defense immunity.

In April 2025 the league fined him $75,000 for repeated finger-gun celebrations. In November 2025 the Grizzlies suspended him one game for conduct detrimental to the team after critical postgame comments about the coaching staff.

## Legacy & Influence

Morant's rise from an overlooked recruit to a top-two pick made him a symbol of mid-major basketball and reshaped recruiting narratives around the Ohio Valley Conference. His aerial style revived national interest in the Grizzlies and anchored the "Grit and Grind" successor era in Memphis. At the same time, his suspensions became a reference case for how the NBA polices player conduct on social media, and his career remains a study in how quickly promise and scrutiny can intertwine.
