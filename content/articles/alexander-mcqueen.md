## Summary

Lee Alexander McQueen (17 March 1969 – 11 February 2010) was a British fashion designer and couturier widely regarded as one of the most influential and provocative creative figures of his generation. He founded his own label, Alexander McQueen, in 1992 and served as chief designer at the French house Givenchy from 1996 to 2001. He won the British Designer of the Year award four times, in 1996, 1997, 2001 and 2003, and was appointed a Commander of the Order of the British Empire (CBE) in 2003.

McQueen combined rigorous Savile Row tailoring with theatrical, often confrontational runway shows that drew on history, nature, technology and his own psychology. Collections such as "Highland Rape" (1995), "VOSS" (2001) and "Plato's Atlantis" (2010) are now studied as landmarks in the history of fashion. His death by suicide at age 40 cut short a career that had already reshaped how fashion could be presented and discussed.

## Early Life & Background

McQueen was born in Lewisham, London, the youngest of six children of Ronald McQueen, a taxi driver, and Joyce McQueen, a social science teacher and genealogist. He grew up in Stratford in east London and left school at 16 with one O-level in art. In 1985 he began an apprenticeship at the Savile Row tailors Anderson & Sheppard, later moving to Gieves & Hawkes and then to theatrical costumiers Angels and Bermans, where he learned historical pattern cutting.

After a period working for the designer Koji Tatsuno in London and Romeo Gigli in Milan, he enrolled on the MA fashion course at Central Saint Martins in 1990. His 1992 graduate collection, "Jack the Ripper Stalks His Victims", was purchased in its entirety by the stylist Isabella Blow, who became his mentor and champion and persuaded him to use his middle name professionally.

## Rise to Prominence

McQueen's early London shows were staged on minimal budgets and quickly earned him a reputation for shock. "Highland Rape" in March 1995, which referenced the Highland Clearances, featured torn lace and tartan and drew accusations of misogyny that the designer rejected. The extremely low-cut "bumster" trousers of the same period became his signature.

In October 1996 LVMH appointed him head designer at Givenchy, succeeding John Galliano. He held the post until March 2001 while continuing to run his own label. In December 2000 the Gucci Group acquired a 51 percent stake in Alexander McQueen, giving him the capital to expand into ready-to-wear, accessories, fragrance and retail stores in London, New York and Milan.

## Key Achievements

- Four British Designer of the Year awards (1996, 1997, 2001, 2003) and the CFDA International Designer of the Year award in 2003.
- Runway shows that are now regarded as performance art: the robot spray-painting of a dress in "No. 13" (1999), the two-way mirrored asylum of "VOSS" (2001) and the Kate Moss hologram in "Widows of Culloden" (2006).
- "Plato's Atlantis" (Spring/Summer 2010) was among the first fashion shows live-streamed online, and its digitally printed dresses and "Armadillo" shoes became defining images of the decade.
- The 2011 Metropolitan Museum of Art retrospective "Savage Beauty" drew about 661,000 visitors, then one of the museum's ten most-visited exhibitions; the 2015 Victoria and Albert Museum version attracted around 493,000.

## Controversies & Criticism

McQueen's work was repeatedly accused of glamorising violence against women, most sharply after "Highland Rape" and after "The Hunger" (1996), which showed a bodice containing live worms. He argued the collections were about empowerment and historical injustice, and many critics later reassessed them as such. His tenure at Givenchy was uneasy: he publicly dismissed his first haute couture collection there as "crap" and clashed with LVMH management, and the house's sales did not grow significantly under him. He was also criticised for using animal materials, including feathers and skins, and for the physical toll his shows placed on models.

## Legacy & Influence

McQueen died on 11 February 2010, nine days after the death of his mother, and was found to have taken his own life. Sarah Burton, his longtime deputy, became creative director in May 2010 and designed the wedding dress of Catherine Middleton in April 2011. The Alexander McQueen brand remains part of the Kering group, with Seán McGirr succeeding Burton in 2023.

His influence persists in the acceptance of narrative-driven, immersive runway presentations, in the fusion of couture technique with technology, and in the continued study of his archive by museums and fashion schools. Designers including Gareth Pugh, Iris van Herpen and Jonathan Anderson have cited him as a formative influence.
