## Summary

Emeril John Lagasse III (born October 15, 1959) is an American celebrity chef, restaurateur, television personality, and cookbook author. He rose to national fame in the 1990s as the face of the Food Network, where his shows *Essence of Emeril* and *Emeril Live* helped transform cooking programs from instructional demonstrations into prime-time entertainment. His catchphrases "Bam!" and "Kick it up a notch!" became widely recognized pieces of American pop culture.

Lagasse built his reputation in New Orleans, first as executive chef of Commander's Palace and then through his own flagship restaurant, Emeril's, which opened in 1990. His cooking blends Creole and Cajun traditions with Portuguese, French, and New American influences. Over three decades he expanded into a restaurant group, a line of cookware and food products, and more than a dozen cookbooks.

He matters because he was among the first chefs to demonstrate that a cook could be a mass-media brand, opening the path for the celebrity-chef era that followed.

## Early Life & Background

Lagasse was born in Fall River, Massachusetts, to a French-Canadian father, Emeril John Lagasse Jr., and a Portuguese-American mother, Hilda. He grew up cooking alongside his mother and worked as a teenager at a Portuguese bakery in Fall River, where he learned bread and pastry making.

Although he was offered a music scholarship to the New England Conservatory of Music as a drummer, he chose to pursue cooking instead. He studied at Diman Regional Vocational Technical High School and then earned a doctorate-equivalent culinary diploma from Johnson & Wales University in Providence, Rhode Island, in 1978. After graduation he worked in kitchens in Paris and Lyon, France, before returning to the United States to cook in New York, Boston, and Philadelphia.

## Rise to Prominence

In 1982, at age 23, Lagasse was hired by Ella and Dick Brennan to succeed Paul Prudhomme as executive chef of Commander's Palace in New Orleans. He held the post for roughly seven and a half years and became closely identified with modern Creole cooking.

In March 1990 he opened Emeril's Restaurant in the city's Warehouse District. *Esquire* named it Restaurant of the Year in 1990, and it later earned recognition from *Wine Spectator* and the James Beard Foundation. He followed with NOLA in the French Quarter in 1992 and Emeril's Delmonico in 1998.

His television career began with appearances on *Great Chefs* and then *How to Boil Water* on the newly launched Food Network in 1993. *Essence of Emeril* premiered in 1994, and *Emeril Live*, taped before a studio audience with a live band, ran from 1997 to 2007 and won a Cable ACE Award in 1997.

## Key Achievements

- **James Beard Awards:** Best Chef: Southeast in 1991, and Emeril's Restaurant received the Foundation's Outstanding Service award in 2019. He was inducted into the James Beard Foundation's Who's Who of Food and Beverage in America.
- **Television:** Hosted or appeared in more than 2,000 episodes of Food Network programming, and later hosted *Emeril's Florida* and the Amazon series *Eat the World with Emeril Lagasse* (2016). He received a Daytime Emmy nomination and a Lifetime Achievement Award at the Daytime Emmys in 2016.
- **Publishing:** Author of roughly 20 cookbooks beginning with *Emeril's New New Orleans Cooking* (1993), several of which reached the *New York Times* bestseller list.
- **Business:** In 2008 he sold most of his media and product businesses, excluding his restaurants, to Martha Stewart Living Omnimedia for a reported $50 million plus stock. In 2002 he founded the Emeril Lagasse Foundation, which had granted more than $15 million to youth culinary and educational programs by the early 2020s.

## Controversies & Criticism

Critics in the food press sometimes argued that Lagasse's showmanship overshadowed his cooking and that *Emeril Live* prioritized entertainment over technique. His 2001 NBC sitcom *Emeril* was cancelled after 10 episodes amid poor reviews. Food Network's decision in 2007 to end *Emeril Live* was widely read as a shift away from his brand of personality-driven programming.

In 2011 he drew criticism for remarks on Fox Business that Obama-era regulations and health-care costs made it hard to run restaurants, comments he later clarified as frustration with compliance burdens rather than a political statement. Several of his later restaurants, including locations in Las Vegas, Orlando, and Bethlehem, Pennsylvania, closed over the 2010s and 2020s, prompting commentary on the durability of celebrity-chef restaurant empires.

## Legacy & Influence

Lagasse is widely credited with establishing the template for the modern celebrity chef and with proving that food television could draw prime-time audiences. He helped popularize Louisiana cooking nationally and drew attention to New Orleans dining before and after Hurricane Katrina, reopening his restaurants within months of the 2005 storm. His son E.J. Lagasse took over as chef patron of Emeril's in 2020, and the restaurant earned a Michelin star in the guide's first Louisiana edition. His foundation's work continues to fund culinary education for young people in New Orleans and beyond.
