## Summary

Alix Earle (born December 16, 2000) is an American content creator, podcast host, and entrepreneur who became one of the most prominent influencers of the early 2020s through her candid "Get Ready With Me" (GRWM) videos on TikTok. She rose from roughly one million followers to more than four million in the final months of 2022, and by 2024 she had surpassed seven million on the platform.

Her appeal rests on an unfiltered, diaristic style: she films while doing her makeup and talks openly about acne, anxiety, dating, and college life. That approach produced the so-called "Alix Earle effect," in which products she mentioned sold out within hours, and it made her a leading example of how parasocial trust translates into commercial reach.

Beyond TikTok, Earle has built a podcast, brand partnerships, a beverage business stake, and television appearances, and she was named to Forbes' 30 Under 30 and Top Creators lists.

## Early Life & Background

Earle was born in Monmouth County, New Jersey, and raised in Monmouth Beach. Her father, Thomas "TJ" Earle, runs Earle, a large New Jersey asphalt and construction firm. Her parents divorced after her father's affair with Ashley Dupré, the escort at the center of the 2008 Eliot Spitzer scandal, who later became Earle's stepmother. Earle has discussed the media attention this brought her family during her childhood.

She attended Red Bank Catholic High School and enrolled at the University of Miami in 2019, graduating in May 2023 with a marketing degree from the Miami Herbert Business School. She posted her first TikToks in 2020 while a student and grew her audience alongside her coursework.

## Rise to Prominence

Earle's growth accelerated in late 2022 when her GRWM videos, often filmed before nights out in Miami, began drawing tens of millions of views. She reached six million TikTok followers by early 2023 and signed with United Talent Agency that year.

In January 2023 she joined Tarte Cosmetics' influencer trip to Dubai, an event that drew widespread attention to both her and the brand. She graduated in May 2023 and moved into full-time content creation.

In September 2023 she launched the podcast *Hot Mess with Alix Earle* on Alex Cooper's Unwell Network. It debuted at number one on Apple Podcasts' overall chart. Reports in 2025 indicated she and Unwell parted ways amid a dispute over the show's ownership, after which she continued the podcast independently.

Her relationship with NFL wide receiver Braxton Berrios, which began in 2023, became a recurring subject of her content.

## Key Achievements

- Named to Forbes' 30 Under 30 (Social Media) in 2023 and the Forbes Top Creators list in 2023 and 2024.
- Appeared as a rookie in the 2024 *Sports Illustrated Swimsuit* issue.
- Partner in SipMargs, a canned margarita brand launched in 2023.
- Brand partnerships have included Rare Beauty, White Fox Boutique, Alo Yoga, and Carl's Jr., including a 2024 Super Bowl-adjacent campaign.
- Competed on season 34 of *Dancing with the Stars* in autumn 2025 with partner Val Chmerkovskiy, finishing as runner-up to Robert Irwin.
- Named to the inaugural TIME100 Creators list in 2025.

## Controversies & Criticism

The 2023 Tarte Dubai trip drew criticism over conspicuous luxury spending and the lack of clear sponsorship disclosure, and commentators have questioned whether Earle's paid partnerships are consistently labeled.

In 2025, screenshots of messages Earle sent as a teenager resurfaced in which she used a racial slur. She issued an apology, saying the messages did not reflect her current values, but the episode prompted debate about accountability for influencers' past conduct.

Critics have also argued that the "Alix Earle effect" promotes overconsumption among young followers, and that her relatable persona obscures the wealth she grew up with.

## Legacy & Influence

Earle helped redefine the influencer template for Gen Z: low-production, confessional, and delivered directly to camera rather than polished and aspirational. Marketers now cite her as a case study in conversion, with brands reporting immediate sell-outs after a single mention. Her move from TikTok to podcasting, television, and product ownership has become a common path for creators seeking longevity beyond a single platform.
