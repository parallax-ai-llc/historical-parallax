## Summary

David Chang is an American chef, restaurateur, author, and television host best known as the founder of the Momofuku restaurant group. Opening Momofuku Noodle Bar in New York's East Village in 2004, he built a brand that fused Korean, Japanese, and American influences in a casual, counter-seating format that helped redefine fine dining in the 2000s.

Chang's influence extends beyond his restaurants. Through the food magazine Lucky Peach, the Netflix series *Ugly Delicious*, a podcast, and a best-selling memoir, he became one of the most visible chefs of his generation, shaping how Asian-American food is discussed in mainstream culture. His career has also included public reckonings with his own temper, mental health, and a 2024 trademark dispute that drew criticism from the very community he had championed.

## Early Life & Background

Chang was born on August 5, 1977, in Arlington, Virginia, to Korean immigrant parents, and grew up in Vienna, Virginia. He attended Georgetown Preparatory School and graduated from Trinity College in Hartford, Connecticut, in 2000 with a degree in religious studies.

After college he taught English in Japan, where exposure to ramen shops and izakaya culture reshaped his view of food. Returning to New York, he enrolled at the French Culinary Institute and worked at Tom Colicchio's Craft and at Café Boulud, followed by stints in Tokyo kitchens.

## Rise to Prominence

Chang opened Momofuku Noodle Bar in 2004 with borrowed money and a small staff. Its pork buns and ramen drew attention, and Momofuku Ssäm Bar followed in 2006. In 2008 he opened Momofuku Ko, a 12-seat tasting-menu counter that earned two Michelin stars in 2009, and co-founded Milk Bar with pastry chef Christina Tosi.

The James Beard Foundation named him Rising Star Chef in 2007, Best Chef: New York City in 2008, and Outstanding Chef in 2013, while Ko won Best New Restaurant in 2009. *Time* listed him among its 100 most influential people in 2010.

The group expanded internationally, including Momofuku Seiōbo in Sydney (2011–2021), locations in Toronto and Las Vegas, the fried-chicken concept Fuku (2015), and Majordōmo in Los Angeles (2018). Momofuku Ko closed in November 2023.

## Key Achievements

- **Momofuku** (2009), co-written with Peter Meehan, became one of the best-selling chef cookbooks of its era.
- **Lucky Peach** (2011–2017), the quarterly he co-founded with Meehan, won multiple James Beard journalism awards.
- **Ugly Delicious** premiered on Netflix in February 2018, followed by *Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner* (2019) and Hulu's *The Next Thing You Eat* (2021).
- **The Dave Chang Show** podcast launched in 2018 with The Ringer.
- **Eat a Peach**, his 2020 memoir, debuted on the *New York Times* best-seller list.
- In November 2020 he became the first celebrity to win the $1 million top prize on *Who Wants to Be a Millionaire*, donating it to the Southern Smoke Foundation for restaurant workers.
- Momofuku Goods, launched in 2020, brought Momofuku Chili Crunch and noodles to retail.

## Controversies & Criticism

In *Eat a Peach*, Chang described screaming at and intimidating employees, and disclosed a bipolar disorder diagnosis and suicidal thoughts. Several former staff said the book understated the harm, and critics questioned whether the industry's celebration of "genius" chefs excused abusive behavior.

In April 2024, Momofuku sent cease-and-desist letters to small producers, many Asian-American owned, over the term "chili crunch," which it had trademarked. The backlash was swift, given that chili crisp is a generic category with deep roots in Chinese cooking. Chang apologized on his podcast, and the company announced it would not enforce the trademark.

He has also been criticized for on-air remarks, including comments on *Ugly Delicious* dismissing certain regional cuisines.

## Legacy & Influence

Chang helped normalize a style of restaurant that is loud, informal, and ambitious at once, and his success opened doors for chefs cooking Asian food without deferring to European conventions. Alumni of Momofuku kitchens, including Christina Tosi and Momofuku Ko's Sean Gray, went on to run acclaimed restaurants. His media work moved conversations about authenticity, immigration, and "ethnic" food into the mainstream.
