## Summary

Max Martin, born Karl Martin Sandberg on 26 February 1971, is a Swedish record producer and songwriter who has shaped mainstream pop music for nearly three decades. Emerging from Stockholm's Cheiron Studios in the mid-1990s, he wrote and produced defining hits for Backstreet Boys, Britney Spears and NSYNC before adapting his approach for later stars including Katy Perry, Taylor Swift, The Weeknd and Ariana Grande.

By 2024 Martin had written 27 songs that reached No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100, more than any songwriter except Paul McCartney, and he holds the record for the most No. 1 singles as a producer. He is credited with codifying a melody-first, hook-dense style of songwriting that became the template for 21st-century pop.

## Early Life & Background

Martin was born in Stenhamra on the island of Färingsö in Ekerö Municipality, outside Stockholm. His father was a police officer and his mother a teacher. He studied French horn through the municipal music school before switching to drums and singing.

In 1985 he co-founded the glam metal band It's Alive, taking the stage name Martin White. The group signed to Cheiron Records, a label founded by producer Denniz PoP (Dag Volle) and Tom Talomaa, and released the album "Earthquake" in 1994. The record sold poorly, but Denniz PoP recognised Sandberg's ear for melody, invited him into the studio and renamed him Max Martin.

## Rise to Prominence

Martin's first major credit came as co-producer of Ace of Base's 1995 single "Beautiful Life." Cheiron then began working with American boy band Backstreet Boys, and Martin co-wrote "Quit Playing Games (with My Heart)" (1996), which reached No. 2 in the United States.

In 1998 he wrote and produced "...Baby One More Time" for the 16-year-old Britney Spears. The song topped the Hot 100 in January 1999, giving Martin his first US No. 1. That same year "I Want It That Way" made Backstreet Boys a global act. Denniz PoP died of cancer in August 1998, and after Cheiron closed in 2000 Martin co-founded Maratone Studios with Talomaa.

After boy-band pop faded, Martin reinvented himself. "Since U Been Gone" (2004), written with Dr. Luke for Kelly Clarkson, fused rock guitars with pop structure and opened a second era of dominance.

## Key Achievements

- **Katy Perry:** Co-wrote "I Kissed a Girl" (2008) and five No. 1 singles from "Teenage Dream" (2010), tying a record set by Michael Jackson.
- **Taylor Swift:** Co-produced "1989" (2014), including "Shake It Off" and "Blank Space." The album won the Grammy for Album of the Year in 2016.
- **The Weeknd:** Co-wrote and produced "Can't Feel My Face" (2015) and "Blinding Lights" (2019), which became the longest-charting Hot 100 song in history.
- **Ariana Grande:** Co-wrote "Problem," "7 rings" and, in 2024, "we can't be friends (wait for your love)," his 27th No. 1 as a writer.
- **Awards:** Grammy for Producer of the Year, Non-Classical (2015); ASCAP Songwriter of the Year a record eleven times; Polar Music Prize (2016).
- **Theatre:** The jukebox musical "& Juliet," built on his catalogue, opened in London in 2019 and on Broadway in 2022.

## Controversies & Criticism

Critics have accused Martin of homogenising pop through a formula he calls "melodic math," arguing that his hits prioritise phonetic flow over lyrical meaning. John Seabrook's 2015 book "The Song Machine" portrayed Cheiron as an industrial hit factory. Martin's protégé Dr. Luke became the subject of Kesha's 2014 abuse allegations, and although Martin was never accused, their long collaboration drew scrutiny. He was also a co-defendant in a copyright lawsuit over "Shake It Off," filed in 2017 and settled in December 2022. Martin rarely gives interviews, which some critics contrast with the scale of his influence.

## Legacy & Influence

Martin transformed Sweden into a pop-export powerhouse and mentored a generation of producers, including Shellback, Savan Kotecha and Rami Yacoub. His emphasis on immediate hooks, tight structure and vocal melody defined the sound of radio pop from 1999 onward. Streaming-era hits like "Blinding Lights" show that his methods remained commercially dominant across three distinct musical generations.
