## Summary

Avicii, born Tim Bergling on 8 September 1989 in Stockholm, was a Swedish DJ and producer who became one of the defining figures of the early 2010s electronic dance music boom. His 2011 single "Levels" and the 2013 folk-influenced hit "Wake Me Up" carried festival-style dance music into mainstream pop charts worldwide.

He retired from touring in 2016 after years of health problems tied to alcohol and the pressures of a relentless schedule. He died by suicide on 20 April 2018 in Muscat, Oman, at age 28. His death prompted a broad public conversation about mental health in the music industry, and his family founded the Tim Bergling Foundation in his name.

## Early Life & Background

Bergling grew up in the Östermalm district of Stockholm, the son of Klas Bergling and actress Anki Lidén. He began producing tracks in his bedroom at 16, posting remixes on electronic music forums. He adopted the name Avicii, derived from Avīci, the lowest level of hell in Buddhist cosmology, because "Tim Berg" was already taken on Myspace. In 2007 he signed to the Dejfitts Plays label, and Arash "Ash" Pournouri became his manager, shaping his early career strategy.

## Rise to Prominence

Releases such as "Seek Bromance" (2010, under the name Tim Berg) built his profile in Europe. "Levels", released in October 2011 and built on a sample of Etta James's "Something's Got a Hold on Me", became a global anthem and earned a Grammy nomination for Best Dance Recording. "I Could Be the One" with Nicky Romero reached number one in the UK in early 2013.

At Ultra Music Festival in Miami in March 2013, he previewed a set blending house music with bluegrass and country musicians, and parts of the crowd booed. Months later, "Wake Me Up" featuring Aloe Blacc, released in June 2013, vindicated the experiment, topping charts in more than 20 countries and reaching number four on the Billboard Hot 100. His debut album "True" followed on 13 September 2013.

## Key Achievements

- **Albums:** "True" (2013), "Stories" (2015), the EP "Avīci (01)" (2017), and the posthumous "Tim" (2019), which included "SOS" and "Heaven" with Chris Martin.
- **Collaborations:** Co-produced Coldplay's "A Sky Full of Stars" (2014) and worked with David Guetta, Madonna, and Nile Rodgers.
- **Recognition:** Two Grammy nominations, MTV and Billboard awards, and a number three placing in DJ Mag's Top 100 DJs poll in 2012 and 2013.
- **Touring:** Headlined major festivals worldwide before performing his final show at Ushuaïa in Ibiza on 28 August 2016.

## Controversies & Criticism

The Ultra 2013 country-tinged set was initially mocked by dance purists, and "Wake Me Up" drew accusations of diluting EDM for pop radio. A 2011 dispute arose when Leona Lewis's "Collide" used the melody of his track "Fade into Darkness" without initial credit; the matter was settled with shared credit.

More serious scrutiny followed the 2017 documentary "Avicii: True Stories", which showed him hospitalized with acute pancreatitis in 2012, having his gallbladder and appendix removed in 2014, and pleading to stop touring. Critics questioned whether his management and industry pushed him too hard, though Pournouri disputed that characterization.

## Legacy & Influence

Avicii helped fuse electronic production with acoustic songwriting, a template later followed by acts such as Kygo and The Chainsmokers. After his death, his family established the Tim Bergling Foundation to support mental health and suicide prevention. A tribute concert at Stockholm's Friends Arena on 5 December 2019 raised funds for the foundation. In 2021 Stockholm's Ericsson Globe was renamed Avicii Arena, and the Avicii Experience museum opened in the city in 2022. The 2024 Netflix documentary "Avicii – I'm Tim" renewed attention to his story.
