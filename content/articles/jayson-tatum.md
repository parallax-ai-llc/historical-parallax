## Summary

Jayson Christopher Tatum (born March 3, 1998) is an American professional basketball player for the Boston Celtics of the National Basketball Association (NBA). A 6-foot-8 forward, he was selected third overall in the 2017 NBA draft and has spent his entire career in Boston, developing from a promising rookie into one of the league's premier scorers and a multiple-time All-NBA First Team selection.

Tatum led the Celtics to the 2024 NBA championship, the franchise's record 18th title, and signed the largest contract in league history at that time shortly afterward. He has also won two Olympic gold medals with the United States. His importance to the Celtics organization, combined with his two-way versatility and durability, places him among the defining players of his generation.

## Early Life & Background

Tatum was born in St. Louis, Missouri, to Justin Tatum and Brandy Cole. His mother was 19 when he was born and later completed law school while raising him. His father played college basketball at Saint Louis University and coached at Christian Brothers College High School. Former NBA guard Larry Hughes is Tatum's godfather.

Tatum attended Chaminade College Preparatory School in St. Louis, where he was named the 2016 Gatorade National Boys Basketball Player of the Year and a McDonald's All-American. He won gold medals with USA Basketball at the 2013 FIBA Americas Under-16 Championship, the 2014 FIBA Under-17 World Championship, and the 2015 FIBA Under-19 World Championship. He played one season at Duke University under Mike Krzyzewski in 2016–17, averaging 16.8 points and 7.3 rebounds after missing the first eight games with a foot injury.

## Rise to Prominence

The Celtics traded down from the first pick to select Tatum third in 2017. As a rookie he averaged 13.9 points, earned All-Rookie First Team honors, and helped a depleted Boston roster reach Game 7 of the 2018 Eastern Conference Finals. He made his first All-Star team in 2020 and was named All-NBA Third Team in both 2020 and 2021.

On January 30, 2021, he scored 60 points against the San Antonio Spurs, tying Larry Bird's franchise single-game record. In 2022 he was named All-NBA First Team for the first time and won the inaugural Larry Bird Trophy as Eastern Conference Finals MVP, though Boston lost the NBA Finals to Golden State in six games. He averaged a career-high 30.1 points in 2022–23 and scored a Game 7 record 51 points against Philadelphia in the 2023 conference semifinals.

## Key Achievements

- NBA champion (2024), defeating the Dallas Mavericks 4–1
- Six-time NBA All-Star (2020–2025); 2023 All-Star Game MVP with a record 55 points
- All-NBA First Team four times (2022–2025), Third Team twice (2020–2021)
- Olympic gold medalist at Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024
- Signed a five-year, $314 million supermax extension in July 2024, then the largest contract in NBA history
- Released his signature Jordan Brand shoe, the Jordan Tatum 1, in 2023

## Controversies & Criticism

Tatum's playoff performances have drawn scrutiny. He shot 36.7 percent from the field in the 2022 Finals and committed 23 turnovers in the series. In the 2024 Finals his teammate Jaylen Brown was named MVP, prompting debate over Tatum's standing among the game's elite. At the 2024 Paris Olympics, coach Steve Kerr left him on the bench for entire games, including the group-stage win over Serbia, generating widespread criticism of both the decision and Tatum's fit on the roster. Commentators have also questioned his late-game shot selection and reliance on isolation play.

On May 12, 2025, Tatum ruptured his right Achilles tendon in Game 4 of the second round against the New York Knicks and underwent surgery the next day. The injury sidelined him for most of the 2025–26 season and raised questions about his long-term explosiveness.

## Legacy & Influence

Tatum ended the Celtics' 16-year championship drought and became the face of the league's most decorated franchise before turning 27. His combination of size, shooting, and defensive versatility has become a template for the modern wing. Off the court, he co-authored the children's book *Baby Dunks-a-Lot* in 2023, and his son Deuce has become a familiar presence at Celtics games.
