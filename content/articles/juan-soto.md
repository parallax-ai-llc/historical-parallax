## Summary

Juan José Soto Pacheco is a Dominican professional baseball outfielder for the New York Mets of Major League Baseball (MLB). He previously played for the Washington Nationals, San Diego Padres, and New York Yankees. Known for exceptional plate discipline, he has led the majors in walks and on-base percentage multiple times and is frequently compared to Ted Williams for his batting eye.

Soto won a World Series with the Nationals in 2019 at age 20 and the National League batting title in 2020 at age 21. In December 2024 he signed a 15-year, $765 million contract with the Mets, the largest contract in professional sports history at the time. His combination of youth, power, and discipline has made him one of the defining hitters of his generation.

## Early Life & Background

Soto was born on October 25, 1998, in Santo Domingo, Dominican Republic. His father, Juan Soto Sr., had played amateur baseball and coached him from an early age. He trained at academies in the Dominican Republic as a teenager, initially drawing modest attention compared with higher-profile prospects.

On July 2, 2015, the Washington Nationals signed him as an international free agent for a $1.5 million bonus. He progressed through the Gulf Coast League and Class A affiliates, and despite missing much of 2017 with hand and ankle injuries, he was promoted rapidly through three minor league levels in early 2018.

## Rise to Prominence

Soto made his MLB debut on May 20, 2018, at age 19, and homered on the first pitch he saw in his first start the following day. He finished the season with 22 home runs and a .406 on-base percentage, placing second in National League Rookie of the Year voting behind Ronald Acuña Jr.

In 2019 he hit 34 home runs with 110 RBI and became a postseason star. His bases-clearing single off Josh Hader in the Wild Card Game and three World Series home runs helped the Nationals defeat the Houston Astros in seven games. In the pandemic-shortened 2020 season, he won the NL batting title with a .351 average and a 1.185 OPS.

He finished second in NL MVP voting in 2021 after leading MLB with a .465 on-base percentage and 145 walks. After declining a reported 15-year, $440 million extension, he was traded to the Padres on August 2, 2022, in a deal that sent prospects including CJ Abrams, MacKenzie Gore, and James Wood to Washington. San Diego traded him to the Yankees on December 6, 2023. In 2024 he hit 41 home runs, scored 128 runs, and delivered the 10th-inning home run in Game 5 of the ALCS that sent the Yankees to the World Series, finishing third in AL MVP voting.

## Key Achievements

- World Series champion (2019) and 2022 Home Run Derby winner
- NL batting champion (2020), the youngest in league history at 21
- Five-time All-Star (2021–2025) and multiple Silver Slugger awards
- Led MLB in walks in 2021, 2022, and 2025, and in on-base percentage in 2020 and 2021
- Signed a record 15-year, $765 million contract with the Mets on December 8, 2024, with no deferred money
- Became the first Mets player with a 40-homer, 30-steal season in 2025, finishing with 43 home runs and 38 stolen bases

## Controversies & Criticism

Soto's rejection of the Nationals' $440 million offer drew criticism from some fans and commentators who viewed it as prioritizing money over loyalty, though others noted the deal's heavy deferrals. His departure from the Yankees for the Mets led to booing during his 2025 return to Yankee Stadium.

Early in the 2025 season, Mets manager Carlos Mendoza publicly addressed instances in which Soto did not run hard on batted balls, and his slow start fueled debate about whether he was living up to his contract. Defensive metrics have consistently rated him as a below-average right fielder, and his signature "Soto Shuffle" after taking pitches has been criticized by some opponents as showmanship.

## Legacy & Influence

Soto reached 200 career home runs and 700 career walks earlier in age than nearly any player in history, and his walk rates have been compared to those of Ted Williams and Barry Bonds. His 2019 postseason established him as one of the most productive young players ever in October baseball.

His contract reshaped the market for elite free agents and underscored the Mets' willingness under owner Steve Cohen to outspend rivals. In the Dominican Republic he is regarded as a successor to stars such as Vladimir Guerrero and Albert Pujols, and his patient approach at the plate has become a model for young hitters.
