## Summary

Paul Bocuse (11 February 1926 – 20 January 2018) was a French chef and restaurateur widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in twentieth-century gastronomy. Based in Collonges-au-Mont-d'Or near Lyon, he became the public face of nouvelle cuisine in the 1970s and helped transform the chef from a kitchen employee into a celebrated public personality.

His flagship restaurant, L'Auberge du Pont de Collonges, held three Michelin stars continuously from 1965 until 2020, the longest such run in the guide's history. Through his restaurants, brasseries, the Bocuse d'Or competition, and the Institut Paul Bocuse, he shaped generations of cooks and established Lyon's reputation as a world capital of food.

## Early Life & Background

Bocuse was born on 11 February 1926 in Collonges-au-Mont-d'Or, into a family of cooks and millers who had worked in the area since the 1760s. His father, Georges Bocuse, ran a small restaurant, and Paul began working in kitchens as a child.

During the Second World War he joined the Free French forces in 1944, was wounded in Alsace, and was treated in an American field hospital. After the war he trained under Eugénie Brazier, the "Mère Brazier," at her restaurant at Col de la Luère, and later at La Pyramide in Vienne under Fernand Point, the chef he most often cited as his mentor. He also spent time at Lucas Carton in Paris.

## Rise to Prominence

Bocuse returned to the family restaurant in Collonges in 1956 and took it over from his father in 1959. The restaurant earned its first Michelin star in 1958, a second in 1962, and a third in 1965. In 1961 he won the Meilleur Ouvrier de France title, one of the country's highest craft distinctions.

Alongside Jean and Pierre Troisgros, Michel Guérard, and Roger Vergé, he became associated with nouvelle cuisine, a movement promoted by critics Henri Gault and Christian Millau from 1973. It favored lighter sauces, shorter cooking times, fresh regional ingredients, and simpler plating, in contrast to the heavy classical repertoire codified by Escoffier. Bocuse's own cooking remained rooted in Lyonnais tradition, and he later distanced himself from the label.

In 1975 he was made a Chevalier of the Légion d'honneur by President Valéry Giscard d'Estaing. For the ceremony at the Élysée Palace he created the black truffle soup "V.G.E.", served under a puff pastry dome, which became his signature dish.

## Key Achievements

- **Three Michelin stars** held without interruption for 55 years, from 1965 to 2020.
- **Bocuse d'Or**, founded in Lyon in 1987, is a biennial competition often described as the world championship of cooking.
- **Institut Paul Bocuse**, established in Écully in 1990, trains students in culinary arts and hospitality management.
- **Brasseries in Lyon**, beginning with Le Nord in 1994, followed by Le Sud, L'Est, and L'Ouest, along with restaurants at Walt Disney World's Epcot in Florida since 1982.
- Named "Chef of the Century" by Gault et Millau in 1989 and by the Culinary Institute of America in 2011.
- Author of *La Cuisine du marché* (1976), published in English as *Paul Bocuse's French Cooking*.

## Controversies & Criticism

Critics observed that Bocuse spent much of his later career travelling, promoting products, and appearing in media rather than cooking, leading to debates about whether a celebrity chef could maintain standards in absentia. Some food writers considered his menu conservative and unchanged for decades.

His personal life drew attention when he openly acknowledged maintaining long-term relationships with three women simultaneously, including his wife Raymonde, a fact he discussed without apology in interviews. His remark that "the best restaurants are run by men" was also criticized, though he trained under and consistently credited Mère Brazier.

In January 2020, two years after his death, Michelin demoted L'Auberge du Pont de Collonges to two stars, stating the restaurant was "excellent but no longer at the level of three stars." The decision provoked criticism in France, with some seeing it as disrespectful to his legacy.

## Legacy & Influence

Bocuse died on 20 January 2018 at his home in Collonges, aged 91, from Parkinson's disease. His funeral at Lyon's Saint-Jean Cathedral drew around 1,500 chefs from around the world. Lyon's central covered market has been named Les Halles de Lyon Paul Bocuse since 2006.

His example established the model of the chef as entrepreneur, brand, and public figure, later followed by Alain Ducasse, Joël Robuchon, and many others. The Bocuse d'Or continues to award the profession's most prestigious competitive title, and the Institut Paul Bocuse has produced thousands of graduates. He remains the reference point for French culinary identity in the modern era.
