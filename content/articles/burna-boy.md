## Summary

Burna Boy, born Damini Ebunoluwa Ogulu on 2 July 1991 in Port Harcourt, Nigeria, is a singer, songwriter and rapper who describes his sound as "Afro-fusion", a blend of Afrobeats, dancehall, reggae, hip hop and R&B. Since his 2012 breakout single "Like to Party", he has become one of the most commercially successful African musicians in history and a central figure in the global spread of Nigerian pop.

His 2020 album *Twice as Tall* won the Grammy Award for Best Global Music Album in March 2021, and he has since become a fixture at the ceremony, performing there in February 2024 as the first Afrobeats artist to do so. He was the first African artist to headline and sell out Madison Square Garden (April 2022) and London Stadium (June 2023), venues that had previously been out of reach for acts from the continent.

Burna Boy's career has also been marked by recurring disputes, including a 2017 assault case in Lagos, public feuds with fellow African artists, and controversial remarks about the Afrobeats genre itself.

## Early Life & Background

Ogulu was born into a musically connected family in Port Harcourt, Rivers State. His grandfather, Benson Idonije, was the first manager of Afrobeat pioneer Fela Kuti, and his mother, Bose Ogulu, later became his manager and the head of his Spaceship Entertainment label. He attended Corona Secondary School in Agbara, Ogun State, before moving to the United Kingdom, where he briefly studied media technology at the University of Sussex and later attended Oxford Brookes University. He left without completing a degree and returned to Nigeria around 2010 to pursue music, initially working with producers in Port Harcourt and Lagos.

## Rise to Prominence

Signed to Aristokrat Records, Burna Boy released "Like to Party" in 2012, followed by his debut album *L.I.F.E* (2013), which established him in the Nigerian market. His second album, *On a Spaceship* (2015), and the *Redemption* EP (2016) came during a turbulent period that included a split from Aristokrat and a period living in London. The 2018 album *Outside* and its single "Ye" marked his international breakthrough, aided by a viral confusion with Kanye West's album of the same name. A small-print billing on the 2019 Coachella poster prompted his response album *African Giant* (2019), which earned his first Grammy nomination for Best World Music Album.

## Key Achievements

- **Grammy Award**: *Twice as Tall* (2020), executive produced with Sean "Diddy" Combs, won Best Global Music Album in March 2021. He has received further nominations for *Love, Damini* (2022), *I Told Them...* (2023) and later singles.
- **Chart records**: *Love, Damini* debuted at number 14 on the US Billboard 200 in July 2022, then a record for a Nigerian artist. *I Told Them...* entered at number 5 in September 2023. His seventh album, *No Sign of Weakness*, was released in July 2025.
- **Live milestones**: sold-out headline shows at Madison Square Garden (28 April 2022), London Stadium (3 June 2023) and Citi Field, New York (8 July 2023), the last making him the first African artist to headline a US stadium.
- **Awards**: BET Award for Best International Act in 2019, 2020 and 2021, and multiple MOBO and MTV Europe Music Awards.

## Controversies & Criticism

In 2017 Lagos police declared Burna Boy wanted in connection with an assault and robbery of singer Mr 2Kay at the Eko Hotel; he was arrested in 2018 and charged with conspiracy. In 2019 he vowed not to perform in South Africa after xenophobic violence against Nigerians, leading to the cancellation of the "Africans Unite" concert and a public feud with rapper AKA. In June 2022 his police escorts were accused of shooting two people at a Lagos nightclub after an altercation allegedly linked to the singer. Critics have also cited habitual late arrivals at shows and his August 2023 comment that Afrobeats "lacks substance", which drew rebukes from peers who felt he was dismissing the genre that built his career.

## Legacy & Influence

Burna Boy is widely credited, alongside Wizkid and Davido, with turning Afrobeats from a regional scene into a global commercial force. His stadium runs demonstrated the touring viability of African acts in North America and Europe, and his Grammy success helped prompt the Recording Academy to create the Best African Music Performance category in 2024. By openly channelling Fela Kuti's political Afrobeat within contemporary pop, he has kept that lineage visible to a new generation of listeners.
