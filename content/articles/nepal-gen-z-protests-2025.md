## Summary

In September 2025, protests led by young Nepalis against a government social media ban and entrenched corruption became one of the most dramatic political upheavals in the country's recent history [^1]. Security forces opened fire on demonstrators at parliament on 8 September. The next day, crowds set fire to parliament and other state buildings, and Prime Minister K. P. Sharma Oli resigned [^1][^3]. Former chief justice Sushila Karki, whom protesters had chosen partly through polls on the chat app Discord, became Nepal's first female prime minister on 12 September [^2]. Officials have put the death toll at 76 [^4].

## Background

On 4 September 2025, the government ordered 26 unregistered social media platforms blocked, including Facebook, X, and YouTube [^1]. Critics said the ban was meant to silence a viral "nepo kids" trend, in which young people contrasted the luxurious lifestyles of politicians' children with widespread poverty, unemployment, and labor migration [^1][^3].

## The Event

Thousands of young people gathered outside the Federal Parliament in Kathmandu on 8 September. When some tried to enter the complex, police used tear gas, water cannon, rubber bullets, and live ammunition. At least 19 people were killed that day [^1]. On 9 September, protesters set fire to the parliament building, the Singha Durbar government complex, the Supreme Court, and the homes of senior politicians, and prisoners escaped from several jails [^1][^3]. Oli resigned that evening, and the army took over security in the capital [^3]. Protest groups held online discussions and polls on Discord and backed Karki as interim leader [^2][^3].

## Response and Aftermath

President Ramchandra Paudel swore in Karki on 12 September, after talks among the president, army chief Ashok Raj Sigdel, and protest representatives [^2]. The social media ban was lifted, parliament was dissolved, and elections were called for 5 March 2026 [^1][^2]. Karki promised that her government would not stay in office more than six months and would hand power to an elected administration [^5]. In the March 2026 election, the Rastriya Swatantra Party won about 182 of 275 seats. Its senior leader, former Kathmandu mayor Balendra "Balen" Shah, a 35-year-old ex-rapper and engineer, defeated Oli in his own Jhapa constituency and was sworn in as prime minister in late March [^6][^7].

## Positive Perspectives

- The youth movement forced a government it considered corrupt and unresponsive to resign within 24 hours of the shootings [^3].
- Protesters' use of Discord polls to pick an interim leader, a former chief justice known for challenging political elites, drew international attention [^3].
- Elections were held on 5 March 2026, the date the interim government had set, and power passed to an elected government [^6][^7].
- Voters turned against established parties, and Wikipedia notes that about two-thirds of the previous parliament's members did not run again [^1].
- The interim government set up a commission to investigate the violence [^1].

## Negative Perspectives

- Security forces used live ammunition against largely young, unarmed protesters, and dozens died on 8 September [^1].
- The arson on 9 September badly damaged key state institutions, including parliament and the Supreme Court [^1].
- Death toll figures changed over time. Nepal's chief secretary first reported 51 deaths and then 72, and the figure of 76 was cited later [^5][^4]. Wikipedia's breakdown includes police officers and prisoners shot during jailbreaks [^1].
- Many prisoners escaped during the unrest, which raised security concerns long after the protests ended [^3].
- The social media ban that set off the crisis was seen by critics as an attack on free expression [^1].

## Recent News

Balendra Shah's government took office in March 2026 after the Rastriya Swatantra Party's landslide victory [^7]. Wikipedia records 2,660 confirmed injuries as of May 2026 [^1].
