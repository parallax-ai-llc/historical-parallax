## Summary

Brady Corbet (born August 17, 1988) is an American film director, screenwriter, and actor. After a two-decade acting career in independent and European art-house cinema, he moved behind the camera and established himself as one of the most ambitious American auteurs of his generation with three features: *The Childhood of a Leader* (2015), *Vox Lux* (2018), and *The Brutalist* (2024).

*The Brutalist*, a 215-minute period epic shot in VistaVision on a budget of under $10 million, won the Silver Lion for Best Director at the Venice Film Festival, earned Corbet the BAFTA and Golden Globe for Best Director, and received 10 Academy Award nominations, winning three. Corbet writes all of his films with his partner, Norwegian filmmaker Mona Fastvold.

He matters because he has repeatedly demonstrated that large-scale, formally rigorous, historically minded cinema can be made outside the studio system, and because he has been an unusually outspoken critic of the economics facing independent directors.

## Early Life & Background

Corbet was born in Scottsdale, Arizona, and raised primarily in Colorado by his mother. He began acting as a child and did not attend university, instead educating himself on set and through a self-directed study of film history. His early exposure to directors such as Michael Haneke, Lars von Trier, and Gregg Araki, all of whom he later worked with, shaped his taste for austere, morally probing European cinema.

## Rise to Prominence

Corbet's acting breakthrough came with Catherine Hardwicke's *Thirteen* (2003) and Gregg Araki's *Mysterious Skin* (2004). He played Derek Huxley in season five of *24* (2006) and appeared in Haneke's English-language remake *Funny Games* (2007). Throughout the 2010s he built a reputation as a serious-minded character actor in *Martha Marcy May Marlene* (2011), von Trier's *Melancholia* (2011), *Simon Killer* (2012, which he co-wrote), Ruben Östlund's *Force Majeure* (2014), and Olivier Assayas's *Clouds of Sils Maria* (2014).

He co-wrote Fastvold's directorial debut *The Sleepwalker* (2014) before making his own, *The Childhood of a Leader*, a fable about the making of a fascist set after World War I. It premiered in the Orizzonti section at Venice in 2015 and won both the Best Director award for that section and the Luigi De Laurentiis Award for best debut film. *Vox Lux* (2018), starring Natalie Portman as a pop star whose career begins after a school shooting, competed at Venice and divided critics.

## Key Achievements

- **The Brutalist (2024):** Starring Adrien Brody as a Hungarian-Jewish architect who emigrates to the United States after the Holocaust. It premiered in competition at Venice on September 1, 2024, and won the Silver Lion. It was distributed by A24.
- **Awards season 2024–25:** Three Golden Globes (Best Motion Picture – Drama, Best Director, Best Actor – Drama). Corbet won the BAFTA for Best Director. At the 97th Academy Awards the film won Best Actor (Brody), Best Cinematography (Lol Crawley), and Best Original Score (Daniel Blumberg) from 10 nominations, including Best Picture, Best Director, and Best Original Screenplay.
- **Writing partnership:** He co-wrote Fastvold's *The Testament of Ann Lee* (2025), which premiered at Venice.

## Controversies & Criticism

In January 2025, editor Dávid Jancsó disclosed that the AI voice tool Respeecher was used to refine the Hungarian-language dialogue of Brody and Felicity Jones, and that generative AI had been used for some architectural drawings in the film's epilogue. Critics questioned whether this undercut the film's craft credentials. Corbet responded that the performances were entirely the actors' own, that the tool only adjusted specific vowel sounds, and that the final drawings were made by human artists.

The film's epilogue, set in Jerusalem, drew debate about its stance on Zionism and Jewish identity, with some reviewers reading it as endorsement and others as critique. *Vox Lux* was also criticized for its handling of school-shooting imagery. Corbet's public remarks that he earned effectively nothing from *The Brutalist* and relied on commercial directing to survive were praised by some as candor and dismissed by others as self-pity.

## Legacy & Influence

Corbet is frequently cited as proof that epic-scale filmmaking remains possible on independent budgets, and his use of VistaVision helped revive interest in the format among cinematographers. His trilogy of films about the 20th century, examining fascism, celebrity, and immigration, has positioned him alongside Paul Thomas Anderson and Robert Eggers as an American director working in a deliberately European tradition of authorial control.
