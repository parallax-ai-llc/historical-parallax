## Summary

Olof Kajbjer Gustafsson, known as olofmeister, is a Swedish former professional Counter-Strike: Global Offensive player born on 31 January 1992. He is best known for his years with Fnatic, where he anchored the roster that dominated the game in 2015, and for a later spell with FaZe Clan. HLTV named him the world's best player of 2015.

His importance rests on a rare combination of consistency and versatility. He played as a rifler but was equally comfortable on the AWP, entry-fragging, or holding a site alone, and he produced his best performances in finals rather than in group stages. Two Major championships, a stretch of six consecutive tournament wins with Fnatic, and several of the most replayed clips in Counter-Strike history make him one of the defining players of the game's first decade.

## Early Life & Background

Gustafsson grew up in Sweden and, like most of his generation of Swedish professionals, played Counter-Strike 1.6 and Counter-Strike: Source before switching to CS:GO after its 2012 release. He competed on smaller Swedish lineups, including H2k, before joining LGB eSports in 2013 alongside Freddy "KRIMZ" Johansson. LGB reached the semifinals of the EMS One Katowice Major in March 2014, which drew attention from larger organizations.

## Rise to Prominence

In August 2014, Fnatic signed olofmeister and KRIMZ to replace two departing players. The new roster won its first event, and olofmeister became the team's most reliable fragger. At DreamHack Winter 2014, the team was involved in the "olof boost" incident, described below, which made his name known far beyond the competitive scene.

The 2015 season established him as the best player in the world. Fnatic won ESL One Katowice 2015 and ESL One Cologne 2015, both Majors, along with a string of premier events. At Katowice he produced the "burning defuse", defusing a bomb while standing in fire against Virtus.pro, a play still shown at nearly every tournament broadcast. HLTV ranked him first in its 2015 Top 20.

In early 2016, Fnatic won six consecutive tournaments before a wrist injury forced olofmeister to miss MLG Columbus 2016 and several months of play. He returned later that year, but the roster was restructured and never regained its earlier dominance.

## Key Achievements

- Two Major championships with Fnatic: ESL One Katowice 2015 and ESL One Cologne 2015.
- HLTV world number one player of 2015, with further Top 20 placements in 2014, 2016, and 2017.
- Joined FaZe Clan in August 2017 and won ESL One New York 2017, the ELEAGUE CS:GO Premier 2017, and the ECS Season 4 Finals within four months.
- Reached the final of the ELEAGUE Boston Major in January 2018, losing to Cloud9 in overtime.
- Won IEM Sydney 2018, ESL One Belo Horizonte 2018, EPICENTER 2018, and BLAST Pro Series Miami 2019 with FaZe.

## Controversies & Criticism

The "olof boost" at DreamHack Winter 2014 remains the most discussed incident of his career. In a quarterfinal against Team LDLC on Overpass, Fnatic used a pixel-walk boost that gave olofmeister a view across much of the map. Fnatic came back from 3-12 to win 16-13. Administrators ruled the position exploited a map bug, ordered a replay, and Fnatic instead forfeited the match. The incident led to a rule change on boosting in most leagues.

In April 2018 he took a leave of absence from FaZe for personal reasons, and his form afterward drew steady criticism. He stepped down from the active roster in May 2020, returned briefly as a stand-in later that year, and was benched again in early 2021. He has not played at the top level since.

## Legacy & Influence

Olofmeister is regularly placed among the best CS:GO players of all time, and the 2015 Fnatic lineup is often cited as the strongest roster of the game's early era. His adaptability across roles influenced how teams built rosters, favoring flexible riflers over rigid specialists. The burning defuse and the olof boost remain two of the most recognizable moments in the game's history, and the boost directly shaped competitive rules on map exploits.
