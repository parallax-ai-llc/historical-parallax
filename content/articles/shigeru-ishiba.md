## Summary

Shigeru Ishiba is a Japanese politician who served as the 102nd and 103rd Prime Minister of Japan from 1 October 2024 to 21 October 2025. A member of the Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) since 1986, he represented Tottori Prefecture in the House of Representatives for nearly four decades and held the defense, agriculture, and regional revitalization portfolios before reaching the premiership on his fifth attempt at the party leadership.

Ishiba built his reputation as a security policy specialist and an internal party critic, a stance that made him popular with the public but distrusted by LDP factions. His short premiership was marked by the loss of the ruling coalition's majorities in both houses of the Diet, negotiations with the United States over tariffs, and the fallout from a party funding scandal he inherited.

## Early Life & Background

Ishiba was born in Tokyo on 4 February 1957 and raised in Tottori Prefecture. His father, Jirō Ishiba, was governor of Tottori from 1958 to 1974 and later Minister of Home Affairs in the cabinet of Masayoshi Ōhira. Ishiba graduated from the Faculty of Law at Keio University in 1979 and joined Mitsui Bank, where he worked until 1983. After his father's death in 1981, former Prime Minister Kakuei Tanaka encouraged him to enter politics. He is a practicing Protestant Christian, a rarity among Japanese politicians, and is known for his interests in military models, railways, and ramen.

## Rise to Prominence

In 1986 Ishiba won a seat in the House of Representatives at age 29, then the youngest member of the chamber. He left the LDP in 1993 during a period of party splits, joined the New Frontier Party, and returned to the LDP in 1997. Under Junichirō Koizumi he served as Director General of the Defense Agency from 2002 to 2004, then as Minister of Defense from 2007 to 2008 under Yasuo Fukuda and Minister of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries from 2008 to 2009 under Tarō Asō.

He contested the LDP presidency in 2008, 2012, 2018, and 2020. In 2012 he won the first round of rank-and-file voting but lost the parliamentary runoff to Shinzō Abe, who then appointed him Secretary-General from 2012 to 2014 and Minister for Regional Revitalization from 2014 to 2016. On 27 September 2024 he defeated Sanae Takaichi in a runoff, 215 votes to 194, and became Prime Minister on 1 October 2024.

## Key Achievements

Ishiba was a leading voice in Japan's post-Cold War defense debate, advocating a stronger Self-Defense Forces posture, a revised Status of Forces Agreement with the United States, and a proposed "Asian NATO" collective security framework. As regional revitalization minister he directed policy to counter depopulation in rural prefectures. As Prime Minister he concluded a July 2025 trade agreement with the Trump administration that set a 15 percent tariff on Japanese exports paired with a $550 billion Japanese investment pledge, and he appointed Shinjirō Koizumi as agriculture minister to address surging rice prices. On 10 October 2025 he issued a personal statement marking 80 years since the end of the Second World War.

## Controversies & Criticism

Ishiba called a snap election for 27 October 2024, weeks into his term. The LDP-Komeito coalition fell to 215 of 465 seats after revelations that the party had sent ¥20 million to branches of candidates it had declined to endorse over the slush fund scandal. He retained office on 11 November 2024 only through the first Diet runoff vote since 1994. In March 2025 he faced criticism for giving ¥100,000 gift vouchers to 15 first-term LDP lawmakers. After the coalition lost its House of Councillors majority on 20 July 2025, he announced his resignation on 7 September 2025. Critics within the LDP faulted his low approval ratings, his frequent policy reversals, and his "Asian NATO" proposal as unrealistic.

## Legacy & Influence

Ishiba was the first LDP leader in decades to govern without a majority in either chamber, forcing the party into issue-by-issue bargaining with opposition parties. His career illustrates the tension between public popularity and factional support within the LDP. He was succeeded by Sanae Takaichi, Japan's first female Prime Minister, on 21 October 2025.
