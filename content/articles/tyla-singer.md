## Summary

Tyla Laura Seethal, known mononymously as Tyla, is a South African singer, songwriter and dancer born on 30 January 2002 in Johannesburg. She rose to global attention in 2023 with the single "Water", a fusion of amapiano, Afrobeats and R&B that she and her team branded "popiano". The song made her the first South African solo artist to enter the Billboard Hot 100 in 55 years and earned her the inaugural Grammy Award for Best African Music Performance.

Tyla matters because she carried amapiano, a genre born in South African townships in the 2010s, onto mainstream pop charts in the United States and Europe. Her dance-led visual style, particularly the viral "Water" choreography, showed how TikTok could turn a regional sound into a global one within weeks. By her mid-twenties she had two Grammy wins, a top-25 album on the Billboard 200 and a Coachella main-stage slot.

## Early Life & Background

Tyla was raised in Edenvale, a suburb east of Johannesburg, in a family of Indian, Zulu, Mauritian and Irish descent. She attended Edenglen High School and matriculated in 2019. She has said she grew up on Rihanna, Aaliyah and Michael Jackson, and she began posting cover songs on Instagram and YouTube as a teenager. Her parents allowed her a gap year after school to pursue music instead of studying mining engineering, a decision she has cited as the turning point in her career.

## Rise to Prominence

Her debut single "Getting Late", featuring producer Kooldrink, was released in December 2019 and gained traction in South Africa after its 2021 music video. Epic Records signed her in 2021. In early 2023 she opened for Chris Brown on the European leg of his Under the Influence tour. "Water" arrived on 28 July 2023, and a dance challenge in which she poured water down her back spread across TikTok. The single peaked at number 7 on the Billboard Hot 100 and reached the top ten in the United Kingdom. Her self-titled debut album followed on 22 March 2024, debuting at number 24 on the Billboard 200, with the singles "Truth or Dare", "ART" and "Jump" featuring Gunna and Skillibeng.

## Key Achievements

- **Grammy Awards**: "Water" won the first-ever Best African Music Performance award at the 66th Grammys on 4 February 2024. "Push 2 Start" won the same category at the 68th Grammys in February 2026.
- **Chart records**: "Water" was the first Hot 100 entry by a South African solo artist since Hugh Masekela's "Grazing in the Grass" in 1968.
- **Other honours**: MTV Video Music Award for Best Afrobeats and BET Award for Best New Artist in 2024, a TIME100 Next listing in 2024 and a 2025 Coachella performance.
- **Releases**: The deluxe album "Tyla +" in October 2024 and the EP "WWP" in July 2025, which included "Bliss" and "Dynamite" with Wizkid.

## Controversies & Criticism

Tyla's self-description as "Coloured", a standard multiracial classification in South Africa, drew backlash from American audiences who read the term as a slur. She addressed the debate in a 2024 statement explaining the South African context. Her 2024 VMA acceptance speech, in which she said African music is broader than the "Afrobeats" label, was praised by some and criticised by others as ungracious. Her association with Chris Brown, including tour and remix work, drew criticism given his history of assault. In December 2023 she cancelled her first headline tour after an injury that temporarily required a wheelchair, which disappointed fans who had bought tickets.

## Legacy & Influence

Tyla is widely credited with bringing amapiano to its largest international audience and with proving that a South African woman could headline global pop. Her Met Gala 2024 appearance in a Balmain sand-sculpted gown made her a fashion reference point, and her choreography-first approach to singles has influenced how African pop acts market music on short-form video. She remains an early-career artist, and the long-term measure of her legacy will depend on whether "popiano" outlives the viral moment that launched it.
