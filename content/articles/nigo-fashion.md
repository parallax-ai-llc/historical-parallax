## Summary

Nigo, born Tomoaki Nagao on 23 December 1970, is a Japanese fashion designer, DJ, record producer, and entrepreneur. He founded the streetwear label A Bathing Ape (BAPE) in Tokyo in 1993 and built it into one of the most recognizable brands in global street fashion, known for its camouflage prints, shark hoodies, and Bape Sta sneakers.

Beyond BAPE, Nigo co-founded Billionaire Boys Club and Ice Cream with Pharrell Williams in 2003, launched the label Human Made in 2010, and served as creative director of Uniqlo UT from 2014. In September 2021 he was appointed artistic director of Kenzo, becoming the first Japanese designer to lead the LVMH-owned house since its founder, Kenzo Takada.

His significance lies in bridging Tokyo's Harajuku street scene with American hip-hop culture and, later, European luxury fashion. Few figures have moved as fluidly between subculture, mass retail, and heritage fashion houses.

## Early Life & Background

Nigo was born in Maebashi, Gunma Prefecture, Japan. As a teenager he became absorbed in American pop culture, hip-hop, and 1980s Tokyo street style. He moved to Tokyo to attend Bunka Fashion College, one of Japan's leading design schools, where he studied alongside Jun Takahashi, the future founder of Undercover.

While a student he worked as an assistant to Hiroshi Fujiwara, the DJ and designer often described as the godfather of Harajuku streetwear. Fujiwara's style-editing and connection to hip-hop shaped Nigo's approach. The nickname "Nigo," meaning "number two" in Japanese, came from his resemblance to Fujiwara and his position as Fujiwara's understudy. He also worked as an editor and stylist for the magazine Popeye.

## Rise to Prominence

In April 1993 Nigo and Jun Takahashi opened the shop Nowhere in Harajuku. Later that year Nigo launched A Bathing Ape, its name a reference to the 1968 film Planet of the Apes and a Japanese idiom about bathing in lukewarm water, a jab at complacent youth. Early production was deliberately tiny, sometimes a few dozen T-shirts per week, creating scarcity that fueled demand.

By the late 1990s BAPE had become a phenomenon in Japan. Nigo's friendship with Pharrell Williams, introduced through Jacob the Jeweler around 2003, brought the brand to American hip-hop. Pharrell, Kanye West, Lil Wayne, and Clipse wore BAPE, and the brand's first US store opened in New York's SoHo in 2005. Nigo and Pharrell co-founded Billionaire Boys Club and Ice Cream in 2003, and Nigo formed the hip-hop group Teriyaki Boyz in 2005, whose song "Tokyo Drift" appeared in the 2006 film The Fast and the Furious: Tokyo Drift.

## Key Achievements

- Founded A Bathing Ape in 1993, growing it to dozens of stores worldwide before selling it to Hong Kong's I.T Group in 2011 for about 230 million Hong Kong dollars. He stepped down as creative director in 2013.
- Co-founded Billionaire Boys Club and Ice Cream with Pharrell Williams in 2003.
- Launched Human Made in 2010, a label rooted in vintage American workwear and mid-century graphics.
- Named creative director of Uniqlo UT in 2014, bringing streetwear collaboration culture to mass retail.
- Appointed artistic director of Kenzo in September 2021, presenting his first collection in January 2022.
- Released the album *I Know Nigo!* in March 2022, featuring Pharrell, A$AP Rocky, Tyler, the Creator, Kid Cudi, and Pusha T.
- Collaborated with Adidas, Louis Vuitton (with Virgil Abloh on the LV² capsules of 2020 and 2021), Kaws, and Nike.

## Controversies & Criticism

BAPE's reliance on remixing existing designs drew criticism. The Bape Sta sneaker, launched in 2000, closely mirrored Nike's Air Force 1, and in 2023 Nike sued BAPE for trademark infringement. Although Nigo had long since left the brand, the case reflected long-standing debate about his approach to appropriation versus homage.

Critics also noted that BAPE's exclusivity, based on extremely limited production, contributed to the resale and hype culture that later defined streetwear. Some observers viewed the brand's collapse in quality perception and financial trouble in the late 2000s as evidence that Nigo overexpanded, opening many stores and licensing aggressively before the 2011 sale. His early Kenzo collections received mixed reviews, with some critics finding them commercially safe.

## Legacy & Influence

Nigo's model of limited drops, celebrity seeding, and cross-industry collaboration became the template for modern streetwear, influencing Supreme's expansion, Off-White, and countless labels. His appointment at Kenzo, alongside Virgil Abloh at Louis Vuitton, marked luxury fashion's formal embrace of streetwear designers. He remains a prolific collector of pop-culture artifacts, and Human Made continues to reflect his archival sensibility. Through BAPE, Pharrell, and Kenzo, Nigo helped make Tokyo a permanent node in global fashion.
