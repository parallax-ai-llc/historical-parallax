## Summary

Ruben Östlund (born 13 April 1974) is a Swedish film director, screenwriter and producer known for satirical dramas that place ordinary people in socially awkward situations and observe how they behave. His films are built around long, static takes, meticulous staging and a cool, sociological interest in group dynamics, masculinity, class and moral cowardice.

Östlund is one of only a handful of filmmakers to have won the Palme d'Or at the Cannes Film Festival twice, for *The Square* (2017) and *Triangle of Sadness* (2022). Both films were nominated for Academy Awards, and *Triangle of Sadness* earned three nominations, including Best Picture and Best Director. His earlier feature *Force Majeure* (2014) became an international breakthrough and was remade in Hollywood as *Downhill* (2020).

Through his production company Plattform Produktion, co-founded in 2002, Östlund has helped shape a distinctive strand of contemporary Scandinavian cinema that combines dark comedy with rigorous formal control.

## Early Life & Background

Östlund was born on Styrsö, an island in the Gothenburg archipelago in western Sweden. He initially studied graphic design before enrolling at the University of Gothenburg's School of Film Directing, from which he graduated in 2001.

During the 1990s he spent several winters in the Alps directing ski films, an experience that shaped his preference for wide, patient shots and his fascination with how people act when observed. His short film *Autumn Man* (2001) and the feature-length documentary *Family Again* (2002) were early demonstrations of his interest in staged social discomfort.

## Rise to Prominence

His debut fiction feature, *The Guitar Mongoloid* (2004), won the FIPRESCI Prize at the Moscow International Film Festival. *Involuntary* (2008), a study of group pressure told through five loosely connected stories, premiered in the Un Certain Regard section at Cannes and established him internationally.

The short *Incident by a Bank* (2010), a single-take reconstruction of a botched robbery, won the Golden Bear for Best Short Film at the Berlin International Film Festival. *Play* (2011), based on a real series of robberies committed by youths in Gothenburg, screened in the Directors' Fortnight at Cannes and won the Coup de Cœur prize.

*Force Majeure* (2014), about a father who flees an avalanche and abandons his family, won the Un Certain Regard Jury Prize at Cannes, received a Golden Globe nomination and became his most widely seen film to that point.

## Key Achievements

- **Palme d'Or, 2017**, for *The Square*, a satire of the contemporary art world starring Claes Bang and Elisabeth Moss. The film was also nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film and won six European Film Awards, including Best Film and Best Director.
- **Palme d'Or, 2022**, for *Triangle of Sadness*, his first English-language film, which follows fashion models on a luxury cruise that collapses into a shipwreck. It received Oscar nominations for Best Picture, Best Director and Best Original Screenplay.
- **Guldbagge Awards** for Best Director and Best Film for *Involuntary*, *Play*, *Force Majeure*, *The Square* and *Triangle of Sadness*.
- **President of the Cannes competition jury** in 2023.
- His next feature, *The Entertainment System Is Down*, set aboard a long-haul flight and starring Kirsten Dunst, Keanu Reeves and Daniel Brühl, was shot in 2024–2025 for release in 2026.

## Controversies & Criticism

*Play* sparked an intense debate in Sweden in 2011. Because its young robbers were Black and their victims mostly white, some critics accused the film of reinforcing racist stereotypes, while others defended it as an honest examination of how liberal guilt paralyzes bystanders. Östlund argued the film depicted a documented pattern of real events.

His broader satirical method has been criticized as cold or contemptuous toward its characters. *Triangle of Sadness* in particular divided critics, with some praising its comic set pieces and others calling its class commentary obvious and its extended seasickness sequence gratuitous.

In 2015 Östlund posted a video of himself reacting to *Force Majeure* missing the Oscar shortlist, which drew both amusement and accusations of self-promotion. He has also been outspoken about smartphone use, comparing it to a drug and calling for restrictions on phones in cinemas, a stance some consider paternalistic.

## Legacy & Influence

Östlund has become one of the most decorated European filmmakers of his generation, and his double Palme d'Or places him alongside directors such as Michael Haneke and Ken Loach. His technique of rehearsing scenes exhaustively and filming dozens of takes has influenced younger Scandinavian directors, and his films are frequently cited in discussions of "cringe" cinema and social satire.

His work has expanded the international audience for Swedish film beyond the legacy of Ingmar Bergman, and his English-language productions have shown that a European auteur can reach Hollywood's awards circuit without abandoning a personal style.
