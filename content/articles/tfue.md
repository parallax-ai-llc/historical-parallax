## Summary

Turner Ellis Tenney, known online as Tfue, is an American streamer, content creator, and former professional esports player. He became one of the most recognizable names of the Fortnite boom between 2018 and 2020, when his aggressive mechanical style, tournament results, and daily Twitch broadcasts made him the most-followed active streamer on the platform after Ninja departed for Mixer in 2019.

Beyond his gameplay, Tfue is significant for his 2019 lawsuit against FaZe Clan, which exposed the terms of influencer contracts in esports and pushed the industry toward more transparent agreements between organizations and talent. His career illustrates both the enormous earning potential and the legal and reputational pressures of the creator economy in competitive gaming.

## Early Life & Background

Tenney was born on January 2, 1998, in Indian Rocks Beach, Florida. He grew up in a coastal environment where surfing and skimboarding were part of daily life, and he appeared in stunt and extreme-sports videos produced by his older brother Jack Tenney, whose YouTube channel JOOGSQUAD attracted a large audience. He was largely homeschooled, which gave him time to develop his gaming skills.

Before Fortnite, Tenney gained attention in the Destiny community for speedruns and raid content, then moved to survival shooters such as H1Z1 and PlayerUnknown's Battlegrounds, where he competed under smaller organizations and built an early following on Twitch.

## Rise to Prominence

Fortnite Battle Royale's rise in early 2018 transformed his career. His fast building and shotgun play produced viral clips, and on April 30, 2018, he joined FaZe Clan as a Fortnite player. Partnering with Dennis "Cloakzy" Lepore, he became a fixture of Epic Games' Summer and Fall Skirmish series, winning the Fall Skirmish opening duos event in September 2018 and collecting hundreds of thousands of dollars in prize money across the season.

His Twitch audience grew alongside the results. By mid-2019 his channel had passed 7 million followers, and following Ninja's exit to Mixer in August 2019 Tfue held the largest following of any active Twitch streamer, later exceeding 11 million. His YouTube channel grew to more than 12 million subscribers.

## Key Achievements

- Won the Fortnite Fall Skirmish Week 1 duos event with Cloakzy in September 2018, one of several six-figure Skirmish placements.
- Qualified for the 2019 Fortnite World Cup Solo Finals in New York, finishing 67th among 100 competitors from millions of entrants.
- Accumulated more than $600,000 in documented tournament winnings, placing him among the top-earning Fortnite players of the game's first two years.
- Became the most-followed active Twitch streamer in 2019 and surpassed 11 million followers on the platform.

## Controversies & Criticism

In May 2018, Twitch suspended Tenney for 30 days after he used a racial slur on stream. In June 2018, Epic Games banned his primary Fortnite account for alleged account selling; Tenney disputed the finding but the ban was upheld, and he played on alternate accounts thereafter.

The most consequential dispute began on May 20, 2019, when he sued FaZe Clan in California, alleging his "Gamer Agreement" was oppressive, that it allowed the organization to take up to 80 percent of certain sponsorship revenue, and that it violated California's Talent Agencies Act. FaZe countersued in New York, arguing it had collected only a small share of his earnings. The parties settled in August 2020, and Tenney left the organization. Critics also noted his sometimes abrasive on-stream behavior and disputes with fellow creators.

## Legacy & Influence

Tfue's lawsuit is widely credited with prompting esports organizations to revise revenue splits and disclose contract terms, and it fed directly into public debate over agency regulation for gaming talent. His duo with Cloakzy helped define the competitive Fortnite meta of 2018, and his mechanics-first playstyle influenced a generation of younger players. After announcing his retirement from full-time streaming in August 2023, he has returned only intermittently, but he remains a reference point for the peak of Fortnite's cultural moment.
