## Summary

Pierpaolo Piccioli is an Italian fashion designer best known for his sixteen-year tenure at Valentino, first as co-creative director alongside Maria Grazia Chiuri from 2008 and then as sole creative director from 2016 to 2024. In March 2025 he was appointed creative director of Balenciaga, succeeding Demna.

Piccioli is widely credited with reviving haute couture as a culturally relevant medium in the late 2010s. His Valentino collections paired the house's classical Roman elegance with saturated color, exaggerated volume, and an emphasis on casting diversity. Critics and industry peers frequently described his work as humanist, and his Fall 2018 and Fall 2019 couture shows are often cited among the most celebrated runway presentations of the decade.

## Early Life & Background

Piccioli was born on 5 August 1967 in Nettuno, a coastal town roughly 60 kilometres south of Rome, where he still lives with his wife, Simona, and their three children. He studied literature at the University of Rome before enrolling at the Istituto Europeo di Design (IED) in Rome, where he met Maria Grazia Chiuri. The two became long-term creative partners.

## Rise to Prominence

In 1989 Piccioli and Chiuri joined Fendi, where they worked on accessories for a decade and contributed to the popularity of the Baguette bag. In 1999 Valentino Garavani recruited them to lead Valentino's accessories division. After Garavani retired in 2008 and Alessandra Facchinetti's brief tenure ended, the pair were named co-creative directors of Valentino in October 2008. Under their direction the house introduced the Rockstud accessory line in 2010, which became a major commercial success, and rebuilt Valentino's couture reputation. The duo received the CFDA International Award in 2015.

When Chiuri departed for Dior in July 2016, Piccioli became sole creative director. His first solo collection, Spring/Summer 2017, was shown in Paris in October 2016.

## Key Achievements

- **Haute couture revival**: His Fall/Winter 2018 couture show received a standing ovation and was widely reviewed as a landmark. The Fall/Winter 2019 couture collection, closed by Naomi Campbell, was praised for its inclusive casting and floral construction.
- **Awards**: Piccioli was named Designer of the Year at the 2018 Fashion Awards in London.
- **Valentino Pink PP**: For Fall/Winter 2022 he developed a signature pink shade with Pantone and presented an entire collection in that single color.
- **Roman couture shows**: In July 2022 he staged "The Beginning" couture collection on Rome's Spanish Steps. In 2021 the "Valentino Des Ateliers" couture show in Venice was created in dialogue with contemporary painters.
- **Collaborations**: He designed a Moncler Genius capsule in 2018, worked with Undercover and Birkenstock, and dressed public figures including Lady Gaga and Zendaya at major awards ceremonies.
- **Balenciaga**: Appointed in March 2025 with a start date of 10 July 2025, he presented his debut Balenciaga collection in Paris in October 2025.

## Controversies & Criticism

The Spring/Summer 2016 ready-to-wear collection, designed with Chiuri and inspired by Africa, drew accusations of cultural appropriation because the show featured mostly white models styled in cornrows. The 2022 Spanish Steps couture show prompted a public dispute with Dior, whose Rome boutique sits on the steps and which complained that the event obstructed its business. Valentino's announcement of Piccioli's exit on 22 March 2024, followed within days by the appointment of Alessandro Michele, also generated criticism from commentators who felt his departure was abrupt.

## Legacy & Influence

Piccioli is regarded as one of the designers who returned haute couture to the center of fashion discourse, demonstrating that couture could carry contemporary ideas about race, gender, and beauty. His color-driven aesthetic, use of monumental volume, and emphasis on inclusive casting influenced runway presentation across the industry. His move to Balenciaga marked a significant shift from the house's streetwear-inflected era under Demna toward a renewed focus on couture craft.
