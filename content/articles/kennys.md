## Summary

Kenny Schrub, known competitively as kennyS, is a French former professional Counter-Strike: Global Offensive player born on 19 May 1995. He is widely regarded as one of the most gifted AWP (sniper) players in the game's history, celebrated for his fast flick shots, aggressive repositioning and highlight-reel plays that helped define the role during the 2014–2017 era.

Over a career spanning more than a decade, he represented VeryGames, Clan-Mystik, LDLC, Titan, Team EnVyUs and G2 Esports. His crowning achievement came at the DreamHack Open Cluj-Napoca Major in November 2015, which he won with EnVyUs while being named the tournament's most valuable player. He collected eleven HLTV MVP medals and appeared in HLTV's annual Top 20 rankings for six consecutive years.

kennyS mattered beyond trophies. His dominance with the AWP was a factor in Valve's decision to slow the weapon's movement speed in 2015, and his popularity brought a large French-speaking audience into Counter-Strike esports.

## Early Life & Background

Schrub was born in Mulhouse, in the Alsace region of eastern France. He began playing Counter-Strike as a child, competing first in Counter-Strike: Source, where the French scene was among the strongest in Europe. By his mid-teens he was known in French online leagues for his unusual reflexes on the AWP, a weapon most young players avoided because of its high price and difficulty.

He transitioned to Counter-Strike: Global Offensive soon after its August 2012 release, joining VeryGames, then the leading French organisation, in late 2012. He was dropped a few months later, a setback that he has described as a formative motivator.

## Rise to Prominence

After leaving VeryGames, kennyS joined Clan-Mystik and won the Electronic Sports World Cup in November 2013, an upset that put him on the international map. In 2014 he moved to LDLC and then to Titan, where his individual statistics were among the best in the world despite the team's mixed results.

In November 2014, Titan was disqualified from the DreamHack Winter Major after teammate Hovik "KQLY" Tovmassian received a VAC ban, costing kennyS a Major appearance through no fault of his own. He joined Team EnVyUs in February 2015, forming a roster with apEX, NBK-, Happy and kioShiMa that reached the ESL One Cologne 2015 final and then won the Cluj-Napoca Major in November 2015.

He transferred to G2 Esports in February 2017, winning ESL Pro League Season 5 and DreamHack Masters Malmö that year. With a rebuilt international G2 lineup he reached the IEM Katowice 2020 final and several BLAST Premier finals before being benched in March 2021. He announced his retirement from competition in 2023, later making brief stand-in appearances.

## Key Achievements

- Major champion and MVP at DreamHack Open Cluj-Napoca, November 2015.
- Eleven HLTV MVP awards, among the most in CS:GO history.
- HLTV Top 20 placements every year from 2013 to 2018, with top-five finishes in 2014 and 2015.
- Titles including ESWC 2013, ESL Pro League Season 5 (2017) and DreamHack Masters Malmö (2017).
- Frequently cited in player and pundit polls as the best AWPer of the pre-2018 era.

## Controversies & Criticism

kennyS was rarely involved in personal controversy. Criticism centred instead on his play: analysts argued that his aggressive style produced spectacular rounds but also costly deaths, and his form declined noticeably between 2018 and 2020. His long stay on G2's bench, during which the organisation struggled to find a buyer for his contract, drew debate about high-salary veterans in a changing market. Some fans also questioned his commitment to a comeback after retirement.

## Legacy & Influence

kennyS shaped how the AWP is played, popularising fast, mobile sniping over passive angle-holding. Valve's March 2015 AWP movement nerf, informally called the "kennyS nerf" by the community, is a lasting mark of his influence. He remains one of the most-watched French esports figures and a reference point for later stars such as ZywOo.
