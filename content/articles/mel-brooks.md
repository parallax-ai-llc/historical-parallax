## Summary

Mel Brooks is an American comedian, writer, director, producer, and actor whose career spans more than seven decades of television, film, recorded comedy, and musical theater. Born Melvin Kaminsky in Brooklyn in 1926, he rose from the writers' room of Sid Caesar's early television programs to become one of the defining voices of American film parody, directing *The Producers* (1967), *Blazing Saddles* (1974), *Young Frankenstein* (1974), and *Spaceballs* (1987).

Brooks is one of a small group of entertainers to have won an Emmy, a Grammy, an Oscar, and a Tony, completing the "EGOT" in 2001 when his stage adaptation of *The Producers* won a record 12 Tony Awards. Through his company Brooksfilms he also produced serious dramas such as *The Elephant Man* (1980), demonstrating a range far beyond broad comedy. His work continues to be cited as a benchmark for satire, and he remained active into his tenth decade.

## Early Life & Background

Brooks was born on June 28, 1926, in Brooklyn, New York, the youngest of four sons of Max and Kate Kaminsky, Jewish immigrants of German and Ukrainian descent. His father died of kidney disease when Mel was two, and the family lived modestly in Williamsburg. A small, frequently bullied child, he learned to deflect hostility with jokes and was drumming in Catskills resorts as a teenager, where he first performed comedy.

He graduated from Eastern District High School in 1944 and briefly attended Brooklyn College before being drafted. Serving as a corporal in the U.S. Army's 1104th Engineer Combat Battalion, he defused land mines in Europe during the final months of World War II. He adopted the stage name Brooks, derived from his mother's maiden name Brookman, to avoid confusion with trumpeter Max Kaminsky.

## Rise to Prominence

After the war Brooks worked the Borscht Belt circuit and, in 1950, joined the writing staff of Sid Caesar's *Your Show of Shows*, later moving to *Caesar's Hour* (1954–1957). The room, which included Carl Reiner, Neil Simon, and Larry Gelbart, became legendary in television history. With Reiner he developed the improvised "2000 Year Old Man" routine, released as an album in 1960 and nominated for a Grammy.

In 1965 he co-created the spy sitcom *Get Smart* with Buck Henry, which ran for five seasons. His feature directorial debut, *The Producers* (1967), starring Zero Mostel and Gene Wilder, won him the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay. In 1974 he released two of the most successful comedies of the decade: *Blazing Saddles*, a western parody co-written with Richard Pryor and others, and *Young Frankenstein*, co-written with Wilder.

## Key Achievements

- **Film**: Directed 11 features, including *Silent Movie* (1976), *High Anxiety* (1977), *History of the World, Part I* (1981), *Spaceballs* (1987), and *Robin Hood: Men in Tights* (1993).
- **Producing**: Founded Brooksfilms in 1980, which produced *The Elephant Man* (eight Oscar nominations), *Frances* (1982), *My Favorite Year* (1982), and David Cronenberg's *The Fly* (1986).
- **Theater**: *The Producers* opened on Broadway in April 2001 and won 12 Tony Awards, a record that still stands. *Young Frankenstein* followed on stage in 2007.
- **Honors**: Kennedy Center Honors (2009), AFI Life Achievement Award (2013), National Medal of Arts (2016), and an Honorary Academy Award (2024). He published the memoir *All About Me!* in 2021 and returned to television with *History of the World, Part II* on Hulu in 2023.

## Controversies & Criticism

Brooks's humor has long relied on ethnic, sexual, and racial provocation. *Blazing Saddles* uses racial slurs extensively, and in 2020 HBO Max added a contextual introduction to the film; Brooks has said the movie could not be made today and has criticized what he describes as excessive caution in modern comedy. *History of the World, Part I* and *Robin Hood: Men in Tights* drew complaints for gay stereotypes and crude gags. Critics have also noted that his later films, particularly *Dracula: Dead and Loving It* (1995), were commercially and critically weak compared with his 1970s peak.

## Legacy & Influence

Brooks helped establish the modern film parody, influencing the Zucker brothers, *Saturday Night Live*, and a generation of writer-directors. *Blazing Saddles* and *Young Frankenstein* are both preserved in the National Film Registry. His marriage to actress Anne Bancroft lasted from 1964 until her death in 2005. Brooks turned 100 in June 2026, and a *Spaceballs* sequel, with his involvement, was announced for release in 2027.
