## Summary

Daniel Boulud is a French-born American chef and restaurateur best known for Daniel, his flagship restaurant on Manhattan's Upper East Side. Since opening it in 1993, he has built a group of restaurants spanning New York, Boston, Miami, Palm Beach, Washington, D.C., Toronto, Montreal, Dubai, and Singapore under the Dinex Group banner.

Boulud is widely credited with helping define modern French fine dining in the United States. His cooking blends classical Lyonnais technique with seasonal American produce, and his restaurants have earned Michelin stars, four-star reviews from The New York Times, and multiple James Beard Foundation awards. He has also written cookbooks, appeared on television, and mentored a generation of chefs who now run their own kitchens.

## Early Life & Background

Boulud was born on March 25, 1955, in Saint-Pierre-de-Chandieu, a village near Lyon, France. He grew up on his family's farm, where his grandmother's cooking and the rhythm of seasonal produce shaped his palate. At 14 he began an apprenticeship at Nandron, a two-Michelin-star restaurant in Lyon, after being nominated as a finalist for the Meilleur Apprenti de France award.

He then trained under some of France's most celebrated chefs, including Roger Vergé at Moulin de Mougins, Georges Blanc in Vonnas, and Michel Guérard at Eugénie-les-Bains. These kitchens grounded him in nouvelle cuisine while preserving the rustic traditions of his upbringing.

## Rise to Prominence

Boulud moved to the United States in 1980 to serve as private chef to the European Commission's delegation in Washington, D.C. In 1982 he relocated to New York, working at the Westbury Hotel's Polo Lounge and then at Le Régence in the Plaza Athénée.

His breakthrough came in 1986 when he became executive chef of Le Cirque, Sirio Maccioni's celebrity-filled restaurant. During his six-year tenure the restaurant received four stars from The New York Times, and Boulud became one of the most recognized French chefs in America.

In May 1993 he opened Daniel in the former Le Cirque space at the Surrey Hotel. The restaurant moved to its current location at 60 East 65th Street in 1998. The New York Times awarded it four stars in 2001 and again in 2009, and the Michelin Guide gave it two stars beginning in 2006, with three stars from 2010 through 2014.

## Key Achievements

Boulud's restaurant empire expanded rapidly after Daniel. Café Boulud opened in 1998, followed by db Bistro Moderne in 2001, which introduced the widely imitated "db Burger" stuffed with braised short rib and foie gras. Bar Boulud opened in 2008 near Lincoln Center, and Boulud Sud in 2011. Le Pavillon, his Midtown restaurant in One Vanderbilt, opened in 2021 and earned one Michelin star.

His honors include the James Beard Foundation awards for Best Chef: New York City in 1992, Outstanding Chef in 1994, Outstanding Restaurateur in 2006, and Outstanding Restaurant for Daniel in 2010. He was named Chevalier of the Légion d'Honneur in 2006 and received the Culinary Institute of America's Chef of the Year award in 2011. His books include Cooking with Daniel Boulud (1993), Letters to a Young Chef (2003), and Daniel: My French Cuisine (2013). He also served as chairman of the Bocuse d'Or USA Foundation, which trains American competitors for the international cooking competition.

## Controversies & Criticism

In 2013 a diner at Daniel swallowed a piece of wire from a grill brush that had contaminated a coq au vin dish. The diner later sued, and the case drew attention to kitchen safety practices at high-end restaurants.

Daniel also faced scrutiny in 2013 when The New York Times critic Pete Wells reduced the restaurant to three stars, criticizing what he described as inconsistent service that treated anonymous diners less attentively than recognized guests. Boulud's restaurants have additionally faced wage-and-hour lawsuits from workers, a common issue across the New York restaurant industry, some of which were settled.

## Legacy & Influence

Boulud's kitchens have functioned as a training ground for American fine dining. Alumni include Alex Lee, Andrew Carmellini, Gavin Kaysen, and Jean-François Bruel, many of whom have gone on to earn Michelin stars and James Beard awards. His emphasis on farm-driven sourcing anticipated the seasonal ethos that now dominates upscale American restaurants.

He has also been a visible philanthropist, serving on the board of Citymeals on Wheels and raising funds for hunger relief. More than three decades after Daniel opened, Boulud remains one of the most influential French chefs working in the United States.
