## Summary

Yotam Ottolenghi is an Israeli-born British chef, restaurateur, and food writer whose name has become shorthand for a vegetable-forward, Middle Eastern-inflected style of cooking. Born in Jerusalem on 14 December 1968, he moved to London in 1997 and opened the first Ottolenghi deli in Notting Hill in 2002 with his business partner Sami Tamimi. The group has since grown to include several delis across London and the flat-iron-shaped restaurant NOPI in Soho.

His influence rests largely on his books. Starting with *Ottolenghi: The Cookbook* in 2008, and continuing through *Plenty* (2010), *Jerusalem* (2012), *Simple* (2018), and *Flavour* (2020), he has sold millions of copies and shaped how home cooks in Britain, the United States, and beyond use ingredients such as tahini, za'atar, sumac, pomegranate molasses, and preserved lemon. His long-running column in *The Guardian* has amplified that reach.

## Early Life & Background

Ottolenghi was born in Jerusalem to Michael Ottolenghi, a chemistry professor of Italian-Jewish descent, and Ruth Ottolenghi, a school principal of German-Jewish background. He grew up in the city and completed compulsory service in the Israel Defense Forces before studying at Tel Aviv University, where he earned a combined bachelor's and master's degree in comparative literature and philosophy. He also worked as a night editor at the *Haaretz* newspaper.

In 1997 he moved to London and enrolled at Le Cordon Bleu, a decision that surprised family and friends who expected an academic career. He then worked as a pastry chef at the Capital Restaurant, Kensington Place, and Launceston Place, before becoming head pastry chef at the Baker & Spice bakery, where he met Sami Tamimi, a Palestinian cook who had also grown up in Jerusalem.

## Rise to Prominence

Ottolenghi, Tamimi, and business partners Noam Bar and Cornelia Staeubli opened the first Ottolenghi deli on Ledbury Road, Notting Hill, in 2002. Its window displays of piled salads and meringues became a visual signature. Further branches followed in Islington (2004), Kensington (2006), Belgravia, Spitalfields, Marylebone, and Chelsea.

In 2006 he began writing "The New Vegetarian" column for *The Guardian*'s Weekend magazine, despite not being vegetarian himself, and this raised his profile well beyond London. NOPI, a more formal restaurant, opened in Soho in 2011, followed by ROVI in Fitzrovia in 2018.

## Key Achievements

- *Ottolenghi: The Cookbook* (2008, with Tamimi) became a bestseller and established the deli's style in print.
- *Plenty* (2010) was a landmark vegetarian cookbook and won the Observer Food Monthly Cookbook of the Year.
- *Jerusalem* (2012, with Tamimi) won the James Beard Award for International Cooking and the IACP Cookbook of the Year in 2013.
- *Simple* (2018) won the James Beard Award for General Cooking in 2019.
- He appeared in the BBC documentaries *Jerusalem on a Plate* (2011) and *Ottolenghi's Mediterranean Feast* (2012).

## Controversies & Criticism

Ottolenghi's work has drawn criticism from some quarters for presenting Palestinian and Levantine dishes under an Israeli or generically "Middle Eastern" banner, with commentators raising questions about culinary appropriation. He and Tamimi have addressed the tension directly in *Jerusalem*, acknowledging shared and contested food histories. He has also been criticised for recipes requiring long ingredient lists and hard-to-find items, a complaint that partly prompted *Simple*. Some in Israel have objected to his public criticism of Israeli government policy, while others have criticised him for not going further.

## Legacy & Influence

Ottolenghi is widely credited with pushing vegetables to the centre of the plate in mainstream Western restaurant and home cooking, and with normalising Middle Eastern pantry staples in British and American supermarkets. The "Ottolenghi effect" is a recognised phrase in food retail, describing spikes in demand for ingredients he features. His test kitchen, run with Noor Murad and others, has extended the brand into further books such as *Shelf Love* (2021) and *Extra Good Things* (2022). He lives in London with his husband, Karl Allen, and their two sons.
