## Summary

Anitta, born Larissa de Macedo Machado on 30 March 1993, is a Brazilian singer, songwriter and actress who became the most internationally successful pop artist to emerge from the country's funk carioca scene. Starting with YouTube uploads and a contract with the Rio de Janeiro funk label Furacão 2000, she reached national fame in 2013 with "Show das Poderosas" and then spent the following decade building a trilingual career in Portuguese, Spanish and English.

Her 2021 single "Envolver" made her the first Brazilian solo artist to top Spotify's global chart, and she followed it with a Best Latin win at the 2022 MTV Video Music Awards and a Best New Artist nomination at the 2023 Grammy Awards. She matters because she carried a working-class Brazilian genre onto stages such as Coachella and Rock in Rio while retaining control of her image and business.

## Early Life & Background

Anitta was born in Rio de Janeiro and grew up in Honório Gurgel, a working-class neighbourhood in the city's north zone. She began singing at age eight in the choir of her local Catholic church. She completed a technical course in administration and worked as an administrative assistant at the mining company Vale before pursuing music full time. Her stage name was inspired by the 1997 Brazilian miniseries *Presença de Anita*.

## Rise to Prominence

In 2010 she signed with Furacão 2000, the label and sound-system collective that dominated Rio funk. Her 2012 song "Meiga e Abusada" drew the attention of Warner Music Brasil, which signed her in 2013. "Show das Poderosas" reached number one in Brazil that year and her self-titled debut album went platinum. Albums *Ritmo Perfeito* (2014) and *Bang* (2015) consolidated her position at home.

From 2017 she pursued a Latin market strategy, collaborating with J Balvin on "Downtown" and releasing the trilingual album *Kisses* in 2019, which earned a Latin Grammy nomination. "Envolver", released in November 2021, climbed to number one on Spotify's global daily chart in March 2022. She performed at Coachella in April 2022 and released the album *Versions of Me* the same month. In 2024 she returned to her roots with *Funk Generation*, an album built entirely on Brazilian funk rhythms.

## Key Achievements

- First Brazilian solo artist to reach number one on Spotify's global chart, with "Envolver" in 2022.
- First Brazilian solo artist to win an MTV Video Music Award, taking Best Latin in 2022.
- First Brazilian artist nominated for Best New Artist at the Grammy Awards, at the 65th ceremony in 2023.
- Multiple Latin Grammy nominations, including for *Kisses* and *Funk Generation*.
- Subject of the Netflix documentary series *Vai Anitta* (2018) and cast in season seven of the Spanish series *Elite* (2023).
- Named to TIME's 100 Next list in 2021.

## Controversies & Criticism

The 2017 video for "Vai Malandra", filmed in the Vidigal favela, was praised for showing her unretouched body but criticised by some commentators for sexualising favela life. Her public endorsement of Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva before the 2022 election drew hostility from supporters of Jair Bolsonaro. In 2023 she openly clashed with Warner Music over her contract and creative control, later leaving for Republic Records. Her 2024 video "Aceita", which referenced Afro-Brazilian religious practice, drew criticism from some evangelical groups.

## Legacy & Influence

Anitta reshaped how Brazilian pop is exported, proving that funk carioca could compete on global streaming platforms without abandoning its Portuguese-language identity. Her carnival street shows, the "Ensaios da Anitta", have become an annual fixture, and her business acumen has been widely cited as a model for artists from the Global South.
