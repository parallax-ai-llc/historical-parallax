## Summary

José Andrés is a Spanish-born American chef, restaurateur, and humanitarian best known for bringing Spanish tapas and avant-garde cooking to the United States and for founding World Central Kitchen (WCK), a nonprofit that delivers fresh meals in disaster zones. Born in Asturias in 1969 and trained under Ferran Adrià at elBulli, he moved to the United States in 1991 and built a restaurant group that today operates more than 30 venues, including the two-Michelin-star minibar in Washington, D.C.

His public profile expanded far beyond food after 2010, when he launched WCK in response to the Haiti earthquake. The organization's response to Hurricane Maria in Puerto Rico in 2017, when it served more than 3.6 million meals, established a model of rapid, locally sourced emergency feeding that it has since applied in Ukraine, Gaza, Turkey, and dozens of other crises.

Andrés has received the James Beard Foundation's Outstanding Chef award, the National Humanities Medal, and the Presidential Medal of Freedom. He has also been a vocal political figure, particularly on immigration and food policy, which has drawn both praise and criticism.

## Early Life & Background

Andrés was born on July 13, 1969, in Mieres, a mining town in the Asturias region of northern Spain. His parents were nurses, and the family moved to the Barcelona area when he was a child. He attended culinary school in Barcelona and, at around age 18, began working at elBulli in Roses, Catalonia, under Ferran Adrià, whose experimental techniques would strongly shape his later cooking. He also completed his Spanish military service as a cook aboard the navy training ship Juan Sebastián de Elcano.

## Rise to Prominence

In 1991, Andrés moved to New York, working briefly at El Dorado Petit before relocating to Washington, D.C., in 1993 to head the kitchen at Jaleo, a tapas restaurant that became a landmark for Spanish food in America. With partners Rob Wilder and Roberto Alvarez, he expanded into a company later known as ThinkFoodGroup, opening Café Atlántico, Zaytinya (2002), minibar (2003), and Oyamel (2004). The Bazaar by José Andrés opened in Los Angeles in 2008, and Mercado Little Spain, a food hall developed with the Adrià brothers, opened in New York's Hudson Yards in 2019. The company was renamed José Andrés Group in 2022.

His PBS series *Made in Spain* and frequent television appearances made him one of the most recognizable chefs in the United States. He became a U.S. citizen in 2013.

## Key Achievements

- **World Central Kitchen**: Founded in 2010; grew from a small Haiti-focused project into an organization with hundreds of millions of dollars in annual revenue that has served hundreds of millions of meals worldwide.
- **Awards**: James Beard Outstanding Chef (2011) and Humanitarian of the Year (2018); *Time* 100 lists in 2012 and 2018; National Humanities Medal from President Obama (2015); Presidential Medal of Freedom from President Biden (January 2025).
- **Michelin recognition**: minibar earned two Michelin stars in 2016.
- **Books and film**: Authored *We Fed an Island* (2018), *Vegetables Unleashed* (2019), and *Change the Recipe* (2025); subject of the 2022 Ron Howard documentary *We Feed People*.
- **Public service**: Appointed co-chair of the President's Council on Sports, Fitness, and Nutrition in 2022.

## Controversies & Criticism

In 2015, Andrés withdrew from a planned restaurant in Donald Trump's Washington hotel after Trump's remarks about Mexican immigrants. Trump sued for $10 million; Andrés countersued, and the parties settled in April 2017. His outspoken political commentary has drawn criticism from conservatives who view it as partisan.

WCK has faced scrutiny over its rapid scaling, coordination with government agencies, and the risks its staff face in conflict zones. On April 1, 2024, an Israeli airstrike killed seven WCK workers in Gaza, prompting Andrés to publicly accuse Israel of targeting the convoy and to temporarily suspend operations. Some critics have questioned WCK's oversight practices, while others argued the organization should not have withdrawn.

## Legacy & Influence

Andrés is widely credited with popularizing Spanish cuisine in the United States and with redefining the role of the celebrity chef as a humanitarian actor. WCK's "first responder" model of emergency feeding, built on local cooks and rapid deployment, has influenced how governments and NGOs approach disaster relief. He remains one of the most influential figures linking gastronomy, philanthropy, and public policy.
