## Summary

Dominique Crenn is a French-born American chef and restaurateur best known as the chef-owner of Atelier Crenn in San Francisco. In November 2018 she became the first woman in the United States to earn three Michelin stars, a milestone that made her one of the most prominent figures in American fine dining.

Largely self-taught and never formally trained in a culinary school, Crenn built a reputation for "poetic culinaria," a style that presents tasting menus as poems and draws on her childhood in Brittany. She has used her platform to advocate for sustainability, gender equity in professional kitchens, and immigrant rights.

Beyond her restaurants, she has become a media figure through Netflix's *Chef's Table* and *Iron Chef: Quest for an Iron Legend*, and she has published a cookbook and a memoir.

## Early Life & Background

Crenn was born on 3 August 1965 in Versailles, France, and was adopted at around 18 months old by Allain Crenn, a politician, and his wife Louise. She grew up between Versailles and the family's home in Brittany, where her father's friendship with a food critic exposed her to serious restaurant cooking at an early age.

She studied economics and international business at the Académie de Commerce International in Paris rather than attending culinary school. In 1988 she moved to San Francisco and began working in professional kitchens.

## Rise to Prominence

Crenn's first significant post was at Stars, Jeremiah Tower's influential San Francisco restaurant, where she worked for about two years. In 1997 she moved to Jakarta to become executive chef at the InterContinental Hotel, reportedly the first woman to hold an executive chef position in Indonesia. She left the following year amid the political unrest of 1998.

Back in the United States, she cooked at the Manhattan Country Club in Manhattan Beach and at Abode in Santa Monica. In 2008 she became executive chef of Luce at the InterContinental San Francisco, which earned a Michelin star in 2009.

She opened Atelier Crenn in the Cow Hollow neighborhood of San Francisco in January 2011. The restaurant received two Michelin stars in 2012, making Crenn the first woman in the United States with two stars, and three stars in the 2019 guide announced in November 2018.

## Key Achievements

- **Three Michelin stars** for Atelier Crenn, the first awarded to a woman-led restaurant in the United States.
- **World's Best Female Chef** from the World's 50 Best Restaurants in 2016.
- **James Beard Award** for Best Chef: West in 2018.
- **Expansion:** Petit Crenn in Hayes Valley (2015) and Bar Crenn (2018), which earned its own Michelin star. In 2023 she opened Golden Poppy in Paris.
- **Books:** *Atelier Crenn: Metamorphosis of Taste* (2015) and the memoir *Rebel Chef: In Search of What Matters* (2020).
- **Television:** featured in *Chef's Table* season 2 (2016) and served as an Iron Chef on Netflix's 2022 revival.

In 2018 she removed land-based meat from her menus, citing environmental concerns, and she sources produce from her own Bleu Belle Farm in Sonoma County. In April 2019 she announced a breast cancer diagnosis and later completed treatment while continuing to run her restaurants.

## Controversies & Criticism

Crenn's decision to drop meat from her menus drew criticism from some ranchers and chefs who argued it oversimplified the environmental debate. In July 2023 Bar Crenn became the first US restaurant to serve cultivated chicken from Upside Foods, a move that some vegan advocates and food writers questioned as inconsistent with her stated principles and dependent on a company whose production claims were later scrutinized by journalists.

She has also been an outspoken critic of the Michelin Guide's treatment of female chefs, and her public political statements have periodically generated backlash. Like many high-end restaurants, Atelier Crenn has faced commentary over its pricing, with tasting menus among the most expensive in San Francisco.

## Legacy & Influence

Crenn's Michelin milestones reshaped expectations about who can reach the top of American fine dining, and she has mentored a generation of women in professional kitchens. Her menus-as-poems format and commitment to sustainable sourcing have been widely imitated, and her willingness to make Atelier Crenn a testing ground for cultivated protein placed a three-star restaurant at the center of debates about the future of food.
