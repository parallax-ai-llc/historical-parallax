## Summary

Syed Sumail Hassan, known competitively as SumaiL, is a Pakistani-American professional Dota 2 player best known for winning The International 2015 with Evil Geniuses at age 16. His arrival in the North American scene in early 2015 was one of the most abrupt debuts in esports history: within eight months of joining a top team he had won two of the largest tournaments ever held and become the youngest player to earn one million dollars in prize money.

SumaiL built his reputation as an aggressive, mechanically gifted mid laner whose confidence on heroes such as Storm Spirit and Ember Spirit defined Evil Geniuses' style for nearly five years. He later played for OG and Nigma Galaxy, but his early peak remains the reference point for his career.

His story also carries cultural weight. Raised partly in Karachi, he became the most prominent Pakistani figure in esports and was named to TIME's list of the 30 Most Influential Teens in 2016.

## Early Life & Background

SumaiL was born on 13 March 1999 in Karachi, Pakistan. He began playing Dota at around age seven in local internet cafés, where he has said he sometimes sold possessions to fund playing time. His family emigrated to the United States in 2014, settling in Rosemount, Minnesota.

In North America he competed on the public matchmaking ladder and in amateur teams, rapidly climbing the ranking system and drawing the attention of professional scouts before he had turned 16.

## Rise to Prominence

In January 2015, Evil Geniuses signed SumaiL to replace Artour "Arteezy" Babaev in the mid lane, a decision widely questioned given his age and lack of LAN experience. One month later, at the Dota 2 Asia Championships in Shanghai, he was a standout performer as Evil Geniuses won the event and a first-place prize of roughly $1.28 million.

In August 2015 the team won The International 2015 in Seattle, defeating CDEC Gaming 3–1 in the grand final for a prize of about $6.6 million. SumaiL's Storm Spirit performances throughout the event made him the tournament's breakout star.

He remained with Evil Geniuses through 2019, reaching third place at The International in 2016, 2018 and 2019. He left the organisation in late 2019, then joined OG in February 2020, rejoined OG for The International 2021, and signed with Nigma Galaxy in 2022. Later years featured shorter stints and stand-in roles.

## Key Achievements

- Champion, The International 2015, with Evil Geniuses.
- Champion, Dota 2 Asia Championships 2015.
- Guinness World Record as the youngest esports player to earn $1 million in prize money, at 16 years old.
- Three third-place finishes at The International (2016, 2018, 2019).
- Named to TIME's 30 Most Influential Teens of 2016.
- Career prize earnings exceeding $3.6 million, among the highest in esports.

## Controversies & Criticism

SumaiL's outspoken personality has generated friction throughout his career. He was publicly critical of former teammates and rival players on social media, and his exits from Evil Geniuses and OG were accompanied by reports of disagreements over practice habits and team dynamics. OG's 2020 split, during the pandemic shift to online play, was attributed by both sides to differing commitments.

Critics have also argued that his post-2019 results never approached his early peak, and that frequent roster changes limited his ability to rebuild consistent form. Supporters counter that he continued to perform at a high individual level on teams with less stability.

## Legacy & Influence

SumaiL's 2015 season reshaped expectations of how young a championship-calibre player could be, and his signing became a template for organisations willing to gamble on unproven talent from public matchmaking. He remains the youngest winner of The International.

Beyond results, he is a symbol for gaming communities in South Asia, particularly Pakistan, where his success brought mainstream attention to esports as a viable career.
