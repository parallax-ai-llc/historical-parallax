## Summary

Søren Bjerg, known competitively as Bjergsen, is a Danish former professional League of Legends player and coach, born 21 February 1996. He is best known for his seven-year tenure as the mid laner of Team SoloMid (TSM) in the North American League of Legends Championship Series (LCS), where he won six domestic titles and multiple regular-season MVP awards.

Bjergsen is widely regarded as the most decorated player in LCS history and the defining face of North American League of Legends during the 2010s. His consistency, mechanical skill on assassins and control mages, and long-term loyalty to a single organization made him one of the most recognizable figures in Western esports.

After retiring in 2020 he served as TSM's head coach for the 2021 season, returned to play for Team Liquid in 2022 and 100 Thieves in 2023, and then stepped away from competition.

## Early Life & Background

Bjerg grew up in Denmark and began playing League of Legends as a teenager. He rose through the European solo-queue ladder in 2012, where his high ranking attracted attention from professional teams. In early 2013 he joined Copenhagen Wolves for the inaugural European LCS Spring Split, but Riot Games' minimum age rule of 17 kept him from playing until his birthday on 21 February 2013.

## Rise to Prominence

Mid-way through 2013 the Copenhagen Wolves roster was acquired by Ninjas in Pyjamas. The team struggled in the Summer Split and was relegated, but Bjergsen's individual performances stood out. In November 2013 TSM signed him to replace founder Andy "Reginald" Dinh in the mid lane, a move that shaped the next decade of North American competition.

Bjergsen reached the NA LCS final in his first split, losing to Cloud9, then won his first championship in Summer 2014 with a 3–2 victory over the same team. TSM went on to win Spring 2015, Summer 2016, Spring 2017, Summer 2017, and Summer 2020 with Bjergsen in the lineup. The team also won IEM Katowice 2015 over the Chinese side WE. He attended the World Championship in 2014, 2015, 2016, 2017 and 2020, reaching the quarterfinals in 2014.

## Key Achievements

- Six LCS championships with TSM (2014–2020), a record for a single player at the time of his retirement.
- Multiple LCS regular-season MVP awards, including Spring 2015, Summer 2016 and Spring 2017.
- IEM Katowice 2015 champion.
- Reported in 2016 as the first League of Legends player to earn a seven-figure annual salary.
- Named a part-owner of TSM in 2019, then head coach for the 2021 season.

## Controversies & Criticism

Bjergsen's career drew persistent criticism over international results. TSM never advanced past the group stage after 2014, and the 0–6 record at Worlds 2020, when TSM entered as North America's first seed, was the worst by a first seed at the event. Critics also argued that TSM's roster construction leaned too heavily on him, a view summarized in the community phrase "Bjergsen and four sandbags."

His 2021 coaching stint was scrutinized after TSM missed Worlds and saw a turbulent roster with players such as Hu "SwordArt" Shuo-Chieh and Heo "Huni" Seung-hoon. His ownership stake in TSM while competing for rival organizations Team Liquid and 100 Thieves in 2022 and 2023 also prompted conflict-of-interest questions, which he addressed by stating the stake was handled in line with league rules.

## Legacy & Influence

Bjergsen defined the standard for Western mid laners during the LCS era and is often called the greatest North American League of Legends player, despite being an import. His loyalty to TSM helped the organization become one of the most valuable esports brands, and his popularity on Twitch drew hundreds of thousands of viewers to LCS broadcasts. Younger players, analysts and commentators frequently cite his champion pool, laning discipline and professionalism as a model for aspiring pros.
