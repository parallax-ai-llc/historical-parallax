## Summary

Taika Waititi is a New Zealand filmmaker, screenwriter, and actor known for blending deadpan comedy with emotional sincerity. He rose from Wellington's comedy scene to become one of the most commercially successful directors of the 2010s, moving between small Māori-centred stories and studio franchises without abandoning his idiosyncratic voice.

His 2019 film *Jojo Rabbit* earned him the Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay, making him the first person of Māori descent to win an Oscar in that category. He also directed *Thor: Ragnarok* (2017), which revitalised the Marvel character and grossed roughly $854 million worldwide.

Beyond his own films, Waititi has been a prominent advocate for Indigenous storytelling, co-creating the FX series *Reservation Dogs* and supporting a generation of Māori and Native American creators.

## Early Life & Background

Taika David Cohen, later known as Waititi, was born on 16 August 1975 in Raukokore in New Zealand's Bay of Plenty and raised largely in Wellington's Aro Valley. His father, Taika Waititi, was an artist and farmer of Te Whānau-ā-Apanui iwi, and his mother, Robin Cohen, was a schoolteacher of Russian Jewish, Irish, and English descent. His parents separated when he was about five.

He attended Onslow College and Victoria University of Wellington, graduating with a Bachelor of Arts in theatre in 1997. At university he formed comedy partnerships with Jemaine Clement, including the duo *So You're a Man* and the troupe *Humourbeasts*, which won the Billy T Award in 1999.

## Rise to Prominence

Waititi's short film *Two Cars, One Night* (2004) received an Academy Award nomination for Best Live Action Short Film at the 77th Academy Awards in 2005. His feature debut *Eagle vs Shark* premiered at Sundance in 2007, followed by *Boy* (2010), which became the highest-grossing New Zealand-made film at the time.

*What We Do in the Shadows* (2014), a vampire mockumentary co-directed with Clement, became a cult hit and spawned an FX television series that ran from 2019 to 2024. *Hunt for the Wilderpeople* (2016) then surpassed *Boy* as New Zealand's top-grossing domestic film. On the strength of these films, Marvel Studios hired him to direct *Thor: Ragnarok*, released in November 2017.

## Key Achievements

- **Jojo Rabbit (2019)**: won the Academy Award, BAFTA, and Writers Guild Award for Best Adapted Screenplay, plus the Toronto International Film Festival People's Choice Award. The film was also nominated for Best Picture.
- **Thor: Ragnarok (2017)**: grossed approximately $854 million worldwide and was widely credited with reinventing the franchise's tone.
- **Thor: Love and Thunder (2022)**: grossed about $760 million worldwide.
- **Television**: co-created *Reservation Dogs* (2021–2023) with Sterlin Harjo, directed the season one finale of *The Mandalorian* (2019) while voicing the droid IG-11, and starred as Blackbeard in *Our Flag Means Death* (2022–2023).
- **Honours**: named a New Zealand Arts Foundation Laureate in 2008 and New Zealander of the Year in 2017, and included in the *Time* 100 list in 2022.

## Controversies & Criticism

In a 2018 interview with *Dazed*, Waititi described New Zealand as "racist as fuck", prompting domestic backlash alongside support from those who agreed with his critique of casual racism. *Jojo Rabbit*, in which he played an imaginary Adolf Hitler, divided critics, with some arguing that its comedic treatment of Nazism was glib.

*Thor: Love and Thunder* drew criticism for uneven tone and rushed visual effects, and Waititi's jokes about the film's VFX during a promotional video were cited by effects workers in broader complaints about Marvel's working conditions. Public remarks in 2023 that he took the Thor job because he was "poor" and had little interest in the character were also criticised as dismissive of fans.

## Legacy & Influence

Waititi's films demonstrated that culturally specific Māori stories could achieve broad commercial success, and his "So You're a Man" era peers, including Clement, form part of a distinct New Zealand comedic sensibility now recognised internationally. Through *Reservation Dogs* and his producing work, he helped bring Indigenous writers, directors, and actors into mainstream American television. His shift between micro-budget features and blockbusters has become a model for filmmakers seeking to retain an authorial voice within franchise systems.
