## Summary

Tems, born Temilade Openiyi on 11 June 1995, is a Nigerian singer, songwriter and record producer whose smoky alto and self-produced, Afrobeats-adjacent R&B made her one of the most globally visible African artists of the 2020s. She emerged from Lagos in 2018 with the self-produced single "Mr Rebel" and reached international audiences through her feature on Wizkid's "Essence" in 2020.

Her importance rests on a run of firsts. "Wait for U", the Future single built on a sample of her song "Higher", debuted at number one on the Billboard Hot 100 in May 2022, making her the first Nigerian artist to top that chart. Its Grammy win in February 2023 made her the first Nigerian woman to win a Grammy, and her co-writing credit on Rihanna's "Lift Me Up" brought an Academy Award nomination the same year.

Beyond the accolades, Tems matters because she arrived as a complete package: writer, vocalist and producer, working outside the male-dominated Nigerian label system until global demand forced the industry to meet her on her own terms.

## Early Life & Background

Tems was born in Lagos to a Nigerian mother and a British-Nigerian father. The family moved to the United Kingdom shortly after her birth, then returned to Lagos after her parents separated when she was around five. She grew up in Lagos and attended Dowen College in Lekki, where she sang in the school choir and taught herself piano.

She studied economics at Monash University's campus in Johannesburg, South Africa, before returning to Nigeria. After a short period in a digital marketing job, she quit to pursue music full time, learning production largely from YouTube tutorials because she could not afford studio time with established producers.

## Rise to Prominence

Her debut single "Mr Rebel" arrived in July 2018, produced entirely by Tems herself. "Try Me", released in August 2019, went viral in Nigeria and established her as a rising voice. "Damages" followed in 2020, and her first EP, *For Broken Ears*, was released on 25 September 2020.

The turning point came with "Essence", a track on Wizkid's October 2020 album *Made in Lagos*. A remix featuring Justin Bieber in August 2021 pushed the song to number nine on the Billboard Hot 100, the first Nigerian song to reach the top ten. Drake enlisted her for "Fountains" on *Certified Lover Boy* in September 2021, the same month she released her second EP, *If Orange Was a Place*, and signed a deal with RCA Records through her Leading Vibe imprint.

## Key Achievements

- **"Wait for U" (2022):** The Future single featuring Drake and Tems debuted at number one on the Hot 100 and won Best Melodic Rap Performance at the 65th Grammy Awards in February 2023.
- **"Lift Me Up" (2022):** Co-written with Rihanna, Ludwig Göransson and Ryan Coogler for *Black Panther: Wakanda Forever*, the song earned nominations for an Academy Award and a Golden Globe.
- **Beyoncé collaboration:** Tems co-wrote and featured on "Move" from *Renaissance* (2022).
- **Born in the Wild (2024):** Her debut album, released on 7 June 2024, included "Me & U", "Burning" and "Love Me JeJe", which reworked Seyi Sodimu's 1997 hit. "Love Me JeJe" won Best African Music Performance at the 67th Grammy Awards in February 2025, her second Grammy.
- **Other honours:** BET Awards for Best Collaboration (2022) and Best International Act (2023), and a NAACP Image Award. In 2024 she launched the Leading Vibe Initiative to support women in African music and joined the ownership group of Major League Soccer club San Diego FC.

## Controversies & Criticism

In December 2020, Tems and fellow singer Omah Lay were arrested in Kampala, Uganda, after performing at a concert that violated COVID-19 restrictions. They were detained for several days and charged with negligent acts likely to spread infection. The charges were dropped after diplomatic intervention by the Nigerian government, and Ugandan authorities faced criticism for targeting the performers rather than the promoters.

Her red-carpet choices have drawn scrutiny. At the March 2023 Academy Awards, a voluminous white gown with a hooded structure blocked the view of attendees seated behind her, prompting widespread mockery online. A feathered Met Gala headpiece two months later drew similar complaints. Some critics have also questioned whether her feature credit on "Wait for U", which relied on a sample rather than a new performance, overstated her role in the record.

## Legacy & Influence

Tems helped move Afrobeats from a genre exported through features into one whose artists win in mainstream categories. Her chart records and Grammy wins opened institutional doors, including the Grammys' creation of a Best African Music Performance category in 2024, which she won in its second year.

Her production-first approach and ambient, downtempo sound influenced a wave of younger Nigerian alté and R&B artists, and her insistence on creative control became a reference point for women navigating a label ecosystem that had rarely centred them.
