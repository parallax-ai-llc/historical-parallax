## Summary

Nick Bosa is an American football defensive end for the San Francisco 49ers of the National Football League (NFL). Selected second overall in the 2019 NFL Draft out of Ohio State, he was named NFL Defensive Rookie of the Year in 2019 and NFL Defensive Player of the Year in 2022, when he led the league with 18.5 sacks.

Bosa belongs to one of the most prominent football families in the United States. His father John Bosa and uncle Eric Kumerow were both first-round picks of the Miami Dolphins, and his older brother Joey Bosa was the third overall pick in 2016. In September 2023 Nick signed a five-year, $170 million extension that briefly made him the highest-paid non-quarterback in NFL history.

His combination of pass-rush technique, run defense, and consistency has made him the centerpiece of a 49ers defense that reached two Super Bowls in his first five seasons.

## Early Life & Background

Bosa was born on October 23, 1997, in Fort Lauderdale, Florida, to John and Cheryl Bosa. He grew up watching his brother Joey develop into an elite pass rusher and followed him to St. Thomas Aquinas High School, a national powerhouse program in Fort Lauderdale. Rated a five-star recruit, he committed to Ohio State, again following Joey, and enrolled in 2016.

## Rise to Prominence

At Ohio State, Bosa recorded 5 sacks as a freshman in 2016. In 2017 he posted 8.5 sacks and 16 tackles for loss, earned first-team All-Big Ten honors, and was named the Big Ten's Smith-Brown Defensive Lineman of the Year. He was also the Defensive MVP of the 2017 Cotton Bowl win over USC.

His 2018 season ended after three games when he suffered a core muscle injury against TCU that required surgery. In October 2018 he withdrew from Ohio State to focus on rehabilitation and draft preparation, a decision that drew both criticism and understanding across college football.

The 49ers drafted him second overall in April 2019. He recorded 9 sacks as a rookie, made the Pro Bowl, and helped San Francisco reach Super Bowl LIV, where the team lost 31-20 to the Kansas City Chiefs.

## Key Achievements

- **NFL Defensive Rookie of the Year (2019)** with 9 sacks and 16 tackles for loss.
- **NFL Defensive Player of the Year (2022)** after leading the league with 18.5 sacks, earning first-team All-Pro honors.
- **Five Pro Bowl selections** (2019, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024) and second-team All-Pro in 2021, when he had 15.5 sacks.
- **Two Super Bowl appearances**, Super Bowl LIV (2019 season) and Super Bowl LVIII (2023 season), both losses to Kansas City.
- **Record contract**: a five-year, $170 million extension signed on September 6, 2023, with $122.5 million guaranteed, ending a training-camp holdout.
- More than 60 career sacks through the 2024 season despite missing most of 2020 with a torn ACL suffered in Week 2 against the New York Jets.

## Controversies & Criticism

Before the 2019 draft, Bosa deleted a number of old social media posts, including tweets calling Colin Kaepernick a "clown" and disparaging Beyoncé and the film *Black Panther*. He said he removed them because he could be drafted by San Francisco and did not want to alienate fans. The episode drew scrutiny given Kaepernick's history with the 49ers.

His 2018 decision to leave Ohio State mid-season was debated as a sign of players prioritizing professional earnings over team loyalty, though many analysts defended it as rational given his injury.

On October 27, 2024, Bosa wore a "Make America Great Again" hat during a live postgame interview following a win over the Dallas Cowboys. The NFL fined him $11,255 for displaying an unapproved political message, and the incident renewed debate over athletes' political expression.

In September 2025 he suffered a second torn ACL in Week 3 against the Arizona Cardinals, ending his season and raising questions about durability under his large contract.

## Legacy & Influence

Bosa is widely regarded as one of the best defensive ends of his generation, notable for winning both Defensive Rookie of the Year and Defensive Player of the Year within his first four seasons. His 2023 contract reset the market for edge rushers and influenced subsequent deals across the league. Together with his brother, father, and uncle, he has cemented the Bosa family as an enduring NFL lineage, and his career remains closely tied to the 49ers' pursuit of a sixth Super Bowl title.
