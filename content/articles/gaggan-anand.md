## Summary

Gaggan Anand (born 4 November 1978) is an Indian chef and restaurateur based in Bangkok, Thailand. He is best known for his eponymous restaurant Gaggan, which held the No. 1 position on the Asia's 50 Best Restaurants list for four consecutive years from 2015 to 2018, and for its successor, Gaggan Anand, which returned to the top of that list in 2025.

Anand built his reputation on "progressive Indian cuisine," applying techniques learned at Ferran Adrià's elBulli to the street food and home cooking of his native Kolkata. His 25-course tasting menus, presented as a row of emoji rather than written dish names, and his theatrical, music-driven service style made him one of the most recognisable figures in modern Asian fine dining.

He matters because he shifted global perceptions of Indian food, proving it could compete at the highest tier of avant-garde gastronomy while remaining rooted in familiar flavours.

## Early Life & Background

Anand was born in Kolkata (then Calcutta), West Bengal, into a Punjabi family of modest means. His father ran a small business that later failed, and the family experienced financial hardship during his teenage years. Before cooking, Anand played drums in a local rock band, an interest that later shaped the loud, music-centred atmosphere of his restaurants.

He studied at the Institute of Hotel Management in Kolkata and began his career with the Taj Group of hotels. Early jobs included catering work and stints in Indian restaurant kitchens, where he grew frustrated with the formulaic "curry house" model of Indian fine dining.

## Rise to Prominence

Anand moved to Bangkok in 2007 to work at Red, an Indian restaurant. In 2010 he secured a short internship at the elBulli research laboratory in Spain, where he absorbed molecular and modernist techniques. In November 2010 he opened Gaggan in a colonial-style house on Soi Langsuan, Bangkok, with backing from local investors.

The restaurant entered the World's 50 Best Restaurants list in 2014 and rose steadily: No. 10 in 2015, No. 7 in 2017, No. 5 in 2018, and No. 4 in 2019. It was featured in the second season of Netflix's *Chef's Table* in 2016, bringing Anand international fame. When the Michelin Guide launched in Bangkok in 2018, Gaggan received two stars.

In August 2019, following a dispute with his business partners, Anand abruptly closed the original Gaggan. Three months later, in November 2019, he opened Gaggan Anand on Sukhumvit Soi 31, a smaller counter-style venue with a single seating.

## Key Achievements

- Four straight years at No. 1 on Asia's 50 Best Restaurants (2015–2018), a record at the time.
- Two Michelin stars for both Gaggan (2018–2019) and Gaggan Anand (from 2021).
- Gaggan Anand ranked No. 3 in Asia in 2024, No. 1 in Asia in 2025, and No. 6 on the World's 50 Best list in 2025.
- Signature dishes such as "Lick It Up," a plate eaten without cutlery to the Kiss song of the same name, and the yogurt explosion spherification.
- Expansion projects including Ms.Maria & Mr.Singh, a Mexican-Indian restaurant in Bangkok with Japanese chef Takeshi "Goh" Fukuyama, and Gaggan at Louis Vuitton, opened in Bangkok in 2024.

## Controversies & Criticism

The 2019 split from his investors was public and acrimonious. Anand said he had been treated as an employee rather than an owner of the brand carrying his name; the partners countered that he walked out on a functioning business and its staff. Legal threats followed, and the original venue never reopened.

His provocative style has also drawn criticism. Profanity features prominently in his branding and menus, and some critics have argued that the concert-like atmosphere and constant tableside theatrics overshadow the food. The price of a meal at Gaggan Anand, well above 10,000 Thai baht per person, has been cited as evidence of fine dining's detachment from the Indian cooking it celebrates. A planned collaborative restaurant with Fukuyama in Fukuoka, Japan, announced in 2017, did not materialise in its original form.

## Legacy & Influence

Anand is widely credited with putting modern Indian cuisine on the global fine-dining map from outside India. Alumni of his kitchens have opened acclaimed restaurants across Asia, and his emoji menu and single-seating format have been imitated by chefs seeking to make tasting menus feel less formal. His career also illustrates the leverage a chef can gain from global rankings and streaming exposure, and the risks of building a brand on a name that investors partly control.
