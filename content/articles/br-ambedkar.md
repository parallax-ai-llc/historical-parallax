## Summary

Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar (14 April 1891 – 6 December 1956) was an Indian jurist, economist, social reformer, and politician who chaired the Drafting Committee of the Constitution of India and served as the country's first Law Minister. Born into the Mahar community, then classed as "untouchable," he became one of the most highly educated Indians of his generation, holding doctorates from Columbia University and the London School of Economics.

Ambedkar spent his public life campaigning against caste discrimination, arguing that untouchability could not be reformed away but required the dismantling of the caste system itself. His negotiations with the British and with Mohandas Gandhi shaped the system of reserved legislative seats for Scheduled Castes that survives in modern India.

In October 1956, weeks before his death, he converted to Buddhism along with roughly 500,000 followers, founding what became the Dalit Buddhist movement. He was posthumously awarded the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour, in 1990.

## Early Life & Background

Ambedkar was born in Mhow, a military cantonment in the Central Provinces, the fourteenth child of Ramji Maloji Sakpal, a subedar in the British Indian Army, and Bhimabai. As a Mahar child he was made to sit apart from classmates and was denied water from the common vessel. He graduated from Elphinstone College, Bombay, in 1912, then won a scholarship from Sayajirao Gaekwad III of Baroda to study at Columbia University, earning an MA in 1915 and a PhD in 1927. In London he completed a DSc at the London School of Economics in 1923 with a thesis titled *The Problem of the Rupee* and was called to the bar at Gray's Inn the same year.

## Rise to Prominence

Returning to Bombay, Ambedkar practised law and founded the Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha in 1924 to promote education among the Depressed Classes. In March 1927 he led the Mahad Satyagraha, asserting the right of untouchables to draw water from the town's Chavdar tank, and in December that year publicly burned the *Manusmriti*. He represented the Depressed Classes at all three Round Table Conferences in London between 1930 and 1932. The British Communal Award of August 1932 granted them separate electorates, prompting Gandhi's fast unto death; the resulting Poona Pact of 24 September 1932 replaced separate electorates with 148 reserved seats in provincial legislatures under joint voting.

## Key Achievements

His 1936 essay *Annihilation of Caste*, written for a Lahore reform group that then cancelled the event, remains his most widely read work. He founded the Independent Labour Party in 1936 and the Scheduled Castes Federation in 1942, and served on the Viceroy's Executive Council as Labour member from 1942 to 1946. Appointed chairman of the Constituent Assembly's Drafting Committee on 29 August 1947, he steered the Constitution to adoption on 26 November 1949; it came into force on 26 January 1950, abolishing untouchability under Article 17. His economic writings influenced the Hilton Young Commission, whose recommendations led to the creation of the Reserve Bank of India in 1935.

## Controversies & Criticism

Ambedkar's relationship with Gandhi and the Indian National Congress was openly hostile, set out in his 1945 book *What Congress and Gandhi Have Done to the Untouchables*. His *Pakistan or the Partition of India* (1940) contained sharp criticisms of Islam that remain contested, and passages in the posthumously published *Riddles in Hinduism* triggered protests in Maharashtra in 1987. He resigned as Law Minister in September 1951 after the Hindu Code Bill, which would have expanded women's rights in marriage and inheritance, stalled in Parliament. In the 1952 general election he lost the Bombay North seat, entering the Rajya Sabha instead. Some scholars have also criticised his characterisation of tribal communities in his early writings.

## Legacy & Influence

Ambedkar embraced Buddhism at Nagpur on 14 October 1956 and died in Delhi on 6 December 1956; *The Buddha and His Dhamma* appeared in 1957. His birthday is observed as Ambedkar Jayanti, and since 2015 India has marked 26 November as Constitution Day. Parties such as the Bahujan Samaj Party trace their politics to him, and a 125-foot statue was unveiled in Hyderabad in 2022. He is widely regarded as the principal architect of India's constitutional order and the founding figure of modern Dalit politics.
