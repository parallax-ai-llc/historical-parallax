## Summary

Joseph Lee Burrow (born December 10, 1996) is an American football quarterback for the Cincinnati Bengals of the National Football League (NFL). He was the first overall pick of the 2020 NFL Draft after a record-setting 2019 season at Louisiana State University (LSU), where he won the Heisman Trophy and led the Tigers to an undefeated national championship.

Burrow matters because he transformed one of the NFL's least successful franchises. In his second professional season he took the Bengals to Super Bowl LVI, the team's first Super Bowl appearance since the 1988 season, and he has since become one of the league's most productive passers and highest-paid players. His career has also been defined by serious injuries, which have shaped both his statistical record and the public debate around his durability.

## Early Life & Background

Burrow was born in Ames, Iowa, while his father, Jimmy Burrow, was coaching at Iowa State. The family moved to The Plains, Ohio, in 2005 when Jimmy became defensive coordinator at Ohio University. Joe attended Athens High School, where he threw for 11,416 yards and 157 touchdowns and was named Ohio's Mr. Football in 2014.

He enrolled at Ohio State in 2015, redshirted, and spent two seasons as a backup to J. T. Barrett. He graduated in three years with a degree in consumer and family financial services and transferred to LSU as a graduate transfer in May 2018.

## Rise to Prominence

Burrow started for LSU in 2018, finishing 10–3 with a Fiesta Bowl win. His 2019 season was historic: 5,671 passing yards, an FBS-record 60 touchdown passes, and a 76.3 percent completion rate. LSU went 15–0 and beat Clemson 42–25 in the national championship game on January 13, 2020. Burrow won the Heisman Trophy by the largest margin in the award's history at that time, and his acceptance speech about poverty in Athens County prompted more than $500,000 in donations to the local food pantry.

The Bengals drafted him first overall on April 23, 2020. His rookie season ended on November 22, 2020, with torn ACL and MCL ligaments in his left knee.

## Key Achievements

- **2021 season:** NFL Comeback Player of the Year, league-best 70.4 percent completion rate, and a franchise-record 525 passing yards against Baltimore on December 26, 2021. He led Cincinnati to its first playoff win since January 1991 and to Super Bowl LVI, a 23–20 loss to the Los Angeles Rams on February 13, 2022.
- **2022 season:** 4,475 yards and 35 touchdowns; the Bengals reached a second consecutive AFC Championship Game, losing 23–20 to Kansas City.
- **Contract:** On September 7, 2023, he signed a five-year, $275 million extension, then the largest annual salary in NFL history.
- **2024 season:** Led the NFL with 4,918 passing yards and 43 touchdown passes and won his second Comeback Player of the Year award, though Cincinnati finished 9–8 and missed the playoffs.

## Controversies & Criticism

Burrow's career has drawn less personal controversy than debate about his circumstances. Critics have questioned whether the Bengals invested enough in pass protection after he was sacked a league-high 51 times in 2021, including nine times in a single playoff game. In the 2025 offseason he publicly pressed the front office to retain Ja'Marr Chase, Tee Higgins, and Trey Hendrickson, an unusual step for a franchise quarterback.

His durability is a recurring point of criticism. A calf injury in 2023 was followed by a season-ending wrist ligament tear on November 16, 2023, and a turf-toe injury on September 14, 2025, required surgery and cost him most of that season. In December 2024 his home near Cincinnati was burglarized during a Monday night game, part of a wider series of thefts targeting professional athletes.

## Legacy & Influence

Burrow is widely credited with ending decades of irrelevance for the Bengals and with reviving LSU's national profile. His 2019 season remains a benchmark for college quarterback play, and his charitable focus on food insecurity led to the Joe Burrow Foundation, established in 2022. Known for a calm demeanor and distinctive off-field style, he has become one of the NFL's most marketable players while playing in one of its smallest markets.
