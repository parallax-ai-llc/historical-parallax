## Summary
At 6:11 p.m. on 13 July 2024, a 20-year-old gunman, Thomas Matthew Crooks, fired eight rounds from a rooftop at a campaign rally near Butler, Pennsylvania, wounding former president and Republican candidate Donald Trump in the ear [^1]. Rally attendee Corey Comperatore was killed shielding his family, and two other spectators, David Dutch and James Copenhaver, were seriously wounded [^1][^2]. A Secret Service counter-sniper killed Crooks seconds after he began firing [^1]. Congressional investigations later described a cascade of preventable security failures, and the Secret Service director resigned [^3][^4].

## Background
Trump was the presumptive Republican nominee and was speaking at an outdoor rally at the Butler Farm Show grounds, days before the party's national convention [^1]. Security was provided by the Secret Service with support from state and local police [^3]. The AGR International building, whose roof overlooked the stage from roughly 400 to 450 feet away, lay outside the Secret Service's secure perimeter [^1].

## The Event
The Secret Service identified a suspicious individual about 45 minutes before the shooting, but did not stop him, according to the Senate report [^3]. Crooks climbed onto the AGR roof with a DPMS Panther Arms DR-15 semi-automatic rifle and opened fire about six minutes after Trump began speaking [^1]. One bullet grazed Trump's right ear; agents covered him and escorted him from the stage as he raised his fist [^1]. Comperatore, 50, an engineer and former chief of the Buffalo Township Volunteer Fire Company, was killed instantly [^1]. A Secret Service counter-sniper fatally shot Crooks 16 seconds after he began firing [^1]. The FBI believes Crooks acted alone but has not identified a motive [^1].

## Response and Aftermath
Secret Service Director Kimberly Cheatle resigned on 23 July 2024, the day after contentious testimony to Congress [^1]. The House of Representatives created a bipartisan task force, chaired by Mike Kelly with Jason Crow as ranking member, which on 10 December 2024 concluded that the shooting was "preventable and should not have happened" after conducting 46 interviews [^4]. On 13 July 2025 the Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee, chaired by Rand Paul, released a final report calling the day "a cascade of preventable failures" [^3]. The Secret Service suspended six employees for 10 to 42 days without pay [^3].

## Positive Perspectives
- The counter-sniper team neutralized the shooter within about 16 seconds, preventing further casualties [^1].
- Both chambers of Congress produced bipartisan or committee investigations that publicly documented security failures [^3][^4].
- The House task force issued recommendations to prevent future failures, including reducing the number of people under Secret Service protection and requiring counter-surveillance assets at large outdoor events [^4].
- Secret Service Director Sean Curran said the agency had "implemented substantive reforms" to address the failures, according to NBC News [^3].
- Accounts of Comperatore shielding his family during the gunfire drew wide public attention [^2].

## Negative Perspectives
- The Senate report found that Secret Service headquarters denied or left unfulfilled at least 10 requests for additional resources, including counter-snipers, for Trump's detail [^3].
- Known line-of-sight vulnerabilities from the AGR building were not addressed, and communication between federal and local officers broke down [^3].
- The committee criticized the agency's discipline, noting that "not a single person has been fired" [^3].
- The Senate report said Cheatle had testified falsely when she told Congress no requests were denied for the Butler rally, according to NPR reporting [^5].
- The FBI has not publicly established Crooks's motive, leaving a key question unresolved [^1].

## Recent News
James Copenhaver, who was shot in the arm and abdomen at the rally, died on 23 September 2026 at age 76 in Pittsburgh; the Allegheny County medical examiner did not say whether his death was directly related to his injuries [^2]. In June 2026 he and his wife had sued the federal government, alleging the Secret Service and Department of Homeland Security "utterly failed" to provide adequate security [^2].
