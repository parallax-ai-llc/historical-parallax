## Summary

Pope Francis, the first pope from the Americas, died on Easter Monday, 21 April 2025, at the age of 88 [^1]. After a funeral attended by about 250,000 people, 133 cardinal electors met in the Sistine Chapel on 7 and 8 May 2025 [^1][^2]. On the fourth ballot they elected Cardinal Robert Francis Prevost of Chicago, who took the name Leo XIV and became the first pope born in the United States [^2][^3]. The conclave was one of the shortest and most geographically diverse in the Church's history [^2][^3].

## Background

Francis, elected in 2013, had been admitted to Rome's Gemelli Hospital on 14 February 2025 with bronchitis that developed into bilateral pneumonia; he was discharged on 23 March after a five-week stay [^1]. He had appointed about 80 percent of the cardinals who would choose his successor, many of them the first cardinal ever appointed from their country [^2]. The electors came from more than 70 countries [^2].

## The Event

On Easter Sunday, 20 April, Francis met U.S. Vice President JD Vance and gave the Easter blessing from St. Peter's Basilica before greeting crowds from his popemobile [^1]. He died the following morning; the Vatican recorded the cause as a stroke followed by irreversible cardiac arrest [^1]. His funeral on 26 April was led by Cardinal Giovanni Battista Re and attended by about 130 foreign delegations, and he was buried, as he had requested, in the Basilica of Santa Maria Maggiore [^1][^3]. Two of the 135 eligible electors stayed home for health reasons, and Cardinal Angelo Becciu, who had given up his rights in 2020, did not vote [^2]. The conclave opened on 7 May, and white smoke rose at 6:07 p.m. on 8 May after the fourth ballot [^2][^3]. Prevost, 69, an Augustinian who had spent more than two decades in Peru and led the Dicastery for Bishops, chose a name honouring Leo XIII, author of the social encyclical *Rerum novarum* [^3].

## Response and Aftermath

In his first address Leo XIV spoke of peace eleven times, calling for an "unarmed and disarming peace" [^3]. He was inaugurated on 18 May 2025 [^2]. Reports after the conclave said Cardinal Pietro Parolin had been an early frontrunner before support from African and Asian cardinals shifted to Prevost, who reportedly received more than 100 votes, well above the 89 required [^2]. Leo XIV signed his first apostolic exhortation, *Dilexi te*, on love for the poor, on 4 October 2025, completing a project Francis had begun [^4]. He made his first foreign trip to Turkey and Lebanon from 27 November to 2 December 2025 [^3].

## Positive Perspectives

- The Vatican described the conclave as one of the fastest in history, giving the Church a new leader within days [^3].
- The electorate was the most geographically diverse in the history of conclaves, reflecting Francis's appointments from the global South [^2].
- Leo XIV brought experience from both the United States and more than two decades as a missionary and bishop in Peru [^3].
- His early teaching, including *Dilexi te*, emphasized continuity with Francis's focus on the poor [^4].
- Francis's funeral drew about 250,000 people and some 130 foreign delegations, showing his global standing [^1].

## Negative Perspectives

- The Survivors Network of those Abused by Priests (SNAP) said it was "gravely concerned" by Leo XIV's election because of his record on abuse cases in Chicago and Peru [^5].
- SNAP had filed a complaint in March 2025 alleging that Prevost's diocese in Chiclayo did not fully investigate allegations against two priests; a Peruvian bishop publicly defended him against the accusations [^5][^6].
- Cardinal Angelo Becciu, who had renounced his cardinal's rights in 2020 amid a financial scandal, initially said he would take part before agreeing to abide by Francis's wish that he stay out [^2].
- Before the conclave, commentators raised concerns about possible outside interference and a "hostile takeover" by ultraconservative politicians and Catholics [^2].
- A 2026 book by Vatican correspondents alleged that a cardinal was found with an active mobile phone inside the Sistine Chapel during the conclave; the Vatican did not confirm or deny it [^2].

## Recent News

The Jubilee year closed on 6 January 2026 after more than 30 million pilgrims visited Rome [^3]. On 25 May 2026 the Vatican published Leo XIV's first encyclical, *Magnifica humanitas*, on protecting human dignity in the age of artificial intelligence [^7].
