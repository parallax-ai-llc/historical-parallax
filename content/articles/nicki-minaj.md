## Summary

Nicki Minaj, born Onika Tanya Maraj on December 8, 1982, is a Trinidadian-American rapper, singer, and songwriter widely regarded as the most commercially successful female rapper in history. She rose from the New York mixtape circuit to a 2009 deal with Lil Wayne's Young Money Entertainment, then released a string of chart-topping albums that fused technical rap with pop melody and theatrical alter egos.

Her importance lies in scale and longevity. She has charted more than 100 entries on the Billboard Hot 100, more than any other woman in rap, and has scored three number-one albums on the Billboard 200 across a career spanning more than a decade. Her success reopened mainstream space for women in hip-hop at a time when few were signed to major labels.

Minaj's career has also drawn sustained criticism over public feuds, lyrical content, statements on social media, and the conduct of people close to her. She remains one of the most discussed and polarizing figures in popular music.

## Early Life & Background

Minaj was born in Saint James, a district of Port of Spain, Trinidad and Tobago, to Carol and Robert Maraj. She lived with her grandmother until age five, when she joined her parents in the South Jamaica neighborhood of Queens, New York. Her childhood was marked by her father's drug addiction and violence, including a 1987 incident in which he set fire to the family home.

She studied drama at Fiorello H. LaGuardia High School of Music & Art and Performing Arts in Manhattan. After graduating she worked as a waitress and office assistant while pursuing acting and, later, rap.

## Rise to Prominence

Minaj first recorded with the Queens group The Hoodstars in the early 2000s, then built a reputation through three mixtapes: *Playtime Is Over* (2007), *Sucka Free* (2008), and *Beam Me Up Scotty* (2009). Lil Wayne signed her to Young Money in August 2009. Her verse on Kanye West's "Monster" in 2010 was widely called the standout of the track and made her a national name.

Her debut album, *Pink Friday*, arrived on November 19, 2010, and reached number one on the Billboard 200 in early 2011. Its single "Super Bass" peaked at number three on the Hot 100 and has been certified diamond. *Pink Friday: Roman Reloaded* (2012) debuted at number one, driven by "Starships." She served as a judge on the twelfth season of *American Idol* in 2013.

## Key Achievements

- **Albums**: *The Pinkprint* (2014), *Queen* (2018), and *Pink Friday 2* (2023). *Pink Friday 2* debuted at number one, her third chart-topping album.
- **Hot 100 number ones**: the "Say So" remix with Doja Cat and "Trollz" with 6ix9ine, both in 2020, and "Super Freaky Girl" in 2022, the first solo debut at number one by a female rapper since 1998.
- **Touring**: the 2024 Pink Friday 2 World Tour became the highest-grossing tour by a female rapper.
- **Honors**: the MTV Video Vanguard Award in 2022 and more than ten Grammy nominations.
- **Film**: voice and acting roles in *Ice Age: Continental Drift* (2012), *The Other Woman* (2014), and *Barbershop: The Next Cut* (2016).

## Controversies & Criticism

The 2014 lyric video for "Only" was criticized for imagery evoking Nazi propaganda, and her label apologized. Long-running feuds with Remy Ma in 2017 and Cardi B, including a physical altercation at a New York Fashion Week party in September 2018, drew extensive coverage. Tracy Chapman sued over an unauthorized sample in the unreleased song "Sorry"; Minaj settled for $450,000 in January 2021.

Her October 2019 marriage to Kenneth Petty, a registered sex offender, prompted criticism, and in 2021 his 1994 victim sued the couple for harassment. Minaj was dismissed from that suit in January 2022. A September 2021 tweet questioning COVID-19 vaccines was widely condemned by public health officials. In May 2024 she was briefly detained in Amsterdam on suspicion of drug possession and fined, forcing a show postponement.

## Legacy & Influence

Minaj's blend of rapid, multi-voiced rapping with pop hooks set a template followed by Cardi B, Megan Thee Stallion, Doja Cat, Ice Spice, and others, many of whom cite her directly. Her fan base, the Barbz, became a model of coordinated online fandom. Chart records once held by men, from Hot 100 entries to number-one debuts, now carry her name, and her career is routinely cited as proof that women in rap can sustain arena-level commercial success across generations of listeners.
