## Summary

Jiro Ono (born October 27, 1925) is a Japanese sushi chef and the owner of Sukiyabashi Jiro, a ten-seat restaurant in the basement of an office building beside Ginza Station in Tokyo. He is widely regarded as one of the most accomplished sushi craftsmen of the modern era, and in 2007 he became the oldest chef ever awarded three Michelin stars.

Ono's international fame grew with the 2011 documentary *Jiro Dreams of Sushi*, which presented his relentless pursuit of small improvements as a model of the Japanese ideal of *shokunin*, the devoted craftsman. His restaurant became a symbol of Edomae-style sushi at its most exacting, attracting heads of state and celebrities while remaining a family business run with his sons.

His importance lies less in invention than in refinement. Ono standardized practices such as serving rice at body temperature, aging fish for precise periods, and massaging octopus for up to 50 minutes, influencing a generation of chefs in Japan and abroad.

## Early Life & Background

Ono was born in Tenryū, now part of Hamamatsu in Shizuoka Prefecture. His family was poor, and he left home at roughly seven years old to work in a local restaurant, sleeping on the premises. He has said the experience taught him that he had no choice but to succeed at the trade.

Around 1951, at about 25, he moved to Tokyo and entered a formal sushi apprenticeship. Over the following decade he trained under established Ginza chefs, absorbing the Edomae tradition, in which fish is cured, marinated, or aged rather than served purely raw.

## Rise to Prominence

In 1965 Ono opened Sukiyabashi Jiro in Ginza. The restaurant offered no appetizers and no menu, only an omakase sequence of roughly 20 pieces of nigiri served over about 30 minutes. Ono built long-term relationships with specialist wholesalers at Tsukiji market, including tuna, shrimp, and rice dealers who reserved their best product for him.

In 2005 the Japanese Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare named him a Contemporary Master Craftsman. When Michelin published its first Tokyo guide in November 2007, Sukiyabashi Jiro received three stars, which it held every year through the 2019 edition.

Director David Gelb's *Jiro Dreams of Sushi* premiered at the Berlin International Film Festival in February 2011 and was released in the United States in March 2012, turning Ono into a global figure at age 85.

## Key Achievements

- Three Michelin stars for 12 consecutive editions (2008–2019), making him the oldest three-star chef at the time of the award.
- Contemporary Master Craftsman designation, 2005.
- Hosted a widely reported dinner for United States President Barack Obama and Prime Minister Shinzo Abe on April 23, 2014. Obama called it the best sushi he had ever eaten.
- Trained his elder son Yoshikazu, who now leads the counter at Ginza, and his younger son Takashi, whose Roppongi Hills branch, opened in 2003, has held two Michelin stars.

## Controversies & Criticism

In November 2019 Michelin removed Sukiyabashi Jiro from its Tokyo guide. The restaurant had stopped accepting reservations from the general public, taking bookings only through hotel concierges and existing regulars, and Michelin ruled it no longer met the criterion of accessibility. Some observers criticized the closed-door policy as exclusionary, particularly toward foreign diners.

The restaurant has also drawn scrutiny over its reliance on bluefin tuna, a species listed as endangered during the years of its greatest fame. Yoshikazu Ono has publicly worried about declining fish stocks. Separately, remarks from the family suggesting women are ill-suited to sushi work were widely condemned as sexist. Critics of the apprenticeship model depicted in the film have questioned whether a decade of unpaid or low-paid training remains defensible.

## Legacy & Influence

Ono turned a ten-seat basement counter into a benchmark for culinary precision. Chefs across Tokyo, New York, and London cite his temperature control, rice seasoning, and pacing as reference points. The documentary made *shokunin* a recognizable concept outside Japan and inspired numerous books, films, and restaurants centered on single-minded mastery. Sukiyabashi Jiro continues under Yoshikazu, preserving a lineage that Ono began more than 60 years ago.
