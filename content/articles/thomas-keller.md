## Summary

Thomas Keller is an American chef, restaurateur, and cookbook author best known for The French Laundry in Yountville, California, and Per Se in New York City. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in modern American fine dining, credited with bringing French technique, rigorous discipline, and a distinctly American sensibility to the country's top restaurants.

Keller became the first American-born chef to hold three Michelin stars at two restaurants simultaneously, a distinction he has maintained since 2007. His restaurant group also includes Bouchon, Bouchon Bakery, and Ad Hoc, and his cookbooks have shaped a generation of professional and home cooks. Beyond his own kitchens, he has mentored many chefs who went on to lead acclaimed restaurants of their own.

## Early Life & Background

Keller was born on October 14, 1955, at Camp Pendleton in Oceanside, California, the son of a United States Marine Corps drill instructor. After his parents divorced, he moved with his mother, Betty, to Palm Beach, Florida, where she managed restaurants. He began working as a dishwasher in one of those kitchens as a teenager and gradually moved to cooking.

He had no formal culinary school training. His key mentor was French chef Roland Henin, whom he met in 1977 at the Dunes Club in Narragansett, Rhode Island. Henin taught him classical technique and instilled the idea that cooking is fundamentally an act of nurturing others.

## Rise to Prominence

Keller worked in kitchens in Florida, Rhode Island, and the Catskills before moving to New York, where he cooked at Polo and La Réserve. In 1983 he traveled to France and staged at several Michelin-starred restaurants, including Guy Savoy and Taillevent.

In 1986 he opened Rakel in Manhattan's SoHo district with a partner. The restaurant earned praise but closed in the early 1990s after the 1987 stock market crash reduced its clientele. Keller then moved to Los Angeles as executive chef at the Checkers Hotel.

In 1992 he discovered The French Laundry, a stone building in Yountville dating to the 1880s, and spent nearly two years raising money from dozens of investors to buy it. He opened the restaurant on July 6, 1994. Within a few years it was being described as the best restaurant in the United States.

## Key Achievements

- **The French Laundry** received three Michelin stars in the first San Francisco Bay Area guide in 2007 and has held them every year since.
- **Per Se**, opened in February 2004 at the Time Warner Center in Manhattan, earned three Michelin stars in the first New York guide in 2006.
- **James Beard Foundation awards** include Best California Chef (1996), Outstanding Chef (1997), Outstanding Restaurant for The French Laundry (2003), and Outstanding Restaurateur (2007).
- **Cookbooks** include *The French Laundry Cookbook* (1999), *Bouchon* (2004), *Under Pressure* (2008), *Ad Hoc at Home* (2009), and *The French Laundry, Per Se* (2020).
- He served as culinary consultant on Pixar's 2007 film *Ratatouille* and created its signature "confit byaldi" dish.
- As president of the Ment'or BKB Foundation, he led the effort that brought Team USA its first Bocuse d'Or gold medal in 2017.
- France named him a Chevalier of the Legion of Honour in 2011.

## Controversies & Criticism

In 2015 the New York attorney general's office reached a $500,000 settlement with Per Se after finding that a service charge applied to private dining events had not been fully distributed to workers, in violation of state labor law.

In January 2016, *New York Times* critic Pete Wells downgraded Per Se from four stars to two, calling the experience inconsistent and overpriced. Keller issued a public apology and said the restaurant would improve.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, Keller drew criticism for joining a White House economic advisory group in 2020 and for suing his insurer over business interruption coverage. The French Laundry was also the setting for California Governor Gavin Newsom's widely criticized November 2020 dinner during state gathering restrictions.

## Legacy & Influence

Keller's kitchens have functioned as a training ground for American fine dining. Alumni include Grant Achatz, Corey Lee, Jonathan Benno, and Eric Ziebold, all of whom went on to lead Michelin-starred restaurants. His emphasis on precision, cleanliness, and the "sense of urgency" in the kitchen became a model for professional cooking in the United States.

*The French Laundry Cookbook* is considered one of the most important American cookbooks of its era, and *Under Pressure* helped popularize sous-vide cooking among professionals. His restaurants remain benchmarks against which American tasting-menu dining is measured.
