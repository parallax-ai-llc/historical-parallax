## Summary

Xanana Gusmão (born José Alexandre Gusmão, 20 June 1946) is a Timorese politician and former guerrilla commander who led the armed resistance against Indonesian occupation and became the first President of independent Timor-Leste. He served as President from 2002 to 2007 and as Prime Minister from 2007 to 2015, returning to the premiership in July 2023.

His seventeen years in the mountains and seven years in Indonesian prisons made him the unifying symbol of the independence movement. As a head of state and government he shaped the young country's institutions, its petroleum policy, and its maritime boundary with Australia. He remains the dominant figure in Timorese politics five decades after the struggle began.

## Early Life & Background

Gusmão was born in Manatuto, then part of Portuguese Timor, to a family of schoolteachers. He studied at the Jesuit seminary at Dare, near Dili, but left in 1962 before completing his studies. He worked as a civil servant, a fisherman, and a journalist, and was conscripted into the Portuguese colonial army from 1968 to 1971. He took the nom de guerre "Xanana" from the American rock band Sha Na Na.

After the 1974 Carnation Revolution in Portugal opened the path to decolonisation, Gusmão joined Fretilin (the Revolutionary Front for an Independent East Timor) in 1975. Fretilin declared independence on 28 November 1975. Indonesia invaded nine days later, on 7 December 1975.

## Rise to Prominence

Gusmão retreated into the interior with Falintil, the armed wing of Fretilin. After the death of Nicolau Lobato in December 1978 and the near-destruction of the movement, he rebuilt the guerrilla force and became its commander in 1981. He moved the resistance away from Marxist ideology and, in 1988, founded the National Council of Maubere Resistance to unite parties across the political spectrum.

Indonesian forces captured him in Dili on 20 November 1992. A Jakarta court sentenced him to life imprisonment in May 1993, which President Suharto commuted to 20 years. From Cipinang prison he continued to direct the resistance and corresponded with foreign governments. In 1998 he became president of the newly formed National Council of Timorese Resistance. After 78.5 percent of voters chose independence in the UN-supervised referendum of 30 August 1999, he was released on 7 September 1999.

## Key Achievements

- **Sustaining the resistance** through the 1980s when Falintil had been reduced to a few hundred fighters, and turning it into a broad national front.
- **First President of Timor-Leste**, elected on 14 April 2002 with 82.7 percent of the vote and inaugurated at independence on 20 May 2002.
- **Prime Minister from 8 August 2007 to 16 February 2015**, leading the reconstruction after the 2006 security crisis and overseeing large infrastructure spending funded by the Petroleum Fund.
- **Maritime boundary treaty with Australia**, signed on 6 March 2018 after he led the conciliation process that ended a decades-long dispute over the Timor Sea.
- **Return as Prime Minister on 1 July 2023** after his party, the National Congress for Timorese Reconstruction, won 31 of 65 parliamentary seats.
- International recognition including the Sakharov Prize (1999) and the Sydney Peace Prize (2000).

## Controversies & Criticism

Critics have accused Gusmão of personalising politics and undermining institutions. In 2014 his government dismissed foreign judges and legal advisers after unfavourable tax rulings, a move condemned by legal groups as an attack on judicial independence. A 2008 rice import contract awarded to a company linked to his daughter drew allegations of conflict of interest. His rivalry with Fretilin leader Mari Alkatiri, particularly during the 2006 crisis and the 2017 to 2018 political deadlock, was blamed for repeated instability. Economists have questioned the Tasi Mane petroleum corridor and the pace of withdrawals from the Petroleum Fund, warning that the sovereign wealth fund could be exhausted within a decade. He survived an assassination attempt on 11 February 2008, the same day President José Ramos-Horta was shot.

## Legacy & Influence

Gusmão is widely regarded as the father of Timorese independence. His insistence on national unity over party ideology defined the resistance and the post-independence settlement, and his handling of the Australia boundary dispute is cited as a model for small states using international law. At the same time, his continued dominance raises questions about succession and the resilience of institutions built around one person.
