## Summary

Dixie D'Amelio (born August 12, 2001) is an American social media personality, singer, and television personality who rose to fame on TikTok in 2019 and 2020 alongside her younger sister, Charli D'Amelio. At her peak she was among the ten most-followed creators on the platform, with roughly 57 million followers, and she leveraged that audience into a recording career, a Hulu reality series, and a family business portfolio.

Her significance lies less in any single work than in what she represents: the first generation of TikTok stars who converted short-form virality into mainstream entertainment careers within months. Her 2020 debut single "Be Happy" became one of the earliest examples of a TikTok creator charting internationally, and *The D'Amelio Show* was one of the first streaming series built around an influencer family.

## Early Life & Background

D'Amelio was born in Norwalk, Connecticut, to Marc D'Amelio, a businessman and former Republican candidate for the Connecticut State Senate in 2018, and Heidi D'Amelio, a former model and photographer. She attended King School in Stamford, Connecticut, where she played lacrosse and ran track, and graduated in 2019. She initially planned to attend college but deferred as her online career accelerated.

## Rise to Prominence

Charli D'Amelio's rapid growth on TikTok in mid-2019 drew attention to the whole family, and Dixie began posting her own videos that year. Both sisters joined the Los Angeles creator collective Hype House in December 2019 and left in 2020. In January 2020, the family signed with United Talent Agency, an early signal that legacy Hollywood viewed TikTok creators as commercial properties.

D'Amelio's account passed 10 million followers in early 2020 and continued to climb through the pandemic, when TikTok usage surged. She starred in the Brat web series *Attaway General* (2020) and hosted the YouTube interview series *The Early Late Night Show*. In June 2020 she released "Be Happy," which reached the Billboard Hot 100 and accumulated well over 100 million streams across platforms. She signed with L.A. Reid's HitCo Entertainment and followed with "One Whole Day" featuring Wiz Khalifa (December 2020), "Roommates" (2021), and "Psycho" featuring Rubi Rose (2021). Her debut album, *A Letter to Me*, was released on June 10, 2022.

## Key Achievements

- **Follower reach:** Approximately 57 million TikTok followers at peak, plus over 20 million on Instagram and several million YouTube subscribers.
- **Earnings:** Forbes estimated her income at $2.9 million in 2020 and $10 million in 2021, making her the second-highest-earning TikTok creator behind her sister.
- **Television:** *The D'Amelio Show* premiered on Hulu on September 3, 2021 and ran for three seasons through 2023, with her depression and anxiety treatment as a central storyline.
- **Business:** Co-founded the Hollister sub-brand Social Tourist (May 2021) and D'Amelio Brands (2022), which launched D'Amelio Footwear in 2023.
- **Podcasting:** Co-hosted *Charli and Dixie: 2 Chix* (2020–2021) with Ramble.

## Controversies & Criticism

In November 2020, a YouTube dinner video in which D'Amelio spat out a snail prepared by chef Aaron May drew widespread accusations of rudeness and entitlement toward the family; Charli lost roughly one million followers in the aftermath. Dixie responded that she had genuinely vomited and that the reaction was edited for comedy.

Her music has received consistently mixed reviews, with critics arguing that her label deals reflected follower counts rather than vocal ability. She has also faced scrutiny over the authenticity of mental health content on *The D'Amelio Show*, though she has said the series depicted her real treatment. In 2023 she disclosed a diagnosis of premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD) and stepped back from posting for periods of time.

## Legacy & Influence

D'Amelio's career established a template later followed by many creators: agency representation, a music deal, a streaming docuseries, and equity in consumer brands, all within two years of going viral. Her openness about depression, medication, and PMDD contributed to broader mental health discussion among Gen Z audiences. Whether her musical output endures, the D'Amelio family's rapid institutionalization of TikTok fame remains a case study in the 2020s creator economy.
