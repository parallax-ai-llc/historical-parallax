## Summary

Ariana Grande-Butera (born June 26, 1993) is an American singer, songwriter, and actress. She first reached audiences as a child actress on Broadway and on Nickelodeon before signing with Republic Records in 2011, and her debut album *Yours Truly* (2013) opened at number one on the Billboard 200. Over the following decade she became one of the defining pop vocalists of her generation, known for a four-octave soprano range, frequent use of the whistle register, and a string of albums that each topped the US charts.

Grande matters beyond her sales figures. Her response to the 2017 Manchester Arena bombing, her rapid-release strategy with *Sweetener* (2018) and *Thank U, Next* (2019), and her later pivot back to acting in *Wicked* (2024) shaped how pop stars manage grief, output, and career breadth in the streaming era. She has won multiple Grammy Awards and received an Academy Award nomination for Best Supporting Actress.

## Early Life & Background

Grande was born in Boca Raton, Florida, to Joan Grande, chief executive of Hose-McCann Communications, and Edward Butera, a graphic design firm owner. Her parents separated when she was around nine. She has an older half-brother, Frankie Grande, a performer and television personality. As a child she sang with the Fort Lauderdale Children's Theater and performed in local productions, including *Annie* and *The Wizard of Oz*. In 2008, at age 15, she made her Broadway debut as Charlotte in the musical *13*, which earned her a National Youth Theatre Association Award.

## Rise to Prominence

From 2010 to 2013 Grande played Cat Valentine on the Nickelodeon sitcom *Victorious*, reprising the role in the spin-off *Sam & Cat* (2013–2014). She signed with Republic Records in 2011. Her first single, "The Way" featuring Mac Miller, reached the top ten of the Billboard Hot 100 in 2013, and *Yours Truly* debuted at number one. *My Everything* (2014) produced "Problem," "Break Free," and "Bang Bang," establishing her as a consistent hitmaker. *Dangerous Woman* (2016) followed with its title track and "Into You."

On May 22, 2017, a suicide bombing at Manchester Arena after her concert killed 22 people. Grande organised the One Love Manchester benefit on June 4, 2017, which raised millions for victims, and she was later named an honorary citizen of Manchester.

## Key Achievements

*Sweetener* (2018) won the Grammy Award for Best Pop Vocal Album. Its follow-up *Thank U, Next* (2019) arrived only six months later, and in February 2019 she became the first solo artist to hold the top three positions on the Hot 100 simultaneously with "7 Rings," "Break Up with Your Girlfriend, I'm Bored," and "Thank U, Next." *Positions* (2020) also debuted at number one, and "Rain on Me" with Lady Gaga won the Grammy for Best Pop Duo/Group Performance in 2021.

Her 2024 album *Eternal Sunshine* yielded two Hot 100 number ones, "Yes, And?" and "We Can't Be Friends (Wait for Your Best Friend)." That November she starred as Glinda in *Wicked*, receiving Academy Award and Golden Globe nominations, and returned for *Wicked: For Good* in 2025. She also launched the r.e.m. beauty cosmetics brand in 2021 and a successful fragrance line, including *Cloud* (2018).

## Controversies & Criticism

In 2015 a leaked video showed Grande licking a doughnut on a shop counter and saying "I hate America," prompting an apology. The "7 Rings" era brought accusations of cultural appropriation, and critics noted her visibly darker complexion in that period compared with her Nickelodeon years. A 2019 Japanese tattoo intended to read "7 Rings" instead spelled "shichirin," a small charcoal grill. Her 2023 relationship with *Wicked* co-star Ethan Slater, which began as both were ending marriages, drew tabloid scrutiny. The 2024 documentary *Quiet on Set* renewed debate about how Nickelodeon presented its young stars, including Grande.

## Legacy & Influence

Grande's vocal technique, particularly her whistle notes and Mariah Carey-influenced runs, has become a reference point for younger pop singers. Her rapid album cycles and direct fan engagement on social media influenced release strategy across the industry. Her dual success in music and film, capped by the *Wicked* nomination, marked her as one of the few 2010s pop stars to move credibly into prestige cinema.
