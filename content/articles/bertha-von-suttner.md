## Summary

Bertha von Suttner (1843–1914) was an Austrian novelist and pacifist who became the first woman awarded the Nobel Peace Prize, in 1905. Born into the Bohemian aristocracy as Countess Kinsky, she turned to writing out of financial necessity and produced the anti-war novel *Die Waffen nieder!* (*Lay Down Your Arms!*) in 1889, which became one of the most widely read books of its era.

She built the organised peace movement in Austria and Germany, founded the Austrian Peace Society in 1891, and helped shape the International Peace Bureau in Bern. Her long friendship with Alfred Nobel is widely credited with influencing his decision to endow a prize for peace.

Suttner died on 21 June 1914, one week before the assassination in Sarajevo that triggered the war she had spent 25 years warning against.

## Early Life & Background

Bertha Sophia Felicita Kinsky von Wchinitz und Tettau was born in Prague on 9 June 1843. Her father, Field Marshal Lieutenant Franz Joseph Kinsky, died shortly before her birth at the age of 75. Her mother, Sophie von Körner, came from a lesser noble family, and the Kinsky relatives kept the widow and children at a distance.

Bertha received a private education in languages and music, and hoped for a career as an opera singer. Her mother's gambling losses at spa casinos steadily consumed the family's money. By 1873, at the age of 30, Bertha took a post as governess to the four daughters of Baron Karl von Suttner in Vienna.

## Rise to Prominence

In the Suttner household she fell in love with the family's son, Arthur Gundaccar von Suttner, seven years her junior. His parents objected, and in 1876 she answered a newspaper advertisement placed by Alfred Nobel for a secretary in Paris. She stayed only about a week before returning to Vienna, but the two maintained a correspondence until Nobel's death in 1896.

Bertha and Arthur married secretly on 12 June 1876 and left for the Caucasus, where they lived for nine years in Mingrelia under the patronage of Princess Ekaterine Dadiani. Both supported themselves by writing. They returned to Austria in 1885 and settled at Harmannsdorf Castle in Lower Austria.

*Die Waffen nieder!* appeared in 1889 after several publishers rejected it. Narrated by a woman who loses relatives to the wars of 1859, 1864, 1866 and 1870, the novel ran through 37 German editions and was translated into more than a dozen languages. Leo Tolstoy compared it to *Uncle Tom's Cabin*.

## Key Achievements

- **Founded the Austrian Peace Society** in 1891 and represented it at the Third International Peace Congress in Rome that year.
- **Co-founded the journal *Die Waffen nieder!*** with Alfred Hermann Fried in 1892, editing it until 1899.
- **Served as vice president of the International Peace Bureau** from its founding in 1892.
- **Attended the Hague Peace Conference of 1899** as the only woman present, running a salon for delegates and journalists.
- **Received the Nobel Peace Prize** for 1905, presented in Christiania in April 1906. She was the second woman to win any Nobel Prize, after Marie Curie in 1903.
- **Toured the United States** in 1904 and again in 1912, meeting President Theodore Roosevelt at the White House.

She and Arthur also co-founded the Association for Defence Against Antisemitism in Vienna in 1891.

## Controversies & Criticism

Nationalist and military circles in Austria and Germany mocked her as "Friedensbertha" and "the Peace Fury". Critics dismissed *Die Waffen nieder!* as sentimental and argued that her salon-style diplomacy among aristocrats and diplomats could not restrain governments.

Socialist pacifists criticised her for keeping the movement separate from the labour movement and for relying on arbitration treaties rather than structural change. Some historians have also questioned how far she actually influenced Nobel's will, noting that Nobel expressed scepticism about peace congresses in his letters to her.

## Legacy & Influence

Suttner died of cancer in Vienna on 21 June 1914. Her portrait appeared on the Austrian 1,000-schilling banknote from 1966 and has been on the Austrian 2-euro coin since 2002. The International Peace Bureau she helped build received its own Nobel Peace Prize in 1910.

*Die Waffen nieder!* was filmed in Denmark in 1914 and remains in print. Her name is attached to schools, streets and peace prizes across Austria and Germany, and she is regularly cited as a founder of the modern international peace movement.
