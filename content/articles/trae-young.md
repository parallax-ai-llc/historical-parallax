## Summary

Rayford Trae Young (born September 19, 1998) is an American professional basketball player, a point guard known for deep three-point range, elite passing, and a flair for drawing fouls. Drafted fifth overall in 2018, he became the face of the Atlanta Hawks for more than seven seasons before a trade to the Washington Wizards in February 2026.

Young's game is built on the modern pull-up three and the lob pass, and he has ranked among the NBA's leaders in both scoring and assists nearly every season of his career. He is a four-time All-Star and led the league in assists in 2024–25.

His polarizing style, including on-court showmanship and a reliance on officiating, has made him one of the most debated guards of his generation. He remains a reference point for undersized playmakers who rely on skill and range rather than size.

## Early Life & Background

Young was born in Lubbock, Texas, the son of Rayford Young, a former Texas Tech guard who played professionally in Europe. The family settled in Norman, Oklahoma, where Trae starred at Norman North High School and was named a McDonald's All-American in 2017.

He chose to stay home and play for the University of Oklahoma. In his lone college season (2017–18) he averaged 27.4 points and 8.7 assists, becoming the first Division I player to lead the nation in both categories in the same season. He was a consensus first-team All-American and earned frequent comparisons to Stephen Curry.

## Rise to Prominence

Young declared for the 2018 NBA Draft after one season. The Dallas Mavericks selected him fifth overall and immediately traded him to Atlanta for Luka Dončić, the third pick, plus a 2019 first-round pick. The swap became one of the most scrutinized draft-night trades of the decade.

After a slow start, Young finished his rookie year on the All-Rookie First Team and was runner-up for Rookie of the Year. In 2019–20 he averaged 29.6 points and 9.3 assists and earned his first All-Star selection.

The 2021 playoffs made him a national figure. Atlanta upset the New York Knicks in five games, with Young bowing to the Madison Square Garden crowd, then beat the Philadelphia 76ers in seven before losing to Milwaukee in the Eastern Conference Finals. That August he signed a five-year rookie-scale maximum extension worth up to about $207 million.

## Key Achievements

- Four-time NBA All-Star (2020, 2022, 2024, 2025).
- All-NBA Third Team in 2021–22, when he became the first player since Nate Archibald in 1972–73 to lead the league in both total points and total assists.
- Led the NBA in assists per game in 2024–25 with 11.6.
- Became the Hawks' all-time assists leader, passing Doc Rivers, in 2024.
- Consensus first-team All-American and NCAA leader in points and assists at Oklahoma in 2018.
- Signed an Adidas signature shoe deal; the Trae Young 1 launched in 2021.

## Controversies & Criticism

Young's foul-drawing techniques, including abrupt stops and hooking defenders' arms, drew heavy criticism and were a principal reason the NBA changed its rules on "non-basketball moves" before the 2021–22 season. Critics also point to his defense, which advanced metrics have consistently rated among the league's weakest at his position.

He was fined $25,000 in 2023 for criticizing officials and $35,000 in January 2024 after being ejected for throwing the ball at a referee. Reports of friction with head coach Nate McMillan in December 2022, including a game Young skipped after a disagreement, preceded McMillan's dismissal the following February. Atlanta's failure to return to the conference finals fueled recurring debate over whether a team can win at the highest level built around him.

## Legacy & Influence

Young helped normalize 30-foot shooting as a primary weapon for a lead guard and revived the lob-heavy pick-and-roll as a central offense. His 2021 playoff run remains the Hawks' deepest since 2015. For a generation of smaller guards, he is proof that vision and range can offset a lack of size, even as his career remains a case study in the tension between individual production and team success.
