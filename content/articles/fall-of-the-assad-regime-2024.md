## Summary
In just 11 days, from 27 November to 8 December 2024, an opposition offensive led by Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham (HTS) swept from northwestern Syria through Aleppo, Hama and Homs and into Damascus, ending more than five decades of rule by the Assad family [^1][^2]. President Bashar al-Assad fled to Russia, where he was granted asylum, and Moscow confirmed his resignation and departure [^1]. HTS leader Ahmed al-Sharaa became Syria's de facto leader and was later named transitional president [^1]. The collapse reshaped the Middle East, though sectarian violence, foreign incursions and questions of accountability have followed [^1].

## Background
Assad's government had survived the civil war that began in 2011 largely through support from Russia, Iran and Hezbollah [^1]. By 2024 those allies were stretched: Russia was focused on its invasion of Ukraine, and Hezbollah was weakened by its conflict with Israel [^1]. Opposition planning for an offensive on Aleppo began in late 2023 but was delayed by Turkish objections; after Assad rebuffed President Recep Tayyip Erdogan's outreach, Turkey allowed the HTS-led forces to proceed [^1].

## The Event
On 27 November 2024 HTS and allied factions launched a surprise attack in Aleppo province, with more than 130 people killed in the first 24 hours [^2]. Rebels entered Aleppo city by 29 November and took most of it within a day, and on 5 December they captured Hama after heavy fighting [^2]. On 6 December, al-Sharaa told CNN that the aim was to remove Assad, while pledging to protect minorities [^1]. Opposition forces took full control of Homs on 7 December, cutting Damascus off from the Alawite coast, as southern rebels closed in on the capital [^1]. Early on 8 December, Assad flew to Russia and opposition forces declared victory on state television [^1]. HTS announced the release of prisoners from Sednaya Prison, one of the country's largest and most notorious detention facilities [^1].

## Response and Aftermath
Prime Minister Mohammad Ghazi al-Jalali stayed to hand over institutions, and on 10 December Mohammed al-Bashir was appointed head of a caretaker government [^1]. Israeli forces moved into the UN buffer zone on the Golan Heights, crossing the 1974 ceasefire line for the first time in 50 years [^1]. On 29 January 2025, al-Sharaa was appointed president for the transitional period, and on 13 March he signed a Constitutional Declaration for a five-year transition [^1]. The United States and the United Kingdom were among Western governments that lifted sanctions on Syria, and on 10 November 2025 al-Sharaa became the first Syrian president to visit the White House [^1].

## Positive Perspectives
- The fall of Damascus was met with widespread public celebrations in Homs, Damascus and elsewhere [^1].
- The release of detainees from Sednaya was seen by HTS and many Syrians as a symbolic end to decades of abuse [^1].
- Al-Sharaa publicly pledged to protect Christians and other minorities and met Christian leaders on 31 December 2024 [^1].
- Syria re-entered international diplomacy, with al-Sharaa addressing the UN General Assembly general debate in September 2025 [^1].
- In 2026 Syrian courts began transitional justice trials of Assad-era figures [^3].

## Negative Perspectives
- The Syrian Observatory for Human Rights reported that security forces and allied fighters killed more than 740 Alawite civilians in clashes in western Syria in March 2025 [^1].
- Alawites, Druze and Ismailis have reported growing discrimination since the change of government [^1].
- Israel's advance beyond the Golan ceasefire line raised concerns about Syrian sovereignty [^1].
- The 2025 Constitutional Declaration concentrated executive power in the presidency and abolished the post of prime minister [^1].
- Assad and his brother Maher remain in Russia, which has refused to extradite them [^3].

## Recent News
In August 2026 a Damascus court sentenced Bashar al-Assad, his brother Maher and several associates to death in absentia for premeditated murder, torture and crimes against humanity. Atef Najib, a cousin of Assad and former Daraa security chief, was also sentenced to death; Russia has not extradited the Assads [^3].
