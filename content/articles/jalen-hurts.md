## Summary

Jalen Alexander Hurts (born August 7, 1998) is an American football quarterback for the Philadelphia Eagles of the National Football League (NFL). A dual-threat passer known for his rushing power and composure, he led the Eagles to two Super Bowl appearances in three seasons and was named Most Valuable Player of Super Bowl LIX in February 2025 after a 40–22 victory over the Kansas City Chiefs.

Hurts' path to the NFL was unusual. He started as a true freshman at Alabama, was benched at halftime of a national championship game, then transferred to Oklahoma for a Heisman-finalist final season. Drafted in the second round in 2020, he was widely viewed as a backup before becoming one of the league's highest-paid players by 2023.

He matters beyond statistics as an example of a quarterback who rebuilt his career after a public demotion, and as the centerpiece of a Philadelphia offense whose short-yardage "tush push" play prompted league-wide rule debates.

## Early Life & Background

Hurts was born in Houston, Texas, and raised in nearby Channelview. His father, Averion Hurts, was the head football coach at Channelview High School, where Jalen played quarterback and also competed as a powerlifter, squatting more than 500 pounds as a teenager. He committed to the University of Alabama and enrolled in January 2016.

## Rise to Prominence

At Alabama, Hurts became the program's first true freshman to start at quarterback since 1984. He was named 2016 SEC Offensive Player of the Year and compiled a 26–2 record as a starter over two seasons. In the College Football Playoff National Championship on January 8, 2018, coach Nick Saban replaced him at halftime with Tua Tagovailoa, who led a comeback win over Georgia. Hurts remained on the roster, and in the December 2018 SEC Championship Game he relieved an injured Tagovailoa to rally Alabama past Georgia.

Having graduated in three years, Hurts transferred to Oklahoma for the 2019 season under Lincoln Riley. He threw for 3,851 yards and 32 touchdowns and rushed for 1,298 yards and 20 touchdowns, winning the Big 12 title and finishing second in Heisman Trophy voting to Joe Burrow.

The Eagles selected him 53rd overall in the 2020 NFL Draft. He replaced Carson Wentz as the starter in December 2020, and in 2021 he led Philadelphia to a 9–8 record and a playoff berth.

## Key Achievements

- **2022 season:** Went 14–1 as a starter, earned Pro Bowl and second-team All-Pro honors, and finished second in NFL MVP voting to Patrick Mahomes.
- **Super Bowl LVII (February 12, 2023):** Passed for 304 yards and rushed for 70 yards and three touchdowns in a 38–35 loss to Kansas City, setting a Super Bowl record for rushing yards by a quarterback.
- **Contract:** In April 2023 he signed a five-year, $255 million extension with $179.3 million guaranteed, briefly the largest annual salary in NFL history.
- **Super Bowl LIX (February 9, 2025):** Completed 17 of 22 passes for 221 yards and two touchdowns, rushed for a quarterback-record 72 yards and a score, and was named Super Bowl MVP as the Eagles beat the Chiefs 40–22.

## Controversies & Criticism

The 2018 Alabama benching generated debate about Saban's handling of the situation, though Hurts' public restraint was widely praised. In the NFL, critics have questioned whether his production depends on elite supporting talent, pointing to Philadelphia's late-2023 collapse from a 10–1 start to an 11–6 finish and a 32–9 wild-card loss to Tampa Bay, a season in which he committed 23 turnovers. Tension over passing volume with receiver A.J. Brown surfaced publicly in 2024 and 2025. The Eagles' quarterback sneak, nicknamed the "tush push," faced a proposed ban that failed at the May 2025 owners' meeting.

## Legacy & Influence

Hurts is one of the few quarterbacks to start Super Bowls for a team after being drafted as a projected backup, and his 2025 title was the Eagles' second in franchise history. He founded the Jalen Hurts Foundation and in 2023 donated roughly $200,000 for air-conditioning units in Philadelphia public schools. His all-female management team, led by agent Nicole Lynn, has been cited as a milestone for representation in sports business.
