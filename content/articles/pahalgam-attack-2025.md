## Summary

On 22 April 2025, gunmen attacked tourists in the Baisaran meadow near Pahalgam in Indian-administered Jammu and Kashmir, killing 26 people [^1]. Most of the dead were Hindu tourists from across India; a Nepali visitor and a local Muslim pony operator, Syed Adil Hussain Shah, were also killed [^1][^2]. The Resistance Front (TRF), which India and the United States regard as a proxy of the Pakistan-based Lashkar-e-Taiba, claimed and then retracted responsibility [^1]. The attack triggered a sweeping diplomatic rupture between India and Pakistan and, within two weeks, India's military strikes known as Operation Sindoor [^1].

## Background

Baisaran, about 7 km from Pahalgam in Anantnag district, is a popular meadow sometimes called "mini Switzerland" [^3]. TRF's initial claim cited opposition to the settlement of non-locals in the region [^1]. Lashkar-e-Taiba is a UN-designated terrorist group [^1].

## The Event

In the early afternoon of 22 April, armed men emerged from the surrounding forest and opened fire on tourists in the meadow [^1]. Survivors reported that the assailants asked victims their names and religion and singled out Hindu men, according to accounts summarized by Wikipedia [^1]. Twenty-six people were killed and about 20 injured [^1]. Syed Adil Hussain Shah, a local pony operator, reportedly tried to protect the tourists and wrestle a gun from one of the attackers before he was shot [^1]. TRF claimed the attack on 22 and 23 April and retracted the claim on 26 April, alleging a hacking of its channels [^1].

## Response and Aftermath

On 23 April India suspended the 1960 Indus Waters Treaty until Pakistan "credibly and irrevocably" ended support for cross-border terrorism, closed the Attari-Wagah border post, cancelled visas issued to Pakistanis under the SAARC scheme, and expelled Pakistan's military advisers [^2]. Pakistan denied involvement, closed its airspace to Indian aircraft, suspended visas for Indians, threatened to suspend the Simla Agreement, and proposed a neutral investigation [^1]. The National Investigation Agency (NIA) took over the case on 27 April [^1]. On 7 May India launched Operation Sindoor against targets in Pakistan and Pakistan-administered Kashmir, starting a four-day conflict [^1]. On 28 July 2025, Indian forces killed three men in Operation Mahadev near Srinagar; Indian investigators said ballistic tests linked their weapons to the attack [^1].

## Positive Perspectives

- Protests were held across Muslim-majority areas of Kashmir, where demonstrators condemned the attack as a blow to Kashmiriyat and shops closed in solidarity [^1].
- The NIA filed a 1,597-page chargesheet on 15 December 2025 naming Lashkar-e-Taiba, TRF and individual accused, giving the case a formal judicial path [^3].
- The United States designated TRF a terrorist organization on 17 July 2025 [^1].
- A UN Security Council monitoring report noted TRF's claims of responsibility, adding international documentation to the record [^1].

## Negative Perspectives

- The attackers deliberately singled out victims by religion, according to survivor accounts [^1].
- The government later admitted a security lapse: Baisaran had been opened to tourists two months early without notifying security forces, and no security personnel were present, drawing opposition criticism [^1].
- Pakistan rejected India's accusations and called for a neutral probe, and the two governments' accounts of responsibility remain opposed [^1].
- The suspension of the Indus Waters Treaty put a decades-old water-sharing framework in doubt [^2].
- The crisis escalated into armed conflict between two nuclear-armed states within two weeks [^1].

## Recent News

On 6 July 2026 the NIA filed a supplementary chargesheet naming Lashkar-e-Taiba chief Hafiz Saeed as an accused, alleging planning and conspiracy from across the border [^4].
