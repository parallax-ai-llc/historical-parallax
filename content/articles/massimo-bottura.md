## Summary

Massimo Bottura (born 30 September 1962) is an Italian chef and restaurateur best known for Osteria Francescana in Modena, a three-Michelin-star restaurant that was ranked number one on The World's 50 Best Restaurants list in 2016 and 2018. His cooking reinterprets the traditional dishes of Emilia-Romagna through modern technique and conceptual, art-inspired presentation.

Beyond the kitchen, Bottura co-founded the non-profit Food for Soul with his wife Lara Gilmore in 2016. The organisation runs community kitchens, known as Refettori, that turn surplus food into meals for people in need. This work, combined with his media presence, has made him one of the most recognisable figures in contemporary gastronomy and a leading voice against food waste.

## Early Life & Background

Bottura was born in Modena, in the Emilia-Romagna region of northern Italy, the youngest of five children in a family that ran a fuel business. He has often described hiding under the kitchen table as a child while his grandmother rolled pasta, a memory he cites as the origin of his cooking. He enrolled in law school but abandoned his studies in 1986, at age 23, to buy and run Trattoria del Campazzo, a roadside restaurant outside Modena. There he trained under the French-born chef Georges Cogny and a local cook, Lidia Cristoni, who taught him traditional pasta-making.

## Rise to Prominence

In 1993 Bottura moved briefly to New York, where he met Lara Gilmore, an American art student he later married. In 1994 he staged at Alain Ducasse's Le Louis XV in Monaco, an experience that shaped his technical approach. He opened Osteria Francescana in Modena on 19 March 1995. Early years were difficult, as local diners resisted his reworked versions of regional classics. A stint at Ferran Adrià's El Bulli in 2000 pushed his food further toward the avant-garde.

Recognition followed: a first Michelin star in 2002, a second in 2006, and a third in the 2012 guide, announced in November 2011. Osteria Francescana entered The World's 50 Best Restaurants in 2009 and climbed to third place from 2013 to 2015, second in 2017, and first in 2016 and 2018. His 2015 appearance in the first episode of the Netflix series *Chef's Table* brought him international fame.

## Key Achievements

- **Signature dishes** such as "Five Ages of Parmigiano Reggiano in Different Temperatures and Textures", "Oops! I Dropped the Lemon Tart", and "The Crunchy Part of the Lasagna" became emblems of modern Italian cooking.
- **Earthquake response**: after the May 2012 Emilia earthquake damaged some 360,000 wheels of Parmigiano Reggiano, he publicised a risotto recipe that helped producers sell the damaged cheese and avoid bankruptcy.
- **Refettorio Ambrosiano** opened in Milan during Expo 2015, feeding people in need with surplus food cooked by visiting chefs. Food for Soul later opened Refettori in Rio de Janeiro (2016), London (2017), Paris (2018) and other cities.
- **Books** include *Never Trust a Skinny Italian Chef* (2014) and *Bread Is Gold* (2017).
- **Expansion**: Franceschetta58 (2011), Gucci Osteria in Florence (2018) and later Beverly Hills, Tokyo and Seoul, and the guesthouse Casa Maria Luigia (2019).
- Named a UN Environment Programme Goodwill Ambassador in 2020.

## Controversies & Criticism

In his early years, Modenese traditionalists accused Bottura of disrespecting regional heritage, and the restaurant came close to closing before national critics championed it. Some commentators have argued that his luxury partnerships, notably with Gucci, sit awkwardly alongside his anti-waste advocacy. Critics of fine dining more broadly have questioned whether high-profile Refettorio projects address structural causes of hunger or mainly generate publicity for participating chefs. Bottura has responded that visibility is what draws volunteers and donors to the cause.

## Legacy & Influence

Bottura helped legitimise creative, technique-driven cooking within Italy, a country long protective of its culinary traditions. Osteria Francescana's two number-one rankings placed Italian fine dining at the global summit, and in 2019 the restaurant was moved to the "Best of the Best" hall of fame. His Refettorio model has been copied by chefs and charities worldwide and reframed food waste as a mainstream concern for the culinary profession.
