## Summary
On 4 December 2024, Brian Thompson, chief executive of the health insurer UnitedHealthcare, was shot dead outside the New York Hilton Midtown in Manhattan as he walked to an investors' meeting [^1]. After a five-day manhunt, police in Altoona, Pennsylvania, arrested 26-year-old Luigi Mangione [^1][^2]. The killing set off a polarised national reaction in which anger at the US health insurance industry was voiced alongside condemnation of the violence [^1]. In August 2026 Mangione pleaded guilty to federal stalking charges and admitted the shooting, while his New York state murder case remains in dispute [^1][^3].

## Background
Thompson led UnitedHealthcare, the insurance arm of UnitedHealth Group, and was in New York for the company's annual investor conference [^1]. Public frustration with claim denials by health insurers was widespread before the attack, and polls afterward found that most Americans believed coverage denials and insurer profits contributed to the killing [^1]. Mangione later told a federal court he had acted "after years of enduring severe pain from a broken back, navigating the obstacles of the health insurance system" [^1].

## The Event
At about 6:44 a.m. on 4 December 2024, a masked gunman shot Thompson outside the hotel and fled [^1]. The words "delay," "deny," and "depose" were written on the cartridge cases found at the scene [^1]. On 9 December, a McDonald's employee in Altoona recognised the suspect, and local police arrested Mangione [^1]. Authorities said he was carrying a 3D-printed pistol and suppressor consistent with those used in the shooting, a handwritten letter criticising the American health care system, and fraudulent identification [^1].

## Response and Aftermath
Mangione was indicted on 11 state charges, including murder in furtherance of terrorism, and four federal charges [^1]. Attorney General Pam Bondi directed federal prosecutors in April 2025 to seek the death penalty, the first such decision of President Donald Trump's second term [^2]. Justice Gregory Carro dismissed the state terrorism-related murder counts on 16 September 2025 [^1]. On 30 January 2026, U.S. District Judge Margaret Garnett dismissed the federal murder charge as technically flawed, which removed the possibility of a death sentence, leaving stalking counts that carry up to life in prison [^2]. UnitedHealth's condolence post on Facebook drew more than 100,000 "laughing" reactions [^1].

## Positive Perspectives
- Police acted quickly on a tip from a member of the public, ending the manhunt within five days [^1].
- Many public officials condemned the violence while calling for reform of insurance practices, with Senator Ron Wyden saying murder is "always unacceptable" but that context mattered [^1].
- The case prompted wide public debate over claim denials and the health insurance system, reflected in national polling [^1].
- The courts tested the limits of terrorism and capital charges, with judges narrowing charges they found legally flawed [^1][^2].

## Negative Perspectives
- Much of the social media reaction showed contempt or mockery toward Thompson and UnitedHealth Group [^1].
- An Emerson College poll found 68 percent of voters viewed the killing as unacceptable, but 41 percent of voters aged 18 to 29 called it "acceptable or somewhat acceptable" [^1].
- UnitedHealth CEO Andrew Witty was criticised after a leaked internal video recorded the day after the killing defended the company's denial practices [^1].
- The federal government's bid to execute Mangione, which it justified by calling the killing a "premeditated, cold-blooded assassination," failed after the judge found the murder charge technically flawed [^2].
- The overlapping state and federal prosecutions have raised unresolved double jeopardy questions [^3].

## Recent News
On 14 August 2026, Mangione pleaded guilty in federal court to two counts of stalking resulting in death, and Judge Garnett set sentencing for 18 December 2026 [^1][^4]. Justice Carro then postponed the state murder trial, which had been set for 8 September, while the defence argues that New York's double jeopardy protections bar it; a hearing is scheduled for 10 December 2026 [^3].
