## Summary

Luka Perković, known professionally as Perkz, is a Croatian professional League of Legends player born on 30 November 1998. He is best known for his six-year tenure with G2 Esports, where he became the most decorated player in the history of Europe's top league, winning eight domestic titles between 2016 and 2020.

Perkz is widely regarded as one of the greatest Western players of all time. He was the face of G2's rise from a promotion-tournament qualifier to a team that won the 2019 Mid-Season Invitational and reached the 2019 World Championship final, the closest any European side has come to a world title since 2011.

His career is also notable for his willingness to change roles. After playing mid lane for four years, he moved to bot lane in 2019 to accommodate Caps, a decision that produced G2's most successful season. His later transfers to Cloud9 and Team Vitality made him one of the most expensive players in the game's history.

## Early Life & Background

Perković was born in Zagreb, Croatia, and grew up there during the 2000s. He began playing League of Legends as a young teenager and reached the top of the European solo-queue ladder while still in secondary school.

His early competitive experience came on smaller European teams in 2014 and 2015, including a stint with Millenium. Because he was still a minor, he was not eligible to play in the top-tier EU LCS until shortly before his seventeenth birthday, a restriction that delayed his professional debut.

## Rise to Prominence

Perkz joined Gamers2, later rebranded as G2 Esports, in 2015. The team won the summer promotion tournament that year and entered the EU LCS for the 2016 Spring Split. G2 won the title in their first split, and Perkz was named Rookie of the Split.

G2 went on to win four consecutive EU LCS titles from 2016 Spring through 2017 Summer. International results lagged behind, with group-stage exits at the 2016 and 2017 World Championships and a runner-up finish to SK Telecom T1 at the 2017 Mid-Season Invitational.

In 2018, G2 reached the Worlds semifinals for the first time. Ahead of 2019, the organization signed mid laner Caps from Fnatic, and Perkz moved to bot lane. The reshuffled roster won both LEC splits, defeated Team Liquid 3-0 in the MSI 2019 final, and reached the Worlds final, where it lost 0-3 to FunPlus Phoenix.

## Key Achievements

- **Eight LEC/EU LCS championships** with G2 Esports: 2016 Spring, 2016 Summer, 2017 Spring, 2017 Summer, 2019 Spring, 2019 Summer, 2020 Spring, and 2020 Summer.
- **Mid-Season Invitational champion** in 2019, the first international trophy won by a European team since Season 1.
- **World Championship finalist** in 2019 and semifinalist in 2018 and 2020.
- **LCS Mid-Season Showdown champion** in 2021 with Cloud9, following a transfer reported at roughly 5 million US dollars.
- **Two-role success**, winning domestic titles as both a mid laner and a bot laner.

## Controversies & Criticism

Perkz built a reputation for outspoken trash talk early in his career. Comments before the 2016 World Championship, combined with G2's group-stage exit that year, drew widespread ridicule and made the team a target of memes for several seasons.

His 2020 move to Cloud9 was criticized by some fans as a step down in competition, and his remarks comparing North American and European play attracted backlash. His 2022 stint on Team Vitality's heavily funded roster ended without a World Championship appearance, and the team's failure was frequently pinned on his form and leadership.

## Legacy & Influence

Perkz remains the benchmark for European excellence in League of Legends. His G2 teams changed how Western squads approached the game, favoring aggressive, unconventional drafts over the more structured styles that dominated before 2019.

His role swap to bot lane is often cited as one of the most consequential roster decisions in LEC history. After leaving Team Heretics at the end of 2023, he stepped back from full-time competition and has since been more visible as a streamer and commentator than as a player.
