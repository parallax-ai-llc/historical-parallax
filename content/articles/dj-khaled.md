## Summary

DJ Khaled, born Khaled Mohammed Khaled on November 26, 1975, is an American DJ, record executive, and media personality. He rose from Miami radio in the late 1990s to become one of hip-hop's most recognizable figures, less for beat-making than for assembling star-studded collaborations and marketing them with relentless enthusiasm.

His albums have produced multiple Billboard 200 number ones, including *Major Key* (2016), *Grateful* (2017), *Khaled Khaled* (2021), and *God Did* (2022). His single "I'm the One" (2017) topped the Billboard Hot 100, and "Higher" with Nipsey Hussle and John Legend won a Grammy Award in 2020.

Khaled's broader impact lies in his role as a curator and brand. His catchphrases, Snapchat persona, and executive work through We the Best Music Group made him a fixture of pop culture beyond music.

## Early Life & Background

Khaled was born in New Orleans, Louisiana, to Palestinian immigrant parents who worked as musicians and later ran small businesses. The family moved to Orlando, Florida, where he grew up. As a teenager he worked in a local record store and began DJing, building relationships with artists such as Lil Wayne and Birdman before they became famous.

In the late 1990s he moved to Miami and joined the radio station WEDR (99 Jamz), where his show "The Takeover" made him a leading voice in Southern hip-hop radio. He also became affiliated with Fat Joe's Terror Squad, serving as the group's DJ.

## Rise to Prominence

Khaled released his debut album, *Listennn... the Album*, in 2006 through Koch Records. It reached number 12 on the Billboard 200 and introduced his formula: posse cuts featuring multiple major artists. *We the Best* followed in 2007 with the singles "We Takin' Over" and "I'm So Hood."

In 2008 he founded We the Best Music Group, and in 2009 he was named president of Def Jam South. Albums such as *Victory* (2010), which contained "All I Do Is Win," and *We the Best Forever* (2011) kept him on the charts through the early 2010s.

His visibility surged in 2015 when his Snapchat videos, filled with motivational sayings such as "major key" and "another one," went viral. A December 2015 episode in which he became lost at sea on a jet ski drew wide attention.

## Key Achievements

- *Major Key* (2016) became his first Billboard 200 number one, followed by *Grateful* (2017), *Khaled Khaled* (2021), and *God Did* (2022).
- "I'm the One" (2017), featuring Justin Bieber, Quavo, Chance the Rapper, and Lil Wayne, debuted at number one on the Hot 100. "Wild Thoughts" with Rihanna and Bryson Tiller peaked at number two.
- "Higher" won the Grammy for Best Rap/Sung Performance in January 2020.
- His 2016 book *The Keys* became a New York Times bestseller.

## Controversies & Criticism

In November 2018 the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission charged Khaled with failing to disclose payments for promoting the Centra Tech cryptocurrency offering. He settled without admitting wrongdoing and paid over $150,000 in penalties and disgorgement.

In May 2018, resurfaced interview comments in which he said he would not perform oral sex on his wife prompted widespread criticism. In 2019 he publicly objected when *Father of Asahd* debuted at number two behind Tyler, the Creator's *Igor*; Billboard reportedly excluded some of Khaled's album bundles from its count.

Critics have also questioned the "producer" label, since he typically serves as an executive producer and curator rather than composing the music himself.

## Legacy & Influence

Khaled helped define the modern hip-hop compilation album as a vehicle for cross-label collaboration. He demonstrated how personal branding and social media could sustain a career built on curation, an approach many later artists and executives adopted. His sons Asahd and Aalam, born in 2016 and 2020, have appeared as credited executive producers on his albums.
