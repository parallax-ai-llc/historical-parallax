## Summary

Anathan "ana" Pham (born 29 June 1999) is an Australian professional Dota 2 player best known as the carry for OG during the team's back-to-back victories at The International in 2018 and 2019. Those two wins made OG the first organisation to win consecutive Internationals, and made ana one of the highest-earning esports players in history while still in his early twenties.

Ana's career is unusual for its pattern of departures and returns. He stepped away from competitive play several times, each time returning to OG shortly before a major campaign. Despite limited time on active rosters, his tournament record includes two Internationals and two Valve Majors, a haul few players of any era can match.

His play is remembered for aggressive, high-risk hero choices, most famously carry Io in the TI9 grand final, and for a willingness to prioritise team fights over conventional farming patterns.

## Early Life & Background

Ana was born in Melbourne, Australia, to a family of Vietnamese heritage. He began playing Dota 2 as a teenager and competed in Australian and Southeast Asian amateur circuits, where his mechanical skill drew attention despite the region's small professional scene.

At 16 he left Australia to pursue Dota 2 full time in China, a rare path for a Western player. He spent early 2016 with Invictus Gaming's secondary roster, iG.Vitality, gaining experience against Chinese teams before returning to the Western scene.

## Rise to Prominence

In September 2016, OG signed ana as its carry following a post-TI6 roster rebuild. The team won the Boston Major in December 2016 and the Kiev Major in April 2017, giving ana two Valve Major titles within his first year on the roster. OG's run at The International 2017 ended in a 7th–8th place finish, after which ana left the team and took a break from competition.

He rejoined OG in June 2018 during a turbulent period in which two players had departed for Evil Geniuses. The reformed roster of ana, Topson, N0tail, Ceb and JerAx qualified for The International 2018 through the open qualifiers and won the event in Vancouver, defeating PSG.LGD 3–2 in the grand final.

Ana again stepped away in early 2019 before returning in March. OG then won The International 2019 in Shanghai, beating Team Liquid 3–0. He went inactive in 2020, returned for The International 2021, where OG finished 7th–8th, and then left the organisation. In 2022 he briefly played for T1 in the Southeast Asian region before again withdrawing from active competition.

## Key Achievements

- **Two-time International champion**: TI8 (2018) and TI9 (2019) with OG, the first back-to-back winner in the tournament's history.
- **Two Valve Major titles**: Boston Major (2016) and Kiev Major (2017).
- **Prize earnings**: roughly $6 million in career winnings, placing him among the top earners in esports at the time of TI9.
- **Iconic performances**: Spectre and Ember Spirit at TI8, and Io as a carry in the TI9 grand final, a pick widely credited with breaking Team Liquid's strategy.

## Controversies & Criticism

Ana's repeated departures drew criticism from fans and analysts who questioned his commitment and OG's reliance on a player who was often inactive. Some observers argued his absences destabilised the roster between Internationals and contributed to OG's weaker results in regular-season events.

His greedy, fight-oriented style was also debated. Critics viewed it as inconsistent, while supporters credited it with OG's ability to swing games from losing positions. His limited public presence and infrequent interviews left much of his decision-making unexplained.

## Legacy & Influence

Ana is regarded as one of the most successful carries in Dota 2 history by results, and his TI9 Io pick remains a reference point for unconventional hero drafting. His path from Australia through China to a top European roster demonstrated that players from minor regions could reach the game's highest level.
