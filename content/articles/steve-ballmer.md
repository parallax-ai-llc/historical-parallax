## Summary

Steven Anthony Ballmer (born March 24, 1956) is an American businessman who served as chief executive officer of Microsoft from January 2000 to February 2014. He joined the company in 1980 as its 30th employee and first business manager, and over 34 years he rose from running operations and sales to leading the entire corporation during a period of enormous growth in revenue and profit.

Since leaving Microsoft, Ballmer has been best known as the owner of the Los Angeles Clippers of the National Basketball Association (NBA), which he bought in 2014 for $2 billion, then a record price for an NBA franchise. He is also a prominent philanthropist and the founder of USAFacts, a nonpartisan initiative that publishes government data. His large retained stake in Microsoft has made him one of the wealthiest people in the world.

Ballmer's tenure is debated: he presided over massive expansion in Windows, Office, and enterprise software while being criticized for missing the shift to mobile and search. He remains a defining figure in the history of the personal computer industry.

## Early Life & Background

Ballmer was born in Detroit, Michigan, and grew up in the suburb of Farmington Hills. His father, Frederic Ballmer, was a Swiss-born manager at Ford Motor Company, and his mother, Beatrice Dworkin, was of Belarusian Jewish descent. He attended Detroit Country Day School, where he excelled in mathematics, and graduated from Harvard College in 1977 with a bachelor's degree in applied mathematics and economics. At Harvard he managed the football team and lived down the hall from Bill Gates. After two years as an assistant product manager at Procter & Gamble, he enrolled at Stanford Graduate School of Business but dropped out in 1980 when Gates recruited him to Microsoft.

## Rise to Prominence

Ballmer joined Microsoft on June 11, 1980, negotiating a salary of $50,000 plus a share of ownership. He led the company's crucial early deals with IBM around MS-DOS, and later headed the operating systems division, sales and support, and then the entire sales organization. He became executive vice president in 1992, president in July 1998, and succeeded Gates as CEO on January 13, 2000. As CEO he oversaw the launches of Windows XP (2001), Xbox (2001), Windows 7 (2009), Azure (2010), and the acquisition of Skype for $8.5 billion in 2011. He announced his retirement in August 2013 and handed the role to Satya Nadella on February 4, 2014.

## Key Achievements

Under Ballmer, Microsoft's annual revenue grew from roughly $25 billion in fiscal 2000 to about $78 billion in fiscal 2013, and net income more than doubled. He built the enterprise and server business, including SQL Server, Exchange, and SharePoint, into a multibillion-dollar division. In August 2014 he completed the purchase of the Clippers, and the team opened the privately financed Intuit Dome in Inglewood in 2024. He and his wife Connie founded the Ballmer Group, which has committed billions to economic mobility programs, and in 2017 he launched USAFacts. Forbes has ranked him among the ten richest people in the world.

## Controversies & Criticism

Critics argue Ballmer failed to anticipate the smartphone era, famously dismissing the iPhone in 2007 as unlikely to gain significant market share. Windows Vista (2007) and Windows 8 (2012) were poorly received, the Zune media player failed against the iPod, and the $7.2 billion acquisition of Nokia's handset business in 2013 was largely written off in 2015. Microsoft's stock price stagnated for most of his tenure despite rising profits. His combative style, including the 2008 push to acquire Yahoo, drew mixed reactions. Internally, the "stack ranking" employee-review system was blamed for stifling collaboration until it was abolished in 2013.

## Legacy & Influence

Ballmer transformed Microsoft into a diversified enterprise software giant whose cloud and server foundations enabled its resurgence under Nadella. His energetic stage presence made him one of technology's most recognizable executives. In sports, his ownership has reshaped the Clippers' identity and set new benchmarks for franchise valuations, while USAFacts has become a widely cited resource for nonpartisan public data.
