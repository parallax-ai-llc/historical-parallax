## Summary

Timbaland, born Timothy Zachary Mosley on March 10, 1972, is an American record producer, rapper, and songwriter whose stuttering, syncopated rhythms reshaped hip-hop and R&B in the late 1990s and mainstream pop in the 2000s. His early work with Aaliyah, Missy Elliott, and Ginuwine established a sound built on off-kilter drum programming, beatboxed textures, and unexpected samples, from baby coos to Middle Eastern strings.

His mid-2000s run with Justin Timberlake and Nelly Furtado produced some of the decade's biggest hits and cemented him as one of the most commercially successful producers of his generation. He has won multiple Grammy Awards, founded the label Mosley Music Group, and co-created the Verzuz battle series, while also drawing criticism over sampling disputes, credit questions, and his more recent embrace of AI-generated music.

## Early Life & Background

Mosley was born in Norfolk, Virginia, and raised in the Virginia Beach area. As a teenager he DJed under the name DJ Timmy Tim and attended Salem High School, where he formed a group with classmates Pharrell Williams and Melvin "Magoo" Barcliff. A 1986 accidental shooting at a Red Lobster where he worked left him partially paralyzed in his right arm for nearly a year, after which he taught himself to make beats with his left hand.

In the early 1990s he joined DeVante Swing's Swing Mob collective, known as "Da Bassment," alongside Missy Elliott, Ginuwine, Static Major, and Playa. DeVante gave him the name Timbaland, a reference to the Timberland boot brand.

## Rise to Prominence

Timbaland's breakthrough came in 1996 with Ginuwine's "Pony" and Aaliwah's *One in a Million*, both of which showcased his skittering, unconventional drum patterns. Missy Elliott's *Supa Dupa Fly* (1997), which he produced entirely, went platinum and confirmed him as a defining voice of the era. As a recording duo, Timbaland & Magoo released *Welcome to Our World* in 1997, with the single "Up Jumps da Boogie" reaching the Billboard Hot 100 top 20.

Aaliyah's "Are You That Somebody?" (1998) and "Try Again" (2000) and Jay-Z's "Big Pimpin'" (2000) followed. His work on Justin Timberlake's *Justified* (2002), including "Cry Me a River," began a long partnership, and the 2006 albums *FutureSex/LoveSounds* and Nelly Furtado's *Loose* made him the dominant pop producer of the period.

## Key Achievements

- Produced or co-produced Hot 100 number ones including "SexyBack," "My Love," "What Goes Around... Comes Around," "Promiscuous," and his own "Give It to Me."
- His solo album *Shock Value* (2007) debuted at number five on the Billboard 200 and produced the hits "The Way I Are" and the OneRepublic remix "Apologize."
- Won Grammy Awards including Best Dance Recording for "SexyBack" (2007) and "LoveStoned/I Think She Knows" (2008).
- Founded Mosley Music Group in 2006 in partnership with Interscope, signing Nelly Furtado, OneRepublic, and Keri Hilson.
- Produced on Jay-Z's *Magna Carta Holy Grail* and Timberlake's *The 20/20 Experience* (both 2013), and served as an executive music producer on the Fox series *Empire* (2015–2020).
- Co-founded Verzuz with Swizz Beatz in March 2020; the series was sold to Triller in 2021 and the pair later regained control after litigation.

## Controversies & Criticism

In 2007 Timbaland was accused of using a Commodore 64 remix of Finnish musician Janne Suni's "Acidjazzed Evening" in Nelly Furtado's "Do It" without permission. A related U.S. suit was dismissed in 2011, but the episode became a widely cited case in debates over sampling ethics. The heirs of Egyptian composer Baligh Hamdi sued over the "Khosara Khosara" sample in "Big Pimpin'"; a federal judge ruled for Timbaland and Jay-Z in 2015.

Critics have also argued that collaborator Nate "Danja" Hills received insufficient public credit for the *FutureSex/LoveSounds* and *Loose* sound. In 2023 Timbaland apologized after saying Justin Timberlake should have "put a muzzle" on Britney Spears. His 2025 launch of Stage Zero, an AI entertainment company fronted by the AI artist "TaTa Taylor," prompted backlash from musicians, as did allegations that he used an independent artist's AI-generated track without consent. Timbaland has also spoken openly about a past opioid addiction that he said nearly killed him around 2011.

## Legacy & Influence

Timbaland's rhythmic vocabulary, built on syncopation, silence, and vocal percussion, is audible across two decades of pop, hip-hop, and R&B, from Pharrell and Kanye West to producers of the 2010s and 2020s. His Aaliyah and Missy Elliott records remain reference points for futuristic R&B, and his 2006–2007 run stands among the most commercially concentrated in modern production history. Through Verzuz and Beatclub he has also shaped how producers and audiences engage with music online.
