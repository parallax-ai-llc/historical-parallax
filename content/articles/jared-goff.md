## Summary

Jared Thomas Goff (born October 14, 1994) is an American football quarterback for the Detroit Lions of the National Football League (NFL). Selected first overall in the 2016 NFL Draft by the Los Angeles Rams, he led the Rams to Super Bowl LIII in the 2018 season and was traded to Detroit in 2021 in the deal that sent Matthew Stafford to Los Angeles.

Initially viewed as a salary-matching throw-in to that trade, Goff instead became the centerpiece of the Lions' turnaround. He quarterbacked Detroit to its first division title in 30 years in 2023, its first playoff win since the 1991 season, and a franchise-record 15 wins in 2024. He has been named to the Pro Bowl four times (2017, 2018, 2022, 2024) and holds numerous Lions passing records.

Goff's career is often cited as a case study in quarterback rehabilitation, showing how a player written off by one franchise can thrive in a different system with strong protection and coaching.

## Early Life & Background

Goff was born in Novato, California, the son of Jerry Goff, a former Major League Baseball catcher who played for the Montreal Expos, Pittsburgh Pirates, and Houston Astros. He attended Marin Catholic High School in Kentfield, where he threw for more than 7,600 yards and 93 touchdowns and led the team to a 13-1 record and a state final appearance as a senior.

He enrolled at the University of California, Berkeley, in 2013 and became the first true freshman in program history to start at quarterback in a season opener. Over three seasons under coach Sonny Dykes, he set Cal career records for passing yards (12,195) and touchdowns (96). In 2015 he threw for 4,719 yards and 43 touchdowns, both Pac-12 single-season records at the time, and earned first-team All-Pac-12 honors.

## Rise to Prominence

The Rams traded up from the 15th pick to select Goff first overall in April 2016, giving up a package of six draft picks to the Tennessee Titans. His rookie season was difficult: he started the final seven games under coach Jeff Fisher and lost all of them.

The hiring of Sean McVay in 2017 transformed his career. Goff threw for 3,804 yards and 28 touchdowns as the Rams went 11-5 and won the NFC West. In 2018 he passed for 4,688 yards and 32 touchdowns, and the Rams reached Super Bowl LIII, losing 13-3 to the New England Patriots. In September 2019 he signed a four-year, $134 million extension with $110 million guaranteed, then the largest guarantee in NFL history.

In January 2021, after McVay's confidence in him visibly cooled, the Rams sent Goff, two first-round picks, and a third-round pick to Detroit for Stafford. The Lions went 3-13-1 in his first season, but Goff steadily improved under coach Dan Campbell and offensive coordinator Ben Johnson. He earned a Pro Bowl selection in 2022 and signed a four-year, $212 million extension with $170 million guaranteed in May 2024.

## Key Achievements

- Led the Lions to a 12-5 record and the NFC North title in 2023, the franchise's first division championship since 1993, and reached the NFC Championship Game.
- Guided Detroit to a 15-2 record and the NFC's top seed in 2024, throwing for 4,629 yards, 37 touchdowns, and a 72.4 percent completion rate.
- Posted a perfect 158.3 passer rating against Jacksonville in November 2024, going 18 of 25 for 412 yards and four touchdowns.
- Set a Rams franchise record with 517 passing yards against Tampa Bay in September 2019.
- First overall draft pick, NFC champion (2018), and four-time Pro Bowler.

## Controversies & Criticism

Goff has faced persistent criticism for playoff performance. He managed just 229 passing yards and a critical late interception in Super Bowl LIII, and in the 2024 divisional round he committed multiple turnovers, including three interceptions, in a 45-31 upset loss to Washington that ended Detroit's best season. Critics also point to his reliance on structure, arguing he has struggled when protection breaks down. His high fumble totals in Los Angeles drew scrutiny, and the Rams' willingness to attach two first-round picks to move his contract was widely read as a public verdict on his value. Supporters counter that his production in Detroit refuted much of that narrative.

## Legacy & Influence

Goff's revival in Detroit reshaped conversations about quarterback evaluation, coaching fit, and the risk of prematurely discarding young passers. He became the face of a Lions franchise long associated with failure, and his name remains a rallying cry at Ford Field. He married model Christen Harper in June 2024, and the couple welcomed a daughter in 2025.
