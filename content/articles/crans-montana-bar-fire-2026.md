## Summary

Shortly after 1:30 am on 1 January 2026, fire swept through Le Constellation, a two-level bar in the Swiss ski resort of Crans-Montana in the canton of Valais. Revellers were celebrating the New Year inside [^1][^2]. Forty-one people died, most of them teenagers and young adults, and about 115 were injured, many with severe burns [^1][^3]. Investigators believe sparklers on champagne bottles were held too close to the basement ceiling and ignited sound-insulating foam [^2][^3]. The disaster led to criminal investigations of the bar's owners and local officials, a nationwide ban on indoor pyrotechnics, and a national debate about fire-safety enforcement in Switzerland [^1][^4].

## Background

Le Constellation could hold about 300 people on a ground floor and a basement, plus a terrace. It drew a mostly young, local clientele [^1]. A French couple, Jacques and Jessica Moretti, bought and remodelled the bar in 2015, installing acoustic foam on the ceiling [^1]. The municipality, which is responsible for fire inspections, later admitted that the bar had not been inspected since 2019, even though inspections are required every year [^1][^2].

## The Event

A photograph published by French media shows a staff member being carried on another's shoulders while holding champagne bottles fitted with lit sparklers. The sparks were centimetres from the basement ceiling, which caught fire [^1]. The blaze became a flashover within seconds, and people in the basement struggled to escape up a narrow staircase and through a small door [^1]. Police were alerted at 1:30 am and deployed 150 personnel, 10 helicopters, and 40 ambulances. The Valais government declared a state of emergency [^1]. Intensive care units in Valais filled up, and 24 patients were flown to hospitals in Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, and Poland under the EU Civil Protection Mechanism [^1]. The victims ranged in age from 14 to 39, and half were under 18. An 18-year-old Swiss victim died in a Zurich hospital at the end of January, bringing the toll to 41 [^1][^2].

## Response and Aftermath

Valais Attorney General Beatrice Pilloud said the evidence pointed to sparkling candles on champagne bottles moved too close to the ceiling [^1]. Prosecutors opened a criminal investigation against the Morettis on suspicion of negligent homicide, negligent bodily harm, and negligent arson [^2]. Jacques Moretti was placed in pretrial detention in January and released on CHF 200,000 bail on 23 January [^1][^2]. Switzerland held a national day of mourning on 9 January. French President Emmanuel Macron and Italian President Sergio Mattarella attended the memorial ceremony in Martigny [^1]. Valais set up a victims' fund, and the Federal Council proposed a CHF 50,000 solidarity payment for each victim or bereaved family [^1].

## Positive Perspectives

- Emergency services responded within minutes. Swiss burn centres and hospitals in five other European countries treated the injured [^1].
- Cantonal building directors adopted a nationwide ban on pyrotechnics in publicly accessible indoor spaces, which took effect on 1 April 2026 [^4].
- Prosecutors widened the investigation beyond the owners to current and former municipal safety officials. A victim's lawyers also filed a criminal complaint against the mayor [^1][^3].
- Valais, the municipality, and the federal government created funds and payments for victims, along with a mechanism to encourage out-of-court settlements [^1].

## Negative Perspectives

- Crans-Montana had inspected only 40 of its 128 bars and restaurants in 2025. An internal audit from 2023 had already flagged dysfunction in the inspection service [^1].
- NZZ am Sonntag reported that investigators seized the owners' phones only eight days after the fire, initially failed to order autopsies, and apparently never searched the owners' home [^5].
- Footage from about 250 public cameras was deleted under a seven-day retention rule before prosecutors requested it [^1].
- Italy criticised the release of Jacques Moretti on bail, and later criticised hospital bills sent to Italian victims. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni called the bills an "insult" [^1].

## Recent News

In mid-April 2026 Valais prosecutors added four more officials to the investigation, bringing the number of people under investigation to 13. The newly added officials include Crans-Montana's councillor responsible for security, a former security councillor, and the current deputy head of public safety [^3]. At that time 38 patients were still in hospitals or rehabilitation clinics [^3]. Swiss authorities agreed to a joint investigation team with Italian prosecutors [^1]. A broader revision of national fire-safety rules, taking the findings into account, is expected in autumn 2027 [^4].
