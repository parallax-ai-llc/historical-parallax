## Summary

On 28 March 2025 at 12:50 local time, a magnitude 7.7 to 7.9 earthquake struck the Sagaing Region of Myanmar near Mandalay, the country's second-largest city [^1]. It was the most powerful earthquake to hit Myanmar since 1912 and killed about 5,456 people, including up to 5,352 in Myanmar and 103 in Thailand, with more than 11,000 injured [^1]. The disaster struck a country already torn by civil war since the 2021 military coup, complicating relief and the reporting of casualties [^1][^2]. In Bangkok, about 1,000 km away, a skyscraper under construction collapsed and became the focus of a major criminal case [^1][^3].

## Background

Myanmar sits between the Indian, Eurasian, Sunda and Burma plates, and the 1,400 km Sagaing Fault, a strike-slip boundary, runs close to Yangon, Naypyidaw and Mandalay [^1]. The fault had produced several large earthquakes in the twentieth century, but the segment between Mandalay and Naypyidaw had not had a major rupture in modern records [^1]. Bangkok's soft clay amplifies long-period shaking from distant earthquakes, making its high-rises vulnerable [^1].

## The Event

The rupture ran about 500 km along the Sagaing Fault and was one of the longest sustained supershear ruptures ever recorded, followed minutes later by a magnitude 6.7 aftershock [^1][^4]. In Mandalay Region, about half of all buildings were severely damaged or destroyed and 3,325 people died, including 207 in the collapse of the Sky Villa condominium [^1]. In Sagaing city about 90 percent of structures were destroyed and the historic Ava Bridge largely collapsed [^1]. In the capital Naypyidaw, 665 deaths were confirmed and the air traffic control tower collapsed [^1]. In Bangkok, the 33-storey State Audit Office tower under construction collapsed, killing 96 people [^3].

## Response and Aftermath

Junta leader Min Aung Hlaing made a rare appeal for international aid, a departure from previous military governments that had refused foreign help after disasters [^4]. Rescue teams came mainly from China, India and other ASEAN states, with India's air force delivering supplies under Operation Brahma [^1][^4]. On 2 April the military announced a 20-day ceasefire to support relief, later extended to 30 April [^2]. In Thailand, investigators probed allegations that substandard steel was used in the collapsed tower, and in August 2025 prosecutors indicted 23 people, including former Italian-Thai Development president Premchai Karnasuta and a China Railway No. 10 executive, for negligence and manslaughter [^3][^5].

## Positive Perspectives

- The junta's appeal for foreign aid opened the country to rescue teams that previous regimes had turned away [^4].
- China, India and ASEAN states quickly sent rescuers and supplies, with India's Operation Brahma airlifting aid [^1][^4].
- The military's announced ceasefire, extended after talks led by Malaysia's prime minister, was intended to give relief workers access [^2].
- Thai authorities moved quickly to investigate the Bangkok collapse, issuing arrest warrants and bringing indictments within months [^3].
- Scientific study of the long supershear rupture is improving understanding of a poorly researched fault system [^1].

## Negative Perspectives

- UN data analysed by Reuters showed the military carried out more than 172 attacks after the ceasefire announcement, 73 of them in quake-affected areas [^2].
- A UN human rights official said it was "business as usual" and that the military had not been repurposed for relief [^2].
- Wikipedia's summary notes that the civil war made disaster relief and information gathering more difficult, leaving casualty figures uncertain [^1].
- The Bangkok tower collapse raised allegations that steel from a previously shut-down factory had been used in its construction [^5].
- The destruction of the Ava Bridge and other infrastructure slowed relief to Sagaing and surrounding areas [^1].

## Recent News

As of 2026 the criminal case over the Bangkok tower collapse remained pending, and after a separate rail accident in January 2026 involving the same contractors, Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul ordered several Italian-Thai Development contracts cancelled [^3].
