## Summary

Nikol Pashinyan (born 1 June 1975) is an Armenian politician and former journalist who has served as Prime Minister of Armenia since 8 May 2018. He came to power through the peaceful street protests of April–May 2018, known as the Velvet Revolution, which ended a decade of rule by Serzh Sargsyan and the Republican Party.

His tenure has been defined by two contrasting arcs: an early wave of anti-corruption reform and democratic optimism, followed by the military defeat in the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war and the loss of the enclave entirely in 2023. Since then he has pursued a peace treaty with Azerbaijan, a pivot away from Russia toward the European Union, and an increasingly confrontational domestic agenda, making him the most consequential and polarising Armenian leader since independence.

## Early Life & Background

Pashinyan was born in Ijevan, in the Tavush region of Soviet Armenia. He enrolled in the journalism department of Yerevan State University in 1991 but was expelled in 1995, shortly before graduation, for his political writing and activism.

He built his career in opposition media. In 1998 he founded the newspaper *Oragir*, which was shut down by court order in 1999. He then launched *Haykakan Zhamanak* (Armenian Times) in 1999 and served as its editor-in-chief until 2012, using it as a platform for sharp criticism of successive governments.

## Rise to Prominence

In 2008 Pashinyan campaigned for opposition candidate Levon Ter-Petrosyan. After the disputed presidential election, clashes on 1 March 2008 left 10 people dead. Pashinyan went into hiding, surrendered in July 2009, and was sentenced in January 2010 to seven years in prison. He was released under an amnesty in May 2011.

He entered the National Assembly in 2012 on the Armenian National Congress list, broke away to found the Civil Contract movement in 2013 (registered as a party in 2015), and was re-elected in 2017 with the Way Out Alliance.

When Sargsyan, barred from a third presidential term, had himself elected prime minister on 17 April 2018, Pashinyan led a protest march from Gyumri to Yerevan. Mass civil disobedience forced Sargsyan to resign on 23 April. Parliament elected Pashinyan prime minister on 8 May 2018, and his My Step Alliance won 70.4% of the vote in snap elections on 9 December 2018.

## Key Achievements

- **Peaceful transfer of power**: The 2018 revolution removed an entrenched government without bloodshed and was followed by anti-corruption prosecutions of former officials.
- **Electoral resilience**: Despite the 2020 defeat, Civil Contract won 53.9% and 71 of 107 seats in the 20 June 2021 snap election.
- **Peace process**: On 8 August 2025 Pashinyan and Ilham Aliyev initialled a peace agreement in Washington, including a transit corridor through Syunik branded the Trump Route for International Peace and Prosperity.
- **Western realignment**: Armenia joined the International Criminal Court in February 2024, froze participation in the Russian-led Collective Security Treaty Organization the same month, hosted an EU monitoring mission from 2023, and passed a law on EU accession in March 2025.

## Controversies & Criticism

The 44-day war of 27 September to 10 November 2020 ended in a Russian-brokered ceasefire that ceded large territories. Thousands of Armenian soldiers died, and critics accused Pashinyan of misjudging Azerbaijan's intentions and mismanaging diplomacy. In September 2023 an Azerbaijani offensive drove more than 100,000 ethnic Armenians out of Nagorno-Karabakh after he had formally recognised the region as Azerbaijani territory.

The 2024 return of four border villages in Tavush triggered protests led by Archbishop Bagrat Galstanyan. In 2025 Pashinyan openly demanded the resignation of Catholicos Karekin II, and prosecutors arrested Galstanyan and billionaire Samvel Karapetyan on coup-plot and incitement charges, moves opponents described as political persecution. The 7 June 2026 parliamentary election was widely framed as a referendum on his peace agenda.

## Legacy & Influence

Pashinyan transformed Armenia from a Russia-dependent semi-authoritarian state into a competitive democracy oriented toward Europe, while presiding over the greatest territorial loss in its modern history. Whether he is remembered as the leader who secured a durable peace or the one who surrendered Karabakh remains the central question in Armenian politics.
