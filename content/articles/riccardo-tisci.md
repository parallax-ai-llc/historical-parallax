## Summary

Riccardo Tisci is an Italian fashion designer best known for his twelve-year tenure as creative director of the French house Givenchy (2005–2017) and his subsequent role as chief creative officer of Burberry (2018–2022). He is widely credited with fusing gothic romanticism, Catholic iconography, sportswear and streetwear into a single luxury vocabulary that reshaped how heritage houses spoke to younger audiences in the 2010s.

Tisci's influence extends beyond the runway. His Rottweiler and Bambi prints turned Givenchy into a streetwear reference point, his Nike collaborations helped normalise designer sneakers, and his close relationships with performers such as Madonna, Beyoncé and Kanye West made him one of the first designers of his generation to operate at the intersection of high fashion and pop culture.

## Early Life & Background

Tisci was born on 8 August 1974 in Taranto, in the Apulia region of southern Italy, and grew up near Como in Lombardy. He was the youngest of nine children and the only son; his father died when he was four, leaving his mother and eight sisters to raise him in modest circumstances. He has repeatedly cited this household of women as the source of his fascination with femininity, strength and religious imagery.

He studied at the Istituto d'Arte Applicata in Cantù before moving to London at 17 to attend Central Saint Martins College of Art and Design, graduating in 1999. In the years that followed he worked for Puma, the Italian labels Coccapani and Ruffo Research, and the designer Antonio Berardi.

## Rise to Prominence

Tisci launched an eponymous label in 2004, presenting a dark, draped collection during Milan Fashion Week that attracted immediate press attention. In February 2005 LVMH appointed him creative director of Givenchy women's ready-to-wear and haute couture, a house that had cycled through John Galliano, Alexander McQueen and Julien Macdonald in the previous decade. Menswear and accessories were added to his remit in 2008.

His early Givenchy collections were criticised as too sombre, but by 2010 he had established a distinctive signature: sharp tailoring, sheer panels, religious and animal motifs, and a diverse casting that was unusual for Paris at the time. In 2010 he cast the transgender model Lea T in a Givenchy campaign, and his Fall 2011 Rottweiler print and 2013 Bambi sweatshirt became defining luxury-streetwear objects. Givenchy's revenues reportedly grew several-fold during his tenure. He departed in January 2017 and was succeeded by Clare Waight Keller.

In March 2018 Burberry named Tisci chief creative officer. His debut show in September 2018, titled "Kingdom", introduced a new Thomas Burberry monogram and a sans-serif logo designed with Peter Saville, along with "B Series" monthly product drops sold through social media. He left Burberry in September 2022 and was replaced by Daniel Lee.

## Key Achievements

- Restored Givenchy to commercial and critical relevance over 12 years, including its haute couture line.
- Designed costumes for Madonna's Sticky & Sweet Tour (2008) and her 2012 Super Bowl halftime performance, and stage wardrobe for Beyoncé's Mrs. Carter Show World Tour (2013).
- Created the artwork for Jay-Z and Kanye West's 2011 album *Watch the Throne*.
- Collaborated with Nike from 2014, reinterpreting the Air Force 1 and other models under the NikeLab banner.
- Designed Kim Kardashian's wedding dress in 2014.
- Rebranded Burberry in 2018, its first logo change in roughly two decades.

## Controversies & Criticism

In February 2019 a Burberry hoodie shown at the "Tempest" collection featured a drawstring knotted like a noose. Model Liz Kennedy publicly objected, and Tisci and CEO Marco Gobbetti apologised and withdrew the piece. His Fall 2015 Givenchy show, styled with "chola"-inspired baby hair and facial piercings, drew accusations of cultural appropriation. Some critics also argued that his streetwear-heavy direction at Burberry diluted the brand's British heritage, and sales growth under his tenure was modest relative to competitors.

## Legacy & Influence

Tisci helped make sneakers, sweatshirts and graphic prints legitimate luxury categories, paving the way for designers such as Virgil Abloh and Demna. His inclusive casting and celebrity partnerships established a template for how heritage houses engage with music and popular culture. His Rottweiler print remains one of the most recognisable fashion images of the 2010s.
