## Summary

Guy Ramsay Fieri (born Guy Ramsay Ferry, January 22, 1968) is an American restaurateur, author, and television host best known for the Food Network series *Diners, Drive-Ins and Dives*, which has aired since 2007. With his bleached spiked hair, goatee, and flame-print shirts, he became one of the most recognizable food personalities in the United States and a fixture of American pop culture.

Fieri rose from a contest win on *The Next Food Network Star* in 2006 to become the network's highest-paid host, with a 2021 contract reported at roughly $80 million over three years and a 2023 renewal reported at about $100 million. His shows, restaurants, cookbooks, and licensed products built a brand centered on accessible, indulgent, working-class American food. Critics have long dismissed his style as loud and unrefined, but his championing of independent restaurants and his charitable work during the COVID-19 pandemic reshaped his public image.

## Early Life & Background

Fieri was born in Columbus, Ohio, and raised in Ferndale, a small town in Humboldt County, California, by parents Jim and Penny Ferry. At age 10 he began selling soft pretzels from a three-wheeled cart called "The Awesome Pretzel," saving money for a trip to France. At 16 he spent a year as an exchange student in Chantilly, France, an experience he credits with shaping his interest in food.

He graduated from the University of Nevada, Las Vegas in 1990 with a bachelor's degree in hotel management. Early jobs included a management role at Stouffer's in Long Beach and district manager at the Louise's Trattoria chain. In 1995 he married Lori Brisson and legally changed his surname to Fieri, restoring the original Italian spelling used by his grandfather. The couple have two sons, Hunter (born 1996) and Ryder (born 2005).

## Rise to Prominence

In 1996 Fieri and business partner Steve Gruber opened Johnny Garlic's in Santa Rosa, California, followed by additional locations and the sushi-barbecue concept Tex Wasabi's in 2003. Friends persuaded him to submit an audition tape to Food Network, and in April 2006 he won the second season of *The Next Food Network Star*.

His first series, *Guy's Big Bite*, premiered in June 2006. *Diners, Drive-Ins and Dives* began as a special in November 2006 and became a regular series in April 2007, taking Fieri across the country in a red 1968 Chevrolet Camaro to profile independent eateries. The show's popularity led to further series including *Guy's Grocery Games* (2013), *Guy's Ranch Kitchen*, and *Tournament of Champions* (2020). He also hosted the NBC game show *Minute to Win It* from 2010 to 2011.

## Key Achievements

- *Diners, Drive-Ins and Dives* has produced more than 40 seasons and visited well over 1,000 restaurants, many of which report lasting sales increases after appearing.
- His 2008 book *Diners, Drive-Ins and Dives: An All-American Road Trip* reached the New York Times bestseller list, followed by several more cookbooks.
- He received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in May 2019, one of only a handful of chefs so honored.
- In 2020 he helped raise more than $21 million for the Restaurant Employee Relief Fund, assisting over 40,000 restaurant workers during the pandemic.
- His restaurant portfolio includes Guy Fieri's Vegas Kitchen & Bar (2014), the Chicken Guy! chain (2018), and the Flavortown Kitchen delivery brand (2020). In 2019 he co-founded Santo Spirits with musician Sammy Hagar.
- In 2015 he officiated a mass wedding of 101 same-sex couples in Miami.

## Controversies & Criticism

Fieri's brash persona made him a frequent target of food critics. The most famous example was Pete Wells's November 2012 New York Times review of Guy's American Kitchen & Bar in Times Square, written entirely as a series of mocking questions and awarding zero stars. The 500-seat restaurant nonetheless operated until December 2017. Anthony Bourdain repeatedly ridiculed him on stage and in print.

In 2011 the producer of *Diners, Drive-Ins and Dives*, David Page, was removed from the show amid a legal dispute in which Page alleged mistreatment and Fieri's side alleged abusive behavior on set. The matter was settled privately. Health advocates have also criticized his shows for celebrating high-calorie food.

## Legacy & Influence

Fieri turned the roadside diner into television gold and gave national exposure to hundreds of family-owned restaurants, a phenomenon sometimes called the "Triple D effect." His outsized image, including the invented "Flavortown," became a durable internet meme, yet his pandemic fundraising and consistent support for small businesses earned him a critical reappraisal. He remains one of the longest-serving and most commercially successful hosts in Food Network history.
