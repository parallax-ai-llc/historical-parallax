## Summary

Gastón Acurio Jaramillo is a Peruvian chef, restaurateur, author and television presenter who is widely credited as the leading figure behind the international rise of Peruvian cuisine. Beginning with a single restaurant in Lima in 1994, he built a group that at its peak operated more than 40 restaurants across roughly a dozen countries, and he reframed cooking in Peru as a matter of national identity and economic development rather than a private trade.

His flagship restaurant, Astrid & Gastón, was ranked among the World's 50 Best Restaurants and was named the best restaurant in Latin America in 2013. Beyond the kitchen, Acurio co-founded the Peruvian Gastronomy Society (APEGA) and the Mistura food festival, and established a tuition-free culinary school for low-income students. He is frequently described in Peru as a culinary ambassador, and his public profile has at times prompted speculation about a political career.

## Early Life & Background

Acurio was born on 30 October 1967 in Lima, Peru, the only son among five children of Gastón Acurio Velarde, a senator and former government minister. His family expected him to follow a legal career, and he enrolled in law at the Pontificia Universidad Católica del Perú. He later continued his studies in Madrid at the Universidad Complutense but abandoned law in favour of cooking, a decision he initially hid from his father.

He trained at a culinary school in Madrid and then at Le Cordon Bleu in Paris, where he met Astrid Gutsche, a German-born pastry student who became his wife and business partner. The couple returned to Lima in 1994.

## Rise to Prominence

In July 1994, Acurio and Gutsche opened Astrid & Gastón in the Miraflores district of Lima, originally as a French-style restaurant. Over the following years the menu shifted decisively toward Peruvian ingredients and techniques, a move that coincided with growing domestic pride in the country's food after decades of political violence and economic crisis.

The first international branch opened in Santiago, Chile, in 2000, followed by locations in Bogotá, Quito, Caracas, Mexico City, Madrid and Buenos Aires. Acurio then developed a portfolio of casual concepts, including Tanta (2003), the cebichería La Mar (2005), Panchita, Chicha in Cusco and Arequipa, and Papacho's. La Mar opened in San Francisco in 2008 and later in Miami. His television programme *Aventura Culinaria*, which toured Peru's regional kitchens, made him a household name at home.

## Key Achievements

- Astrid & Gastón reached 14th place on the World's 50 Best Restaurants list in 2013 and was ranked first on the inaugural Latin America's 50 Best Restaurants list the same year, when Acurio also received the Diners Club Lifetime Achievement Award.
- In 2014 the flagship moved to Casa Moreyra, a restored 17th-century hacienda in San Isidro.
- He co-founded APEGA in 2007 and launched Mistura in 2008, which grew into the largest food festival in Latin America, drawing hundreds of thousands of visitors annually.
- He founded the Pachacútec culinary school in Ventanilla in 2007, offering free professional training to students from Lima's poorer districts.
- His books include *500 años de fusión* (2008) and *Peru: The Cookbook* (Phaidon, 2015). He appeared in the documentaries *Perú Sabe* (2012), alongside Ferran Adrià, and *Buscando a Gastón* (2014).

## Controversies & Criticism

Acurio's rapid international expansion drew mixed reviews. The New York branch of La Mar, opened in 2009, closed within about a year after lukewarm critical reception, and several other overseas outlets were later shut or sold. Critics in Peru have argued that the "gastronomic boom" he championed mainly benefited urban elites and celebrity chefs while small farmers and rural cooks saw limited gains. Others have questioned the commercial motives behind his social initiatives. His repeated public denials of presidential ambitions in 2011 and 2015 did little to quiet debate about his political influence.

## Legacy & Influence

Acurio transformed the status of Peruvian cooking, helping make Lima a global culinary destination and inspiring a generation of chefs, including Virgilio Martínez and Pía León. His model of linking cuisine to biodiversity, national branding and social inclusion has been adopted by governments and tourism boards across Latin America. Within Peru, he is regularly ranked among the country's most trusted public figures.
