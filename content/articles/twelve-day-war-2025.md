## Summary

The Twelve-Day War was a conflict between Israel and Iran from 13 to 24 June 2025, in which the United States joined by bombing three Iranian nuclear sites [^1]. Israel opened the war with surprise strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities, military commanders and nuclear scientists, and Iran answered with hundreds of ballistic missiles and drones fired at Israel [^1]. On 22 June U.S. B-2 bombers struck Fordow, Natanz and Isfahan in Operation Midnight Hammer, and a ceasefire announced by President Donald Trump took hold on 24 June [^1][^2]. How much the strikes set back Iran's nuclear program remained disputed, and the confrontation resumed on a larger scale in 2026 [^3][^4].

## Background

Israel had long described Iran's nuclear program as an existential threat, while Iran maintained that the program was peaceful [^1]. On 12 June 2025 the International Atomic Energy Agency's board declared Iran in breach of its nuclear obligations, the day before Israel attacked [^1]. Since 2019 Iran had exceeded the limits set by the 2015 nuclear deal, and in September 2025 the IAEA reported that its enriched uranium stockpile was 48 times the deal's limit, according to the UK, France and Germany [^5].

## The Event

Israel's campaign struck the Natanz, Isfahan and Fordow sites and killed senior commanders including Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps chief Hossein Salami and armed forces chief of staff Mohammad Bagheri, along with at least 10 leading nuclear scientists [^1]. Iran's Operation True Promise III launched more than 550 ballistic missiles and more than 1,000 drones, hitting military, energy and government sites as well as apartment buildings and a hospital [^1]. On 22 June seven B-2 bombers dropped 14 GBU-57 Massive Ordnance Penetrators on Fordow and Natanz, the weapon's first combat use, while a submarine fired more than 30 Tomahawk missiles [^2]. Iran retaliated on 23 June with a missile attack on the U.S. base at Al Udeid in Qatar, and Trump announced a ceasefire that took effect on 24 June [^1].

## Response and Aftermath

Casualty figures differ by source: Iran's Foundation of Martyrs and Veterans Affairs reported 1,060 deaths, while the U.S.-based group HRANA counted 1,190 deaths, including 436 civilians and 435 military personnel [^6]. In Israel, 28 deaths were reported at the time of the ceasefire, and later counts list 32 civilians and one off-duty soldier killed, with more than 3,200 people hospitalized [^1][^6]. Trump said the nuclear sites were "obliterated," but a leaked preliminary Defense Intelligence Agency assessment said the program had been set back by only a few months; the White House called that assessment "flat-out wrong" [^3]. Iran suspended cooperation with the IAEA, which it accused of providing information to Israel [^1]. The UK, France and Germany triggered the UN "snapback" mechanism, and UN sanctions on Iran were reimposed on 27 September 2025 [^5].

## Positive Perspectives

- The United States, Germany and several other governments defended the Israeli strikes as a measure to prevent nuclear proliferation [^1].
- Operation Midnight Hammer was the largest operational B-2 strike in history and the first combat use of the GBU-57 bunker-buster [^2].
- The White House said the strikes ended Iran's ability to build a nuclear weapon [^3].
- A ceasefire was reached within 12 days, before the war spread further across the region [^1].

## Negative Perspectives

- More than 1,000 people were killed in Iran, hundreds of them civilians according to HRANA, and Iranian missiles struck homes and a hospital in Israel [^1][^6].
- The UN and most countries expressed concern, and Muslim-majority and Arab states condemned Israel's attacks [^1].
- The leaked U.S. intelligence assessment that Iran's enriched uranium had not been destroyed contradicted official claims of total destruction [^3].
- Iran's suspension of IAEA cooperation reduced international monitoring of its nuclear program [^1].
- The ceasefire did not resolve the underlying conflict, which escalated into a wider war in 2026 [^4].

## Recent News

On 28 February 2026 the United States and Israel launched hundreds of air strikes across Iran, beginning a new war that Wikipedia describes as an escalation of the Twelve-Day War; despite temporary ceasefires and an Islamabad Memorandum signed on 17 June 2026, that conflict was listed as ongoing [^4].
