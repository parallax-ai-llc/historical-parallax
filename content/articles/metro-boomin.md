## Summary

Metro Boomin, born Leland Tyler Wayne on September 16, 1993, is an American record producer, DJ, and songwriter. He is among the most commercially successful hip-hop producers of the 2010s and 2020s, known for dark, cinematic trap beats and long-running partnerships with Future, 21 Savage, and Travis Scott.

His work moved from behind-the-scenes production to headline releases. His debut solo album, *Not All Heroes Wear Capes* (2018), and later projects *Savage Mode II* (2020), *Heroes & Villains* (2022), and the Future collaborations *We Don't Trust You* and *We Still Don't Trust You* (2024) all debuted at number one on the Billboard 200.

Metro Boomin matters because he helped define the sound of Atlanta trap for a global audience while proving that a producer could function as a lead artist. His producer tag, voiced by Future, became one of the most recognizable audio signatures in modern rap.

## Early Life & Background

Wayne was born and raised in St. Louis, Missouri. He began making beats at 13 using FL Studio after briefly playing bass in a middle school band. While attending Parkway North High School, he reportedly produced several beats a day and sent them to rappers through social media.

His mother, Leslie Joanne Wayne, drove him repeatedly from St. Louis to Atlanta so he could meet artists in person. He enrolled at Morehouse College in Atlanta in 2011 to study business management but left after one semester to pursue production full time.

## Rise to Prominence

Early placements came through Atlanta's Brick Squad circle, including work with OJ da Juiceman and Gucci Mane. His breakthrough was Future's "Karate Chop" (2013), which began a long partnership that included Future's album *Honest* (2014) and the mixtape *Monster* (2014).

In 2014 he produced "Tuesday" by ILoveMakonnen featuring Drake, which reached number 12 on the Billboard Hot 100 and earned a Grammy nomination for Best Rap/Sung Collaboration. He co-produced "Jumpman" from Drake and Future's *What a Time to Be Alive* (2015), and in 2016 he produced Migos' "Bad and Boujee," his first Hot 100 number one, and Post Malone's "Congratulations."

His collaborative EP *Savage Mode* (2016) with 21 Savage established a signature partnership. He followed it with *Perfect Timing* with Nav (2017), *Without Warning* with 21 Savage and Offset (2017), and *Double or Nothing* with Big Sean (2017). In 2017 he founded his label, Boominati Worldwide, in partnership with Republic Records.

## Key Achievements

- *Not All Heroes Wear Capes* (November 2, 2018) debuted at number one on the Billboard 200 with singles "Space Cadet" and "10 Freaky Girls."
- *Savage Mode II* (October 2, 2020), narrated by Morgan Freeman, debuted at number one.
- *Heroes & Villains* (December 2, 2022) debuted at number one; "Creepin'" with The Weeknd and 21 Savage peaked at number three on the Hot 100.
- He produced and curated the *Spider-Man: Across the Spider-Verse* soundtrack (June 2, 2023).
- *We Don't Trust You* (March 22, 2024) and *We Still Don't Trust You* (April 12, 2024) with Future both debuted at number one. "Like That," featuring Kendrick Lamar, topped the Hot 100.
- He has received multiple Grammy nominations, including Producer of the Year, Non-Classical.

## Controversies & Criticism

Metro Boomin was a central figure in the 2024 rap feud between Kendrick Lamar and Drake. "Like That" contained Lamar's dismissal of Drake and J. Cole, and Drake responded on "Push Ups" and "Family Matters" with lines aimed at Metro. Metro then released the instrumental "BBL Drizzy" in May 2024 and offered a cash prize for the best verse over it, which critics called a public escalation of a personal dispute.

In October 2024 a woman filed a civil lawsuit in Los Angeles alleging that he sexually assaulted her in 2016. Metro Boomin denied the allegations through his attorney and described the suit as an extortion attempt. Some critics have also argued that his solo albums rely heavily on guest vocalists, questioning how much they function as producer-led artistic statements.

## Legacy & Influence

Metro Boomin expanded the role of the hip-hop producer into a chart-topping brand with albums, tours, and a label. His ominous synth melodies, heavy 808s, and orchestral flourishes shaped the trap sound adopted by countless younger producers. The death of his mother in June 2022, to which he dedicated *Heroes & Villains*, became a public part of his story and gave his later work a more reflective frame. By his early thirties he had a catalog of number one albums that few producers of any era have matched.
