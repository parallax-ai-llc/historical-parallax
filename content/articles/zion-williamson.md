## Summary

Zion Lateef Williamson is an American professional basketball player for the New Orleans Pelicans of the National Basketball Association (NBA). A 6-foot-6, roughly 285-pound power forward, he became one of the most hyped prospects in the sport's history while still in high school, combining unusual mass with explosive leaping ability that made his dunks a viral phenomenon.

After a single season at Duke University in which he swept the major national player of the year awards, Williamson was selected first overall by the Pelicans in the 2019 NBA draft. He has since been named an NBA All-Star twice and has posted some of the most efficient scoring numbers of any young player in league history.

His career has been defined as much by injury and durability questions as by production. He missed his entire third season with a foot fracture and has repeatedly lost long stretches to hamstring and knee problems, making him a case study in the gap between talent and availability.

## Early Life & Background

Williamson was born on July 6, 2000, in Salisbury, North Carolina, to Sharonda Sampson, a former track athlete and teacher, and Lateef Williamson, a former college football lineman. His parents divorced when he was young, and he was raised largely by his mother and stepfather, Lee Anderson, a former college basketball player who coached him from an early age. The family lived in Florence, South Carolina, before settling in Spartanburg.

He attended Spartanburg Day School, a small private school, where he averaged 36.8 points and 13.0 rebounds as a senior. He led the school to three consecutive South Carolina Independent School Association state championships from 2016 to 2018. Highlight clips of his dunks drew tens of millions of views online, and he was named a McDonald's All-American in 2018. He committed to Duke in January 2018, choosing the Blue Devils over Clemson, Kentucky, Kansas, and North Carolina.

## Rise to Prominence

At Duke in 2018–19, Williamson averaged 22.6 points, 8.9 rebounds, and 2.1 steals while shooting 68.0 percent from the field. He won the Wooden Award, the Naismith Award, and the Associated Press Player of the Year, and was named ACC Player of the Year and ACC Rookie of the Year.

On February 20, 2019, in a nationally televised game against North Carolina, his Nike shoe split open 33 seconds into the game, causing a knee sprain. The incident became a global news story, and Nike's stock fell about 1 percent the next day. He returned in the ACC Tournament and led Duke to the Elite Eight, where the team lost to Michigan State.

The Pelicans took him first overall on June 20, 2019. He signed a shoe deal with Jordan Brand the following month, reported at around $75 million over seven years.

## Key Achievements

- **NBA All-Star** in 2021, at age 20, and again in 2023.
- **NBA All-Rookie First Team** in 2020 after averaging 22.5 points in 24 games.
- **2020–21 season**: 27.0 points per game on 61.1 percent shooting, making him one of the few players ever to score 25 or more per game on better than 60 percent from the field.
- **Consensus National Player of the Year** at Duke in 2019.
- In July 2022 he signed a five-year rookie maximum extension worth up to roughly $231 million, though the contract contained weight and conditioning benchmarks tied to guaranteed money.

## Controversies & Criticism

Williamson has faced persistent criticism over his conditioning and weight, which the Pelicans formally wrote into his 2022 contract. In January 2025 the team suspended him for one game for repeated violations of team policy, including missing a team flight.

He was involved in a lengthy legal dispute with Prime Sports Marketing and its president, Gina Ford, after he ended a marketing agreement signed in April 2019. In 2021 a federal judge in North Carolina ruled the contract void under the state's athlete-agent law, and Ford's counterclaims alleging improper benefits during his recruitment were dismissed.

In January 2025 a former girlfriend filed a civil lawsuit in Texas alleging sexual assault and abuse. Williamson denied the allegations through his attorneys.

## Legacy & Influence

Williamson's college season is regarded as one of the most dominant in the modern era, and he remains a reference point for discussions of the one-and-done system and athlete compensation in college sports. His shoe failure in 2019 accelerated debates about amateur athletes' rights that preceded the NCAA's name, image, and likeness reforms.

In the NBA, his per-minute production has been elite, but he had played in fewer than half of the Pelicans' possible regular-season games through his first six seasons. His career is frequently cited alongside those of other injury-limited stars when analysts assess how franchises should weigh health risk against upside.
