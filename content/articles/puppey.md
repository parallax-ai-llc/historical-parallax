## Summary

Clement "Puppey" Ivanov (born 6 March 1990) is an Estonian professional Dota 2 player and the long-serving captain of Team Secret. He is best known as the captain of Natus Vincere (Na'Vi), the team that won the first International in 2011, and as a co-founder of Team Secret in 2014.

Puppey is regarded as one of the most durable figures in competitive Dota. He competed at every edition of The International from 2011 through 2023, a streak of thirteen consecutive appearances that no other player matched. As a support player and drafter, he is associated with unconventional hero picks and a hands-on approach to roster building.

His importance to the scene rests less on any single title than on longevity, leadership, and the organisational model of Team Secret, which became one of the most successful Western teams of the late 2010s.

## Early Life & Background

Ivanov was born in Tallinn, Estonia, into a Russian-speaking family. He began playing the original Defense of the Ancients mod for Warcraft III as a teenager and competed on European online ladders and in small LAN events during the late 2000s. His nickname derives from "puppy."

By 2009 he was playing for international DotA squads, including Kingsurf International and a short stint with mousesports, gaining experience against Chinese and European teams before the transition to Dota 2.

## Rise to Prominence

In late 2010 Puppey joined the newly formed Ukrainian organisation Natus Vincere alongside Danil "Dendi" Ishutin, Alexander "XBOCT" Dashkevich, Ivan "ArtStyle" Antonov, and Dmitriy "LighTofHeaveN" Kupriyanov. In August 2011 the team won the inaugural International at Gamescom in Cologne, defeating EHOME 3–1 for the $1,000,000 first prize.

Na'Vi reached the grand final again at The International 2012, losing 1–3 to Invictus Gaming, and at The International 2013, losing 2–3 to Alliance in a series still cited as one of the best in the game's history. After a 7th–8th finish in 2014, Puppey left Na'Vi and in August 2014 co-founded Team Secret with Kuro "KuroKy" Takhasomi, Johan "N0tail" Sundstein, Tal "Fly" Aizik, and Gustav "s4" Magnusson.

## Key Achievements

- **The International 2011 champion** with Na'Vi; runner-up in 2012 and 2013.
- **Team Secret's first title run in 2015**, including The Summit 3, ESL One Frankfurt, and MDL 2015.
- **Two Dota Pro Circuit Major wins in 2019**: the Chongqing Major in January and the MDL Disneyland Paris Major in May.
- **DreamLeague Season 13 (Leipzig Major) in January 2020**, followed by a dominant run of online tournament wins through the pandemic year, including OMEGA League, ESL One Germany, and DreamLeague Season 14.
- **Third place at The International 2021** and **runner-up at The International 2022**, where Secret advanced from the Last Chance Qualifier before losing the final 0–3 to Tundra Esports.
- Career prize winnings exceed $3.5 million, placing him among the highest-earning esports players.

## Controversies & Criticism

Team Secret's early years drew criticism for frequent roster changes, with high-profile players such as Arteezy, Zai, and EternaLEnVy leaving after short tenures. In 2016 Jacky "EternaLEnVy" Mao publicly accused the organisation of withholding prize money and mishandling player payments. The parties later described the dispute as resolved, but the episode damaged Secret's reputation for a period.

Puppey has also been criticised for on-stream outbursts, including a widely circulated clip of him striking his monitor. Some commentators have questioned whether his drafting choices, at times highly experimental, cost Secret in elimination matches.

## Legacy & Influence

Puppey's streak of thirteen consecutive Internationals ended in 2024 when Team Secret failed to qualify, closing an era. He helped define the captain role in Dota 2, combining in-game leadership with control over recruitment and team direction. Team Secret's model of a player-founded organisation influenced later teams such as OG. His long association with heroes like Chen and Enchantress and his reputation as a drafter remain reference points for support players.
