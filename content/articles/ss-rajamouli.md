## Summary

S.S. Rajamouli (born Koduri Srisaila Sri Rajamouli, 10 October 1973) is an Indian film director and screenwriter who works primarily in Telugu cinema. Over a career spanning more than two decades, he has directed twelve feature films without a commercial failure, a record that has made him one of the most bankable filmmakers in India.

His two-part epic *Baahubali* (2015, 2017) and the period action drama *RRR* (2022) redefined the scale and reach of Indian cinema. *Baahubali 2: The Conclusion* became the first Indian film to gross more than ₹1,000 crore, and *RRR* brought India its first Academy Award for Best Original Song with "Naatu Naatu" in 2023. Rajamouli is widely credited with dissolving the boundary between regional and "pan-Indian" filmmaking.

## Early Life & Background

Rajamouli was born in Raichur, Karnataka, and raised in Kovvur, Andhra Pradesh, in a family already embedded in cinema. His father, K. V. Vijayendra Prasad, is a screenwriter who went on to write most of his son's films, and his cousin M. M. Keeravani is a composer who has scored every Rajamouli feature. Rajamouli showed little interest in formal study, and he has said he read *Amar Chitra Katha* comics and the *Mahabharata* obsessively as a child.

He entered the industry as an assistant to director K. Raghavendra Rao and directed the Telugu television serial *Santhi Nivasam* before moving to features. In 2001 he married costume designer Rama Rajamouli, who has designed costumes for his films since.

## Rise to Prominence

Rajamouli debuted with *Student No. 1* (2001), starring Jr. NTR, which became a commercial hit. He followed it with *Simhadri* (2003), *Sye* (2004), *Chatrapathi* (2005), *Vikramarkudu* (2006) and *Yamadonga* (2007), each profitable and several remade in Hindi, including *Vikramarkudu* as *Rowdy Rathore* (2012).

*Magadheera* (2009), a reincarnation fantasy with Ram Charan, was at the time the highest-grossing Telugu film ever made and established his taste for large-scale spectacle. *Eega* (2012), in which a murdered man is reborn as a housefly, won two National Film Awards and demonstrated that he could carry a film on visual effects and a high-concept premise.

## Key Achievements

- ***Baahubali: The Beginning* (2015)** won the National Film Award for Best Feature Film and grossed roughly ₹650 crore worldwide.
- ***Baahubali 2: The Conclusion* (2017)** grossed about ₹1,800 crore worldwide, the highest total for an Indian film at the time, and won the National Film Award for Best Popular Film.
- ***RRR* (2022)** grossed over ₹1,200 crore. "Naatu Naatu" won the Golden Globe and the Academy Award for Best Original Song. Rajamouli won Best Director from the New York Film Critics Circle, the first Indian to do so.
- He received the **Padma Shri** in 2016.
- In November 2025 he unveiled *Varanasi*, a globe-trotting adventure starring Mahesh Babu, Priyanka Chopra Jonas and Prithviraj Sukumaran, scheduled for 2027.

## Controversies & Criticism

Critics have questioned the politics of Rajamouli's films. *Baahubali* was faulted for depicting the dark-skinned Kalakeya tribe as savage antagonists, and *RRR* drew criticism for placing Gond revolutionary Komaram Bheem within Hindu iconography and for reducing British characters to caricatures. Rajamouli has said his films are fantasies, not histories.

In 2017 the Karnataka Film Chamber called for a boycott of *Baahubali 2* over actor Sathyaraj's earlier remarks on the Cauvery water dispute. In 2022 the decision to campaign *RRR* for the Oscars independently after India selected *Chhello Show* as its official entry prompted debate over the fairness of that process. At the *Varanasi* launch in November 2025, his remarks about losing faith in Lord Hanuman during a troubled shoot drew police complaints and criticism from religious groups. He later clarified that he meant no offence.

## Legacy & Influence

Rajamouli proved that a Telugu-language film could dominate box offices across India and abroad, triggering the wave of "pan-Indian" productions such as *KGF* and *Pushpa*. His use of visual effects, mythological framing and multi-year production schedules raised the industry's ambition and budgets. *RRR*'s Oscar campaign also gave Indian popular cinema an unprecedented foothold with Western critics and audiences.
