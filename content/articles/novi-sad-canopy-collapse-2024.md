## Summary
At 11:52 on 1 November 2024, the concrete canopy over the entrance of the main railway station in Novi Sad, Serbia, collapsed onto the pavement below, killing 16 people and severely injuring another [^1]. The station had reopened only months earlier after a renovation linked to China's Belt and Road railway project [^1]. Public suspicion that corruption and poor oversight caused the disaster set off a student-led protest movement that spread across Serbia and became the largest challenge to President Aleksandar Vucic's government since 2012 [^1][^2]. As of April 2026 no official cause or responsibility had been assigned [^1].

## Background
The station was built in 1964 with an unusual design in which a heavy concrete canopy hung from a slender cantilevered roof on steel tension elements [^1]. Experts quoted on Wikipedia suggested a service life of about 50 years for the canopy, and the building stood for 57 years without major renovation [^1]. In 2013 China, Hungary and Serbia agreed to rebuild the Budapest-Belgrade railway, and renovation of the Novi Sad station began in 2021 [^1]. The works involved a Chinese consortium of China Railway International and China Communications Construction Company, with the station reopening on 5 July 2024 [^1].

## The Event
Just before noon on 1 November 2024, the canopy fell onto people waiting and walking outside the station entrance [^1]. Fourteen people died at the scene or soon after, and two more died of their injuries in the following months, bringing the toll to 16 [^1]. Citizens laid flowers and lit candles at the station and in Freedom Square [^1]. The Novi Sad Higher Public Prosecutor's Office opened an investigation and questioned more than 40 people, including Construction Minister Goran Vesic [^1].

## Response and Aftermath
Vesic resigned on 4 November 2024, followed by Trade Minister Tomislav Momirovic on 20 November [^1]. On 30 December 2024, Vesic and 12 others were indicted over the collapse [^1]. Students began blockading universities and organising silent tributes and marches, which grew into nationwide demonstrations over corruption and media freedom [^1][^2]. Prime Minister Milos Vucevic resigned on 28 January 2025 [^1]. On 15 March 2025, a rally in Belgrade drew between 275,000 and 325,000 people according to the independent Public Assembly Archive, while the interior ministry put the figure at least 107,000 [^3].

## Positive Perspectives
- Students organised a largely peaceful movement that crossed political lines, uniting people from different backgrounds [^3].
- Public pressure led to the resignations of the construction minister, the trade minister and the prime minister [^1].
- Prosecutors brought indictments against 13 people within two months of the disaster [^1].
- The movement turned into an organised political force, entering an electoral list for the 2026 parliamentary elections [^4].

## Negative Perspectives
- Protesters alleged that corruption led to flawed renovation work, and the cause of the collapse remained officially undetermined as of April 2026 [^1][^2].
- On 24 December 2025 the Novi Sad High Court dropped charges against Vesic and five other defendants for insufficient evidence, a decision under appeal [^1].
- Vucic called the protests "unregistered, criminal" gatherings, and pro-government media labelled critics terrorists and foreign agents [^2].
- Official and independent crowd estimates differed sharply, feeding disputes over the movement's scale [^3].

## Recent News
On 14 September 2026, students submitted the "Student List - Students Win" to Serbia's Republic Electoral Commission to compete in early parliamentary elections set for 25 October 2026, after nearly two years of protests [^4].
