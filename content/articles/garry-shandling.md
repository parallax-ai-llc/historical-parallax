## Summary

Garry Shandling (November 29, 1949 – March 24, 2016) was an American comedian, actor, and writer best known for creating and starring in two television series that reshaped the sitcom: *It's Garry Shandling's Show* (Showtime, 1986–1990) and *The Larry Sanders Show* (HBO, 1992–1998). His self-aware, anxious stage persona and his willingness to expose the mechanics of television made him a central figure in the shift from traditional multi-camera comedy toward the single-camera, mockumentary, and "showbiz satire" formats that dominated later decades.

Shandling began as a sitcom writer, became a stand-up, and served as a regular guest host of *The Tonight Show* in the mid-1980s. He turned down several offers to host his own network late-night program, preferring to satirize the genre instead. *The Larry Sanders Show* received 56 Emmy nominations, a Peabody Award, and a BAFTA, and is frequently cited among the best television comedies ever made.

## Early Life & Background

Shandling was born in Chicago, Illinois, to a Jewish family and raised in Tucson, Arizona, where his parents moved partly to seek treatment for his older brother Barry, who had cystic fibrosis and died when Garry was 10. He studied electrical engineering and then marketing at the University of Arizona, later taking postgraduate creative writing courses. In the early 1970s he moved to Los Angeles, where he sold a script to *Sanford and Son* and went on to write for that series and *Welcome Back, Kotter*. A serious car accident in 1977 prompted him to commit to performing stand-up full time.

## Rise to Prominence

Shandling made his first *Tonight Show* appearance on March 18, 1981, and by 1986 was one of Johnny Carson's most frequent guest hosts. In 1986 he and Alan Zweibel created *It's Garry Shandling's Show* for Showtime, a sitcom in which the character openly addressed the audience, discussed the plot, and acknowledged the studio set. It ran for 72 episodes and was also aired on Fox. In 1992 he co-created *The Larry Sanders Show* with Dennis Klein, playing an insecure late-night host surrounded by his producer (Rip Torn) and sidekick (Jeffrey Tambor). The series ran 89 episodes over six seasons and drew A-list guests appearing as unflattering versions of themselves.

## Key Achievements

- Won the 1998 Primetime Emmy for Outstanding Writing for a Comedy Series (with Peter Tolan) for the *Larry Sanders* finale, "Flip."
- *The Larry Sanders Show* earned 56 Emmy nominations, a 1993 Peabody Award, multiple CableACE Awards, and a BAFTA.
- Hosted the Grammy Awards four times (1990–1993) and the Primetime Emmy Awards in 2000 and 2004.
- Film roles included *Hurlyburly* (1998), *What Planet Are You From?* (2000), *Zoolander* (2001), *Over the Hedge* (2006), and Senator Stern in *Iron Man 2* (2010) and *Captain America: The Winter Soldier* (2014).
- Published *Confessions of a Late Night Talk Show Host: The Autobiography of Larry Sanders* (1998).

## Controversies & Criticism

In 1994 Shandling's former fiancée and *Larry Sanders* cast member Linda Doucett was dropped from the show after their relationship ended; she sued for sexual harassment and wrongful termination, and the case was settled in 1997 for a reported $1 million. In 1998 Shandling sued his longtime manager Brad Grey, who was also the show's executive producer, for $100 million, alleging conflicts of interest and breach of fiduciary duty. Grey countersued for $10 million, and both suits were settled in 1999. Court filings in the 2006 prosecution of private investigator Anthony Pellicano later indicated Shandling had been a target of illegal wiretapping during that dispute. Some critics also faulted the pace of his output, noting long gaps between projects.

## Legacy & Influence

Shandling died in Los Angeles on March 24, 2016, at age 66; the coroner attributed his death to a pulmonary embolism. His fourth-wall-breaking and behind-the-scenes formats influenced *Curb Your Enthusiasm*, *The Office*, *30 Rock*, and *Extras*, among others. He was also a mentor to a generation of comedians, including Judd Apatow, Sacha Baron Cohen, and Kevin Nealon, many of whom attended the weekly basketball game at his home. Apatow's two-part HBO documentary *The Zen Diaries of Garry Shandling* (2018) and the companion book *It's Garry Shandling's Book* (2019) drew on decades of Shandling's private journals to document his Buddhist practice and creative process.
