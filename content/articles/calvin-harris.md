## Summary

Calvin Harris, born Adam Richard Wiles on 17 January 1984, is a Scottish DJ, record producer, singer and songwriter. He emerged in 2007 as an electro-pop artist who sang his own material, then transformed himself into one of the most commercially successful producers of the 2010s, writing and producing hits for Rihanna, Ellie Goulding, Dua Lipa, Sam Smith and others.

His 2012 album *18 Months* set a UK chart record by yielding nine top-ten singles from a single studio album, and "We Found Love" with Rihanna spent ten weeks at number one in the United States. Forbes ranked him the world's highest-paid DJ for six consecutive years from 2013 to 2018.

Harris is significant for bridging underground dance music and mainstream pop radio, helping define the EDM-pop sound that dominated global charts in the early 2010s, and for demonstrating that a producer could operate as a headline artist in his own right.

## Early Life & Background

Harris was born in Dumfries, Scotland, the youngest of three children of a biochemist father and a homemaker mother. He began recording in his bedroom as a teenager, releasing his first tracks under the name Stouffer in 2002. He chose the stage name Calvin Harris because he thought it sounded racially ambiguous and would let his soul-influenced music be judged without preconceptions.

After leaving school he worked in a fish-processing factory and at a Marks & Spencer store to fund recording equipment. A brief move to London in 2004 was unsuccessful, and he returned to Dumfries, where he uploaded demos to MySpace. Those recordings attracted the attention of the manager Mark Gillespie and led to a publishing deal with EMI and a record deal with Columbia in 2006.

## Rise to Prominence

His debut album, *I Created Disco*, was released in June 2007 and reached number eight in the UK, driven by the singles "Acceptable in the 80s" and "The Girls." That year he also produced two tracks for Kylie Minogue's album *X*.

In 2008 he featured on Dizzee Rascal's "Dance wiv Me," his first UK number one. His second album, *Ready for the Weekend* (2009), topped the UK chart, as did its lead single "I'm Not Alone." Around 2011 Harris stopped singing lead vocals and repositioned himself as a producer and DJ working with guest vocalists, a decision that preceded his biggest commercial period.

## Key Achievements

- **18 Months (2012):** Reached number one in the UK and produced nine top-ten singles, surpassing the record previously held by Michael Jackson. "We Found Love" won the 2013 Grammy for Best Short Form Music Video.
- **Awards:** Ivor Novello Songwriter of the Year in 2013, and Brit Awards in 2019 for British Producer of the Year and British Single for "One Kiss" with Dua Lipa, which spent eight weeks at UK number one.
- **Later albums:** *Motion* (2014) yielded "Summer" and "Blame." *Funk Wav Bounces Vol. 1* (2017) featured Frank Ocean, Pharrell Williams and Katy Perry, and was followed by *Vol. 2* in 2022.
- **Earnings:** Forbes estimated his income at 66 million US dollars in 2014, the highest of any DJ.
- **Recent hits:** "Miracle" with Ellie Goulding held UK number one for eight weeks in 2023.

He has also released house and techno records under the alias Love Regenerator since 2020 and held long-running residencies in Las Vegas and Ibiza.

## Controversies & Criticism

In 2014 Harris prevented Rita Ora from performing "I Will Never Let You Down," which he had written and produced, at the Teen Choice Awards after their relationship ended. He also withheld other songs from her planned album.

In July 2016, after his relationship with Taylor Swift ended, it emerged that Swift had co-written "This Is What You Came For" under the pseudonym Nils Sjöberg. Harris posted a series of tweets criticising Swift and her team, which he later deleted and expressed regret over.

Critics have sometimes described his chart-oriented production as formulaic, and early lyrics such as those on "The Girls" drew accusations of sexism.

## Legacy & Influence

Harris was central to bringing dance production into the centre of Anglo-American pop, and his collaborations established a template followed by later producer-artists. His rejection of vocal duties in favour of guest singers reshaped how DJs were marketed as headline stars. Commercially, his combination of streaming success, touring and Las Vegas residencies made him a benchmark for DJ earnings throughout the 2010s. He married the broadcaster Vick Hope in September 2023.
