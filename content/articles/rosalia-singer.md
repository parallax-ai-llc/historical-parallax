## Summary

Rosalía Vila Tobella, known mononymously as Rosalía, is a Spanish singer, songwriter and producer born on 25 September 1992 in Catalonia. She trained for years as a flamenco vocalist before fusing that tradition with reggaeton, R&B, electronic pop and, most recently, orchestral and choral music.

Her 2018 album *El mal querer* turned a conservatory thesis project into an international breakthrough, and *Motomami* (2022) made her one of the most critically acclaimed pop artists of the decade. She is a multiple Grammy and Latin Grammy winner whose work has expanded the commercial reach of Spanish-language music while provoking ongoing debate about genre, identity and cultural ownership.

## Early Life & Background

Rosalía was born in Sant Cugat del Vallès and raised in Sant Esteve Sesrovires, a small town near Barcelona. She discovered flamenco as a teenager through friends and was drawn to the singer Camarón de la Isla. She studied at the Taller de Músics and then at the Escola Superior de Música de Catalunya (ESMUC), where she spent eight years learning flamenco cante under the guitarist and teacher José Miguel "Chiqui" Vizcaya. Before her recording career she performed at weddings, small venues and tablaos, and appeared on the Spanish television show *Tú sí que vales* in 2008.

## Rise to Prominence

Her debut album, *Los Ángeles* (2017), was a sparse collection of traditional flamenco songs arranged with producer Raül Refree, and it earned her a Latin Grammy nomination for Best New Artist. The single "Malamente", released in May 2018 and produced with El Guincho (Pablo Díaz-Reixa), brought her global attention with its blend of palmas, trap beats and striking visuals. *El mal querer* followed on 2 November 2018. Conceived as her ESMUC graduation project and structured around a 13th-century Occitan novel, it won Album of the Year at the 2019 Latin Grammys and a Grammy in January 2020. Collaborations with J Balvin ("Con altura", 2019), Ozuna and Travis Scott ("TKN", 2020) cemented her position in the Latin and global pop markets.

## Key Achievements

*Motomami*, released on 18 March 2022, mixed reggaeton, bachata, jazz and experimental production; it was the best-reviewed album of the year on Metacritic and won Album of the Year at the 2022 Latin Grammys and Best Latin Rock or Alternative Album at the 2023 Grammys. The accompanying world tour ran through 2022. She released the joint EP *RR* with Rauw Alejandro in 2023. Her fourth album, *Lux* (7 November 2025), recorded with the London Symphony Orchestra and sung in more than a dozen languages, was led by the single "Berghain" featuring Björk and Yves Tumor, and became her highest-charting album in the United States.

## Controversies & Criticism

Rosalía has been criticized in Spain for cultural appropriation: as a non-Romani Catalan artist, her use of flamenco, gitano slang and Andalusian imagery has been called extractive by some Roma commentators and flamenco purists. Her wins in "Latin" categories at the 2019 MTV Video Music Awards and the Latin Grammys prompted debate about whether a European artist should be classified alongside Latin American musicians. Tracks such as "Chicken Teriyaki" (2022) drew further accusations of borrowing from cultures she has no roots in. She has said she approaches these traditions with study and respect, and she has notably avoided political statements, which some critics have also faulted.

## Legacy & Influence

Rosalía helped prove that Spanish-language and genre-hybrid records could achieve critical consensus and commercial success in Anglophone markets without English-language crossover singles. Her visual language, choreography and willingness to reinvent her sound on each album have influenced a generation of Spanish and Latin artists, and *Motomami* is widely cited as a landmark of 2020s pop experimentation. With *Lux*, she extended that experimentation into classical and sacred forms, further blurring the boundary between pop and art music.
