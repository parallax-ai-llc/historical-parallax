## Summary
On 29 October 2024 a cut-off low, known in Spain as a DANA, brought extreme rainfall to eastern Spain and caused flash floods that devastated the Horta Sud suburbs south of Valencia [^1]. The floods are among the deadliest in modern Spanish history: Wikipedia's infobox lists 241 deaths nationwide, most of them in Valencia province, while later court reporting has cited about 230 deaths [^1][^2]. The regional government's emergency alert did not reach phones until 20:11, after many towns were already under water [^1]. Anger over the response led to mass protests, the resignation of regional president Carlos Mazon in November 2025, and a long-running judicial investigation [^1][^2].

## Background
Valencia has a long history of deadly floods, including the 1957 flood, when the Turia river overflowed and devastated the city [^1]. After the 2023 regional election, Mazon's new government closed the Valencian Emergency Unit, calling it a "superfluous expense" [^1]. On 25 October 2024 a meteorologist from the state weather agency AEMET warned the coming DANA could become a high-impact storm [^1].

## The Event
AEMET issued an orange warning for southern Valencia at 06:42 on 29 October and red warnings for the Valencian interior at 07:31 and 07:36 [^1]. At about 13:00 Mazon told the press the storm was expected to ease by 18:00 [^1]. In the afternoon, rain over the inland mountains swelled the Poyo ravine and the Magro and Jucar rivers [^1]. At 18:00, the town of Turis recorded 184.6 mm of rain in one hour, a Spanish record [^1]. Floodwater swept through Paiporta, Picanya, Catarroja, Alfafar, Sedavi and neighbouring towns, catching people in streets, garages and cars; a bridge in Picanya was destroyed at 19:25 [^1]. The Generalitat sent its ES-Alert phone warning only at 20:11, advising residents to stay indoors [^1]. Further flooding affected Andalusia, Castilla-La Mancha and other regions in the following weeks [^1].

## Response and Aftermath
Thousands of volunteers from across Spain walked into the flooded towns with shovels and supplies, often arriving before military emergency units or police [^1]. On 3 November, residents of Paiporta threw mud at King Felipe VI, Queen Letizia, Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez and Mazon, shouting "murderers" [^1]. About 130,000 people marched in Valencia on 9 November to demand Mazon's resignation [^1]. The central government approved a €10.6 billion aid decree, and total damage was estimated at about €10.7 billion, with more than 100,000 vehicles damaged [^1]. A Catarroja judge, Nuria Ruiz Tobarra, led the criminal investigation into the emergency management on the day of the floods [^2].

## Positive Perspectives
- A large, self-organised volunteer movement helped clear mud and deliver food and water in the first days [^1].
- Private donors, including Amancio Ortega and Real Madrid, contributed to relief funds [^1].
- The New Turia Riverbed canal, built after the 1957 flood and completed in 1973, ran at capacity on 30 October 2024 and spared Valencia city proper [^1].
- Repair costs were assessed early, with about €1.7 billion estimated for buildings and €500 million for water infrastructure [^1].
- The judicial investigation has examined decisions on the day of the floods, and the regional high court said the case could continue [^2].

## Negative Perspectives
- Critics said the 20:11 phone alert came hours after AEMET's red warnings and after deadly flooding had begun [^1].
- Mazon was criticised for a lengthy lunch with a journalist during the emergency, and his delayed response became the focus of protests [^2].
- People's Party leader Alberto Nunez Feijoo blamed AEMET, but AEMET's timeline contradicted him, and meteorologists warned that blaming the agency could erode trust in warnings [^1].
- The closure of the regional emergency unit in 2023 was cited by critics as weakening preparedness [^1].
- The death toll has been reported differently over time, and victims' groups continue to seek accountability [^1][^2].

## Recent News
In February 2026 Judge Ruiz Tobarra asked the High Court of Justice of the Valencian Community to try Mazon for negligence, saying his retreat to a private dining room was "a decisive factor" in the death toll. On 16 March 2026 the court rejected the request, ruling the law gave the regional president no specific emergency duties, though the decision could be appealed and the wider investigation continued [^2].
