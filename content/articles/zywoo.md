## Summary

Mathieu "ZywOo" Herbaut (born 9 November 2000) is a French professional Counter-Strike player who has competed for Team Vitality since late 2018. Primarily an AWPer who also rifles at an elite level, he is widely regarded as one of the two or three greatest players in the history of the game, alongside Oleksandr "s1mple" Kostyliev.

ZywOo was named the world's best player by HLTV in 2019, 2020, 2023 and 2024, a record four times, and did so in his debut full season at 19 years old. He anchored Vitality's victories at the BLAST.tv Paris Major in 2023 and the BLAST.tv Austin Major in 2025, earning the Most Valuable Player award at both events.

His importance goes beyond trophies. He is the central figure in the most successful era of French Counter-Strike, and his consistency across CS:GO and Counter-Strike 2 has kept Vitality at the top of the game through multiple roster overhauls and an engine change.

## Early Life & Background

Herbaut was born in Villeneuve-Saint-Georges, a suburb south-east of Paris, and grew up in the Île-de-France region. He began playing Counter-Strike as a child, initially on Counter-Strike: Source, before moving to Global Offensive after its 2012 release.

He was competing in French amateur and semi-professional leagues by his mid-teens. Unlike many contemporaries, he did not come through an academy system. He built his reputation on public matchmaking ladders and in small national tournaments, where his aim and unusually calm decision-making became known to French coaches and scouts.

## Rise to Prominence

In 2017 and 2018 ZywOo played for Vexed Gaming and then the historic French organisation against All authority (aAa), regularly posting top statistics in European tier-two competition. Team Vitality signed him in October 2018 as part of a rebuilt lineup led by Dan "apEX" Madesclaire with Cédric "RpK" Guipouy and Alex "ALEX" McMeekin.

His impact was immediate. Vitality won cs_summit 4, ECS Season 7 and EPICENTER 2019 in his first year, and HLTV ranked him the number one player of 2019 ahead of s1mple. He repeated the achievement in 2020 during the online era, when Vitality won DreamHack Masters Spring Europe and the BLAST Premier Fall Final.

In 2022 Vitality moved to an international roster, adding Danish stars Peter "dupreeh" Rasmussen and Emil "Magisk" Reif with coach Danny "zonic" Sørensen. After a slow start the team won ESL Pro League Season 16, and in 2023 ZywOo delivered IEM Rio and the Paris Major on home soil.

## Key Achievements

- **HLTV Player of the Year** in 2019, 2020, 2023 and 2024, the most in history.
- **Major champion** twice, Paris 2023 and Austin 2025, with the MVP award at both.
- **More than twenty HLTV event MVP awards**, the most of any player.
- **2025 winning streak** with Vitality across ESL Pro League Season 21, IEM Melbourne, PGL Bucharest, IEM Dallas and the Austin Major, one of the longest series streaks in the game's history.
- Additional titles include IEM Cologne 2024, ESL Pro League Seasons 17 and 19, and multiple BLAST Premier finals.

## Controversies & Criticism

ZywOo has avoided personal scandal, but his career has drawn recurring criticism on competitive grounds. Between 2019 and 2022 Vitality repeatedly exited Majors before the final, including a quarterfinal loss to AVANGAR at Berlin 2019 and to Natus Vincere at Antwerp 2022, leading commentators to argue that the organisation built a system too dependent on one player.

The 2022 decision to abandon a fully French lineup for an international roster was unpopular with some domestic fans, and ZywOo's initially limited English was cited as a risk. Critics have also noted that his flat public demeanour and reluctance to give interviews leave him less visible than rivals of similar stature.

## Legacy & Influence

ZywOo redefined expectations for a debut season and reopened the debate over the greatest player of all time, previously considered settled in s1mple's favour. His four HLTV number one rankings across two game versions are the benchmark by which younger stars such as Danil "donk" Kryshkovets are measured.

For French Counter-Strike, he restored the country to the top of the game after the decline of the Envy and G2 eras and became the face of Vitality's expansion into a global esports brand.
