## Summary

Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo is a Mexican physicist, energy scientist, and politician who took office on October 1, 2024, as the 66th President of Mexico. She is the first woman and the first person of Jewish heritage to hold the office, winning the June 2, 2024 election with roughly 59.8 percent of the vote, the largest share recorded since Mexico's democratic transition.

Before the presidency she served as Head of Government of Mexico City from 2018 to 2023, and earlier as environment secretary of the capital and as mayor of the Tlalpan borough. Her career bridges academia and politics: she holds a doctorate in energy engineering and contributed to reports of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC).

Sheinbaum matters because she consolidated the political movement built by her predecessor, Andrés Manuel López Obrador, while facing pressure over cartel violence, judicial reform, and trade tensions with the United States.

## Early Life & Background

Sheinbaum was born on June 24, 1962, in Mexico City. Her father, Carlos Sheinbaum Yoselevitz, was a chemical engineer of Lithuanian Ashkenazi descent, and her mother, Annie Pardo Cemo, is a biologist of Bulgarian Sephardic descent. Both parents were active in the 1968 student movement, and politics was a constant presence in the household.

She studied physics at the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM), graduating in 1988, then completed a master's degree in 1994 and a doctorate in energy engineering in 1995. From 1991 to 1994 she carried out research at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory in California, focusing on energy consumption in Mexican industry and transport. As a student she was part of the 1986–1987 university council movement opposing tuition increases at UNAM.

## Rise to Prominence

In 2000, López Obrador, then newly elected Head of Government of Mexico City, appointed Sheinbaum as the capital's Secretary of the Environment. She held the post until 2006, overseeing the launch of the Metrobús bus rapid transit system and the second deck of the Periférico ring road.

She was a contributing author to the IPCC Fourth Assessment Report, and the panel shared the 2007 Nobel Peace Prize. In 2014 she helped found the National Regeneration Movement (Morena). She won the Tlalpan borough in 2015 and, on July 1, 2018, was elected Head of Government of Mexico City with about 47 percent of the vote. She resigned on June 16, 2023, to seek Morena's presidential nomination, which she secured that September.

## Key Achievements

- **Historic election:** Won the 2024 presidency with about 35.9 million votes, defeating Xóchitl Gálvez and Jorge Álvarez Máynez, and became Mexico's first female head of state.
- **Urban transport:** As city leader she built the Cablebús cable-car network, expanded Metrobús, and electrified parts of the bus fleet.
- **Security results:** Her government, with security secretary Omar García Harfuch, reported a decline in daily homicides of over 25 percent during her first year, and transferred 29 senior cartel figures to the United States in February 2025.
- **Scientific credentials:** She is the first Mexican president with a doctorate in a natural science field.

## Controversies & Criticism

The collapse of the Enrique Rébsamen school in Tlalpan during the September 19, 2017 earthquake killed 26 people, including 19 children, and critics faulted borough oversight of illegal construction. On May 3, 2021, an overpass on Metro Line 12 collapsed, killing 26, and opposition figures blamed maintenance failures under her administration.

As president she pushed through a constitutional reform under which federal judges, including the Supreme Court, are elected by popular vote. The first judicial election on June 1, 2025 drew turnout of roughly 13 percent, and critics argued the change weakened judicial independence. The dissolution of autonomous bodies such as the transparency institute INAI drew similar objections.

Her handling of United States tariffs imposed by President Donald Trump in 2025 was praised for restraint but criticized for concessions on migration and security cooperation. In November 2025, the murder of Uruapan mayor Carlos Manzo triggered large youth-led protests against cartel violence.

## Legacy & Influence

Sheinbaum's presidency is still in progress, but she has already reshaped Mexican political history by breaking the gender barrier at the highest office while sustaining approval ratings above 70 percent through much of her first year. Her tenure will likely be judged on whether she can lower violence, manage the trade relationship under the 2026 USMCA review, and balance her scientific background against a governing coalition dependent on state oil and energy policy.
