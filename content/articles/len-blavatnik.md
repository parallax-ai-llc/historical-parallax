## Summary

Sir Leonard "Len" Blavatnik (born Leonid Valentinovich Blavatnik, 14 June 1957) is a Ukrainian-born American and British businessman, investor, and philanthropist. He founded Access Industries in 1986 and built one of the world's largest private fortunes through post-Soviet aluminium and oil ventures, petrochemicals, media, and technology. Forbes has consistently ranked him among the 50 wealthiest people in the world, with a net worth exceeding $30 billion in the mid-2020s.

Blavatnik's holdings span Warner Music Group, the sports-streaming service DAZN, film and theatre investments, and stakes in chemical and technology companies. He topped the Sunday Times Rich List in 2015 and 2021 and was knighted in 2017 for services to philanthropy.

His giving, exceeding $1 billion, has reshaped institutions from Oxford to Harvard, yet his early fortune from Russian privatisation and his political donations have made him a recurring subject of scrutiny.

## Early Life & Background

Blavatnik was born in Odessa, Ukrainian SSR, to a Jewish family and grew up in Yaroslavl, north-east of Moscow. He studied at the Moscow State University of Railway Engineering before his family emigrated to the United States in 1978. He earned a master's degree in computer science from Columbia University in 1981 and became a US citizen in 1984. He later completed an MBA at Harvard Business School in 1989, where he met future business partner Viktor Vekselberg's circle and fellow investors.

## Rise to Prominence

Blavatnik founded Access Industries in New York in 1986. After the collapse of the Soviet Union he invested in Russian privatisation auctions, co-founding the aluminium producer SUAL with Vekselberg and joining the Alfa Group of Mikhail Fridman to acquire Tyumen Oil Company (TNK) in 1997. In 2003 the AAR consortium (Alfa, Access, Renova) merged TNK with BP's Russian assets to form TNK-BP, with BP paying roughly $6.75 billion. When Rosneft bought TNK-BP in 2013 for about $55 billion, Blavatnik's share was estimated at around $7 billion.

In 2005 Access acquired the Dutch chemical group Basell for about €4.4 billion. Basell bought Lyondell Chemical in 2007 for roughly $12.7 billion in a highly leveraged deal that ended in Chapter 11 bankruptcy in January 2009. Blavatnik injected fresh capital during the restructuring, and the reorganised LyondellBasell became one of the most profitable investments of his career.

## Key Achievements

- Acquired Warner Music Group in May 2011 for $3.3 billion; its June 2020 IPO valued the company at around $13 billion.
- Built DAZN, launched in 2016, into a global sports-streaming service through sustained multibillion-dollar funding.
- Took early stakes in technology companies including Spotify and Zalando, and owns the Theatre Royal Haymarket in London.
- Donated £75 million to the University of Oxford in 2010 to establish the Blavatnik School of Government, which opened in 2015.
- Gave $200 million to Harvard Medical School in 2018, creating the Blavatnik Institute.
- Funded the Blavatnik Awards for Young Scientists (since 2007), the Blavatnik Building at Tate Modern (2016), and galleries at the V&A and British Museum.

## Controversies & Criticism

The Oxford gift drew an open letter in 2015 from academics and activists who argued the university should not accept money linked to Russian privatisation-era wealth. Blavatnik has rejected the "oligarch" label, stressing his American citizenship and independence from the Kremlin. Critics also note his long partnership with Vekselberg, who was sanctioned by the United States in 2018, although Blavatnik himself has never been sanctioned and cut remaining Russian ties after the 2022 invasion of Ukraine.

His political donations have attracted attention, including $1 million to Donald Trump's 2017 inaugural committee and multimillion-dollar contributions to Republican and Democratic causes. DAZN's aggressive rights spending and large losses have raised questions about the venture's sustainability.

## Legacy & Influence

Blavatnik is one of the largest individual benefactors of higher education, science, and the arts in Britain and the United States. The Blavatnik School of Government, the Blavatnik Awards, and named institutes at Harvard, Yale, Stanford, and Tel Aviv University give his name lasting institutional presence. In business, he exemplified the transition from post-Soviet resource fortunes into Western media and technology ownership, with Warner Music and DAZN marking his influence on the global entertainment landscape.
