## Summary

Clare Smyth (born 7 April 1978) is a Northern Irish chef and restaurateur, and the first British woman to hold three Michelin stars. She rose through some of Europe's most demanding kitchens before becoming head chef, and later chef-patron, of Restaurant Gordon Ramsay in Chelsea, where she maintained the restaurant's three-star status for nearly a decade.

In 2017 she opened her own restaurant, Core by Clare Smyth, in Notting Hill, London. Core won two Michelin stars within its first year and three stars in the 2021 guide, an unusually rapid ascent. Her cooking is known for elevating humble British ingredients, most famously a signature dish built around a potato dressed with dulse seaweed, trout roe and herring.

Smyth was appointed MBE in 2013, was named The World's Best Female Chef in 2018, and cooked for the evening reception of the wedding of Prince Harry and Meghan Markle the same year. She has since expanded internationally with Oncore by Clare Smyth in Sydney, Australia.

## Early Life & Background

Smyth was born in County Antrim, Northern Ireland, and grew up on a farm, an upbringing she has credited with shaping her respect for produce and seasonality. At 16 she left home to pursue cooking in England, taking a post at Grayshott Hall in Surrey while completing formal training at Highbury College in Portsmouth.

Her early career included stages and positions at well-regarded kitchens, including Michael Caines at Gidleigh Park in Devon and Bibendum in London. These years established a pattern of deliberately seeking out the most rigorous environments available.

## Rise to Prominence

Smyth joined Restaurant Gordon Ramsay at Royal Hospital Road in 2002. Determined to broaden her classical foundations, she left in 2005 to work under Alain Ducasse at Le Louis XV in Monaco, one of the most celebrated three-star restaurants in the world.

She returned to Restaurant Gordon Ramsay in 2007 as head chef, becoming the first woman in the United Kingdom to run a restaurant holding three Michelin stars. She was made chef-patron in 2012 and retained all three stars throughout her tenure. In 2013 she was appointed Member of the Order of the British Empire for services to the hospitality industry.

Smyth left the Ramsay group in 2016 to establish an independent restaurant. Core by Clare Smyth opened in Notting Hill in August 2017, with a menu rooted in British produce rather than the luxury ingredients typical of fine dining.

## Key Achievements

- Core received two Michelin stars in October 2018, roughly 14 months after opening, and was promoted to three stars in January 2021.
- She was named The World's Best Female Chef 2018 by The World's 50 Best Restaurants.
- Core was ranked the number one restaurant in the UK at the National Restaurant Awards in 2019.
- She was chosen to cook for the evening reception of the royal wedding at Frogmore House on 19 May 2018.
- Oncore by Clare Smyth opened at Crown Sydney in November 2021 and quickly earned top ratings in Australia's Good Food Guide.
- She has served as a judge on the BBC's Great British Menu.

## Controversies & Criticism

Smyth's career has been notably free of major scandal. Criticism has tended to focus on the wider fine-dining model she represents: high tasting-menu prices at Core, and long working hours in Michelin-level kitchens. Smyth has spoken publicly about improving kitchen culture and hours in her own restaurants.

She has also expressed discomfort with the "female chef" framing, arguing that gender-specific awards, while raising visibility, risk implying a separate standard. Some commentators questioned whether accepting the World's Best Female Chef award was consistent with that position.

## Legacy & Influence

Smyth is widely regarded as the most decorated woman in British restaurant history, and her success has been cited as a milestone for women in professional kitchens. Her approach at Core, applying three-star technique to ingredients such as potatoes, carrots and lamb, has influenced a generation of British chefs to look to domestic produce rather than imported luxury.

Through Core and Oncore she has also built a reputation as a mentor, with several former staff going on to lead acclaimed kitchens of their own.
