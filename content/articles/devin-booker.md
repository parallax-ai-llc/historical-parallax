## Summary

Devin Armani Booker is an American professional basketball player who has spent his entire NBA career with the Phoenix Suns since being selected 13th overall in the 2015 draft. A 6-foot-5 shooting guard known for his mid-range scoring and durability under heavy usage, he became the franchise's all-time leading scorer and the face of its return to relevance in the 2020s.

Booker's significance rests on a combination of individual production and team turnaround. He was the youngest player ever to score 70 points in an NBA game, earned All-NBA First Team honors in 2022, and led the Suns to the 2021 NBA Finals after a decade without a playoff appearance. He also won Olympic gold medals with the United States in 2021 and 2024.

His career has also carried scrutiny. Critics have pointed to early-career losing, high-profile playoff exits, and the Suns' inability to convert an expensive roster into a championship.

## Early Life & Background

Booker was born on October 30, 1996, in Grand Rapids, Michigan. His father, Melvin Booker, was a standout at the University of Missouri and played professionally overseas; his mother, Veronica Gutiérrez, raised him in Michigan. Before his junior year of high school, Booker moved to Moss Point, Mississippi, where his father served as an assistant coach at Moss Point High School. He was named a McDonald's All-American in 2014.

Booker played one season at the University of Kentucky under John Calipari in 2014–15. Coming off the bench for a team that went 38–1, he averaged 10.0 points per game and was named SEC Sixth Man of the Year before declaring for the NBA draft.

## Rise to Prominence

Phoenix drafted Booker at age 18. He made the NBA All-Rookie First Team in 2016 and, on March 24, 2017, scored 70 points against the Boston Celtics, becoming the youngest player to reach that mark. He won the 2018 Three-Point Contest and that July signed a five-year, $158 million maximum extension.

The turning point came in the 2020 Orlando bubble, where the Suns went 8–0 and Booker earned his first All-Star selection. The trade for Chris Paul that offseason transformed the team: Phoenix reached the 2021 NBA Finals, losing to Milwaukee in six games while Booker averaged 28.2 points in the series. In 2021–22 the Suns posted a franchise-record 64 wins, and Booker finished fourth in MVP voting.

## Key Achievements

- Four-time NBA All-Star (2020, 2021, 2022, 2024).
- All-NBA First Team (2022) and Third Team (2024).
- Youngest player to score 70 points in a game (2017); scored 62 points against Indiana in January 2024.
- Phoenix Suns all-time leading scorer, passing Walter Davis in 2024.
- Olympic gold medalist with Team USA at Tokyo 2020 (held 2021) and Paris 2024.
- Signed a four-year, $224 million supermax extension in July 2022, followed by a two-year, $145 million extension in July 2025.

## Controversies & Criticism

Booker's 70-point game came in a loss, and Celtics players openly mocked the Suns for fouling late to extend the game, fueling early claims that his statistics were empty. His first five seasons produced no playoff appearances.

In the 2022 Western Conference semifinals, the top-seeded Suns lost Game 7 at home to Dallas by 33 points, with Booker scoring 11 on 3-of-14 shooting. After Phoenix assembled Booker, Kevin Durant, and Bradley Beal, the team was swept by Minnesota in the 2024 first round and missed the playoffs entirely in 2025, prompting Durant's trade. Booker has also drawn criticism for frequent technical fouls and arguing with officials.

## Legacy & Influence

Booker restored the Suns as a contender and became one of the era's most reliable mid-range scorers, often compared stylistically to Kobe Bryant, whom he cited as an influence. His loyalty to a single franchise through losing seasons, and his two Olympic titles, have made him a central figure in Phoenix sports history.
