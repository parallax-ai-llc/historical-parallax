## Summary

Jimmy Choo (born Choo Yeang Keat, 15 November 1948) is a Malaysian shoe designer whose name became one of the most recognisable luxury footwear labels of the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries. Trained in his father's Penang workshop and later at Cordwainers Technical College in London, he built a reputation in the late 1980s and 1990s for handmade couture shoes worn by celebrities, editors and Diana, Princess of Wales.

In 1996 he co-founded Jimmy Choo Ltd with British Vogue accessories editor Tamara Mellon, turning a small East London atelier into a global ready-to-wear brand. Choo sold his half of the company in 2001 for about £10 million and has had no operational role in the label since, though it still carries his name and was sold to Michael Kors Holdings in 2017 for roughly US$1.2 billion.

Choo matters both as a craftsman and as a case study in luxury branding. He remains an active advocate for shoemaking education and, in 2021, opened his own fashion academy in London.

## Early Life & Background

Choo was born in George Town, Penang, then part of British Malaya, into a family of Hakka Chinese shoemakers. He learned the trade from his father and is widely reported to have made his first pair of shoes at age 11. In the early 1980s he moved to London to study at Cordwainers Technical College in Hackney, graduating in 1983. He supported himself by cleaning restaurants and doing odd jobs while training.

## Rise to Prominence

In 1986 Choo opened a workshop in a former hospital building in Hackney, producing a few pairs of bespoke shoes each week. An eight-page spread in British Vogue in 1988 brought his work to the attention of the fashion press. From 1990 Diana, Princess of Wales, became a regular client, and Choo made shoes for her until her death in 1997. His hand-finished, elegant designs attracted an international clientele of actresses and royalty.

Recognising commercial potential, Tamara Mellon approached Choo, and in 1996 the two founded Jimmy Choo Ltd to produce ready-to-wear collections in Italy. The first standalone store opened on Motcomb Street in London in 1996, followed by New York and Los Angeles. Frequent appearances on the HBO series *Sex and the City* from 1998 made the label a household name.

## Key Achievements

- **Global brand:** Jimmy Choo Ltd grew from a single London store to more than 150 boutiques worldwide. The company passed through Equinox Luxury Holdings (2001), Lion Capital (2004, £101 million), TowerBrook Capital (2007, £185 million) and Labelux (2011, about £500 million) before listing on the London Stock Exchange in 2014 and being acquired by Michael Kors in 2017.
- **Honours:** Choo was awarded an OBE in 2002 for services to the shoe and fashion industry, and holds the Malaysian title Datuk, conferred by the Sultan of Pahang in 2000.
- **Education:** He has served as a spokesperson for the British Council promoting UK education, holds an honorary professorship at De Montfort University, and in 2021 founded the JCA London Fashion Academy in Mayfair.

## Controversies & Criticism

The most persistent dispute concerns authorship of the brand. Choo sold his 50 percent stake to Equinox in April 2001 for about £10 million, a fraction of the company's later valuations, and has said publicly that he felt uneasy seeing shoes he did not design sold under his name. Tamara Mellon's 2013 memoir *In My Shoes* portrayed Choo as reluctant to scale the business and credited the label's design direction largely to his niece, Sandra Choi, who became creative director. Choo's supporters counter that his craftsmanship and reputation were the foundation on which the brand was built. Critics have also noted the gap between his bespoke, handmade ethos and the mass-produced luxury goods now sold under the label.

## Legacy & Influence

Choo's career illustrates how an artisan's name can be converted into a multi-billion-dollar asset, and the tensions that follow. Beyond the brand, his lasting contribution lies in mentoring: he continues to teach, lecture and champion technical training for young designers in Britain and Malaysia. He is regarded in Malaysia as one of the country's most successful cultural exports and a symbol of Penang's craft heritage.
