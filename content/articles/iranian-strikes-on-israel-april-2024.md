## Summary
On the night of 13-14 April 2024, Iran launched more than 300 drones, cruise missiles, and ballistic missiles at Israel in an operation it called "True Promise" [^1][^2]. It was Iran's first direct military assault on Israel, according to the Associated Press [^2]. Israel, with help from the United States, the United Kingdom, France, and Jordan, said it intercepted about 99 percent of the projectiles [^1][^2]. The attack marked a turning point that led to further direct exchanges between the two states in 2024, 2025, and 2026 [^3][^4][^5].

## Background
On 1 April 2024, an Israeli airstrike destroyed an annex of the Iranian embassy compound in Damascus, killing 16 people, including Brigadier General Mohammad Reza Zahedi and other officers of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) [^1]. Numerous countries and international organizations condemned the Damascus strike, and Iran vowed retaliation [^1].

## The Event
Late on 13 April, Iran began launching waves of weapons from its territory; the Associated Press, citing the Israeli military, reported 170 drones, more than 30 cruise missiles, and more than 120 ballistic missiles [^2]. Israeli, American, British, and Jordanian forces engaged many of the weapons before they reached Israel; missiles were intercepted over Jordan, which said it acted in self-defense of its airspace [^1][^2]. A small number of ballistic missiles struck Nevatim Airbase in the Negev, causing limited damage to a runway and a C-130 transport aircraft, according to Wikipedia [^1]. A 7-year-old Bedouin girl, Amina Hassouna, was critically injured by shrapnel, and about 31 other people were treated mainly for minor injuries or anxiety [^1]. Hezbollah fired rockets at Israeli positions the same night [^2].

## Response and Aftermath
US President Joe Biden said American forces had helped Israel "take down nearly all" of the incoming drones and missiles [^2]. Iran's mission to the United Nations said the retaliation "can be deemed concluded" but warned of a stronger response if Israel made "another mistake," while the IRGC warned the US against intervening [^1][^2]. On 19 April, Israel carried out limited strikes on Iranian military sites, including one near Isfahan [^1]. On 25 April the United Kingdom announced new sanctions targeting Iran's drone industry [^1].

## Positive Perspectives
- A multinational coalition coordinated to intercept most of the projectiles, which Israeli officials and allies described as a defensive success [^1][^2].
- Civilian casualties were limited given the scale of the barrage, according to the Associated Press [^2].
- Jordan intercepted weapons crossing its airspace; Haaretz, as cited on Wikipedia, reported that Jordan's air force downed about 20 percent of the drones [^1].
- Israel's response on 19 April was limited in scope, and the April exchange did not immediately escalate into open war [^1].

## Negative Perspectives
- The attack broke a long-standing pattern of indirect conflict and set a precedent for direct state-on-state strikes [^2].
- A 7-year-old girl was critically wounded, and residents near Nevatim were ordered into protective spaces, according to the AP [^2][^1].
- The Damascus strike that preceded it, which hit a diplomatic compound, was condemned by numerous countries and organizations [^1].
- Iran launched a second attack, True Promise II, on 1 October 2024 using only ballistic missiles; estimates ranged from about 180 to more than 400 [^3].
- Subsequent rounds, including the June 2025 Twelve-Day War, showed the April deterrence exchange had failed to stabilize the rivalry [^4].

## Recent News
The confrontation that began in April 2024 escalated sharply: Israel and Iran fought the Twelve-Day War from 13 to 24 June 2025, ending in a US- and Qatar-mediated ceasefire [^4]. On 28 February 2026 the United States and Israel launched coordinated strikes on Iran, in which Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei was killed, as confirmed by Iranian state media [^5].
