## Summary

Fernando Gabriel Tatís Medina Jr., born January 2, 1999, is a Dominican professional baseball player for the San Diego Padres of Major League Baseball (MLB). He debuted as a shortstop in 2019 and has since moved to right field, where he won a Gold Glove and Platinum Glove in his first season at the position. He is the son of former major league third baseman Fernando Tatís Sr.

Tatís became one of the sport's most marketable young stars, combining power, speed, and a flamboyant style of play. In February 2021 he signed a 14-year, $340 million extension with San Diego, at the time the longest contract in MLB history. His career has also included serious injuries and an 80-game suspension for a performance-enhancing drug violation in 2022.

## Early Life & Background

Tatís was born in San Pedro de Macorís, Dominican Republic, a city known for producing major league shortstops. His father played 11 MLB seasons and famously hit two grand slams in one inning for the St. Louis Cardinals on April 23, 1999. Growing up around professional baseball, Tatís trained at academies in the Dominican Republic and drew scouts' attention as a lean, athletic teenager.

The Chicago White Sox signed him as an international free agent on July 2, 2015, for a reported $700,000 bonus. Before he played a professional game, the White Sox traded him and pitcher Erik Johnson to the Padres for James Shields on June 4, 2016, a deal later regarded as one of the most lopsided of its era.

## Rise to Prominence

Tatís rose quickly through San Diego's farm system, hitting 22 home runs at Double-A in 2017 as an 18-year-old and 16 more in 2018 before a thumb injury ended his season. The Padres put him on the Opening Day roster on March 28, 2019, when he was 20. He hit .317 with 22 home runs and 16 stolen bases in 84 games before a back injury ended his season, finishing third in National League Rookie of the Year voting.

In the shortened 2020 season he hit 17 home runs in 59 games, won a Silver Slugger, finished fourth in NL MVP voting, and helped the Padres reach the postseason for the first time since 2006.

## Key Achievements

In 2021 Tatís led the National League with 42 home runs, added 25 stolen bases, started the All-Star Game, won a second Silver Slugger, and finished third in MVP voting. He played much of that season through repeated left shoulder subluxations.

After returning from suspension on April 20, 2023, he moved permanently to right field and hit 25 home runs with 29 steals. He won the NL Gold Glove, the Platinum Glove as the league's best overall defender, and a Fielding Bible Award, becoming the first player to win a Platinum Glove in his first season at a new position. In the 2024 postseason he homered four times in seven games as San Diego reached the Division Series. He was named an All-Star again in 2025.

## Controversies & Criticism

On August 17, 2020, Tatís hit a grand slam on a 3-0 count with a seven-run lead against the Texas Rangers, prompting a national debate over baseball's unwritten rules.

In the 2021–22 offseason he was involved in a motorcycle accident in the Dominican Republic and fractured his left wrist, an injury not disclosed until spring training. On August 12, 2022, while rehabbing, he received an 80-game suspension after testing positive for the anabolic steroid clostebol. He said the substance came from a medication used to treat ringworm. He missed the entire 2022 season, and Padres leadership publicly criticized his judgment. As a minor leaguer he also sold a share of future earnings to the firm Big League Advance, which reportedly entitles it to a percentage of his contract.

## Legacy & Influence

Tatís remains one of the defining players of the Padres' modern era and a central figure in MLB's efforts to market a younger, more expressive generation of stars. His successful transition from shortstop to elite right fielder is frequently cited as an example of athletic versatility, and his contract reshaped expectations for long-term deals with players in their early twenties.
