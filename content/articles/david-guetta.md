## Summary

David Guetta (born Pierre David Guetta, 7 November 1967) is a French DJ and record producer who became one of the most commercially successful electronic music artists in the world. Emerging from the Paris club scene of the late 1980s, he spent nearly two decades as a working DJ and promoter before a run of pop-oriented dance singles in 2009 turned him into a global star.

Guetta is widely credited with helping to bring electronic dance music into mainstream American pop, most visibly through his production of the Black Eyed Peas' "I Gotta Feeling" (2009) and collaborations with Kelly Rowland, Akon, Sia, Nicki Minaj, Usher and Bebe Rexha. He has won two Grammy Awards, topped the *DJ Mag* Top 100 DJs poll three times (2011, 2020 and 2021), and is reported to have sold more than 50 million records.

## Early Life & Background

Guetta was born in Paris to a Moroccan-Jewish father, sociologist Pierre Guetta, and a Belgian mother. He began DJing at 17 at the Broad Club in Paris and, from 1988, hosted a house-music radio show and residencies at clubs such as Le Palace. In 1990 he released a first single, "Nation Rap", with French rapper Sidney Duteil. During the 1990s he managed the Paris venue Le Palace and, with future wife Cathy Lobé (married 1992, divorced 2014), organized parties at Les Bains Douches. The couple have two children.

## Rise to Prominence

In 2001 Guetta co-founded Gum Productions with producer Joachim Garraud and launched the Fuck Me I'm Famous party brand at Pacha in Ibiza. His debut album *Just a Little More Love* (2002), featuring vocalist Chris Willis, sold over 300,000 copies in Europe. *Guetta Blaster* (2004) and *Pop Life* (2007), the latter including "Love Is Gone", built a European following.

The turning point came with *One Love* (2009). "When Love Takes Over" with Kelly Rowland reached number one in the UK, and "Sexy Bitch" with Akon became his first US Top 10 hit. The same year he produced "I Gotta Feeling" for the Black Eyed Peas, which spent 14 weeks at number one on the Billboard Hot 100.

## Key Achievements

- **Grammy Awards**: Best Remixed Recording, Non-Classical in 2010 for "When Love Takes Over" and in 2011 for his remix of Madonna's "Revolver".
- **Nothing but the Beat (2011)**: produced "Titanium" with Sia, "Without You" with Usher and "Turn Me On" with Nicki Minaj; the album was certified platinum in several countries.
- **Listen (2014)**: included "Lovers on the Sun", "Dangerous" and "Hey Mama", the last of which reached number 8 on the Billboard Hot 100.
- **Official UEFA Euro 2016 song**: "This One's for You" with Zara Larsson.
- **Later hits**: "2U" with Justin Bieber (2017), "Flames" with Sia (2018), "Bed" with Joel Corry and Raye (2021) and "I'm Good (Blue)" with Bebe Rexha (2022), a UK number one nominated for a Grammy.
- **Charity**: his 2020 "United at Home" livestreams from Miami, New York, the Louvre and Dubai raised more than $1.5 million for COVID-19 relief.
- **Catalogue sale**: in 2021 he sold his recorded music catalogue to Warner Music Group in a deal reported at around $100 million.

## Controversies & Criticism

Guetta has been a lightning rod for debates about authenticity in dance music. Critics, including deadmau5, have accused him and similar DJs of "pressing play" on stage, and questions have been raised about the extent of his own studio work relative to collaborators such as Garraud, Fred Rister and Giorgio Tuinfort. A 2014 Tomorrowland clip in which he appeared disoriented went viral; he attributed it to exhaustion and emotion. In May 2020 he was criticized for playing a track sampling Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream" speech as a tribute to George Floyd during a livestream, which many found tone-deaf. In 2023 he drew scrutiny for using an AI-generated imitation of Eminem's voice in a live set, prompting debate over AI ethics in music.

## Legacy & Influence

Guetta's fusion of house production with mainstream pop vocals helped define the EDM boom of the early 2010s and set a template followed by producers such as Calvin Harris and Avicii. He demonstrated that a DJ could function as a chart-topping pop producer, reshaping how American radio treated electronic music. His longevity across three decades, from Paris club residencies to festival headlining and multi-billion-stream singles, makes him one of the most influential figures in electronic music history.
