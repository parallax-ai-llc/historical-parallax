## Summary
On 25 December 2024, an Azerbaijan Airlines Embraer E190 flying from Baku to Grozny, Russia, was damaged by a Russian surface-to-air missile as it approached Grozny, then crashed near Aktau, Kazakhstan, after crossing the Caspian Sea [^1]. Of the 67 people on board, 38 died, including both pilots, and 29 survived with injuries [^1]. Moscow at first avoided admitting responsibility, straining relations with Azerbaijan, until President Vladimir Putin acknowledged in October 2025 that Russian air defences had downed the plane [^2]. In April 2026 the two governments announced a settlement including compensation [^3].

## Background
In the weeks before the crash, Chechnya and other parts of southern Russia had faced repeated Ukrainian drone attacks during the Russo-Ukrainian war [^1]. The aircraft, registered 4K-AZ65, was operating a scheduled flight from Heydar Aliyev International Airport to Grozny [^1]. About 40 minutes after takeoff, as it entered Russian airspace, the crew reported losing GPS navigation because of jamming, and fog was reported at Grozny [^1].

## The Event
About 81 minutes into the flight, as the jet approached Grozny, passengers reported an explosion and shrapnel striking the aircraft [^1]. The pilots initially attributed the event to a bird strike, declared an emergency by squawking 7700, and turned east over the Caspian Sea toward Kazakhstan [^1]. With its hydraulic systems failing, the crew struggled to control the plane, which crashed near Aktau International Airport [^1]. Images of the wreckage showed holes in the fuselage, some containing foreign metal fragments, damage inconsistent with a bird strike and resembling a surface-to-air missile impact [^1].

## Response and Aftermath
On 26 December, Azerbaijani officials concluded that a Russian missile fired against Ukrainian drones had hit the plane, and on 27 December The New York Times reported investigators believed a Pantsir-S1 system was responsible [^1]. On 28 December, Putin apologised to President Ilham Aliyev for the "tragic incident" in Russian airspace but did not admit responsibility [^1]. The next day Aliyev said Russia had accidentally shot down the plane, accused it of trying to "hush up" the crash, and demanded an admission of guilt, punishment and compensation [^1]. Kazakhstan's preliminary report on 4 February 2025 said the crash was likely caused by "external objects" penetrating the aircraft, and investigators recovered a Pantsir-S missile fragment from the fuselage [^1].

## Positive Perspectives
- The surviving crew kept the damaged aircraft flying for more than an hour across the Caspian, and 29 people survived [^1].
- Kazakhstan's transport ministry issued a preliminary report within six weeks, and insurer AlfaStrakhovanie began paying compensation to passengers under the Montreal Convention in early 2025 [^1].
- Putin's October 2025 admission was his first public acceptance of responsibility, and he pledged compensation and a legal assessment of officials' actions [^2].
- Aliyev thanked Putin for keeping the investigation "under your personal control," easing a diplomatic crisis [^2].
- The April 2026 joint statement formally recognised that a Russian air defence system had caused the crash [^3].

## Negative Perspectives
- Russian authorities did not acknowledge responsibility for more than nine months after the crash [^1][^2].
- Aliyev accused Russia of trying to obscure what happened [^1].
- Rosaviatsia said a "Carpet" closed-skies protocol had been imposed at Grozny that day because of Ukrainian drones, yet the passenger jet was still approaching the city when air defences fired [^1].
- After the hit, the damaged jet was told not to attempt landing at Makhachkala because of weather and had to cross the Caspian Sea with failing controls [^1].
- Putin attributed the downing to a "technical malfunction" of missiles that exploded about 10 metres from the plane [^2].
- Under the 2026 settlement, Baku's demand that those responsible be punished effectively disappeared, according to JAMnews [^3].

## Recent News
On 15 April 2026, the Russian and Azerbaijani foreign ministries said they had reached a settlement on the crash, including compensation. JAMnews reported that the deal formalised payments already made through insurance, including 358.4 million rubles to victims and families [^3].
