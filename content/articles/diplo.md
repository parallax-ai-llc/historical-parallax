## Summary

Diplo, born Thomas Wesley Pentz on November 10, 1978, is an American DJ, record producer, and label founder. He is best known as the founder of the Mad Decent label, the leader of the electronic dancehall project Major Lazer, and a member of the duos Jack Ü (with Skrillex) and Silk City (with Mark Ronson). His production credits include work for M.I.A., Beyoncé, Usher, Madonna, Justin Bieber, and Dua Lipa.

Diplo's importance lies in how he moved regional and global styles such as Brazilian baile funk, Jamaican dancehall, Baltimore club, and moombahton into mainstream pop and festival music. Songs he co-wrote or produced, including "Paper Planes," "Lean On," "Where Are Ü Now," and "Electricity," have sold or streamed in the hundreds of millions and earned him three Grammy Awards. He has also been a divisive figure, facing sustained criticism over cultural appropriation and, later, a series of sexual misconduct allegations that he has denied.

## Early Life & Background

Pentz was born in Tupelo, Mississippi, and raised primarily in Florida, spending time in Miami and Daytona Beach. His stage name comes from the dinosaur Diplodocus, a childhood fascination. After a period at the University of Central Florida, he moved to Philadelphia and studied film at Temple University, where he worked in after-school programs and began DJing at local clubs.

In Philadelphia around 2003 he co-founded the Hollertronix parties with DJ Low Budget, mixing hip-hop, Baltimore club, and electro. The parties and their mix CDs earned national press and established his reputation as a genre-blending selector.

## Rise to Prominence

Diplo released his debut album, *Florida*, on the British label Big Dada in 2004. That same year he produced M.I.A.'s mixtape *Piracy Funds Terrorism*, beginning a partnership that culminated in "Paper Planes" (2007), which reached number 4 on the Billboard Hot 100 and was nominated for Record of the Year at the 2009 Grammys.

He founded Mad Decent in 2006. The label released early work by Dillon Francis and Flosstradamus and, in 2013, Baauer's "Harlem Shake," which spent five weeks at number 1 in the United States. In 2008 he co-directed the documentary *Favela on Blast* about Rio de Janeiro's baile funk scene, and in 2009 launched Major Lazer with the producer Switch.

## Key Achievements

- **Major Lazer**: "Lean On" (2015), featuring MØ and DJ Snake, became Spotify's most-streamed song at the time and exceeded 1 billion YouTube views. The group's 2016 free concert in Havana drew an estimated crowd of more than 400,000.
- **Jack Ü**: "Where Are Ü Now" with Justin Bieber (2015) won the Grammy for Best Dance Recording, and the duo's album won Best Dance/Electronic Album in 2016.
- **Silk City**: "Electricity" with Dua Lipa (2018) won Best Dance Recording at the 2019 Grammys.
- **Production work**: Usher's "Climax" (2012), Beyoncé's "Run the World (Girls)" (2011), and Madonna's "Bitch I'm Madonna" (2015).
- **Other projects**: the supergroup LSD with Sia and Labrinth (2018), the country album *Diplo Presents Thomas Wesley Chapter 1: Snake Oil* (2020), and the BBC Radio 1 show *Diplo & Friends* (2011–2020).

## Controversies & Criticism

Critics have long accused Diplo of profiting from Black and Caribbean music cultures without adequate credit, particularly through Major Lazer's dancehall imagery and his early baile funk releases. M.I.A. publicly disputed his share of credit for her early work, and he had public feuds with Taylor Swift and Lorde in 2014.

In 2020 he obtained a restraining order against a woman who later sued him alleging sexual assault and distribution of intimate images. The Los Angeles City Attorney declined to file charges in 2021. Additional civil suits in 2023 and 2024 alleged that he recorded or shared explicit videos without consent. Diplo has denied all of the allegations and described some of the claims as extortion attempts.

## Legacy & Influence

Diplo helped define the 2010s model of the producer-DJ as pop hitmaker, label head, and brand. Mad Decent's block parties and roster shaped the American EDM and trap scenes, and his cross-genre approach anticipated the global-pop blending later common in reggaeton, Afrobeats, and dancehall crossovers. His career also remains a reference point in debates about appropriation and accountability in dance music.
