## Summary

Walter Max Ulyate Sisulu (18 May 1912 – 5 May 2003) was a South African anti-apartheid activist and one of the most influential leaders of the African National Congress (ANC). He served as ANC Secretary-General from 1949 to 1954 and later as Deputy President from 1991 to 1994. He spent more than 25 years in prison, most of them on Robben Island alongside Nelson Mandela.

Sisulu is widely regarded as the strategist and organiser behind the ANC's transformation from a cautious petitioning body into a mass movement. He recruited Mandela and Oliver Tambo into the ANC Youth League, helped draft the 1949 Programme of Action, and was a founding figure of the armed wing Umkhonto we Sizwe (MK) in 1961.

Convicted of sabotage at the Rivonia Trial in 1964 and sentenced to life imprisonment, he was released in October 1989 and played a central role in the negotiations that ended apartheid. His impact lies less in public oratory than in the quiet institution-building that held the liberation movement together across five decades.

## Early Life & Background

Sisulu was born on 18 May 1912 in Ngcobo, in the Transkei region of the Eastern Cape. His mother, Alice Manse Sisulu, was Xhosa; his father, Albert Victor Dickinson, was a white magistrate's clerk who played no role in his upbringing. Raised by his mother and uncle, he attended an Anglican mission school but left at about age 15 after his uncle's death to support the family.

He moved to Johannesburg in 1928 and worked in a series of jobs, including at a dairy, in the gold mines, in a bakery, and as a domestic worker. He was dismissed from the bakery for organising a strike, an early sign of his political instincts. In 1944 he married Albertina Thethiwe, a nurse who became a prominent activist in her own right. The couple had five children, among them Max Sisulu, later Speaker of the National Assembly, and Lindiwe Sisulu, later a cabinet minister.

## Rise to Prominence

Sisulu joined the ANC in 1940 and became one of the founders of the ANC Youth League in 1944 with Anton Lembede, Nelson Mandela, and Oliver Tambo. By then he was running a small estate agency, one of the few Black-owned businesses in Johannesburg, and used it as an informal recruiting office. He introduced Mandela to the ANC and arranged Mandela's first job at a law firm.

In 1949 the Youth League's Programme of Action was adopted by the ANC, and Sisulu was elected Secretary-General, a full-time post he held until 1954. He was a principal organiser of the 1952 Defiance Campaign, in which more than 8,000 volunteers were arrested for deliberately breaking apartheid laws. That year he was convicted under the Suppression of Communism Act and given a suspended sentence. He was among the 156 defendants charged in the Treason Trial of 1956, which ended in acquittals in 1961.

After the ANC was banned in 1960, Sisulu went underground and helped establish MK in 1961. He was arrested at Liliesleaf Farm in Rivonia on 11 July 1963.

## Key Achievements

- **ANC Youth League co-founder (1944)** and architect of the 1949 Programme of Action, which committed the ANC to boycotts, strikes, and civil disobedience.
- **Defiance Campaign organiser (1952)**, the first large-scale, multiracial civil disobedience campaign against apartheid.
- **Rivonia Trial defendant (1963–1964)**, sentenced to life imprisonment on 12 June 1964 with Mandela, Govan Mbeki, Ahmed Kathrada, and others.
- **Political education on Robben Island**, where he was a leading figure in the prisoners' informal "university" and in structuring the ANC's internal organisation.
- **Released on 15 October 1989** and elected ANC Deputy President at the 1991 Durban conference, the first legal ANC conference in South Africa in three decades.
- Awarded the Isitwalandwe Medal, the ANC's highest honour, in 1992.

## Controversies & Criticism

Sisulu joined the South African Communist Party in 1955, and his close alignment with the party drew criticism from Africanists within the ANC who opposed both communist influence and multiracial alliances. This tension contributed to the 1959 breakaway that formed the Pan Africanist Congress.

His support for the turn to armed struggle in 1961 was condemned by the apartheid state as terrorism and remains debated by historians who question whether MK's early sabotage campaign was strategically effective. Critics of the later ANC also note that the post-1994 political dynasty formed by his children raised questions about elite entrenchment, though these criticisms concern his family rather than his own conduct.

## Legacy & Influence

Sisulu died in Johannesburg on 5 May 2003, thirteen days before his 91st birthday, and was given a state funeral. Mandela described him as his mentor and "the man who taught me to think politically." Walter Sisulu University in the Eastern Cape, established in 2005, bears his name, as do numerous streets, schools, and a botanical garden in Roodepoort.

His reputation rests on organisational discipline, consensus-building, and personal modesty. Historians credit him with keeping the ANC coherent through banning, exile, and imprisonment, and with shaping the negotiating posture that produced South Africa's 1994 democratic transition. The Walter and Albertina Sisulu family remains one of the most prominent in South African public life.
