## Summary

Teddy Swims, born Jaten Collin Dixon on September 25, 1992, is an American singer and songwriter from Conyers, Georgia. He blends soul, R&B, country, pop, and rock with a gritty, gospel-inflected voice, and his heavily tattooed, bearded image became a distinctive part of his brand. His stage name combines a childhood nickname with the acronym "Someone Who Isn't Me Sometimes."

He built an audience through YouTube cover videos beginning in 2019, signed with Warner Records the same year, and broke through globally with "Lose Control," released in June 2023. The song reached No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 in March 2024, was named Billboard's No. 1 song of 2024, and went on to set records for chart longevity. He earned a Grammy nomination for Best New Artist in 2025.

Swims matters because he demonstrated a slow-build path to superstardom in the streaming era, with a single that took months to peak and then stayed relevant for well over a year. He also helped bring a raw, retro-soul vocal style back into mainstream pop and adult radio formats.

## Early Life & Background

Dixon was raised in Conyers, a suburb east of Atlanta. He attended Salem High School, where he played offensive line on the football team before turning toward musical theater, which he credited with teaching him vocal control and stage presence.

After school he spent most of the 2010s in the Atlanta music scene, fronting several bands across very different genres. These included metalcore and post-hardcore projects such as Wildheart, as well as soul and funk cover acts that played bars and weddings. Those years gave him a wide stylistic range and a reputation as a working musician rather than a manufactured act.

## Rise to Prominence

In 2019 he began posting cover songs to YouTube under the name Teddy Swims. Versions of Shania Twain's "You're Still the One," Michael Jackson's "Rock with You," and Lewis Capaldi's "Someone You Loved" drew millions of views within months. Warner Records signed him in late 2019.

He released a run of EPs, including *Unlearning* (2021), *Tough Love* (2022), and *Sleep Is Exhausting* (2022), and toured steadily. His debut album, *I've Tried Everything but Therapy (Part 1)*, arrived on September 15, 2023. Its lead single "Lose Control" climbed slowly on radio and streaming, taking roughly eight months to reach the top of the Hot 100 on the chart dated March 2, 2024. A follow-up single, "The Door," also charted, and *Part 2* was released on January 24, 2025, led by "Bad Dreams."

## Key Achievements

- **"Lose Control" reached No. 1** on the Billboard Hot 100 in March 2024 and finished as the No. 1 song on Billboard's 2024 year-end chart.
- In 2025 the song **surpassed the record for most weeks in the Hot 100's top 10**, previously held by The Weeknd's "Blinding Lights," and became the longest-charting single in the Hot 100's history.
- **Grammy nomination for Best New Artist** at the 67th Annual Grammy Awards in February 2025.
- "Lose Control" reached the top 10 in the United Kingdom, Australia, and Canada and earned multi-platinum certifications in the United States.
- Two studio albums, *I've Tried Everything but Therapy (Part 1)* (2023) and *(Part 2)* (2025), both charting in the Billboard 200's top 40.

## Controversies & Criticism

Swims has attracted relatively little scandal. The main criticisms have been artistic and structural. Some critics argue that his early success was built on covers rather than original songwriting, and that his mainstream material is more polished and less adventurous than his band-era work. Others have questioned whether "Lose Control" earned its record chart run on merit or on Billboard's methodology, which weights radio airplay and streaming in ways that favor slow-burning adult contemporary hits.

His genre-blurring, blue-eyed soul style has also drawn debate about authenticity and appropriation, a familiar discussion around white artists working in Black musical traditions. Swims has consistently credited his influences and collaborators in interviews. He has spoken openly about past substance use and mental health struggles, themes reflected in his album titles.

## Legacy & Influence

Swims' career is often cited as evidence that patient artist development can still work in a viral-first industry. "Lose Control" became a case study in radio-driven longevity, and its success encouraged labels to keep working slow-building singles rather than abandoning them after a few weeks.

Musically, he helped reopen a lane for full-throated soul singing in pop, alongside artists such as Benson Boone and Chris Stapleton. His crossover between country, R&B, and rock audiences reflects the broader collapse of genre boundaries in the 2020s.
