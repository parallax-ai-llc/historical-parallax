## Summary

Payal Kapadia is an Indian film director, screenwriter, and documentary filmmaker based in Mumbai. She works at the border between documentary and fiction, building films from voiceover, found footage, and long observational passages rather than conventional plot. Her subjects are recurring: women's inner lives, migration to the city, and the political climate of contemporary India.

She became internationally prominent in 2021 when her documentary *A Night of Knowing Nothing* won the L'Œil d'Or at the Cannes Film Festival. In 2024 her first fiction feature, *All We Imagine as Light*, won the Cannes Grand Prix, the first Indian film to receive that award and the first Indian entry in the main competition in three decades.

Kapadia matters because she brought Indian independent cinema back to the centre of the world festival circuit while working outside the commercial Hindi and regional industries. Her rise also exposed tensions between India's official film institutions and its art-house filmmakers.

## Early Life & Background

Kapadia was born on 28 April 1986 in Bombay, now Mumbai. Her mother is Nalini Malani, one of India's pioneering video and installation artists, and Kapadia has said that growing up around experimental image-making shaped her approach to cinema. She was educated at Rishi Valley School in Andhra Pradesh and then studied economics at St. Xavier's College, Mumbai.

In 2012 she enrolled in the film direction programme at the Film and Television Institute of India (FTII) in Pune. During her studies she made several shorts, including *Watermelon, Fish and Half Ghost* (2014) and *The Last Mango Before the Monsoon* (2015), which won the FIPRESCI Prize and a Special Jury Mention at the Oberhausen International Short Film Festival.

## Rise to Prominence

Her short *Afternoon Clouds* (2017) was selected for the Cinéfondation section at Cannes, the only Indian film at the festival that year. A further short, *And What Is the Summer Saying* (2018), premiered at the Berlin International Film Festival.

*A Night of Knowing Nothing* premiered in the Directors' Fortnight at Cannes on 7 July 2021. Assembled from letters written by a fictional film student to an estranged lover, along with footage of student protests across India, it won the L'Œil d'Or for best documentary and travelled widely on the festival circuit.

*All We Imagine as Light*, a French-Indian co-production shot largely in Malayalam and Hindi, premiered in competition at Cannes on 23 May 2024. It follows two nurses from Kerala, played by Kani Kusruti and Divya Prabha, sharing a flat in Mumbai. The film won the Grand Prix two days later.

## Key Achievements

- **L'Œil d'Or, Cannes 2021**, for *A Night of Knowing Nothing*.
- **Grand Prix, Cannes 2024**, for *All We Imagine as Light*, the first Indian film to win the prize and the first in competition since *Swaham* in 1994.
- **Two Golden Globe nominations** at the 82nd ceremony in January 2025, for Best Director and Best Non-English Language Film. She was the first Indian filmmaker nominated for Best Director.
- **BAFTA nomination** for Best Film Not in the English Language in 2025, and the Gotham Award for Best International Feature.
- **Cannes competition jury member** in 2025 under president Juliette Binoche.

## Controversies & Criticism

In 2015 Kapadia took part in the 139-day FTII student strike against the appointment of actor Gajendra Chauhan as institute chairman. She was among students charged by Pune police over the confinement of a director during the protest, and FTII withheld her grant and barred her from an exchange programme. The institute later funded her 2017 Cannes trip, and its congratulations after the 2024 win were widely noted as ironic.

The Film Federation of India passed over *All We Imagine as Light* as the country's 2024 Academy Award submission in favour of *Laapataa Ladies*. Jury chair Jahnu Barua described her film as "a European film taking place in India", a remark that drew criticism from filmmakers and critics who argued the committee had rejected the year's most acclaimed Indian film. Some Indian commentators have also questioned whether her reliance on European co-financing shapes the films' perspective.

## Legacy & Influence

Kapadia's success demonstrated that Indian films made outside mainstream studio systems could compete for the highest festival honours. *All We Imagine as Light* appeared on numerous critics' lists of the best films of 2024, including a citation from Barack Obama, and it opened distribution paths for other independent South Asian productions.

Her hybrid method, combining documentary material with fiction, has influenced younger filmmakers at FTII and beyond. She has said she intends to continue setting films in Mumbai, with further features in development.
