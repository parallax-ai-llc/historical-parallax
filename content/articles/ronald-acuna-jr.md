## Summary

Ronald José Acuña Blanco Jr. is a Venezuelan professional baseball outfielder for the Atlanta Braves of Major League Baseball (MLB). Signed as an international amateur in 2014, he debuted in April 2018 at age 20 and won the National League Rookie of the Year Award that season.

Acuña is best known for his 2023 campaign, in which he became the first player in MLB history to hit 40 home runs and steal 70 bases in a single season. He was the unanimous NL Most Valuable Player that year. His combination of power, speed, and arm strength has made him one of the most recognizable players of his generation, though two torn anterior cruciate ligaments (ACLs) interrupted his prime years.

## Early Life & Background

Acuña was born on December 18, 1997, in La Guaira, Vargas state, Venezuela, and grew up in the coastal town of La Sabana. Baseball ran in the family. His father, Ronald Acuña Sr., spent eight seasons as a minor league outfielder in the New York Mets and Minnesota Twins organizations, and his grandfather Romualdo Blanco pitched in the minors. Cousins Alcides Escobar and Kelvim Escobar both played in the major leagues.

He signed with the Atlanta Braves on July 2, 2014, receiving a signing bonus of $100,000, a modest sum compared with the top international prospects of that class.

## Rise to Prominence

Acuña climbed rapidly through the Braves system. In 2017 he advanced from High-A to Triple-A in one season, hitting .325 with 21 home runs and 44 stolen bases, and was named Baseball America's Minor League Player of the Year. That autumn he became the youngest MVP in Arizona Fall League history at age 19.

He made his MLB debut on April 25, 2018, against the Cincinnati Reds. In August 2018 he homered in five consecutive games and hit leadoff home runs in three straight games. He finished with 26 home runs and 16 stolen bases in 111 games and won NL Rookie of the Year. On October 7, 2018, he became the youngest player to hit a postseason grand slam, in Game 3 of the NL Division Series against the Los Angeles Dodgers.

In April 2019 he signed an eight-year, $100 million contract extension with two club options that could extend it to ten years and $124 million. That season he hit 41 home runs and led the NL with 127 runs and 37 stolen bases, finishing three steals short of a 40-40 season.

## Key Achievements

- **2018 NL Rookie of the Year** and four-time All-Star selection (2019, 2021, 2022, 2023), plus a fifth selection in 2025.
- **2023 NL MVP**, awarded unanimously. He hit .337 with 41 home runs, 73 stolen bases, 217 hits, and 149 runs, leading MLB in hits, runs, steals, and on-base percentage (.416).
- **First 40-70 season in MLB history.** His 40th home run on September 27, 2023, against the Chicago Cubs also made him the fifth member of the 40-40 club. He was earlier the first player to reach 30 home runs and 60 steals.
- **Braves single-season stolen base record** with 73 in 2023.
- **Hank Aaron Award** and multiple Silver Slugger Awards, including 2019, 2020, and 2023.

## Controversies & Criticism

Acuña has faced repeated criticism over hustle. On August 18, 2019, manager Brian Snitker benched him mid-game for failing to run out a fly ball. Similar complaints resurfaced in 2022. In April 2025, while rehabbing, he posted and deleted a social media message referencing his 2019 benching after teammate Jarred Kelenic was not disciplined for a comparable lapse, prompting public discussion about inconsistent standards.

His 2019 extension drew commentary that the Braves had secured a team-friendly deal well below market value, though Acuña said he valued long-term security.

Injuries have shaped his career as much as any dispute. He tore his right ACL on July 10, 2021, missing the Braves' World Series championship run, and tore his left ACL on May 26, 2024, ending that season. He returned on May 23, 2025, homering in his first at-bat back.

## Legacy & Influence

Acuña's 2023 season redefined expectations for combined power and speed in the modern game and coincided with rule changes that encouraged base stealing. He remains a central figure for the Braves and a prominent Venezuelan athlete whose success has drawn attention to the country's baseball pipeline. Whether his post-injury years match his peak will determine his standing among the era's elite players.
