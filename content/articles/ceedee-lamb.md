## Summary

Cedarian DeLeon "CeeDee" Lamb (born April 8, 1999) is an American football wide receiver for the Dallas Cowboys of the National Football League (NFL). Selected 17th overall in the 2020 NFL Draft out of the University of Oklahoma, he has become one of the league's most productive pass-catchers and the focal point of the Cowboys' passing attack.

Lamb's 2023 season stands as the statistical peak of his career to date. He led the NFL with 135 receptions, set franchise single-season records for catches and receiving yards (1,749), and earned first-team All-Pro honors. In August 2024 he signed a four-year, $136 million contract extension, at the time one of the largest deals ever given to a wide receiver.

He is widely regarded as a top-tier receiver of his generation, combining route-running, contested-catch ability, and yards-after-catch production. His inheritance of the Cowboys' No. 88 jersey, previously worn by Drew Pearson, Michael Irvin, and Dez Bryant, places him in a lineage that carries significant weight in Dallas.

## Early Life & Background

Lamb was born in Opelousas, Louisiana. After Hurricane Katrina struck in 2005, his family relocated to the Houston, Texas area. He attended John and Randolph Foster High School in Richmond, Texas, where he emerged as one of the state's top receiving prospects. As a senior in 2016, he recorded 98 receptions for 2,032 yards and 33 touchdowns and was rated a four-star recruit.

He committed to the University of Oklahoma, choosing the Sooners over programs including Texas and Texas A&M.

## Rise to Prominence

At Oklahoma from 2017 to 2019, Lamb played alongside Heisman Trophy quarterbacks Baker Mayfield, Kyler Murray, and Jalen Hurts. He totaled 173 receptions for 3,292 yards and 32 touchdowns in three seasons and helped the Sooners win three consecutive Big 12 championships and reach the College Football Playoff each year. His 2019 season (62 catches, 1,327 yards, 14 touchdowns) earned him unanimous All-American recognition and a Biletnikoff Award finalist nod.

The Cowboys drafted him in the first round in April 2020. As a rookie he caught 74 passes for 935 yards and five touchdowns despite quarterback Dak Prescott's season-ending injury in Week 5. Lamb made his first Pro Bowl in 2021 (79 receptions, 1,102 yards) and took over as the team's primary receiver after Amari Cooper was traded in March 2022.

## Key Achievements

- **2022:** 107 receptions, 1,359 yards, 9 touchdowns; Pro Bowl and second-team All-Pro.
- **2023:** NFL-leading 135 receptions, 1,749 receiving yards, 12 receiving touchdowns; first-team All-Pro; broke Michael Irvin's 1995 franchise yardage record (1,603). On December 30, 2023, he caught 13 passes for 227 yards against Detroit, a Cowboys single-game record.
- **2024:** 101 receptions and 1,194 yards while playing through a shoulder injury; fourth consecutive Pro Bowl selection before being placed on injured reserve in December.
- **Contract:** Four-year, $136 million extension signed August 26, 2024, including $100 million guaranteed.
- Through his first five seasons he became the fastest Cowboys player to reach 5,000 career receiving yards.

## Controversies & Criticism

Lamb's 2024 holdout drew criticism from segments of the fan base and media. He skipped mandatory minicamp and all of training camp before the extension was finalized days before the season, and team owner Jerry Jones publicly aired frustration during negotiations.

On the field, he has been criticized for visible sideline frustration directed at coaches and quarterbacks, particularly during losses in 2023 and 2024. During the 2025 season, a stretch of uncharacteristic dropped passes, including several in key moments, prompted scrutiny of his focus, and a high ankle sprain suffered in September 2025 sidelined him for multiple games. Critics have also pointed to the absence of postseason success, as the Cowboys have not advanced past the Divisional Round during his tenure.

## Legacy & Influence

Lamb's career remains in progress, but his 2023 season is already among the best by any receiver in Cowboys history, and he has re-established the No. 88 jersey as a marquee position on one of the NFL's most visible franchises. His extension helped reset the receiver market alongside contemporaries Justin Jefferson and Ja'Marr Chase. Whether his legacy ultimately matches Irvin's will depend largely on postseason results in the years ahead.
