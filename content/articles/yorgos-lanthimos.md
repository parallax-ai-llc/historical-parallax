## Summary

Yorgos Lanthimos (born 23 September 1973) is a Greek film director, screenwriter, and producer whose deadpan, formally rigorous films have made him one of the most distinctive voices in twenty-first-century cinema. Beginning with low-budget Greek features such as *Dogtooth* (2009), he became the central figure of what critics labelled the "Greek Weird Wave," then moved into English-language filmmaking with *The Lobster* (2015) without softening his sensibility.

His films are recognisable by their flat line delivery, absurd rule-bound societies, wide-angle and fisheye lenses, and sudden eruptions of violence or sexuality. *The Favourite* (2018) and *Poor Things* (2023) brought him mainstream recognition, earning a combined 21 Academy Award nominations and establishing his long-running collaboration with actress Emma Stone. He has won major prizes at Cannes, Venice, and the BAFTAs and has been nominated for the Academy Award for Best Director twice.

## Early Life & Background

Lanthimos was born and raised in Athens, Greece. His father was a basketball player and coach, and Lanthimos played the sport competitively as a teenager before turning to film. He studied business briefly before enrolling at the Hellenic Cinema and Television School Stavrakos in Athens, where he trained in film and television directing.

During the 1990s he directed television commercials, music videos, experimental theatre productions, and dance videos. He was also part of the creative team behind the opening and closing ceremonies of the 2004 Athens Olympic Games.

## Rise to Prominence

His first feature, *My Best Friend* (2001), was co-directed with comedian Lakis Lazopoulos. *Kinetta* (2005), his solo debut, premiered at the Toronto International Film Festival and established his interest in ritual and repetition. The breakthrough came with *Dogtooth* (2009), a story of three adult children imprisoned by their parents in a suburban compound. It won the Un Certain Regard prize at the 2009 Cannes Film Festival and was nominated for Best Foreign Language Film at the 83rd Academy Awards. *Alps* (2011) followed, winning the Best Screenplay award at the Venice Film Festival.

*The Lobster* (2015), starring Colin Farrell and Rachel Weisz, was his first English-language film. It won the Jury Prize at Cannes and earned Lanthimos and co-writer Efthymis Filippou an Oscar nomination for Best Original Screenplay. *The Killing of a Sacred Deer* (2017) shared the Best Screenplay award at Cannes.

## Key Achievements

- **The Favourite (2018)**: Won the Grand Jury Prize at Venice, received 10 Academy Award nominations including Best Picture and Best Director, and won 7 BAFTAs. Olivia Colman won the Academy Award for Best Actress.
- **Poor Things (2023)**: Won the Golden Lion at Venice and the Golden Globe for Best Motion Picture, Musical or Comedy. It received 11 Academy Award nominations and won 4, including Best Actress for Emma Stone.
- **Kinds of Kindness (2024)**: Competed at Cannes, where Jesse Plemons won the Best Actor award.
- **Bugonia (2025)**: An English-language reworking of the Korean film *Save the Green Planet!*, starring Stone and Plemons. It premiered in competition at the 2025 Venice Film Festival and was released in October 2025.

He has also directed the short films *Nimic* (2019) and *Bleat* (2022) and has published books of his on-set photography.

## Controversies & Criticism

Lanthimos's work has repeatedly divided critics and audiences. *Dogtooth* drew objections for its depictions of incest and animal violence, and *The Killing of a Sacred Deer* was criticised for its cruelty toward children within the plot. Some reviewers have described his films as cold, misanthropic, or mannered, treating characters as specimens rather than people.

*Poor Things* prompted debate over its explicit sex scenes involving a protagonist whose mind begins as that of a child, with some commentators questioning whether the film's feminist framing was undermined by a male director's gaze. Others noted that it discarded the Scottish setting and political framing of Alasdair Gray's 1992 novel. *Kinds of Kindness* received notably mixed reviews, with critics arguing that its three-part structure repeated his themes without extending them.

## Legacy & Influence

Lanthimos is widely credited with bringing Greek cinema to international attention after the 2008 financial crisis and with proving that an uncompromising art-house style can succeed commercially. His recurring collaborators, including Filippou, cinematographer Robbie Ryan, editor Yorgos Mavropsaridis, and Stone, have formed one of the most consistent creative teams in contemporary film. His influence is visible in a generation of filmmakers who use affectless performance and absurd premises to examine social conformity, power, and control.
