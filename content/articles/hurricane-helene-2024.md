## Summary
Hurricane Helene struck Florida's Big Bend as a Category 4 storm late on 26 September 2024 and then drove catastrophic flooding deep into the southern Appalachians [^1]. With at least 253 deaths and about $78.7 billion in damage attributed to it, Helene was the deadliest hurricane to hit the U.S. mainland since Katrina in 2005 [^1]. Western North Carolina was hit hardest, with more than 100 deaths, more than 2,000 landslides and record river crests [^1][^2]. Rebuilding roads, water systems and communities in the mountains is expected to take years [^3].

## Background
Helene began as a broad low in the western Caribbean on 22 September 2024 and became a named tropical storm on 24 September near the Yucatan Peninsula [^1]. Very warm Gulf of Mexico waters fed rapid intensification, and it reached Category 4 on the evening of 26 September [^1]. After landfall it moved quickly inland, carrying strong winds and heavy rain into Georgia, the Carolinas and Tennessee before stalling over Tennessee [^1].

## The Event
Helene made landfall near Perry, Florida, with maximum sustained winds of 140 mph, the strongest hurricane on record to strike the Big Bend [^1][^2]. Storm surge damaged communities from Tampa Bay northward, and wind damage spread across southern Georgia, where crop and timber losses reached about $5.5 billion [^1]. As the storm moved inland it caused wind damage around Augusta and flooding in metro Atlanta, and more than 7.4 million customers lost power across the country [^1].

The worst disaster came in the southern Appalachians. Rain totals reached 30.78 inches at Busick, North Carolina, and three major rivers broke their historical crest records [^2]. Asheville and surrounding Buncombe County were cut off, Chimney Rock village was largely destroyed, and thousands of miles of roads and bridges were damaged, including sections of Interstate 40 in the Pigeon River Gorge [^1][^3]. Helene weakened to a post-tropical cyclone over Tennessee on 27 September and dissipated on 29 September [^1].

## Response and Aftermath
The National Hurricane Center's final report, released in March 2025, counted at least 175 direct deaths, with North Carolina the deadliest state and South Carolina second [^2]. Flooding of a Baxter International plant in Marion, North Carolina, which made about 60 percent of the country's sterile intravenous and dialysis fluids, caused IV fluid shortages and rationing in hospitals through the end of 2024 [^1]. Federal, state and volunteer crews carried out search and rescue in isolated mountain valleys [^1]. False claims about the Federal Emergency Management Agency spread online, and threats led federal aid workers to briefly pause or relocate work in Rutherford County [^1].

## Positive Perspectives
- North Carolina's Department of Transportation reported that more than 99 percent of roads closed by Helene had reopened by July 2026 [^3].
- Transportation Secretary Daniel Johnson said the agency had "completed decades' worth of work in less than two years" [^3].
- The NHC produced a detailed final report within six months, documenting rainfall, wind and fatality data to guide future planning [^2].
- Communities and volunteers across the region organised large-scale relief and rescue efforts in isolated mountain areas [^1].

## Negative Perspectives
- North Carolina, hundreds of miles from the landfall point, recorded the most deaths of any state, showing how deadly the inland threat proved [^2].
- Some Swannanoa residents said no search and rescue teams had reached them as of 29 September [^1].
- The Baxter plant flood exposed how concentrated production of essential medical supplies had become [^1].
- Misinformation and threats against FEMA workers disrupted relief efforts [^1].
- Major routes remain incomplete: the $2 billion rebuild of I-40 is expected to run until late 2028, and U.S. 176 between Saluda and Tryon until late 2029 [^3].

## Recent News
As of July 2026, NCDOT said more than 99 percent of Helene-damaged roads had reopened, with permanent repairs in the Hickory Nut Gorge near Chimney Rock and Lake Lure targeted for 2029 [^3].
