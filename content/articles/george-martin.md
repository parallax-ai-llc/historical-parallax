## Summary

Sir George Henry Martin (3 January 1926 – 8 March 2016) was a British record producer, arranger, composer and audio engineer, best known for producing nearly every recording by the Beatles between 1962 and 1970. Often called the "Fifth Beatle," he translated the band's ideas into orchestral scores, tape experiments and studio techniques that redefined what a pop record could be.

Trained in classical music and seasoned on comedy records at EMI's Parlophone label, Martin brought both formal knowledge and an appetite for novelty to the studio. His string arrangement for "Yesterday" (1965), the sped-up baroque piano solo on "In My Life" and the orchestral crescendo of "A Day in the Life" (1967) are among the most recognisable moments in popular music.

Beyond the Beatles, he founded AIR Studios, produced dozens of hits for artists from Cilla Black to Jeff Beck, and in 1997 produced "Candle in the Wind 1997," the best-selling single in UK chart history. He received six Grammy Awards, a CBE in 1988 and a knighthood in 1996.

## Early Life & Background

Martin was born in Highbury, north London, the son of a carpenter. His family had no musical training, but he taught himself piano after the family acquired one, and by his teens he was leading a dance band. He attended St Ignatius' College and Bromley County Grammar School.

He served in the Fleet Air Arm of the Royal Navy from 1943 to 1947, then studied piano and oboe at the Guildhall School of Music and Drama from 1947 to 1950. After a short spell at the BBC's classical music department, he joined EMI's Parlophone label in November 1950 as assistant to its head, Oscar Preuss. When Preuss retired in 1955, Martin, aged 29, took over the label.

## Rise to Prominence

At Parlophone, Martin produced classical, jazz and, distinctively, comedy records, including hits by Peter Sellers, Spike Milligan, Flanders and Swann and the cast of Beyond the Fringe. Editing gags and sound effects on tape gave him the technical fluency he later applied to rock.

On 6 June 1962 he auditioned the Beatles at Abbey Road after other labels had rejected them. He signed the group, judged drummer Pete Best inadequate for recording, and used session drummer Andy White on the single version of "Love Me Do." From "Please Please Me" (1963) onward, Martin's productions dominated the British charts; in 1963 alone his records held the UK number one spot for 37 weeks.

In 1965 he left EMI, whose flat salary gave him no royalties on his hits, and founded Associated Independent Recording (AIR) with three fellow producers. AIR Studios opened in London in 1969, and AIR Montserrat followed in 1979 before Hurricane Hugo destroyed it in 1989.

## Key Achievements

- Produced all Beatles albums except *Let It Be*, including *Revolver* (1966), *Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band* (1967) and *Abbey Road* (1969).
- Scored the films *A Hard Day's Night* (1964), earning an Academy Award nomination, *Yellow Submarine* (1968) and *Live and Let Die* (1973).
- Produced 30 UK number one singles and 23 in the United States.
- Produced Jeff Beck's *Blow by Blow* (1975), America's mid-1970s hits, Paul McCartney's *Tug of War* (1982) and Elton John's "Candle in the Wind 1997."
- Oversaw *The Beatles Anthology* (1995–96) and, with his son Giles, the *Love* remix album for Cirque du Soleil (2006).
- Author of the memoir *All You Need Is Ears* (1979).

## Controversies & Criticism

The replacement of Pete Best has drawn criticism, though Martin maintained he only objected to Best's studio playing and did not demand his dismissal. In 1971 John Lennon wrote to Martin objecting that a press interview overstated the producer's role in the band's music, a tension over credit that recurred in later Beatles histories. Martin himself was publicly critical of Phil Spector's orchestral overdubs on *Let It Be* (1970), and he declined to work on the project after Spector's involvement. Some critics have also noted that his post-Beatles output rarely matched the innovation of the 1960s work.

## Legacy & Influence

Martin established the producer as a creative collaborator rather than a technician, a model followed by Brian Eno, Rick Rubin and many others. Techniques he popularised, including varispeed, tape loops and orchestration in pop, became standard. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1999. Martin died at his home in Wiltshire on 8 March 2016, aged 90.
